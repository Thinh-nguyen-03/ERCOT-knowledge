ORDER REINSTATING LOW SYSTEM-WIDE OFFER CAP
On February 12, 2021, in response to an extreme winter weather event, Governor Greg
Abbott issued a Declaration ofa State ofDisaster for all counties in Texas. Further, on Februaryl 5,

2021, the Electric Reliability Council of Texas, Inc. (ERCOT) declared its highest state of
emergency, an Energy Emergency Alert Level 3 (EEA3), due to exceptionally high electric

demand exceeding limited supply. ERCOT remained in EEA3 through 9AM on February 19,
2021. Recovery from the emergency conditions is continuing as market activities stabilize.

On February 16, 2021, in response to significant market anomalies, the Commission found

good cause to suspend, and issued an order suspending, the use of the low system-wide offer cap
(LCAP) until after the Commission's next regularly scheduled open meeting. The Commission
ordered ERCOT to use the high system-wide offer cap (HCAP) until the LCAP is reinstated.
When administering the scarcity-pricing mechanism, the system-wide offer cap is set equal

to the HCAP at the beginning of each calendar year and maintained at this level until the peaker

net margin during a calendar year exceeds a threshold of three times the cost of new entry of new

generation plants.1 Once the peaker-net-margin threshold is achieved, the system-wide offer cap
is set at the LCAP for the remainder of the calendar year.2 The LCAP is set at the greater of either

$2,000 per megawatt-hour (MWh) and $2,000 per megawatt (MW) per hour or 50 times the
natural-gas-price-index value determined by ERCOT expressed in dollars per MWh and dollars
per MW per hour.3

The peaker-net-margin threshold was exceeded during the ERCOT operating day of
February 16, 2021.

Due to exceptionally high natural gas prices at the time, and without

scarcity pricing mechanism as provided by Commission rule.
