ERCOT PROPOSAL TO ADJUST LONG-TERM LOAD FORECAST
Pursuant to 16 Texas Administrative Code (TAC) § 25.370(e)(2)(B), Electric Reliability
Council of Texas, Inc. (ERCOT) requests that the 2026 Preliminary Long-Term Load Forecast
(LTLF) be adjusted as described in this filing.

As discussed by the Public Utility Commission of Texas (Commission) at the May 7,2026,

Open Meeting, this proposal is anticipated to be considered by the Commission at the June 18,
2026, Open Meeting. Accordingly, this is timely filed. 1

I.

BACKGROUND

On April 15, 2026, ERCOT filed the LTLF in Project No. 58777 in accordance with

ERCOT's recommendation that it be used for the load forecast modeling input to Strategic Energy
& Risk Valuation Model (SERVM) for performance ofthe 2026 Reliability Assessment. 2 ERCOT
previously recommended that the LTLF be used for the load forecast modeling input to the

Reliability Assessment in order to ensure that a consistent load forecast would be used across all
transmission planning and resource adequacy assessment initiatives.

The LTLF was developed

using criteria from the 2026 RIP Compliance Plan, which establishes interim criteria for the
inclusion of the load of a Large Load customer in ERCOT's load forecasts until the permanent

criteria recently adopted in 16 TAC § 25.370 can be implemented. 3

The LTLF forecasts peak load is 278,003 megawatts (MW) in 2029 and 367,790 MW in

2032. In its April 15th filing, ERCOT indicated that it had concerns with using these values in the
Reliability Assessment and any other transmission and resource adequacy analysis.

At the

Commission' s April 17, 2026, Open Meeting, the Commission indicated that adjusting the LTLF
to a more realistic value may be warranted. At the May 7,2026, Open Meeting, ERCOT confirmed
to the Commission that it would propose an adjustment to the LTLF.

16 TAC § 25.370(e) requires ERCOT to use the load data provided by Distribution Service
Providers (DSPs) for transmission planning studies and resource adequacy assessments. The rule

provides only two avenues for ERCOT to adjust the DSP-provided load data: (1) to correct errors
or account for the withdrawal or cancellation of a Large Load customer' s request for

interconnection, or (2) in consultation with Commission Staff, to request approval from the
Commission to adjust the load data if "the adjustment is supported by actual historical realization
rates or other objective, credible, independent information."

The latter process in subsection

(e)(2)(B) requires that ERCOT file its request for adjustment with the Commission at least 30 days

prior to the Open Meeting at which the Commission is requested to consider the adjustment
proposal.

The process also requires ERCOT to publish an associated Market Notice.

The

Commission retains discretion to consider the matter at an earlier open meeting. ERCOT must

include in its request in detail the data, methodology, and calculations used for the proposed
adjustment and the specific reasoning for the request. Unless the Commission establishes a
different deadline, public comment on ERCOT's request is due 14 days after ERCOT's filing.
II.

PROPOSED ADJUSTMENT TO PRELIMINARY LONG-TERM LOAD FORECAST

Due to the unprecedented scale of the load included in the LTLF and known supply chain

constraints extending through the medium term which bring into question the likelihood of the
realization timeline for the provided load forecast values, ERCOT requests that the process in 16
TAC § 25.370(e)(2)(B) be used to adjust the LTLF to a more realistic forecast. After consulting

with Commission Staff and some Transmission and/or Distribution Service Providers (TDSPs),
ERCOT proposes two options to adjust the LTLF: one adjustment option using a historical
realization rate and the other option using the forecast developed in the Large Load Batch Zero

process. As described in greater detail below, ERCOT recommends that the Batch Zero load
forecast option be used to adjust the LTLF, including for use as the load forecast input for the 2026

Triennial Reliability Assessment modeling.

2 of 9

A. Adjustment Option Using Historical Realization Rate
One option to adjust the LTLF is based on the historical realization rates ofthe Large Loads

identified in the TDSPs ' responses to ERCOT ' s 2025 Large Load Request for Information (RFI )
Under this approach, ERCOT would propose to calculate the historical realization rate as the
number of computational Large Loads that were observed to have been energized by March 1,

2026 , divided by the number of computational Large Loads identified by TDSPs in the 2025 Large

Load RFI response . In recent years , ERCOT has annually issued a Large Load RFI to all TDSPs
in the ERCOT Region to gather information on expected load to inform ERCOT's forecasts and
studies. TDSPs are required to provide information on Large Loads (i.e., 75 MW or greater).
Among other information, the 2025 RFI requested each Large Load' s name and location, load

type, the anticipated month that the load will first be energized, the initial MW amount to be
energized, and the load energization MW ramp schedule from summer 2025 through summer

2035 . ERCOT issued the 2025 Large Load RFI to TDSPs on December 5 , 2024 , and responses

were received by January 31,2025.4
In calculating the historical realization rates, ERCOT would limit its evaluation to
computational Large Loads (i.e., data centers and cryptomining facilities).

Other Large Load

types, such as oil and gas and industrial loads, have a lengthy history in the ERCOT Region, which
provides greater confidence in their energization and ramp rate timeframes. Furthermore, although

"medium loads" (i.e., greater than 25 MW but less than 75 Mw) are also included in the LTLF,5

ERCOT does not propose to adjust those because that category was not addressed in the 2026 RTP
Compliance Plan and accordingly they do not have the same criteria as Large Loads. If medium
loads were adjusted, this would have a relatively minimal impact on the LTLF because they
represent a relatively small portion ofthe LTLF compared to core base economic growth and Large

Loads: for instance, in the LTLF for 2032 a total of 258 medium loads were included totaling

13,473 MW, ofwhich 157 are computational medium loads totaling 8,766 MW.

Second, this adjustment option can be applied

will be built to serve the anticipated load.
B. Adjustment Option Using Batch Zero Forecast
A second option to adjust the LTLF is to use the Large Load forecast developed through

the Batch Study process for Batch Zero, which is currently being finalized in Planning Guide

Revision Request (PGRR) 145. As the Commission is aware, ERCOT has been working with
stakeholders to transition from the current Large Load Interconnection Study process implemented
through PGRR115, which entailed sequential study of Large Load interconnection requests, to a

Batch Study process in which clusters ofLarge Load interconnection requests that have met certain
milestones are studied together. Transmission can then be developed based on the Large Load

confirmed through the Batch Study process. PGRR145's Batch Zero would be a one-time process
to facilitate the transition to this new study approach. For the purposes of the Batch Zero study,

PGRR145 proposes to categorize Large Loads into three categories: load that has already been
sufficiently studied for interconnection and therefore is considered "base load" for Batch Zero,8
load that requires additional interconnection study in Batch Zero ("studied load"),9 and load that
has not met sufficient criteria to be included in Batch Zero and therefore will require study under

The benefits of using the Batch Zero forecast to adjust the LTLF include that it will

ultimately result in an actionable transmission development plan (after a refinement study) and
therefore is a firmer starting point of anticipated load. This is because the TSPs will execute on a

transmission plan to build out the ERCOT System based on the loads identified through the Batch
Zero process. Furthermore, the Batch Zero forecast is based on criteria that have received greater

industry scrutiny than the criteria in the 2026 RTP Compliance Plan, which was developed before
the extensive stakeholder discussions on PGRR145. In accordance with the requirement of 16
TAC § 25.370(e)(2)(B), use of the Batch Zero load forecast to adjust the LTLF would be

"objective, credible, independent information" because it is load information provided in a manner

The primary drawback of using the Batch Zero forecast to adjust the LTLF is the delay

until mid-August 2026 when the LTLF could be finalized.

ERCOT anticipates that this would

delay the delivery of the results of the 2026 Triennial Reliability Assessment until November or
December 2026.

Although this adjustment option would result in greater delay than the historical realization
rate adjustment option, ERCOT recommends that the Batch Zero adjustment option is justified

because the benefit of a more reliable load forecast outweighs the cost of the additional time
required. Accordingly, ERCOT recommends that the Commission approve ERCOT's use of the
Batch Zero load forecast to adjust the LTLF and that this adjusted LTLF be used for performance

ofthe 2026 Triennial Reliability Assessment.

C. Market Notice and Next Steps
As required by 16 TAC § 25.370(e)(2)(B), ERCOT is issuing a Market Notice to solicit
public comment on this adjustment proposal.12

Pending any alternative schedule that the

Commission may provide, responsive comments would be due 14 days after this filing by June 1,

2026.
Due to regulatory timelines established in the Protocols and necessary development time,

any adjustment to the LTLF will impact ERCOT's transmission planning and resource adequacy
studies, including specifically the Capacity, Demand, and Reserves (CDR) Report, the Regional
Transmission Plan (RTP), and the Triennial Reliability Assessment.

Based on the anticipated

impact to these development timelines, ERCOT is separately filing in Project No. 55999 a request
for good cause exceptions to not perform the May 2026 CDR and the 2026 RTP.

If the

Commission grants those requests, ERCOT will nevertheless provide certain limited information
in lieu ofthe May 2026 CDR and the 2026 RTP. Additional details are included in that filing.
