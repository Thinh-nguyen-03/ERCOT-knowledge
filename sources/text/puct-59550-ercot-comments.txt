ERCOT COMMENTS IN RESPONSE TO COMMISSION STAFF QUESTIONS
Electric Reliability Council of Texas, Inc. (ERCOT) files these comments in response to

the Public Utility Commission of Texas (Commission) Staff' s Questions for Comment filed on
August 20,2026. The deadline to file comments is September 17,2026, therefore these comments
are timely filed. An executive summary is included as the last page to this filing.
I.

COMMENTS TO QUESTIONS

Pursuant to 16 Texas Administrative Code (TAC) § 25.509(d), the Commission is required

to "review each of the system-wide offer cap programs to determine whether to update aspects of
each program." This proceeding initiates the first such review and subsequent reviews are to be

performed quinquennially (i.e., every five years) hereafter.
ERCOT agrees that it is appropriate to evaluate such programs on this cadence given the
continuing changes in the ERCOT Region in order to ensure that the values and formula settings

of these programs are set at the appropriate levels to achieve their intended purposes. ERCOT

here provides initial responses to Commission Staff' s Questions and looks forward to continuing
to engage with stakeholders and the Commission as this review proceeds.
A. Ouestion 1(a): Scarcity Pricing Mechanism (SPM). The current Low System-Wide
Offer Cap (LCAP) is $2,000 per MWh for energy offers and $2,000 per MW per hour
for ancillary service (AS) offers. The current High System-Wide Offer Cap (HCAP)
is $5,000 per MWh for energy offers and $5,000 per MW per hour for ancillary
service offers. Do either of these values need to be updated? Why?
ERCOT does not have a position at this time whether the LCAP and HCAP should be

changed but supports the performance of a holistic evaluation of whether those offer caps should
be updated. If the results ofthe Triennial Reliability Assessment indicate that reliability standard

of 16 TAC § 25.508 for the ERCOT Region is not being met, ERCOT intends to provide
recommendations in Project No. 58777 for market design changes and would evaluate the HCAP

B. Ouestion 1(b): With the implementation of RTC+B, there is a difference between the
day ahead (DAM) and real-time (RTM) system offer caps for both energy and AS.
The DAM offer cap is $5,000 per MWh per hour, and the RTM offer cap is
$2,000/MWh. Should this difference continue? Why?

ERCOT recommends that the current approach, in which the SWCAP in RTM is set at
$2,000 and the SWCAP in DAM is set at the effective VOLL, be maintained. The use of different
offer caps for DAM and RTM was a deliberate design decision reflected in design principles for

RTC+B.2 Given the voluntary nature of DAM (willing buyer and willing seller) and the lack of

Beyond the ability to hedge and indicate anticipated high-pricing days in the RTM, the

DAM and RTM differential also has a fundamental role as the single most important control on
uncollateralizeable credit exposure in the market. The reason lies in where credit constraints can

and cannot bind. A Market Participant can be shut out of the DAM when it exhausts its available
credit limit, so Day-Ahead exposure is inherently credit-constrained. Real-Time exposure is not:

an LSE remains exposed to Real-Time settlement regardless of price and cannot be "locked out"

of serving its load. The SWCAP in the RTM accordingly acts as the ceiling on the exposure that

ERCOT cannot prevent through ordinary credit administration and that, in a default, would be
uplifted to the market. Keeping the SWCAP for RTM at $2,000 with a DAM SWCAP of VOLL

concentrates the higher price ceiling in the market segment where credit limits can bind and holds
down the ceiling in the segment where they cannot. Converging the two caps upward would raise
the uncollateralizable Real-Time exposure by up to 150% (see Table 1, which relates to a stress-

testing framework currently being developed by ERCOT that is further described in response to

Question 4 below) and undo a core post-Winter Storm (WS) Uri protection. Therefore, this is
another reason that ERCOT recommends that a difference between the DAM and RTM SWCAPs

be maintained.

C. Ouestion 1(c): Should any of the scarcity pricing mechanism features listed below be
modified? Why? i. Peaker net margin calculation; ii. LCAP trigger threshold; and iii.
Reimbursement for operating losses when the LCAP is in effect.
At this time, ERCOT does not have a position on whether modifications should be

considered to the Peaker Net Margin (PNM) calculation, LCAP trigger threshold, or
reimbursement for operating losses when LCAP is in effect. ERCOT intends to consider these as
part of its due diligence prior to recommending market design changes, if any, in the Triennial

Reliability Assessment process.
In regard to the credit risk impact of the PNM calculation and the LCAP trigger threshold,

note that both function as levers on the duration input to market credit exposure. A trigger design
that causes the LCAP to engage sooner, or a PNM construction that shortens the interval of highest

prices, reduces the duration term and therefore reduces market stress for credit exposure; the
converse would increase it. ERCOT recommends that if a change to either is considered that any

such change be evaluated for its effect on scarcity duration in addition to its effect on pricing.
Cost of New Entry (CONE) is a key component of the PNM calculation. Note that the
CONE for planning purposes is currently distinct from the CONE for PNM calculation purposes.
A CONE value of $105 per kilowatt year (kW-year) is used to calculate PNM.3 Following a study
performed by The Brattle Group (Brattle) in 2024, CONE for planning purposes was set to $140

per kilowatt year (kW-year).4 Brattle is currently concluding a CONE study to update that value
for planning purposes in association with the Triennial Reliability Assessment. 5 Because the

PNM-related CONE value of $105 per kW-year is now dated at more than a decade old and
because the same factors that led to the increase in the planning CONE also affect the PNM,

ERCOT recommends that the Commission should reevaluate the value of CONE used to calculate
the PNM threshold. Because gas-fired Generation Resources currently represent approximately

F. Ouestion 4: What credit risk concerns should the Commission consider if either the
SPM or the EPP were to be adjusted, and how should existing credit obligations be

recalibrated in response?
As explained in the responses above, the SWCAP programs are not only price-formation

and reliability instruments but are also the primary inputs that determine market-wide credit

exposure and accordingly the collateral that the market must carry to absorb a severe system
scarcity event. Raising the SWCAP or ECAP, or constricting the EPP's trigger or duration, would
inherently and proportionally increase market-wide financial stress exposure. This is not a matter

of forecast or judgment; it follows mechanically from how scarcity-priced exposure accrues and
from how ERCOT's stress-testing framework (currently under development and described further
below) measures it. For that reason, any change to price or offer caps or the duration of such

ERCOT is working with an outside consultant to develop a stress-testing framework whose
purpose is to identify, measure, and mitigate potential default risk before it materializes. ERCOT

has discussed at the Technical Advisory Committee's Credit Finance Subgroup (CFSG) that

development of this stress-testing framework is underway and plans to present the framework to
stakeholders upon completion. After advancing the framework through CFSG, ERCOT plans to
incorporate the process into the ERCOT Protocols in an NPRR. The framework measures each
Market Participant' s potential exposure in a severe scarcity event and compares it against that
Participant' s available liquidity and capital.

The measurement is centered on the concept that

financial exposure at default is a function of volume, price, and duration . 1_n this proceeding ,
the Commission is reviewing two ofthose parameters: price and duration. In the framework, price
is bounded by the applicable offer cap: HCAP, LCAP, or ECAP. The framework treats duration

as the number of hours that scarcity pricing persists, which is shaped by the trigger design of the
SPM and by the effectiveness window of the EPP when activated. The framework' s price and
duration assumptions are taken from the EPP-pricing scenario and applied across Market
Participants.

The third framework parameter, volume, is the Market Participant-specific

characteristic ofthe amount of load exposed to the RTM.
Because exposure scales linearly with both price and duration, any increase in a cap or in
the EPP's duration window flows through to exposure with no offsetting mechanism. The

framework then measures resilience with an exposure coverage ratio:
Exposure Coverage Ratio = Available Liquidity + Potential Exposure.
Available liquidity is effectively fixed in the days surrounding a system stress event.
Raising the cap or extending the EPP duration therefore increases the denominator (i.e., potential
exposure) while the numerator (i.e., available liquidity) holds constant. Every affected Market
Participant' s coverage ratio falls, more Market Participants cross below the framework' s high-risk

threshold, and the aggregate stress exposure that the ERCOT market must be prepared to absorb
rises. The mitigation required-collateral or potentially credit insurance-is itself a function of
exposure at default, so it rises in lockstep. This is the sense in which a change to the caps or the
EPP duration increases financial stress exposure inherently and proportionally, as previously

stated.

Tables 1 and 2 illustrate this linkage using an illustrative scarcity window offour days (i.e.,

96 hours), which is in line with the approximately 104-hour emergency period experienced during
WS Uri. The amounts are per megawatt of Real-Time-exposed load and are provided to show
proportionality, not to forecast any particular Market Participant' s exposure. These make clear

that at the current $2,000 SWCAP in the RTM, a four-day event accrues roughly $192,000 of
exposure per exposed megawatt, converging the RTM SWCAP upward to the $5,000 DAM

SWCAP level, which would raise that to approximately $480,000, a 150% increase, before any
change in Market Participant behavior. Extending the EPP duration window produces the same

proportional effect through the duration term. Neither lever is offset elsewhere in the market

design, which is why the credit impact is inherent rather than contingent.
ERCOT recommends that the Commission incorporate the stress-testing framework and
credit-related ERCOT Protocols, including Protocols § 16.11 and the Total Potential Exposure

(TPE) calculation, as a companion consideration of any SWCAP program change contemplated in
the quinquennial review.

This will ensure that price-formation and credit-risk decisions are

considered together rather than in separate proceedings.

The outputs of the stress-testing

framework-including an annual, publicly available stress-testing results report-would provide
the Commission and ERCOT market stakeholders a recurring, quantified view of how the offer
cap and EPP settings translate into credit exposure over time.
