Governor Abbott’s August 3 directive

PUBLIC

August 3, 2026 Directive

Key takeaways

Governor Abbott directed the Commission and ERCOT to complete “a comprehensive
verification and audit” of all data centers advancing through the interconnection process
before allowing those loads to proceed.

• ERCOT took immediate
actions to address Governor
Abbott’s directive.

Actions taken by ERCOT
•

August 3, 2026 – Issued a Market Notice pausing the Batch Zero study process and
delaying final classification of all Large Loads.

•

Paused approvals to energize data centers or virtual currency mining facilities (crypto
facilities) that are 75 MW or greater.

•

August 10, 2026 – Filed with Commission a request for three good cause exceptions
for PGRR 145 along with a status update on related matters.
•

•

• The verification and audit
process will have an impact
on the Batch Zero process
and timeline.

The Commission granted all three requests on August 20, 2026.

Planned activities to address the August 3rd directive.

Key Changes to Batch Zero and the Large Load Interconnection
Process in Response to Governor Abbott’s Directive

PUBLIC

1

ERCOT has begun a verification and audit of the eligibility of all Large Loads
provisionally included in Batch Zero. ERCOT will file the Batch Zero Eligibility
Verification Report with the Commission by December 10, 2026.

2

ERCOT will send RFIs to the Interconnecting Transmission Service Providers and
Distribution Service Providers of both medium Load (25-75 MW) and Large Load
data centers and crypto mining facilities to gather certain information regarding the
impact of these Loads on their communities. ERCOT will file the Community Impact
Review Report with the Commission by December 10, 2026.

Key Takeaways:
•

Energization pause impacts Large Load
data centers and crypto facilities not yet
connected to the ERCOT system. Large
Loads must meet eligibility verification to
move forward.

•

Two separate and distinct work streams.
 “Verification and audit” process
impacts Batch Zero interconnection
process.

ERCOT has paused the energization of new Large Load data centers and crypto
mining facilities until the completion of the verification and audit and community
impact review.
ERCOT provided provisional Batch Zero Large Load project classification (base,
studied, excluded) to the Interconnecting TSPs on September 3, 2026. Final project
inclusion/ exclusion determination will be based on the results of the eligibility
verification and audit and provided in December. (Originally, PGRR 145 required
ERCOT to provide final classification on August 7, 2026.)
ERCOT is still determining the impact to the Batch Zero Study timeline (Originally,
PGRR 145 required ERCOT to complete the study by April 9. 2027.)

Batch Zero Large Load Conditional Classifications

Interconnecting Transmission Service Providers (TSPs) received the
conditional classification for each of their Large Loads on September 3,
2026.
Included information:
•

Large Loads reclassified from a base load eligibility path to a studied
load path (subject to the PGRR 145 verification process)

•

Large Loads conditionally included subject to a future good cause
exception related to eligibility criteria (9 Large Loads)

•

Large Loads conditionally included subject to curing deficiencies in
dynamic model data

•

Large Loads excluded from Batch Zero and the reason for exclusion

Dispute Processes:

Key Takeaways:
•

Conditional classification list released
September 3

•

362 Large Loads in either base or
studied load categories

•

Some Large Loads are included subject
to resolving other issues; almost all notyet-energized Large Loads are subject
to PGRR 145 verification process

Key Definitions:
•

Batch Zero: ERCOT's one-time
transitional study of the reliability
impacts of Large Loads that seek to
interconnect to the ERCOT system and
meet certain eligibility criteria under
Planning Guide Revision Request
(PGRR) 145

•

Large Loads: 75 MW or greater

1. A developer that has a Large Load excluded from Batch Zero may
dispute its exclusion through ERCOT's Alternative Dispute Resolution
(ADR) process under ERCOT Protocols Section 20.
2. A developer with a Large Load submitted as base load but reclassified
as studied load may dispute using the process in Planning Guide
Section 9.3.1. This is a defined dispute timeline process.
