## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e5, e1, e3, e34, e38, e43): The Protocols define a mass transition as the transition of ESI IDs from one competitive retailer to a POLR or designated CR (or between TDSPs), in a quantity or timeframe identified by Applicable Legal Authority. [e5] The PUCT designates POLR providers and sets requirements for POLR service. [e1][e3] In a Mass Transition necessitated by the default of a Counter-Party representing a QSE associated with an LSE, ERCOT may adjust the TPE of Counter-Parties representing QSEs that are qualified as POLRs to reflect the estimated Incremental Load Exposure resulting from the Mass Transition. [e34] For the first-hand 2021 case, the PUCT found ERCOT transitioned 9,926 Griddy customers to POLRs between February 26 and March 2, 2021. [e38] Failure to serve as a POLR if required by the PUCT under 25.43 is listed among significant violations that can support revocation of a REP certificate. [e43]

## Uncited guidance text (check for new facts, numbers, dates, names)

- (Say it like this) (Guidance) The POLR is the retailer of last resort that picks up customers when their REP fails, so no one loses service. The PUCT designates volunteers and the largest retailers every two years, and ERCOT moves the customers through a mass transition, volunteers first.
- (Scenarios) - ERCOT starts a mass transition (see the mass-transition topic for the triggers).
