## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e4, e12, e13, e9, e14, e15, e8) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 25.43: PUCT rule 25.43 makes POLR service available to any requesting retail customer and to customers ERCOT transfers because their REP failed to provide service or meet its obligations to ERCOT. [e3] There are two types of POLR providers, VREPs and LSPs. A VREP is a REP that has volunteered to provide POLR service, and an LSP is a REP designated to provide it. [e4][e12][e13] There are four customer classes: residential, small non-residential, medium non-residential and large non-residential. [e4] A POLR provider offers a basic standard service package, limited to basic firm service and call center facilities. [e9] PURA says the standard package is offered at a fixed, nondiscountable rate approved by the commission. [e14] If no REP applies to be POLR for an area on reasonable terms, PURA lets the PUCT require a REP to become the POLR as a condition of receiving or maintaining a certificate under Section 39.352. [e15] POLR providers serve two-year terms. [e8]
- **How it works #2** (cites e16, e17, e18, e19, e20): Selection cycle (even-numbered years). The PUCT determines the eligibility of certified REPs in each even-numbered year for a term scheduled to commence in January of the next year. [e16] A REP files by July 10 of each even-numbered year, by service area, the customer classes it serves and, for each, its ESI ID count and retail MWh for the annual period ending March 31 of that year. [e17] Eligibility is specific to each POLR area and customer class. [e18] Among the grounds for ineligibility: the sum of the REP's percentage of ESI IDs and percentage of retail MWh in the POLR area for the class is less than 1.0, [e19] and the REP or its predecessor will not have served customers in Texas for at least 18 months on the date the term starts. [e20] These are two of several listed conditions, not the full list.
- **How it works #3** (cites e21, e22, e23): VREPs. A REP may request to be a VREP from June 1 through July 31 of each even-numbered year, unless the executive director sets otherwise. [e21] The filing must show the REP can absorb a mass transition without technical or financial distress, [e22] and state which classes it will serve in each POLR area and how many ESI IDs it will take in each transition event. [e23]
- **How it works #4** (cites e24, e28, e29, e27): LSPs. For each customer class in each POLR area the PUCT designates up to 15 LSPs, the eligible REPs with the greatest retail MWh market share, and staff designates them by October 15 of each even-numbered year. Being a VREP does not affect eligibility to be an LSP. [e24] An LSP may ask the PUCT to designate an affiliate to provide POLR service on its behalf, with a filing at least 30 days before the affiliate begins. [e28] The LSP remains responsible and liable for the affiliate's POLR service. [e29] An LSP can ask the PUCT to be relieved of further transfers if it shows it cannot maintain its financial integrity. [e27]
- **How it works #5** (cites e42, e30, e45): Who gets which customers in a mass transition. ERCOT transfers customers first to VREPs, up to the number each volunteered, using the VREP list in a non-discriminatory manner, and never more than a VREP said it would serve. [e42] If ESI IDs exceed what VREPs are designated to serve, ERCOT assigns the rest to LSPs by their share of retail MWh on a random basis within a class and POLR area; a VREP that is also an LSP and volunteers at least 1% of its market share is exempt from the LSP allocation up to 1% of the class and POLR area. [e30] The Retail Market Guide lists "assign ESI IDs to the POLR(s) as directed by ALA and the POLR rule" among ERCOT's pre-launch responsibilities. [e45]
- **How it works #6** (cites e6, e46, e11, e37, e36, e7, e35, e40): Pricing. A VREP must use a market-based, month-to-month product and the same product for all customers in a mass transition in the same class and POLR area. [e6][e46] The rule sets a maximum rate for POLR service charged by an LSP, with a formula for the residential class. [e11] Under it the LSP rate is a sum of non-bypassable charges, an LSP customer charge and an LSP energy charge divided by kWh used. [e11] Non-bypassable charges include TDU charges and credits, ERCOT administrative charges, nodal fees or surcharges, RUC capacity short charges attributable to LSP load and taxes. [e37] The rule states the LSP customer charge as $0.09 per kWh. [e36] Beginning on the 10th of each month, the LSP energy charge is the preceding month's average real-time SPP for the load zone, applied to the customer's kWh and multiplied by 120%. [e7] That energy charge must not exceed 160% of the preceding month's LSP energy charge, and the applicable load zone is the one in the customer's TDU territory with the highest historical average RTSPP. [e35] An LSP may instead serve transitioned customers on a market-based month-to-month product if it does so for all transitioned customers in the same class and POLR area. [e40]
- **How it works #7** (cites e25, e26, e41): Moving off the POLR rate. An LSP must move transitioned residential and small non-residential customers to a market-based month-to-month product no later than the 61st day of service (or the next billing cycle after day 60), [e25] except that it is not required to move a customer delinquent in POLR charges as of day 60. [e26] The POLR provider is financially responsible for the costs of providing electricity to customers from when the transfer is complete until the customer stops taking service under the rule. [e41]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e5, e1, e3, e34, e38, e43): The Protocols define a mass transition as the transition of ESI IDs from one competitive retailer to a POLR or designated CR (or between TDSPs), in a quantity or timeframe identified by Applicable Legal Authority. [e5] The PUCT designates POLR providers and sets requirements for POLR service. [e1][e3] ERCOT may adjust the credit exposure of Counter-Parties for QSEs that are POLRs to reflect the load they would take on in a mass transition. [e34] For the first-hand 2021 case, the PUCT found ERCOT transitioned 9,926 Griddy customers to POLRs between February 26 and March 2, 2021. [e38] Failure to serve as a POLR if required by the PUCT under 25.43 is listed among significant violations that can support revocation of a REP certificate. [e43]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e10, e33, e31, e32, e47) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 25.43: Cooperatives and municipal utilities. The PUCT's 25.43 selection rules do not apply when a co-op or muni designates its own POLR provider for its territory, [e10] and a cooperative may delegate its authority to select POLR providers to the PUCT under subsection (r). [e33] PURA says a municipally owned utility on initiating customer choice may designate itself or others as POLR [e31] and an electric cooperative shall designate itself or another entity. [e32] The Retail Market Guide requires an opt-in MOU or EC without an affiliated POLR that has not delegated to the PUCT to give ERCOT its POLR allocation methodology; see the mass-transition topic. [e47]

- **Key facts #3** (cites e8, e24): - POLR providers serve two-year terms; up to 15 LSPs per class and POLR area. [e8][e24]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e42, e30): - VREPs are used first in a mass transition; LSPs take the rest by market share. [e42][e30]

[e5] The PUCT's selection rules do not apply where an electric cooperative or municipally owned utility designates its own POLR provider, unless a cooperative delegates that authority to the PUCT. [e10] A VREP is also not a "volunteer to be dropped in": it states in advance how many ESI IDs it will take per class and area, and ERCOT cannot assign more. [e23][e42]
