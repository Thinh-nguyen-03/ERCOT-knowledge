## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e3, e2): A Provider of Last Resort (POLR) is a retail electric provider the PUCT designates, in areas where customer choice is in effect, to serve as providers of last resort. [e1] The PUCT rule says that service is available to any requesting customer and to customers transferred because their REP failed. [e3] PURA says that if a REP fails to serve its customers, the POLR must offer the standard retail service package with no interruption of service. [e2]
- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e4, e12, e13, e9, e8) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 25.43: PUCT rule 25.43 makes POLR service available to any requesting retail customer and to customers ERCOT transfers because their REP failed to provide service or meet its obligations to ERCOT. [e3] There are two types of POLR providers, VREPs and LSPs. A VREP is a REP that has volunteered to provide POLR service, and an LSP is a REP designated to provide it. [e4][e12][e13] There are four customer classes: residential, small non-residential, medium non-residential and large non-residential. [e4] A POLR provider offers a basic standard service package, limited to basic firm service and call center facilities. [e9] POLR providers serve two-year terms. [e8]
- **How it works #2** (cites e6, e11, e7): Pricing differs by type. A VREP must use a market-based, month-to-month product. [e6] The rule sets a maximum rate for POLR service charged by an LSP, with a formula for the residential class. [e11] Under it, beginning on the 10th of each month the LSP energy charge is the preceding month's average real-time SPP for the applicable load zone, applied to the customer's kWh and multiplied by 120%. [e7]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e5, e1, e3): When a retailer fails, ERCOT moves its customers in a mass transition, defined in the Protocols as the transition of ESI IDs from a competitive retailer to a POLR or designated CR. [e5] The PUCT designates POLR providers and sets requirements for POLR service. [e1][e3]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e1): - PURA requires the PUCT to designate POLRs where customer choice is in effect. [e1]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e4): - Two POLR types (VREP, LSP) and four customer classes. [e4]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e8): - POLR providers serve two-year terms. [e8]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e7): - The LSP energy charge is the preceding month's average real-time SPP for the load zone times the customer's kWh, multiplied by 120%. [e7]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e6): - A VREP serves on a market-based, month-to-month product. [e6]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e5, e10): POLR is not the same as mass transition: the POLR is the receiving provider and mass transition is the process that moves the ESI IDs. [e5] The PUCT's selection rules do not apply where an electric cooperative or municipally owned utility designates its own POLR provider, unless a cooperative delegates that authority to the PUCT. [e10]
- **Questions #2** (cites e2, e3): A POLR provider: customers are transferred by ERCOT to a VREP or LSP, with no interruption of service. [e2][e3]
- **Questions #4** (cites e11, e7) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 25.43: By the 16 TAC 25.43(m)(2) formula, which sets a maximum rate. Its energy charge is the preceding month's average real-time SPP for the load zone, applied to the customer's kWh and multiplied by 120%. [e11][e7]

- (Say it like this) (Guidance) The POLR is the retailer of last resort that picks up customers when their REP fails, so no one loses service. The PUCT designates POLRs, and ERCOT moves the customers through a mass transition.
