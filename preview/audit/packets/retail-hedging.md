## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **How it works #1** (cites e9): **What an LSE has to cover.** ERCOT's training for Load Serving Entities lists the tools for hedging wholesale market costs: the Day Ahead Market (energy, congestion, capacity), bilateral trades (energy, capacity, ancillary service) and Congestion Revenue Rights (congestion). [e9]
- **How it works #2** (cites e10, e11, e12, e38, e37): **What is left unhedged is settled at real time.** In the LSE training, real-time energy imbalance is the negative of supplies minus obligations, times the real-time settlement point price. [e10] In the Protocols, a QSE's Energy Imbalance at a Load Zone is priced at the Real-Time Settlement Point Price on a net of amounts that includes its Self-Schedules, its cleared DAM Energy Bids and its Energy Trades as buyer, against offsets that include its AML (Adjusted Metered Load, excluding Non-WSL ESR Charging Load). [e11][e12][e38] So load that is covered by a day-ahead award or a bilateral buy is settled in real time only on the difference. The IMM notes that load is not fully exposed to real-time prices to the extent LSEs own generation or bilaterally contract for supply ahead of the real-time market. [e37]
- **How it works #3** (cites e13, e14, e16): **Worked example from ERCOT training (illustrative numbers, Load Zone West, one hour, 100 MW).** [e13][e14][e16]
- **How it works #4** (cites e13): - No hedge: the load buys all energy in real time at $40. [e13]
- **How it works #5** (cites e14): - Day-ahead hedge: a 100 MW DAM award at the load zone costs $50/MWh, a charge of $5,000 per hour. [e14]

## Uncited guidance text (check for new facts, numbers, dates, names)

- (Say it like this) (Guidance) A retailer's hedge is whatever covers its load against real-time prices: owned generation, bilateral PPAs, futures, or day-ahead positions. Day-ahead cover smooths volatility, but it does not shield against a sustained price rise. Exposure shares in the IMM report are measured from ERCOT settlement data, so they leave out bilateral hedges the IMM has no information on.
