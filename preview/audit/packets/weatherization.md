## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **How it works #1** (cites e14, e15, e16, e19): **Who and when it applies.** A generation entity is an ERCOT-registered resource entity acting for an ERCOT-registered generation or energy storage resource. [e14] A resource with an ERCOT-approved notice of suspension of operations for the summer or winter season is not required to comply until the return-to-service date in its notice of change of designation; a new or repowered resource, or a transmission facility energized for the first time, must meet the rule before its commissioning date or first energization. [e15][e16] The rule defines the winter season as December 1 to February 28 of the following year. [e19]
- **How it works #2** (cites e20, e21, e22, e24, e23): **What winter measures look like.** The entity must implement measures that could reasonably be expected to ensure sustained operation of all cold weather critical components, such as adequate wind breaks, insulation and enclosures, and freeze protection equipment tested or verified before and monthly during winter. [e20][e21] On top of that, beginning in 2023, resources must be prepared for the 95th percentile minimum average 72-hour wind chill temperature in ERCOT's historical weather study for their weather zone. [e22] Summer measures include maintaining hot weather critical components and verifying them before and monthly during summer, plus preparedness for the greater of the maximum ambient temperature the resource has sustained operations at or the 95th percentile maximum average 72-hour temperature for its weather zone. [e24][e23]
- **How it works #3** (cites e4, e13, e26, e25, e27, e28, e39) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 3.21: **Declarations.** Each generation entity must submit a declaration of winter weather preparedness between November 1 and December 1, and a declaration of summer weather preparedness between May 1 and June 1. [e4][e13] The declaration must identify the resources, give the minimum ambient temperature at which each has experienced sustained operations, and include a notarized attestation by the entity's highest-ranking representative with binding authority. [e26][e25] A declaration is also needed before a mothballed, outaged or decommissioned resource returns to service. [e27] ERCOT files a compliance report with the commission by December 20 on winter declarations. [e28] For gas-fired resources, the summer declaration submission also brings a declaration of natural gas pipeline coordination under Protocol Section 3.21. [e39]
- **How it works #4** (cites e5, e29, e30, e31, e32, e6): **Inspections and cure.** ERCOT must inspect every resource at least once every three years and may prioritize by criticality, time since last inspection, weather-related outages and other vulnerabilities. [e5][e29] ERCOT gives at least 72 hours' written notice unless otherwise agreed. [e30] If a resource is not in compliance, ERCOT must give a reasonable cure period, and it must report to commission staff any entity that does not cure in time. [e31][e32] A violation is a Class A violation that may carry a penalty up to $1,000,000 per violation per day. [e6]
- **How it works #5** (cites e7, e17, e18, e34, e33): **After a failure.** If a resource has a repeated or major weather-related forced interruption of service, ERCOT notifies the entity and staff, and the entity must contract a qualified professional engineer to assess its preparation measures, plans, procedures and operations. [e7] A major interruption is a failure to start for 12 or more continuous hours, or loss of 50% or more of the seasonal net maximum sustainable rating for 12 or more continuous hours, in a weather emergency; a repeated one is three or more qualifying occurrences from separate weather emergencies within three years. [e17][e18] The engineer must be independent of the entity and its affiliates, perform a root cause analysis and corrective action plan, and the entity must submit the assessment to the PUCT and ERCOT within 15 calendar days of receiving it. [e34][e33]
- **How it works #6** (cites e38, e35): **Transmission providers.** TSPs have parallel duties, including confirming the operability of all systems and subsystems containing cold weather critical components, and ERCOT must select at least ten percent of substations or switchyards for inspection every three years. [e38][e35]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e10, e11, e40, e42, e41): The rule's statutory backdrop: SB 3 (87th Legislature) added a provision directing the commission by rule to require generators in the ERCOT region to prepare their assets for weather emergencies, with the independent organization for the ERCOT power region inspecting for compliance and reporting violations. [e10][e11] The SB 3 provision applies to municipally owned utilities, electric cooperatives, power generation companies and exempt wholesale generators that sell at wholesale in ERCOT, and a parallel provision covers electric cooperatives, municipally owned utilities and transmission and distribution utilities providing transmission service. [e40][e42] The commission must impose an administrative penalty on an entity that violates the rule and does not remedy it within a reasonable time. [e41]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e12, e9, e43, e44) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2021: The FERC-NERC Uri report describes the earlier approach: ERCOT started a winter weatherization spot check program for the 2011/2012 winter after the February 2011 cold event, and the report said it appears there is no meaningful follow-up when entities fail to finish winter preparations by the December 1 deadline. [e12][e9] ERCOT's October 2021 roadmap proposed requiring a CEO-signed letter twice a year attesting to weatherization preparations and planned 30+ summer spot checks where it had previously checked only before winter. [e43][e44]
- **ERCOT context #3** (cites e8, e45): The reliability standard rule links to this one: the rule defines weatherization effectiveness as the assumed percentage reduction in weather-related unplanned outages for generation and storage resources in the model, due to compliance with 25.55. [e8] The PUCT's emergency operations plan rule keeps weather emergency response annexes distinct from the preparations required under 25.55. [e45]
- **ERCOT context #4** (cites e46, e49, e50, e47, e48) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2026: Recent practice: ERCOT's January 2026 Winter Storm Fern materials say power plants and transmission facilities are required to be weatherized, and that ERCOT planned at least 450 inspections that winter.
