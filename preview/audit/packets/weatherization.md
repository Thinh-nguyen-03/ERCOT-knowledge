## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2, e3) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 25.55: Weatherization standards are the PUCT's weather emergency preparedness rule, 16 TAC 25.55, which applies to ERCOT and to generation entities and transmission service providers in the ERCOT power region. [e1] Under it, a generation entity must complete winter preparation measures for each resource by December 1 each year and summer measures by June 1. [e2][e3]
- **How it works #1** (cites e4, e13, e5, e11): Each generation entity must submit a declaration of winter weather preparedness between November 1 and December 1, and a declaration of summer weather preparedness between May 1 and June 1. [e4][e13] Every resource interconnected to the ERCOT power region must be inspected at least once every three years, and SB 3 has the independent organization certified under PURA 39.151 for the ERCOT power region inspect generation assets and report violations to the commission. [e5][e11]
- **How it works #2** (cites e6, e7): A violation is a Class A violation and may carry a penalty up to $1,000,000 per violation per day. [e6] If a resource has a repeated or major weather-related forced interruption of service, ERCOT notifies the entity and staff, and the entity must contract a qualified professional engineer to assess its preparation measures, plans, procedures and operations. [e7]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e10, e11, e12, e9): The rule's statutory backdrop: SB 3 (87th Legislature) added a provision directing the commission by rule to require generators in the ERCOT region to prepare their assets for weather emergencies, with the independent organization for the ERCOT power region inspecting for compliance and reporting violations. [e10][e11] The FERC-NERC Uri report describes the earlier approach: ERCOT started a winter weatherization spot check program for the 2011/2012 winter after the February 2011 cold event, and the report said it appears there is no meaningful follow-up when entities fail to finish winter preparations by the December 1 deadline. [e12][e9]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e8): The reliability standard rule links to this one: the rule defines weatherization effectiveness as the assumed percentage reduction in weather-related unplanned outages for generation and storage resources in the model, due to compliance with 25.55. [e8]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e2, e3): - Winter measures must be complete by December 1 each year; summer measures by June 1. [e2][e3]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e4, e13): - Declarations of winter preparedness are due between November 1 and December 1; summer declarations between May 1 and June 1. [e4][e13]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e5): - ERCOT inspects each resource at least once every three years. [e5]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e6): - A violation is a Class A violation and may be subject to a penalty not to exceed $1,000,000 per violation per day. [e6]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e7): - A repeated or major weather-related forced interruption requires an independent engineer assessment. [e7]
- **Say it like this #1** (cites e2, e5, e6, e7): Texas now has a PUCT rule that makes every generator prepare for each season, declare it, be inspected at least every three years, and face penalties of up to $1 million a day. [e2][e5][e6] After a repeated or major weather-related forced interruption, ERCOT notifies the unit, which must contract with a qualified professional engineer to assess its preparation. [e7]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e6, e1): Weatherization is a compliance duty with penalties, not a paid service. Firm fuel supply (FFSS) is a separate service (see the ffss topic), and the two should not be mixed up. [e6] The rule also covers transmission service providers, not only generators. [e1]
- **Questions #2** (cites e2, e3, e4, e5): Complete season-specific preparation measures by December 1 (winter) and June 1 (summer), submit a declaration of preparedness, and be available for inspection. [e2][e3][e4][e5]
- **Questions #4** (cites e5): At least once every three years for every resource interconnected to the ERCOT power region. [e5]
- **Questions #6** (cites e7): ERCOT notifies the entity and PUCT staff, and the entity must contract with a qualified professional engineer to assess its measures, plans, procedures and operations. [e7]
