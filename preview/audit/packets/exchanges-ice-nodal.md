## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e4, e8, e5, e6, e7, e12) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2026: **Contract terms (saved specifications dated 18 November 2026).** The contract settles in cash against the "ELECTRICITY-ERCOT-NORTH 345KV HUB-REAL TIME" price published by ERCOT. [e3] The size is 1 MW, the price is quoted in US dollars per MWh with a one-cent price quotation convention (the specification says minimum price fluctuation may vary by trade type), and up to 50 consecutive monthly contract periods can be listed. [e4][e3][e8][e5] Trading ends on the last business day of the contract period, and the final payment date is the sixth clearing organization business day after that. [e6][e7] The final settlement is the average of the Reference Price A prices. [e12]
- **How it works #2** (cites e4, e10, e11, e27, e13, e30, e17): **Peak versus off-peak.** The peak contract (symbol ERN) prices each Monday through Friday, excluding NERC holidays, and the daily price is the average of the settlement point prices for hours ending 0700-2200 CPT. [e4][e10][e11] The off-peak contract (symbol NEB) uses, for each weekday excluding NERC holidays, the average of the SPPs for hours ending 0100-0600 and 2300-2400 CPT, and for each Saturday, Sunday and NERC holiday the average for all hours ending 0100-2400 CPT. [e27][e13] The Protocols' definition of On-Peak Hours (hours ending 0700 to 2200 CPT, Monday through Friday excluding NERC holidays) and the CRR auction's 5x16 block use the same hours. [e30][e17]
- **How it works #3** (cites e1, e2, e3): **Monthly price from daily prices.** The Description says the contract is based on the mathematical average of daily prices, each calculated by averaging the peak (or off-peak) hourly prices ERCOT publishes for the reference location. [e1][e2] The Reference Price description says the price for a Pricing Date is that day's Specified Price per MWh for the Delivery Date, published by ERCOT. [e3]
- **How it works #4** (cites e15, e14, e16, e18): **A second type: OTC heat rate contract.** ICE also publishes an OTC (not futures) specification for an ERCOT Panhandle Heat Rate 345kV Hub Off-Peak contract. [e15] Its final settlement is the heat rate agreed at the time of the transaction multiplied by the NYMEX last-day settlement price for the delivery month, and payment is due on the 20th of the month after the delivery period unless a master agreement says otherwise. [e14][e16] Daily settlement of that contract is determined by NGX from price data across spot, forward and derivative markets. [e18]
- **How it works #5** (cites e21, e23, e24, e25): **What an exchange is, in general (FERC staff primer, tier 2, not ERCOT-specific).** FERC's staff primer says many financially settled energy contracts traded on electronic brokerage platforms are, after Dodd-Frank, cleared cash-settled energy futures contracts. [e21] It says futures markets generally are more liquid than forward markets because futures are traded on centralized exchanges. [e23] It describes margin as collateral posted as a percentage of the current market value of a contract, which lets participants trade without paying cash for the full value. [e24] It also describes OTC markets as bilateral, where tailoring a contract to the counterparties' needs is a chief benefit. [e25]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e1, e3, e19, e28, e26): The saved ICE specifications show the ERCOT North 345KV Hub real-time price is the underlying for the exchange-traded power future, which settles in cash on a monthly average of the published prices. [e1][e3] ERCOT itself runs the day-ahead and CRR forward markets (the IMM's description); the ICE contracts sit outside them, and ERCOT's own training describes the DAM as a centralized forward market facilitated by ERCOT and bilateral trades as a decentralized forward market of QSE-to-QSE transactions. [e19][e28][e26]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e31, e29, e3): The UT Austin report on Winter Storm Uri lists ICE as a "nonERCOT market" through which generation may be sold, alongside PPAs, other bilateral contracts and ERCOT day-ahead awards. [e31] Real-time prices are the reference point for all of these. The IMM says only a small share of power is settled in real time, but real-time prices shape price expectations in the day-ahead and bilateral forward markets. [e29] The ICE contracts reference the real-time hub price directly. [e3]
- **ERCOT context #3** (cites e20): For market participants, the IMM lists participating in futures markets among the ways LSEs manage price exposure, alongside owning generation, bilateral PPAs and day-ahead purchases. [e20]
- **ERCOT context #4** (cites e22): FERC's primer says Nodal Exchange offers locational (nodal) futures contracts to market participants in the organized electricity markets, as well as electricity contracts for forward months at RTO hubs, zones and nodes; it does not say which ERCOT products Nodal Exchange lists. [e22]
