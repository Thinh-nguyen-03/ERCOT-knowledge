## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e9, e1, e2): ICE Futures U.S. lists monthly cash-settled futures on ERCOT power, including the ERCOT North 345KV Real-Time Peak and Off-Peak Fixed Price Futures. Each is based on the average of daily prices built from the hourly electricity prices ERCOT publishes for the reference location. [e9][e1][e2]
- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e4, e8, e5, e6, e7): The contract settles in cash against the "ELECTRICITY-ERCOT-NORTH 345KV HUB-REAL TIME" price published by ERCOT. [e3] The size is 1 MW, the price is quoted in US dollars per MWh with a one-cent price quotation convention (the specification says minimum price fluctuation may vary by trade type), and up to 50 consecutive monthly contract periods can be listed. [e4][e3][e8][e5] Trading ends on the last business day of the contract period, and the final payment date is the sixth clearing organization business day after that. [e6][e7]
- **How it works #2** (cites e1, e2): The peak contract averages the peak hourly prices and the off-peak contract averages the off-peak hourly prices; the specific hours are in the block-products topic. [e1][e2]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e1, e3): The saved ICE peak specification shows that the ERCOT North 345KV Hub real-time price is the underlying for this exchange-traded power future, which settle in cash on a monthly average of the published prices. [e1][e3] The saved sources contain no information on Nodal Exchange.
- **Key facts #1** (cites e9): - ICE Futures U.S. is the listing exchange for the contracts in the saved specifications. [e9]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e4): - Contract size is 1 MW. [e4]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e5): - Listing cycle is up to 50 consecutive monthly contract periods. [e5]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e6): - Last trading day is the last business day of the contract period. [e6]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e7): - Final payment is the sixth clearing organization business day after the last trading day. [e7]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e3, e9, e1): These ICE futures settle on real-time prices, not day-ahead prices. [e3] They are listed by ICE Futures U.S., not by ERCOT; the specification refers to hourly prices published by ERCOT. [e9][e1]
- **Questions #2** (cites e1, e3): A monthly average of ERCOT-published peak hourly prices at the ERCOT North 345KV Hub real-time price. [e1][e3]
- **Questions #4** (cites e5): Up to 50 consecutive monthly contract periods. [e5]
- **Link to block-products** (cites e1, e2): related Block Products (5x16, 2x16, 7x8, ATC): The ICE ERCOT North contracts come as a peak and an off-peak monthly block. [e1][e2]
- **Link to hb-north** (cites e3): related HB_NORTH (North Hub): The ICE ERCOT North peak contract settles on the ERCOT North 345KV Hub real-time price. [e3]
- **Link to trading-hub** (cites e3): related Trading Hubs: ICE's reference price is an ERCOT-published hub settlement price. [e3]
- **Link to rtm** (cites e3): determines Real-Time Market (RTM): The peak contract settles against the real-time price published by ERCOT for the North 345KV Hub. [e3]
- **Link to dart-spread** (cites e3): related DART Spread: ICE futures settle against real-time prices rather than day-ahead prices, so they bear on how forwards compare with real-time outcomes. [e3]
- **Link to retail-hedging** (cites e9, e3): related Retail Load Hedging: The ICE contracts are named Fixed Price Futures and settle in cash against the real-time price. [e9][e3]
## Uncited guidance text (check for new facts, numbers, dates, names)

- (Say it like this) (Guidance) ICE lists monthly cash-settled ERCOT North power futures, peak and off-peak, that settle against ERCOT's real-time hub prices. They give market participants a standardized way to lock in a monthly price without a bilateral contract.
