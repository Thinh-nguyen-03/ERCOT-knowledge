## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2, e17): ERS is a special emergency service that ERCOT procures and deploys to help prevent or alleviate an actual or anticipated Energy Emergency Alert (EEA) event. [e1] The protocols define it as an emergency service and state that it is not an Ancillary Service. [e2] The Protocols give the goal as promoting reliability prior to and during energy emergencies. [e17]
- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e46, e18, e19): **Who can provide it.** An ERS resource is either a load (or an aggregation of loads) or a dispatchable generator that is not registered as a Generation Resource. [e3] It must be represented by a QSE, and offers go to ERCOT through a QSE. [e46] A weather-sensitive ERS Load must be either exclusively residential sites or exclusively non-residential sites that qualify under the regression baseline evaluation. [e18] The minimum offer is 0.5 MW for an ERS Load on the weather-sensitive baseline and 0.1 MW for all other ERS capacity offers, and ERS Resources may be aggregated to reach it. [e19]
- **How it works #2** (cites e4, e5, e20, e54, e55, e26, e38): **What is bought.** ERCOT issues requests for proposals for four standard contract terms (December through March, April and May, June through September, October and November) and buys from four service types: weather-sensitive or non-weather-sensitive, each as ERS-10 or ERS-30. [e4][e5] A QSE may offer one resource into any or all four service types, but ERCOT awards only one service type per ERS Resource. [e20] The December 2026 RFP, for example, covers December 1, 2026 through March 31, 2027, and asks for a single hourly capacity price in $ per MW per hour for each Time Period, with Time Periods evaluated separately and not linked. [e54][e55] The ERS-10 or ERS-30 number is the response time: ten minutes or 30 minutes from the ERCOT Dispatch Instruction, held for the entire Sustained Response Period. [e26] The ramp periods are likewise ten minutes for ERS-10 and 30 minutes for ERS-30. [e38]
- **How it works #3** (cites e24, e27): **Exclusivity.** A QSE must affirm that no offered capacity from any site has been or will be committed to any other product, service or program in the same hours, and the list expressly includes Ancillary Services, SCED and TDSP standard offer programs. [e24] The same rule appears from the other direction: contracted ERS capacity may not be used for Ancillary Services during a contracted ERS Time Period, without limiting passive (voluntary) Load response if the resource still meets its performance and availability requirements. [e27]
- **How it works #4** (cites e30, e31, e32, e33, e34, e48, e49, e50) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2025: **Procurement mechanics (Attachment Q).** ERCOT builds a capacity demand curve for each ERS Time Period, procures all ERS products together within the limits of that curve, and maximizes MW procured subject to the Time Period's expenditure limit. [e30] The ERS offer cap in the December 5, 2025 version of Attachment Q is $80/MW/hr for every Time Period in the program year, and offers above it are rejected automatically. [e31] The $75 million is allocated across the program year, which runs from the December through March term to the October through November term. [e32] ERCOT assigns each Time Period a high, moderate or low risk designation and a risk-weighting factor from 1 to 100. [e33] The capacity inflection point is where capacity can only be procured below the offer cap while respecting the expenditure limit. [e34] The highest accepted offer for a Time Period sets one clearing price for every ERS service type in that Time Period, and if offers at the same price would exceed the limit they are considered in random order. [e48][e49] Funds not used by the end of a program year are not carried forward. [e50]
- **How it works #5** (cites e21, e22, e23, e15, e57): **Contract periods and obligations.** Resources are obligated for 24 hours of cumulative deployment time in each Contract Period of the December through March term and 12 hours in each Contract Period of every other term. [e21] When a service type's obligations are exhausted, that Contract Period ends at the end of the Operating Day of exhaustion. [e22] ERCOT may then renew the obligations of resources that opted in during the offer process, and that election is irrevocable once committed, except that a QSE may revoke it by the end of the second Business Day of a renewal Contract Period (paragraph (iv)). [e23][e15] Deployment is also limited by the PUCT rule to the contracted hours, up to a maximum of 24 cumulative hours in an ERS contract period. [e57]
- **How it works #6** (cites e6, e16, e28, e44) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2022: **Deployment.** When PRC falls below 3,000 MW and is not projected to recover above 3,000 MW within 30 minutes following the deployment of Non-Spin, ERCOT may deploy available contracted ERS-10 and ERS-30 via an XML message. [e6] A pending change (NPRR1238, to take effect upon system implementation) would change the 30-minute test to run from the deployment of Non-Spin and all VECL. [e16] After release, a deployed resource must be able to return to a condition capable of meeting its performance requirements within ten hours. [e28] The IMM's 2022 report says the PRC-based deployment trigger came from a PUCT direction in October 2021, enacted in the Protocols as NPRR1106. [e44]
- **How it works #7** (cites e29, e60): **Self-provision.** A QSE may self-provide all or part of its ERS obligation on a schedule ERCOT publishes, and ERCOT reduces the Time Period expenditure limit by the clearing price for those self-provision offers. [e29][e60]
- **How it works #8** (cites e36, e25, e35, e39, e37, e40, e56): **Payment, charges and performance.** The PUCT rule has ERCOT pay each QSE as-bid, by market clearing price or another mechanism, adjusted for availability and event performance. [e36] Deployment itself brings no additional ERS payment beyond whatever the QSE earns through Real-Time energy imbalance or other Settlement. [e25] The charge is allocated by load ratio share, where a QSE's share for a Time Period is its total Load divided by total ERCOT Load in that Time Period. [e35] Performance is measured from 15-minute interval data: the event performance factor is the time-weighted average of interval performance factors over the Sustained Response Period. [e39] A QSE whose portfolio availability and event performance factors both equal or exceed 0.95 is deemed to have met its requirements for the Contract Period and is not subject to suspension. [e37] ERCOT must send each QSE an event performance report within 45 days after the end of a term in which a deployment occurred. [e40] ERCOT must report on ERS effectiveness to the PUCT each year by April 15. [e56]
- **Formula #1** (cites e32, e33, e47): The Protocols give two simple formulas in Attachment Q, both in words below. The expenditure limit for a Time Period is the funds determined for allocation in the program year multiplied by that Time Period's allocation factor, which is its risk-weighting factor times its number of hours times the offer cap, divided by the sum of the same product over all Time Periods. [e32][e33] The capacity inflection point for each Time Period is then its expenditure limit divided by the number of hours in the Time Period times the offer cap: [e47]
- **Formula #2** (cites e47): `CapInflectionPoint_(TP) = ExpenditureLimit_(TP) ÷ [# hrs_(TP)×OfferCap]` [e47]
- **Formula #3** (cites e34): Read it as: the MW that could be bought for every hour of the Time Period at the offer cap and still stay within the Time Period's budget. [e34]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e9, e41, e10): The IMM describes ERS as an auction in which industrial and commercial consumers offer to be curtailed. For the 2024 program year ERCOT procured an average of 1,147 MW per hour at an average clearing price of $7.36 per MWh. [e9] For the 2025 program year (December 2024 through November 2025) the average was 1,666 MW per hour, up 45%, at $4.36 per MWh, down 41%, and the IMM says this likely reflects the influx of cryptocurrency mining facilities. [e41] By the end of 2025, cryptocurrency mining operations accounted for more than half of all ERS capacity. [e10]

- **Common confusion #1** (cites e2, e26, e24, e25): ERS is not a normal ancillary service, and the protocols say so. [e2] The "10" and "30" are response times, not MW or hours. [e26] ERS capacity is exclusive: the contracted capacity cannot also be committed to other products or programs in the same hours. [e24] And a deployment does not pay extra: deployment brings no additional ERS payment. [e25]
- **Questions #4** (cites e8, e35, e7, e15): ERCOT charges each QSE based on its load ratio share, which is its total Load divided by total ERCOT Load in the Time Period. [e8][e35] The total is within a $75 million 12-month spending cap unless the PUCT decides otherwise (with up to $25 million more allowed for renewals when ERS has been exhausted). [e7][e15]
- **Questions #6** (cites e30, e31, e48, e33): It builds a demand curve per Time Period capped by an offer cap and a budget for that Time Period, and the highest accepted offer sets one price for all four service types. [e30][e31][e48] The budget is allocated across Time Periods using a risk-weighting factor. [e33]
