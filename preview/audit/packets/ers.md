## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2): ERS is a special emergency service that ERCOT procures and deploys to help prevent or alleviate an actual or anticipated Energy Emergency Alert (EEA) event. [e1] The protocols define it as an emergency service and state that it is not an Ancillary Service. [e2]
- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e4, e5): An ERS resource is either a load (or an aggregation of loads) or a dispatchable generator that is not registered as a Generation Resource. [e3] ERCOT issues requests for proposals for four standard contract terms (December through March, April and May, June through September, October and November) and buys from four service types: weather-sensitive or non-weather-sensitive, each as ERS-10 or ERS-30. [e4][e5]
- **How it works #2** (cites e6, e16, e7, e15, e8): Deployment is a late-stage step. When Physical Responsive Capability (PRC) falls below 3,000 MW and is not projected to recover above 3,000 MW within 30 minutes of Non-Spin deployment, ERCOT may deploy contracted ERS-10 and ERS-30 by XML message. [e6] A pending change (NPRR1238, to take effect upon system implementation) would change the 30-minute test to run from the deployment of Non-Spin and all VECL. [e16] ERCOT may spend a maximum of $75 million in a 12-month period on ERS unless the PUCT decides otherwise, may exceed that by up to $25 million for ERS contract renewals when ERS has been exhausted, and the cost is charged to QSEs by load ratio share. [e7][e15][e8]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e9, e12, e10): The IMM describes ERS as an auction in which industrial and commercial consumers offer to be curtailed, and its 2024 report says ERS had been deployed for nine events since inception, most recently in September 2023. For the 2024 program year ERCOT procured an average of 1,147 MW per hour at an average clearing price of $7.36 per MWh. [e9] There were no ERS deployments in 2024. [e12] By the end of 2025, cryptocurrency mining operations accounted for more than half of all ERS capacity. [e10]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e11, e13): The IMM lists ERS among a group of out-of-market products and argues that such programs compensate entities outside the energy and ancillary service markets, which will sharply reduce the expected shortage revenues that investors need. [e11][e13]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e2): - ERS is not an Ancillary Service; it is deployed to prevent or alleviate an EEA event. [e2]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e4, e5): - Four standard contract terms and four service types (weather-sensitive or not, ERS-10 or ERS-30). [e4][e5]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e7, e15): - Spending cap: $75 million in a 12-month period unless the PUCT decides otherwise, plus up to $25 million for renewals when ERS has been exhausted. [e7][e15]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e6): - Deployment may follow PRC below 3,000 MW and Non-Spin deployment. [e6]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e9): - 2024 program year: an average of 1,147 MW per hour procured at an average of $7.36 per MWh. [e9]
- **Say it like this #1** (cites e3, e4, e1, e6, e2, e8): (Guidance) ERS is an emergency product: ERCOT contracts loads and dispatchable generators not registered as Generation Resources for set contract terms [e3][e4] and may deploy them when PRC is low, to help prevent or alleviate an EEA event. [e1][e6] It is not an Ancillary Service, and the cost is charged to QSEs by load ratio share. [e2][e8]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e2, e6, e8): ERS is not a normal ancillary service, and the protocols say so. [e2] ERCOT calls contracted ERS by XML message, and the cost is charged to QSEs by load ratio share. [e6][e8]
- **Questions #2** (cites e6, e1): ERCOT may deploy ERS when PRC is below 3,000 MW and not projected to recover above 3,000 MW within 30 minutes after Non-Spin deployment. [e6] The purpose is to prevent or alleviate an actual or anticipated EEA event. [e1]
- **Questions #4** (cites e8, e7, e15): ERCOT charges each QSE based on its load ratio share during the relevant ERS time period and contract term, within a $75 million 12-month spending cap unless the PUCT decides otherwise (with up to $25 million more allowed for renewals when ERS has been exhausted). [e8][e7][e15]
- **Questions #6** (cites e11, e13): The IMM argues programs like ERS compensate entities outside the energy and ancillary service markets to add supply or reduce demand in tight conditions, which will sharply reduce the expected shortage revenues investors need to build or keep dispatchable generation. [e11][e13]
