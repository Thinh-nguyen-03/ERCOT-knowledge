## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2, e3): A Large Load is one or more facilities at a single site with aggregate peak Demand of at least 75 MW behind common points of interconnection. [e1] The Batch Zero Process is a transitional interconnection process that evaluates qualifying Large Loads together, on a system-wide basis, rather than one at a time. [e2][e3]
- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e5): Planning Guide Section 9 records the move from individual Large Load interconnection studies to the Batch Zero Process. Batch Zero has two parts: a Batch Zero Interconnection Study and a Batch Zero Refinement Study. [e3] ERCOT made the change because each new large project could invalidate studies for projects further along in the queue, which caused costly restudies and delays. [e5]
- **How it works #2** (cites e22, e4, e27, e23, e8): Each request is sorted into base load, studied load or excluded load. Base load is a mature project that gets its full allocation without a restudy. Excluded load is deferred to a later batch. [e22] A studied load may get its full request, part of it, or be told to wait for a later batch if the grid cannot take it yet. [e4] There are two optional pathways. [e27] The first is the Withdrawal-Limited Private Use Network (WLPUN): a private network with both generation and a Large Load, whose withdrawal limit at its point of interconnection is set by ERCOT's system-wide study. [e23] The second is the Provisional Controllable Load Resource (PCLR). A PCLR site lets ERCOT curtail it when local transmission constraints bind, and in return it gets some grid access before the full transmission build-out. [e8]

- **Common confusion #1** (cites e12, e13, e8, e21) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2026: (Guidance) Queue size, the Batch Zero eligible amount and the Batch Zero allocated amount are three different numbers. The queue is everything requested. About 205 GW was eligible as of July 2026 [e12], and the conditional base plus studied load was about 100 to 192 GW over 2028 to 2032 as of September 2026. [e13] Also, a PCLR is not the same as an ordinary CLR. It is a pathway for getting interconnected, and it takes effect only after system implementation. [e8][e21]
