## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e38): ERCOT (Electric Reliability Council of Texas, Inc.) is a Texas nonprofit corporation that the PUCT has certified as the Independent Organization for the ERCOT Region. [e1] PURA defines an independent organization as an independent system operator or other person sufficiently independent of any producer or seller of electricity that its decisions will not be unduly influenced by them. [e38]
- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e1, e15): State law requires a power region to have an independent organization with four jobs: nondiscriminatory access to the transmission and distribution systems, reliability and adequacy of the regional network, timely transfer of information about a customer's choice of retail electric provider, and accurate accounting of electricity production and delivery among generators and wholesale buyers and sellers. ERCOT is that organization for the ERCOT Region. [e2][e1] ERCOT's own summary of these is: maintain system reliability, facilitate competitive wholesale and retail markets, and ensure open access to transmission. [e15]
- **How it works #2** (cites e3, e36) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 25.361: Day to day, ERCOT runs the operational and market functions of the ERCOT system: it procures and deploys ancillary services, schedules resources and loads, and manages transmission congestion. It also runs settlement and billing, which includes assessing market participants' creditworthiness and setting and enforcing security (collateral) requirements. [e3] PUCT rule 25.361 adds the instantaneous balancing of generation and load, monitoring resource adequacy, nondiscriminatory transmission access and supervising requests to interconnect new generation. [e36]
- **How it works #5** (cites e35): ERCOT's rules bind the industry. REPs, municipal utilities, cooperatives, power marketers, TDUs and power generation companies must follow ERCOT's scheduling, operating, planning, reliability and settlement rules; failure can lead to action on their PUCT certificate or an administrative penalty under PURA. [e35]
- **Formula #1** (cites e30, e32, e31): The system administration fee: the PUCT authorizes the fee to fund ERCOT's budget, and ERCOT converts it into a charge on each QSE. [e30] For each QSE and 15-minute settlement interval, the charge is the Load Administration Fee Factor (LAFF, the fee in $/MWh) times the QSE's Real-Time Adjusted Metered Load (RTAML, the adjusted metered load at the settlement points it represents, in MWh). [e32][e31] In other words, the fee is paid on load, through the QSE. [e30][e31]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e9, e29, e10, e11, e37, e28, e18): ERCOT is directly responsible and accountable to the PUCT. The commission has complete authority to oversee and investigate ERCOT's finances, budget and operations. [e9] ERCOT must submit its entire proposed budget, which the PUCT reviews annually or biennially and may approve, disapprove or modify. [e29] After approving the budget, the PUCT sets a range for the system administration fee charged to wholesale buyers and sellers. [e10] Protocols that ERCOT adopts cannot take effect until the commission approves them, and the board's protocol process must require PUCT approval of a market impact statement first. [e11][e37] ERCOT is also subject to Sunset review alongside the PUCT, but cannot be abolished under the Sunset Act. [e28] FERC staff say FERC regulates all RTOs/ISOs except ERCOT. [e18]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e12, e13, e26, e25, e24, e16, e22) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2025, 2022: The board is set by statute. It has two PUCT commissioners as nonvoting ex officio members (one of them the presiding officer), the Public Counsel as a voting ex officio member for residential and small commercial consumers, the CEO as a nonvoting ex officio member, and eight members chosen by the selection committee for executive-level experience in fields such as finance, trading, risk management or electric market design. [e12] The selection committee has three members, appointed by the governor, the lieutenant governor and the speaker of the house; it picks the members and the chair and vice chair, with help from an outside consulting firm. [e13][e26] Board members must be Texas residents, legislators may not serve, and anyone with a fiduciary duty or assets in the ERCOT electricity market is disqualified. [e25][e24] ERCOT's 2025 annual report describes a 12-member board. [e16] The bylaws approved December 20, 2022 still describe an 11-member board with the PUCT Chair as the only commissioner, so on this point they lag the statute. [e22][e12]
- **ERCOT context #3** (cites e19, e20, e21, e23): ERCOT is a membership organization. Members must qualify in one of seven segments: Cooperative, Independent Generator, Independent Power Marketer, Independent REP, Investor-Owned Utility, Municipal, or Consumer (commercial, industrial and residential subsegments); except for consumers, a member needs an actual financial interest in the ERCOT retail or wholesale market. [e19] Membership carries little control: apart from Corporate Members' right to elect TAC Representatives by segment, members have no voting rights on ERCOT's governance. [e20] Annual dues under the bylaws are $2,000 for a Corporate Member and $500 for Associate or Adjunct Members, with lower dues for residential and commercial consumers. [e21] TAC, the stakeholder committee those members elect, sets the priority of projects approved through the NPRR, SCR and guide revision processes. [e23]
