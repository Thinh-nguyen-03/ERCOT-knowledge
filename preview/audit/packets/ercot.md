## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1): ERCOT (Electric Reliability Council of Texas, Inc.) is a Texas nonprofit corporation that the PUCT has certified as the Independent Organization for the ERCOT Region. [e1]
- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e1): State law requires a power region to have an independent organization with four jobs: nondiscriminatory access to the transmission and distribution systems, reliability and adequacy of the regional network, timely transfer of information about a customer's choice of retail electric provider, and accurate accounting of electricity production and delivery among generators and wholesale buyers and sellers. ERCOT is that organization for the ERCOT Region. [e2][e1]
- **How it works #2** (cites e3): Day to day, ERCOT runs the operational and market functions of the ERCOT system: it procures and deploys ancillary services, schedules resources and loads, and manages transmission congestion. It also runs settlement and billing, which includes assessing market participants' creditworthiness and setting and enforcing security (collateral) requirements. [e3]
- **How it works #3** (cites e4): In settlement, ERCOT is the central counterparty. It is the sole buyer to each seller and the sole seller to each buyer for energy, ancillary services, RUC, ERS and other products it pays or charges for. Bilateral trades between participants and self-arranged products are the exception. [e4]
- **How it works #4** (cites e14): ERCOT also holds comprehensive authority over planning bulk transmission projects that affect the system's transfer capability, and it supervises and coordinates the planning of the TSPs. [e14]
- **How it works #5** (cites e5): ERCOT is not a profit-seeking market participant. The Protocols bar it from profiting financially from its role, and from using its discretion over ancillary service procurement or energy deployment to influence, set or control prices. [e5]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e9, e10, e11): ERCOT is directly responsible and accountable to the PUCT. The commission has complete authority to oversee and investigate ERCOT's finances, budget and operations. [e9] ERCOT is funded by a system administration fee charged to wholesale buyers and sellers. The PUCT sets the fee range after it approves ERCOT's budget. [e10] Protocols that ERCOT adopts cannot take effect until the commission approves them. [e11]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e12, e13): The board is set by statute. It has two PUCT commissioners as nonvoting ex officio members (one of them the presiding officer), the Public Counsel as a voting ex officio member for residential and small commercial consumers, the CEO as a nonvoting ex officio member, and eight members chosen by the selection committee for executive-level experience in fields such as finance, trading, risk management or electric market design. [e12] The selection committee has three members, appointed by the governor, the lieutenant governor and the speaker of the house. [e13]
- **ERCOT context #3** (cites e8) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2026: As of the October 2026 fact sheet, ERCOT describes itself as a membership-based 501(c)(4) nonprofit governed by a board and overseen by the PUCT and the Texas Legislature. It also performs financial settlement for the wholesale market and handles retail switching for more than 8 million premises in competitive choice areas. [e8]

## Uncited guidance text (check for new facts, numbers, dates, names)

- (Say it like this) (Guidance) ERCOT is the PUCT-certified nonprofit that runs the Texas grid and its wholesale market: it schedules resources and loads, manages congestion, procures reserves, and settles the transactions it administers as the central counterparty. It answers to the PUCT, which approves its budget, its fee and its protocol changes, so ERCOT is not a self-governing industry club.
