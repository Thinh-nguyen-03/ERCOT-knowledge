## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2, e3): NERC is the North American Electric Reliability Corporation, certified by FERC as the Electric Reliability Organization (ERO) responsible for establishing mandatory Reliability Standards, which FERC must approve. [e1] Texas RE (Texas Reliability Entity, called Texas Regional Entity in the report) is one of the Regional Entities, which are independent regional bodies with delegated authority from NERC to propose and enforce Reliability Standards. [e2][e3]
- **How it works #1** (cites e1, e2, e4): NERC writes the mandatory standards and the Regional Entities administer and enforce them regionally under NERC's delegated authority. [e1][e2] In ERCOT, ERCOT itself carries reliability functions: it serves as a Balancing Authority and Planning Coordinator and shares Transmission Operator duties with transmission utilities in its footprint. [e4]
- **How it works #2** (cites e5): Texas RE staff also worked alongside ERCOT on winter readiness. ERCOT and Texas RE staff typically visited 75-80 generating units per year from winter 2011/2012 to winter 2019/2020 to assess winter readiness. [e5]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e6, e3, e8, e7) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2021: After the February 2021 event, FERC, NERC and all six Regional Entities, including Texas RE, formed an inquiry team whose scope included lessons for NERC cold weather Reliability Standards. [e6][e3] A footnote in the report says that in August FERC approved NERC Reliability Standard revisions that add a requirement for generating units to have a cold weather preparedness plan. [e8] The Protocols also let the NERC Regional Entity submit NPRRs. [e7]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e1): - NERC is the FERC-certified ERO and develops mandatory Reliability Standards. [e1]
