## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e7, e16) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2024: The Performance Credit Mechanism (PCM) is a reliability program, proposed but never implemented, in which LSEs would have to buy "performance credits" earned by generators for being available during the top 30 hours of highest risk, at a centrally set clearing price. [e1] It did not go ahead as designed: in December 2024 PUCT Staff recommended not moving forward with the PCM as currently designed, and the minutes of the December 19, 2024 open meeting record that the PUCT adopted Staff's final recommendation on the PCM. [e7][e16]
- **How it works #1** (cites e1, e40, e34, e17, e12, e20, e23, e24, e25) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2023: As E3 described it (per the IMM's 2023 report), generators earn credits by being available in the top 30 hours of highest risk, and LSEs must buy them at a centrally determined clearing price. [e1] The credit requirement is fixed before the compliance period, but settlement happens afterward, based on the credits actually produced. [e40] The IMM therefore called the PCM a novel form of capacity market, settled ex post on after-the-fact availability rather than ex ante on expected availability. [e34] On February 29, 2024 ERCOT and E3 filed a draft strawman that set out 37 design-parameter decisions, and the PUCT selected the final parameters on August 29, 2024. [e17][e12] The chosen design used a $1 billion gross cost cap, and the assessment used the CONE the Commission selected, $140/kW-year (a Frame CT with a 20-year life, levelized in real dollars). [e20][e23] E3 described the penalty as follows: a generator that clears forward but is unavailable pays two percent of the forward price and must buy back the full quantity at the actual PCM price. [e24] The IMM described the demand curve as set using trailing one-year net energy and ancillary service revenues. [e25]
- **How it works #2** (cites e3, e4, e27, e5, e6, e28, e29, e44): State law fences in any such program. The commission may not require LSEs or retail customers to buy such credits unless net cost to the market does not exceed $1 billion annually, less the cost of lawful interim or bridge solutions; the commission may adjust that limit for peak demand growth and inflation with a base year of 2026 (among other conditions). [e3] Credits go only to dispatchable generation, ERCOT must buy them centrally, a generator cannot earn more credits than it bid into the forward market, and a unit earns credit only for being available in real time during the tightest intervals, defined seasonally by the PUCT. [e4] The program also needs a penalty structure that gives load a net benefit, a single ERCOT-wide price with no locational differences, and removal of any bridge-solution market changes within one year of the program's start. [e27] ERCOT must begin implementing real-time co-optimization before the program starts. [e5] Before adopting it, the PUCT must require ERCOT and the IMM to complete an updated assessment of the program's cost to and effects on the ERCOT market and to submit a cost-benefit report to the commission and the Legislature. [e6] The statute does not require the PUCT to adopt any credit program at all. [e28] If one were adopted, a credit-receiving generator could not retire a dispatchable unit while participating unless the retirement begins after September 1, 2028, or the PUCT finds it is required by federal law or would relieve significant financial hardship. [e29] Once a program existed, the IMM would have to evaluate its benefits against its costs every two years and report to the Legislature. [e44]
- **Formula #1** (cites e3, e21) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 39.1594: Statutory cost limit (PURA 39.1594(a)(1)): net cost of credits to the market must not exceed $1 billion per year minus the cost of any lawful interim or bridge solutions. The PUCT may scale the $1 billion in proportion to the highest year-over-year net peak demand and for inflation, both from a 2026 base year. [e3] PUCT Staff's position was that the only way to ensure compliance with this net-cost language was to cap gross PCM cost at $1 billion. [e21]
- **Formula #2** (cites e24) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2%: Non-performance settlement under the chosen design, as E3 described it: penalty at 2% of the forward market price, plus buy-back of the full quantity at the actual PCM market price.
