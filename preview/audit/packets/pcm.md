## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e7) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2024: The Performance Credit Mechanism (PCM) is a proposed ERCOT reliability program in which LSEs would have to buy "performance credits" earned by generators for being available during the top 30 hours of highest risk, at a centrally set clearing price. [e1] The saved sources show it only as a proposal: in December 2024 PUCT Staff recommended not moving forward with the PCM as designed. [e7]
- **How it works #1** (cites e1, e12): As designed, generators earn credits by being available in the highest-risk hours (the top 30 hours), and LSEs must buy them at a centrally determined price. [e1] The PUCT selected the final design parameters on August 29, 2024. [e12]
- **How it works #2** (cites e3, e4, e5, e6): State law fences in any such program. The commission may not require LSEs or retail customers to buy such credits unless net cost to the market does not exceed $1 billion annually, less the cost of lawful interim or bridge solutions (among other conditions). [e3] Credits go only to dispatchable generation, ERCOT must buy them centrally, a generator cannot earn more credits than it bid into the forward market, and a unit earns credit only for being available in real time during the tightest intervals, defined seasonally by the PUCT. [e4] ERCOT must begin implementing real-time co-optimization before the program starts. [e5] Before adopting it, the PUCT must get an updated cost-benefit assessment from ERCOT and the IMM and report it to the Legislature. [e6]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e2, e11): The PUCT adopted the PCM as its preferred Phase II market design on January 19, 2023, but delayed implementation until the Legislature weighed in. [e2] In November 2023 ERCOT added a multi-step ORDC price floor as the "bridge solution" until the PCM could be implemented. [e11]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e15, e8, e9, e7, e10) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2024: PUCT Staff reviewed the ERCOT/E3 and IMM assessments in December 2024. Its memo, summarizing the ERCOT/E3 assessment, says the PCM as designed was anticipated to incentivize 780 MW of additional dispatchable generation and reduce projected long-term equilibrium LOLE from 2.67 to 2.18 days per year. [e15][e8] ERCOT/E3 put the net cost at approximately $12 million, while the IMM said long-term net costs with the gross cap may be $350 million to $725 million. [e9] Staff recommended not moving forward, and Chairman Gleeson wrote that he did not believe the PCM as designed would provide the reliability benefits needed, and that resources were better directed to RTC+B and DRRS. [e7][e10]
