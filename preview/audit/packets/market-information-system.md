## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e10, e4, e5): The MIS has two restricted areas. The Secure Area is available only to registered Market Participants, and the Certified Area only to a specific Market Participant. [e2][e10] ERCOT posts to the MIS as directed by the Protocols and, apart from information requested by a Market Participant, may not use the MIS to post information beyond what the Protocols or market guides specifically require. [e4] The restricted areas can carry ERCOT Critical Energy Infrastructure Information (ECEII) where the Protocols require it, and then only authorized individuals with ECEII-eligible credentials may access it. [e5]
- **How it works #2** (cites e9, e12, e13, e14): ERCOT can also post to the Certified Area information a Market Participant requests but the Protocols do not require, to the extent the request is reasonable, in ERCOT's sole discretion. [e9] ERCOT may use its website to communicate information not posted to the MIS. [e12] ERCOT must maintain a list of all posting requirements in the Protocols and market guides and post that list and its changes on the website. [e13] Where information must be disseminated under NERC Reliability Standards, ERCOT uses the MIS as necessary, adds it to the posting list, and notifies Market Participants by Market Notice. [e14]
- **How it works #3** (cites e15, e16) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 12.3: Design requirements (Protocols 12.3, current text): ERCOT must give all Market Participants access to the MIS on a nondiscriminatory basis, and the MIS must at a minimum provide all information required by PUCT or other governmental regulations. [e15] It must include security measures for ECEII and Protected Information, follow industry standards for commercial websites including query and search, and give easy navigation based on the posting list. [e16]
- **How it works #4** (cites e25, e26, e27, e28, e29, e36): Practice. For settlement, QSE and CRRAH statements and invoices sit in the MIS Certified Area and are available only to the entities owning the data with a matching DUNS Number. [e25] A user needs the appropriate MIS role from the entity's User Security Administrator, including QSE Extracts (for QSEs) or CRR Extracts (for CRRs) to view and download statements, invoices and settlement extracts. [e26] The same statements, invoices and extracts are also available via the API, using the report type IDs in the ERCOT Market Information List (EMIL) with the GetReport function. [e27] The Settlements Calendar is posted as an extract on the ERCOT website. [e28] On a QSE or CRRAH statement a negative amount is a payment due to the QSE/CRRAH and a positive amount is due to ERCOT. [e29] Ad hoc invoices are normally posted on the MIS Certified Area. [e36]
- **How it works #5** (cites e30, e31, e32, e33): Disputes. Settlement disputes can be filed through the MIS web portal, which authenticates with the Market Participant's MIS credentials, or through the API. [e30][e31] ERCOT posts a summary dispute report and a Market Participant-specific dispute extract on the MIS Certified Area; the extract shows all non-closed, non-withdrawn disputes plus those closed or withdrawn within the last 120 days. [e32][e33]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e18, e19, e20, e21, e22, e23, e24) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 25.506: Public posting timelines come from the PUCT's rule 25.506, which says ERCOT must post the information it requires at a publicly accessible location on its website, never disclosing competitively sensitive consumption data, and make the information available to all market participants. [e18] The saved copy shows an effective date of 05/28/25. Aggregated information, starting with the quantities and prices of offers for energy and each type of ancillary capacity service in the form of supply curves, is posted two calendar days after the day. [e19] Entity-specific information is posted 60 calendar days after the day, except where otherwise prescribed. [e20] Exceptions to the 60 days: the highest-priced offer selected or dispatched in each interval ERCOT-wide is posted with the offering entity's name three calendar days after the day, [e21] and, when clearing prices exceed 50 times a gas price index, the offer pairs at or above that level are posted seven calendar days after the day. [e22] State Estimator data (transmission flows, voltages and tap positions for each hour) is among the 60-day items, but ERCOT must release relevant data earlier if, in its sole discretion, that is necessary to explain unexpected market operations or system events. [e23] Forced outages and derates are posted in resource-specific form not later than the third business day after the outage or derate ends. [e24]
