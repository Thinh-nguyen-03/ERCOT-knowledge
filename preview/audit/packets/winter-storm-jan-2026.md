- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e20, e2, e10): Winter Storm Fern was the January 2026 cold weather and freezing precipitation event that ERCOT managed without calling for conservation, issuing an EEA or having systemwide outages. [e1] ERCOT's Board update dates it January 23-27, with cold across the whole region and precipitation in the upper half of the state; the IMM dates it January 21-27. [e20][e2][e10]
- **How it works #1** (cites e27, e23, e60, e61, e62, e63, e24, e31, e32): Preparation. ERCOT's post-event report (January 28, 2026) lists mandatory weatherization with at least 450 inspections planned for the winter, more reserves, earlier commitment, RTC+B, backup fuel, and TCEQ enforcement discretion and DOE orders obtained in advance. - **How it works #2** (cites e6, e34, e4, e42, e43, e3, e19, e16, e5, e36, e35): Demand came in below forecast. ERCOT's peak forecast was 84,500 MW, a figure that assumed no demand response and was about 5 GW above the February 2025 actual peak because of about 4 GW of load growth and colder temperatures (18.9F system temperature at peak versus 24.7F). [e6][e34] The maximum unofficial instantaneous peak was 75,922 MW, not a new winter record; the January monthly overview gives an hourly peak of 75,572 MW on January 26. [e4][e42][e43] ERCOT says demand was lower than forecast mainly because of slightly warmer weather in some areas and widespread school and business closures, and it estimates that oil and gas began reducing about 4,200 MW and cryptocurrency mining about 4,100 MW, with smaller cuts from steel (420 MW), data centers (170 MW) and hydrogen and electrofuel (75 MW). [e3][e19][e16][e5][e36] ERCOT's Board update says the forecast overestimated peak consumption on Monday, January 26 and Tuesday, January 27. [e35]
- **How it works #3** (cites e15, e37, e38, e8, e29, e30): Supply. About 13.5 GW of wind nameplate was unavailable due to icing, though actual lost output was smaller because wind speeds were low. [e15] The usual North Texas gas restrictions were issued, many generators reported pipeline Operational Flow Orders, and FFSS kept 340 MW of gas-restricted units running; ERCOT says non-IRR outages stayed at a level consistent with recent winter storms. [e37][e38][e8] Batteries kept enough state of charge for the morning and evening peaks; lower demand and higher wind overnight on January 25-26 meant less discharge for the January 25 evening peak and more charge left for the January 26 morning peak. [e29][e30]
- **How it works #4** (cites e17, e13, e44, e7, e33, e28, e26): Transmission.
