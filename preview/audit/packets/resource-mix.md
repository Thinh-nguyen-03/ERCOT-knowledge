## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e2): ERCOT's resource mix is the makeup of its generation, by installed capacity and by energy produced, across natural gas, wind, solar, coal, nuclear, storage and other resources. In 2025 natural gas produced 41.1% of ERCOT's generation, coal 12.9% and solar 13.9%, with wind's share nearly flat from 2024. [e2]
- **How it works #1** (cites e5) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2025: Capacity and energy shares differ because resources run at different rates. The IMM puts 2025 overall capacity factors at 31.6% for wind and 23.8% for solar, and notes that solar's contribution at the net peak has collapsed as that hour moves into the evening. [e5] So a resource's share of installed MW is not its share of MWh or its share of the hardest hours to serve.
- **How it works #2** (cites e1, e4): New entry is now dominated by solar and storage. In 2025 about 14.8 GW came online: 6.2 GW solar, 7.3 GW storage, 1.2 GW wind and 280 MW of combustion turbines. [e1] Since 2021 solar additions have averaged 5.7 GW a year and storage 3.3 GW. [e1] Storage capacity reached more than 17,000 MW and 31,500 MWh at the end of 2025. [e4]
- **How it works #3** (cites e14, e6): Location matters. The IMM says the West zone is a substantial net exporter because Panhandle renewables more than offset Permian Basin imports. [e14] About 4.7% of wind generation was curtailed in 2025. [e6]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e12, e13, e7, e8, e9) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2021, 2025: The shift is fast. In February 2021, ERCOT's board deck showed installed gas at 51,667 MW (47.45%), coal at 13,630 MW (12.52%) and wind at 31,390 MW (28.83%). [e12][e13] By October 2026 ERCOT's fact sheet lists 41,926 MW of utility-scale solar and 21,537 MW of installed energy storage. [e7][e8] The December 2025 CDR expects solar to keep leading capacity growth, followed by battery storage. [e9]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e3, e2, e10, e11): On the energy side, solar's generation share went from 7.2% in 2023 to 10.4% in 2024 and 13.9% in 2025, while gas fell to 41.1% in 2025. [e3][e2] Gas's 41.1% share in 2025 is well above coal's 12.9% and solar's 13.9%, and EIA lists natural gas as the primary energy source for Texas as a whole. [e2][e10] For an older statewide baseline, EIA reported that in February 2020 wind produced 22% and coal 13% of Texas net generation, and that nuclear consistently generates about 10%. [e11]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e2): - 2025 generation shares (IMM): gas 41.1%, solar 13.9%, coal 12.9%; wind nearly flat versus 2024. [e2]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e3): - 2024 generation shares (IMM 2024 report): gas 44.2%, coal 12.6%, solar 10.4%. [e3]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e1): - 2025 new capacity: about 14.8 GW, of which 6.2 GW solar and 7.3 GW storage. [e1]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e4): - ESRs: more than 17,000 MW and 31,500 MWh at end-2025, up almost 80% in a year. [e4]

- **Common confusion #1** (cites e5, e10): (Guidance) Capacity share is not energy share: wind and solar have capacity factors of 31.6% and 23.8%, so MW share and MWh share differ, and for solar the capacity factor at the evening net peak is far lower still. ## Uncited guidance text (check for new facts, numbers, dates, names)

- (Say it like this) (Guidance) Gas still produces the largest quoted share of ERCOT energy, but most new capacity is solar and batteries, so the question is shifting from "how much capacity" to "what is available after sunset." That is why net load, storage duration and gas units' evening role matter more each year.
