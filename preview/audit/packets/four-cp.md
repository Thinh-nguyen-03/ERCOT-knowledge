## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2): 4CP is the method ERCOT-region transmission costs are allocated today: a TSP's rate is its approved transmission cost of service divided by the average ERCOT coincident peak demand in June, July, August and September, and each distribution service provider pays that rate times its own previous-year average demand in those four intervals. [e1] Wholesale transmission in ERCOT is priced on a postage stamp basis under PURA. [e2]
- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e1, e4): Each summer month has one coincident peak interval; the IMM describes these as the highest 15-minute system load intervals in each month from June through September. A load's share of demand in those four intervals sets its transmission charge for the next year. [e3][e1] Distribution providers recover the wholesale charges from their customers through a Transmission Cost Recovery Factor, updated on March 1 and September 1. [e4]
- **How it works #2** (cites e3, e5, e6): By reducing demand during these intervals, entities can lower their share of transmission charges ("4CP response"). [e3] The IMM puts transmission charges at $8 to $9 per MWh in inflation-adjusted terms since 2021, and ERCOT estimated load fell about 1,500 MW in the August 2025 4CP interval. [e3][e5] On 7/22/2026, the all-time demand record day, ERCOT estimated a maximum 4CP reduction of about 492 MW, and a separately reported maximum reduction from cryptomining loads of about 3,299 MW. [e6]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e9, e7, e8, e10, e11, e12): 4CP is under active revision. SB 6 (June 2025) ordered the PUCT to evaluate whether 4CP still assigns transmission costs appropriately and to amend its rules by December 31, 2026. [e9][e7][e8] A March 2026 Staff draft report recommended a coincident peak method with more CPs and a longer measurement interval. [e10] A proposal for publication was set for consideration at the PUCT's July 9, 2026 open meeting that would replace 4CP with 12CP measured in 30-minute intervals; it is a proposal, not an adopted rule. [e11][e12]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e15, e13): The IMM argues 4CP response suppresses prices and weakens scarcity pricing when the peak intervals no longer match system scarcity. [e15] It recommends annual load ratio share allocation, or a hybrid with floating coincident peaks of one hour. [e13]
