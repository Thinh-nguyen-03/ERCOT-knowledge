## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **How it works #1** (cites e20, e19, e1, e4): Each summer month has one coincident peak. The Protocols define it as the highest monthly Settlement Interval 15-minute MW peak for the whole ERCOT Transmission Grid, and the ERCOT average 4CP is the average of the June to September peaks. [e20] ERCOT calculates each DSP's 4CP and the ERCOT average 4CP from Settlement data and posts these billing determinants by December 1 each year. [e19] Each DSP's previous-year average demand in those four intervals then sets its monthly transmission charge; the wholesale storage load exclusion is written into the ERCOT coincident peak demand used to compute the TSP's rate. [e1] Distribution providers pass changes in the wholesale transmission costs billed by TSPs through to their customers with a Transmission Cost Recovery Factor (TCRF), updated on March 1 and September 1. [e4]
- **How it works #2** (cites e3, e46, e42, e5, e6): By reducing demand during these intervals, entities can lower their share of transmission charges ("4CP response"). [e3] The IMM puts transmission charges at $8 to $9 per MWh in inflation-adjusted terms since 2021 (its 2024 report gave about $10 per MWh for 2024). [e3][e46] ERCOT estimated about 2,500 MW of 4CP load reduction in 2024 (roughly 1,000 MW less than in 2023) and about 1,500 MW in the August 2025 4CP interval, per the IMM. [e42][e5] On 7/22/2026, the all-time demand record day, ERCOT estimated a maximum 4CP reduction of about 492 MW, and a separately reported maximum reduction from cryptomining loads of about 3,299 MW. [e6]
- **Formula #1** (cites e1, e20) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 25.192: Current rule (16 TAC 25.192, in effect): TSP annual rate = commission-approved transmission cost of service ÷ ERCOT average 4CP demand (June to September, excluding wholesale storage load); monthly DSP charge = TSP monthly rate × the DSP's previous-year average 4CP demand. [e1] Each coincident peak is a 15-minute Settlement Interval. [e20]
- **Formula #2** (cites e12, e14, e33, e47, e34): Proposed rule (Project 58000, not adopted): replace the four summer peaks with 12CP, the system monthly peak 30-minute interval in each of the 12 months from October 1 to September 30. [e12][e14] A DSP's 12CP load would include transmission-voltage load and block load transfers but exclude export flows and wholesale storage load. [e33] For each large load customer, ERCOT would add to the DSP's measured 12CP load the difference between that customer's large load minimum billing demand (below) and its measured 12CP load. [e47][e34]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e43, e28, e49, e29, e30): 4CP dates from the mid-1990s. The IMM says the method was approved in 1996. [e43] PUCT Staff's history describes the first ERCOT-wide rate as 70% "access fee" on a postage stamp basis and 30% "impact fee" on a megawatt-mile basis, combined into an annual facilities charge billed to each utility per 4CP MW monthly. [e28][e49] SB 7 (1999; the draft's text reads "SB 78") then required pricing based entirely on the postage stamp method, and the rate became the old access-fee formula applied to 100% of TCOS. [e29][e30]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e9, e7, e22, e8, e23, e10, e24, e25, e27, e26, e48, e31): 4CP is under active revision. SB 6 (June 2025) ordered the PUCT to evaluate whether 4CP still assigns transmission costs appropriately, including alternatives such as multiple seasonal peaks or different interval lengths, and to amend its rules by December 31, 2026. [e9][e7][e22][e8] Staff opened Project 58484 on August 1, 2025. [e23] Its March 2026 draft report (comments due April 13, 2026) recommended a coincident peak method with more CPs and a longer measurement interval, and a minimum demand charge for large loads based on contracted peak demand for 10 to 15 years. [e10][e24][e25] Staff's draft credits 4CP with stable rates (4CP values moving less than 3% a year on average) but says 15-minute intervals give too sharp a signal. [e27][e26] Its analysis of 2023-2024 data finds system load peaking around HE 18 while net load and real-time prices peak around HE 20. [e48][e31]
- **ERCOT context #3** (cites e11, e38, e32, e37, e34, e35, e36, e45, e39, e40, e21, e41): The proposal for publication in Project 58000 was prepared for the PUCT's July 9, 2026 open meeting, with comments due August 11, 2026. [e11][e38] Beyond 12CP, it would require block load transfers to pay transmission costs, make interconnection fees and forfeited financial security under 25.194 offset TSP revenue requirements, and require non-opt-in DSPs to give ERCOT settlement-quality meter data for each large load. [e32][e37] A REP serving a large load would be charged for that customer on a minimum billing demand: the greatest of contracted peak demand, the highest non-coincident peak in the past year, or 12CP demand. [e34][e35] Billing would start when service is available, whether or not the customer has energized, for at least 20 years. [e36] New 25.252 would require large loads to pay contribution in aid of construction and to sign interconnection agreements of at least 20 years. [e45][e39] The PUCT also asked whether large computational loads should get separate rate treatment; that is a question for comment, not a proposal. [e40] PURA already requires large loads to contribute to the cost of interconnecting them. [e21] If the rule is adopted, each TSP must file a tariff compliance update within 30 days. [e41]
- **ERCOT context #4** (cites e15, e50, e51, e44, e13): The IMM argues 4CP response suppresses prices and weakens scarcity pricing when the peak intervals no longer match system scarcity. [e15] It also says the correlation between congestion costs and peak demand over the last five years (Figure 44, 2021-2025) is too weak to justify allocating on peak. [e50][e51][e44] It recommends annual load ratio share allocation, or a hybrid that would allocate a portion of T-COS on a predetermined number of floating coincident peaks lasting one hour. [e13]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e20, e19): - Interval: highest monthly 15-minute Settlement Interval peak; determinants posted by December 1. [e20][e19]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e42, e5, e6): - 4CP response: about 2,500 MW in 2024; about 1,500 MW in the August 2025 interval; about 492 MW estimated on July 22, 2026. [e42][e5][e6]

- **Say it like this #1** (cites e1, e20, e3, e8, e12, e36): (Guidance) Today, each distribution provider's wholesale transmission charge for the next year is set by its demand in four summer 15-minute peak intervals, so flexible loads curtail at those moments to shrink their share. [e1][e20][e3] SB 6 ordered the PUCT to fix the method by the end of 2026; the July 2026 proposal moves to twelve monthly 30-minute peaks and bills large loads on at least their contracted demand for 20 years, but it is still a proposal. [e8][e12][e36]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e15, e11, e10, e25, e36): (Guidance) 4CP is a transmission cost allocation rule, not an energy price or a reliability program; 4CP response is cutting load to cut transmission charges, not responding to scarcity, and the IMM says the two can diverge. [e15] The 12CP change is proposed in Project 58000, not adopted; the March 2026 Project 58484 report is a Staff draft. [e11][e10] Note the two different large-load terms: Staff's draft said 10 to 15 years, the proposal for publication says no less than 20 years. [e25][e36]
- **Questions #2** (cites e1, e20, e19): It is the TSP's approved transmission cost of service divided by average ERCOT coincident peak demand over June to September, excluding wholesale storage load. Each DSP pays the monthly rate times its previous-year average 4CP demand. [e1] Each peak is the highest 15-minute Settlement Interval of the month, and ERCOT publishes the determinants by December 1. [e20][e19]
- **Questions #4** (cites e15, e44, e13): It says 4CP response can suppress prices and reduce generator revenues when the peak intervals no longer reflect scarcity, and that peak demand only weakly correlates with congestion costs. [e15][e44] It supports annual load ratio share allocation or a hybrid with floating one-hour coincident peaks. [e13]
- **Questions #6** (cites e7, e8, e10, e12, e34): SB 6 required the PUCT to evaluate 4CP and amend its rules by December 31, 2026. [e7][e8] Staff's March 2026 draft recommended more CPs and longer intervals, and the July 2026 proposal for publication would adopt 12CP with 30-minute intervals plus a large-load minimum billing demand. [e10][e12][e34]
- **Questions #8** (cites e26, e14): Staff's draft says 15-minute intervals give too sharp a signal in too narrow a window and over-value peak avoidance relative to the transmission cost avoided. [e26] Twelve 30-minute monthly peaks would spread cost responsibility over more, longer intervals. [e14]
- **Questions #10** (cites e34, e35, e36, e1): Under the proposal (not adopted), its minimum billing demand would be the greatest of its contracted peak demand, its highest non-coincident peak in the past year, or its 12CP demand, and billing would start at the level of available service whether or not it has energized. [e34][e35][e36] Under today's 4CP rule it pays on its actual demand in the four summer intervals. [e1]
- **Questions #12** (cites e48, e31, e15): Staff's analysis of 2023-2024 data finds system load peaks around HE 18 while net load and real-time prices peak around HE 20. [e48][e31] So load cut at the gross peak may not be load cut when scarcity is highest. [e15]
