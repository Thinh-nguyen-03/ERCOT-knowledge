## Uncited guidance text (check for new facts, numbers, dates, names)

- (Say it like this) (Guidance) "Uplift is ERCOT passing through reliability costs that the market prices did not cover, such as paying units it ordered on-line, and every load-serving QSE pays its share, mostly based on how much load it served in those intervals (for RUC make-whole, capacity-short QSEs pay first). The charge is not a penalty on us; it rises when ERCOT relies more on out-of-market actions like RUC and RMR, and we can budget for it per MWh."
- (Say it like this) (Guidance) For a finance audience: "Think of it as a pro-rata pass-through by load share, booked on the day-five statement, trued up on day 55, and possibly again at 180 days."
- (Scenarios) - (Reasoning) Start with one sentence finance can repeat, then the mechanism, the allocation, the trend and a budget number.
- (Scenarios) - Suppose (illustrative) a day's RUC make-whole uplift is $500,000 and our Load Ratio Share is 4%: our charge is about $20,000 for that day.
- (Scenarios) - (Reasoning) Recommend budgeting uplift per MWh of load, checking whether our QSE was capacity-short in the RUC intervals, and watching RUC and RMR trends in ERCOT and IMM reports.
- (Scenarios) - (Reasoning) Reconcile by mapping each statement charge type to the IMM's list, then compare per-MWh rather than dollars, since our share moves with our LRS.
