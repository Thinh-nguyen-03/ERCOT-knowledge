## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **How it works #3** (cites e6, e12, e13): For each new CREZ the commission's order specifies the geographic extent, the major transmission improvements (new and upgraded lines by voltage level and a general description of where new lines interconnect), and an estimate of the maximum generating capacity the transmission is expected to accommodate. [e6][e12] Each selected TSP had to file all required CREZ CCN applications not later than three years after the final order designating a CREZ, unless the commission granted an extension for good cause. [e13]
- **How it works #4** (cites e8, e9, e14, e10): Financial commitment for a CREZ is sufficient to grant CCNs for its transmission facilities if the sum of renewable generating capacity under any combination of the rule's paragraph (3)(A) to (D) categories is at least 50% of the zone's designated capacity. [e8] The rule fixes 50% at 1,595.5 MW for Panhandle A and 1,196.5 MW for Panhandle B. [e9] For the McCamey, Central and Central West CREZs approved in Docket Number 33672, the rule treats installed generation, generation under construction and planned projects with signed interconnection agreements as satisfying the financial commitment test. [e14] A renewable generator that elects to post collateral under paragraph (3)(D) must post $15,350 per MW of planned project capacity, or $10,000 per MW where the capacity is supported by leasing agreements of at least 20 years. [e10]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e15, e16) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2011: The IMM's 2011 report said that the physical limit of its most active constraint, the West to North interface, would over the long term increase as CREZ transmission projects were completed. [e15] The same report said low West-zone prices were generally caused by high wind output in the West that frequently results in severe congestion on transmission interfaces from the West zone to the other zones. [e16]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e17, e18, e19): By 2020 the IMM was reporting the Panhandle GTC constraint as the second most costly constraint of the year, at $140 million of congestion, mostly caused by planned outages including ETT maintenance outages; almost 4.6 GW of generation, about 90% wind, was in the Panhandle by the end of 2020. [e17] The IMM said that activating the constraints in the Panhandle GTC, the Lewisville area, the Lynx to Tombstone 138 kV line and the Eagle Mountain to Morris Dido 138 kV line dispatched wind down and increased generation in the North. [e18] In 2022 the IMM said the Panhandle GTC ($129 million in 2021) fell to seventh because of the resolution of CREZ maintenance outages completed in 2022, while the West Texas Export GTC ($304 million) was the most costly constraint. [e19]
- **Timeline #1** (cites e1, e3): - 2005: SB 408 directs the PUCT to facilitate new transmission for CREZ, alongside an RPS increase to 5,880 MW. [e1][e3]
- **Timeline #2** (cites e5): - 1 December 2006: the date by which the PUCT rule required ERCOT to give the commission a statewide wind potential and transmission constraint study. [e5]
- **Timeline #3** (cites e15) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2011: - 2011: the IMM expects the West to North interface limit to rise as CREZ transmission projects are completed. [e15]
- **Timeline #4** (cites e17): - 2020: Panhandle GTC is the second most costly constraint ($140 million). [e17]
- **Timeline #5** (cites e19): - 2022: the IMM refers to CREZ maintenance outages completed in 2022 and the Panhandle GTC falls from first (2021, $129 million) to seventh. [e19]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e17, e19): - Panhandle GTC congestion: $140 million in 2020 and $129 million in 2021 (IMM); the West Texas Export GTC was the costliest constraint in 2022 at $304 million. [e17][e19]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e1, e3, e17, e19, e11): CREZ is a transmission-planning policy, not a market product like a CRR; the saved sources do not describe CREZ as a settlement or pricing mechanism. [e1][e3] Congestion continued after the CREZ build: the IMM kept reporting Panhandle and West export constraints (and in 2022 linked the earlier Panhandle GTC cost to CREZ maintenance outages). [e17][e19] The rule itself contemplates congestion from excess development. [e11]
- **Questions #8** (cites e17, e19, e11): The sources do not give a full causal account. What the IMM reports is that Panhandle GTC congestion in 2020 was mostly caused by planned outages including maintenance on ETT structures, and that West Texas Export GTC congestion in 2022 was mostly caused by transmission maintenance outages in the West combined with high renewable output. [e17][e19] The rule also anticipated that excess development beyond a zone's order could produce congestion SCED cannot resolve. [e11]
- **Questions #10** (cites e11): If requested transmission service exceeds the maximum renewable capacity in the order, and the commission finds SCED dispatch priority insufficient to resolve the resulting congestion, it may open a proceeding and consider limiting interconnection or setting dispatch priorities. [e11]
- **Link to texas-legislature** (cites e1, e3): related Texas Legislature: The Legislature created CREZ through SB 408 in 2005. [e1][e3]
- **Link to puct** (cites e1, e4) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 25.174: related PUCT (Public Utility Commission of Texas): SB 408 directed the PUCT to facilitate the transmission process, and 16 TAC 25.174 sets how the PUCT designates zones and orders transmission. [e1][e4]
- **Link to wind-generation** (cites e3): related Wind Generation: The CREZ transmission was planned to connect roughly 18 GW of wind capacity. [e3]
- **Link to congestion** (cites e11): related Transmission Congestion: The PUCT rule lets the commission act if excess development in a CREZ causes congestion that SCED cannot resolve. [e11]
- **Link to gtc** (cites e19): related GTC (Generic Transmission Constraint): The Panhandle and West Texas Export GTCs are the constraints the IMM tied to Panhandle and West generation export; the IMM linked the fall of the Panhandle GTC's cost in 2022 to completed CREZ maintenance outages. [e19]
- **Link to binding-constraint** (cites e15): affects Binding Constraint: The IMM expected the physical limit of the West to North interface constraint to rise as CREZ transmission projects were completed. [e15]
- **Link to hb-west** (cites e16): related HB_WEST (West Hub): The IMM ties low West-zone prices to high wind output and congested interfaces out of the West. [e16]
- **Link to tdsp** (cites e7): related TDSP (Transmission & Distribution Service Provider): Under the PUCT rule the commission selects the entity or entities responsible for constructing CREZ transmission improvements. [e7]
- **Link to nodal-market** (cites e11): related Nodal Market: The CREZ rule refers to the security constrained economic dispatch mechanism used to establish dispatch priority of CREZ resources, and lets the commission consider dispatch priorities if it is insufficient. [e11]
