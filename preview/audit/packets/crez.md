## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e3, e4, e7) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 25.174: Competitive Renewable Energy Zones (CREZ) were zones for which, according to a University of Texas timeline, the Texas Legislature, via SB 408 in 2005, directed the PUCT to facilitate the design and construction of new transmission. [e1] SB 408 provided authority for the PUCT to direct ERCOT to plan for transmission to connect approximately 18 GW of wind capacity. [e3] The PUCT's rule, 16 TAC 25.174, sets out how zones were designated and how transmission for them was ordered and built. [e4][e7]
- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e1): The mechanism began legislatively: SB 408 designated the framework and authorized the PUCT to direct ERCOT to plan the transmission. [e3] It came alongside an increase in the renewable portfolio standard (RPS) to 5,880 MW of renewable capacity. [e1]
- **How it works #2** (cites e4, e5, e6, e7): Under the PUCT rule, zones are designated through contested-case proceedings that commission staff initiate. [e4] ERCOT had to give the commission a study of statewide wind production potential and the transmission constraints most likely to limit deliverability. [e5] The commission issues a final order within six months of the proceeding's start, unless it finds good cause to extend, and each order specifies the geographic extent of the CREZ. [e6] The commission selects the entity or entities responsible for constructing the transmission improvements and establishes a completion schedule. [e7]
- **How it works #3** (cites e8, e9, e10): Financial commitment for a CREZ is sufficient to grant CCNs for its transmission facilities if the sum of renewable generating capacity under any combination of the rule's paragraph (3)(A) to (D) categories is at least 50% of the zone's designated capacity. [e8] The rule fixes 50% at 1,595.5 MW for Panhandle A and 1,196.5 MW for Panhandle B. [e9] A renewable generator that elects to post collateral under paragraph (3)(D) must post $15,350 per MW of planned project capacity, or $10,000 per MW where the capacity is supported by leasing agreements of at least 20 years. [e10]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e2, e11): By the end of 2020, approximately 25,000 MW of wind and 4,000 MW of solar photovoltaic capacity were installed in ERCOT, surpassing the RPS. [e2] The rule anticipates that too much generation in a zone could cause congestion that SCED alone cannot resolve; in that case the commission may consider limiting interconnection or setting dispatch priorities in the CREZ. [e11]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e1): - In 2005 the RPS was increased to 5,880 MW, and SB 408 directed the PUCT to facilitate transmission for CREZ. [e1]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e3): - Transmission planning was for roughly 18 GW of wind capacity. [e3]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e4, e6): - Zones are designated in PUCT contested cases, with a final order due within six months unless extended. [e4][e6]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e8, e9): - Financial commitment threshold: at least 50% of designated capacity (1,595.5 MW for Panhandle A, 1,196.5 MW for Panhandle B). [e8][e9]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e10): - Collateral for a generator electing to post it under paragraph (3)(D): $15,350 per MW of planned capacity, or $10,000 per MW with leases of at least 20 years. [e10]
- **Key facts #6** (cites e2): - End of 2020: about 25,000 MW of wind and 4,000 MW of solar PV installed in ERCOT. [e2]
- **Say it like this #1** (cites e3): (Guidance) CREZ was the Texas policy of designating renewable zones and ordering new transmission to carry their output, planned around about 18 GW of wind. [e3] The PUCT's rule covers how zones were picked, who built the lines and how generators had to show commitment.
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e1, e3, e11): CREZ is a transmission-planning policy, not a market product like a CRR; the saved sources do not describe CREZ as a settlement or pricing mechanism. [e1][e3] The rule does, however, contemplate congestion from excess development. [e11]
- **Questions #2** (cites e1, e3): A 2005 legislative initiative (SB 408) directing the PUCT to facilitate new transmission to serve designated Competitive Renewable Energy Zones, with planning for about 18 GW of wind. [e1][e3]
- **Questions #4** (cites e4, e6, e7): Through a PUCT contested case, with a final order specifying the zone, then selection of transmission builders and a schedule. [e4][e6][e7]
- **Questions #6** (cites e8): By reaching at least 50% of the zone's designated capacity, summed over the categories in paragraph (3)(A) to (D) of the rule. [e8]
