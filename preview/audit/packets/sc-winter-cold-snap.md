## Uncited guidance text (check for new facts, numbers, dates, names)

- (Say it like this) (Guidance) "Winter risk in ERCOT is mostly a supply question: whether gas keeps flowing to the plants and whether units and wind turbines freeze, with the morning peak as the tightest hour. The rules include a December 1 winter-preparation deadline, firm fuel service and COP updates when cold-weather notices are issued, and Winter Storm Fern got through with no EEA, but gas restrictions and icing still showed up and the load forecast itself can be wrong in either direction."
- (Say it like this) (Guidance) For a trading follow-up: "In Fern the risk premium showed up day-ahead: January day-ahead prices averaged well above real-time because of weather uncertainty, so buying protection late is expensive."
- (Scenarios) - (Reasoning) Frame it as supply risk first, demand second, and price third.
- (Scenarios) - (Reasoning) Close with positions: confirm hedges for the morning and evening peaks, check gas supply and fuel-switching at any owned units, and agree in advance what notice level changes the plan.
- (Scenarios) - (Reasoning) If the unit is not FFSS and cannot switch fuel, the honest COP update is the derate; a unit that stays in the plan and then fails leaves its QSE short in real time.
- (Scenarios) - Suppose (illustrative) a 300 MW unit is derated to 150 MW for a 4-hour morning peak at $1,000/MWh: the replacement energy is 150 x 4 x 1,000 = $600,000 if its output was sold forward.
