## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2): A REP is an entity that sells electric energy to retail customers in Texas but does not own or operate generation assets and is not an MOU or EC. [e1] The PUCT's rule says the same: a REP sells to retail customers in the state and may not own or operate generation assets. [e2]
- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e4, e9, e12): Two layers apply: PUCT certification and ERCOT registration. A person must obtain a REP certificate from the PUCT before purchasing, taking title to, or reselling electricity to provide retail electric service, and may certify as an Option 1, Option 2 or Option 3 REP. [e3][e4] PURA requires the applicant to show financial and technical resources, managerial and technical ability, resources to meet customer protection requirements, and an office in Texas. [e9] A REP that outsources functions remains responsible for them. [e12]
- **How it works #2** (cites e13, e11, e6): At ERCOT, a REP is a Competitive Retailer and so an LSE, and it must demonstrate PUCT certification under Section 25.107. [e13][e11] It must have a QSE, and ERCOT must approve the QSE designation before the CR enrolls Customer ESI IDs. [e6]
- **How it works #3** (cites e8): Each ESI ID has at most one REP of record at a time. [e8]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e5, e7): REPs exist because retail customers in Texas, other than customers of non-opted-in cooperatives and municipally owned utilities, have had customer choice since January 1, 2002. [e5] After that date a transmission and distribution utility may not sell electricity except to buy for its own needs. [e7]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e10): PURA also requires the PUCT to designate REPs as providers of last resort in areas where customer choice is in effect. [e10]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e3, e4): - REP certificate from the PUCT is required before buying and reselling electricity at retail; there are Option 1, 2 and 3 certification routes. [e3][e4]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e5): - Customer choice began on January 1, 2002 for most retail customers. [e5]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e2): - A REP may not own or operate generation assets. [e2]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e8): - One REP of record per ESI ID at a time. [e8]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e9): - PURA requires an in-state office as a certification condition. [e9]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e7, e14): (Guidance) A REP is not a utility: a transmission and distribution utility may not sell electricity except to buy for its own needs. [e7] A REP is also not the only retailer: MOUs and ECs that offer Customer Choice are Competitive Retailers too. [e14]
- **Questions #2** (cites e2): No. The PUCT definition says a REP may not own or operate generation assets. [e2]
- **Questions #4** (cites e3, e11, e6, e15): A PUCT REP certificate, ERCOT registration as an LSE, a QSE whose designation ERCOT has approved, and completion of Retail Market Testing as a CR. [e3][e11][e6][e15]
- **Link to lse** (cites e13): is-a LSE (Load Serving Entity): LSEs include Competitive Retailers, which includes REPs. [e13]
- **Link to qse** (cites e6): related QSE (Qualified Scheduling Entity): A REP must have its QSE designation approved by ERCOT before enrolling customer ESI IDs. [e6]
- **Link to retail-choice** (cites e5): part-of Retail Choice / Competitive Retail Market: REPs exist because Texas gave most retail customers a choice of provider from January 1, 2002. [e5]
- **Link to tdsp** (cites e2, e7): contrasts-with TDSP (Transmission & Distribution Service Provider): A TDU may not sell electricity, and a REP may not own generation. [e2][e7]
- **Link to esi-id** (cites e8): related ESI ID: Each ESI ID has at most one REP of record at a time. [e8]
## Uncited guidance text (check for new facts, numbers, dates, names)

- (Say it like this) (Guidance) A REP is the certified retailer that sells power to Texas customers who have choice; it needs a PUCT certificate and a QSE designation approved by ERCOT. By rule it cannot own generation.
