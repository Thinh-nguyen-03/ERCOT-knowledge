## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **Link to lse** (cites e13): is-a LSE (Load Serving Entity): LSEs include Competitive Retailers, which includes REPs. [e13]
- **Link to qse** (cites e6): related QSE (Qualified Scheduling Entity): A REP must have its QSE designation approved by ERCOT before enrolling customer ESI IDs. [e6]
- **Link to retail-choice** (cites e5): part-of Retail Choice / Competitive Retail Market: REPs exist because Texas gave most retail customers a choice of provider from January 1, 2002. [e5]
- **Link to tdsp** (cites e2, e7): contrasts-with TDSP (Transmission & Distribution Service Provider): After January 1, 2002 a transmission and distribution utility may not sell electricity except to buy for its own needs, and a REP may not own generation. [e2][e7]
- **Link to esi-id** (cites e8): related ESI ID: Each ESI ID has at most one REP of record at a time. [e8]
- **Link to polr** (cites e34, e35): related POLR (Provider of Last Resort): A provider of last resort must offer the standard retail service package to any requesting customer and to customers of a REP that fails to serve them. [e34][e35]
- **Link to mass-transition** (cites e37): causes Mass Transition: ERCOT initiates a Mass Transition of the ESI IDs of a breaching market participant that is also an LSE when its rights are revoked or agreements terminated. [e37]
- **Link to credit-tpe** (cites e6): related Credit, Collateral & TPE: ERCOT must approve a REP's QSE designation before the CR enrolls Customer ESI IDs; see the topic for credit and TPE mechanics. [e6]
- **Link to retail-hedging** (cites e28): related Retail Load Hedging: Wholesale indexed products may not be offered to residential or small commercial customers. [e28]

## Uncited guidance text (check for new facts, numbers, dates, names)

- (Say it like this) (Guidance) A REP is the certified retailer that sells power to Texas customers who have choice; it needs a PUCT certificate and a QSE designation approved by ERCOT. By rule it cannot own generation, and the customer protection rules limit how it can price and collect from residential and small commercial customers.
