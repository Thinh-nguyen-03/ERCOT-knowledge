## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2): A REP is an entity that sells electric energy to retail customers in Texas but does not own or operate generation assets and is not an MOU or EC. [e1] The PUCT's rule says the same: a REP sells to retail customers in the state and may not own or operate generation assets. [e2]
- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e4, e9, e12): **Two layers: PUCT certification and ERCOT registration.** A person must obtain a REP certificate from the PUCT before purchasing, taking title to, or reselling electricity to provide retail electric service, and may certify as an Option 1, Option 2 or Option 3 REP. [e3][e4] PURA requires the applicant to show financial and technical resources, managerial and technical ability, resources to meet customer protection requirements, and an office in Texas. [e9] A REP that outsources functions remains responsible for them. [e12]
- **How it works #2** (cites e16, e17): **The three certificate options.** In the PUCT's rule an Option 1 REP provides its offerings to any customer class based on geographic service area; an Option 2 REP limits its offerings to specifically identified customers, each contracting for one megawatt or more of capacity; an Option 3 REP sells exclusively to a retail customer other than a small commercial or residential customer, from a distributed generation facility owned by a registered power generation company on the same geographic site. [e16] An Option 2 applicant files a notarized affidavit that it will contract only for one megawatt or more, and must file a notarized affidavit from each customer, stating the customer understands and accepts the REP's ability to provide continuous and reliable service based on its financial, managerial and technical resources. [e17]
- **How it works #3** (cites e18, e19, e20, e21, e22, e23) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2026: **Option 1 financial requirements.** The ongoing financial requirements apply to Option 1 REPs and not to Option 2 or 3. [e18] An Option 1 REP must show access to capital through either a commission-approved irrevocable guaranty agreement, from an affiliate, an investment-grade financial institution or a power supplier, with the guarantor holding an investment-grade rating or meeting tangible net worth, current ratio and debt-to-capitalization tests, [e19] or an irrevocable stand-by letter of credit sized by the number of ESI IDs served: $750,000 below 50,000 ESI IDs and $1,500,000 at 50,000 or more, in the rule text saved here (the footer reads "effective 11/4/2026"). [e20] For its first 24 months serving load, a REP may not make a distribution that leaves shareholders' equity below one million dollars, and the prescribed equity requirement lapses after 24 continuous months of serving load. [e21] Customer deposits must be covered at 100% at each calendar month close by an escrow account, segregated cash account or letter of credit. [e22] A REP must notify the PUCT within three working days of a bankruptcy filing, an involuntary bankruptcy proceeding or other insolvency, including default with the independent organization or a TDU. [e23]
- **How it works #4** (cites e13, e11, e6, e15, e8): **At ERCOT.** A REP is a Competitive Retailer and so an LSE, and it must demonstrate PUCT certification under Section 25.107. [e13][e11] It must have a QSE, and ERCOT must approve the QSE designation before the CR enrolls Customer ESI IDs. [e6] All CRs must complete Retail Market Testing before commencing operations with ERCOT. [e15] Each ESI ID has at most one REP of record at a time. [e8]
- **How it works #5** (cites e24): **Customer protection rules that shape REP behavior.** The items below summarize PUCT customer protection rules and PURA provisions. [e24]
- **How it works #6** (cites e24): - The PUCT's customer protection rules are minimum, mandatory requirements; customers other than residential, small commercial, or non-residential customers aggregated above 50 kilowatts may agree to higher or lower protection, except under the listed sections (unauthorized change of REP, unauthorized charges, certain customer access and complaint handling provisions, and acknowledgment of risk for certain commercial contracts). [e24]
