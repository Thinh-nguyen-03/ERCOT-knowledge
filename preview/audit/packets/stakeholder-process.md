## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **Definition #1** (cites e3, e4, e5, e6, e7): The stakeholder process is the committee path that ERCOT Protocol changes follow: the Protocol Revision Subcommittee (PRS), then the Technical Advisory Committee (TAC), then the ERCOT Board, then the PUCT. [e3][e4][e5][e6] PURA requires ERCOT's governing body to maintain a formal process for adopting new or revised protocols as a condition of keeping its certification. [e7]
- **How it works #1** (cites e55, e63): Who may start it. Any Market Participant, any ERCOT Member, PUCT Staff, the Reliability Monitor, the NERC Regional Entity, the IMM and ERCOT may submit a revision request, among others. [e55] Those parties, and others, must use this process before asking the PUCT or another Governmental Authority to make ERCOT change the Protocols, except for good cause shown. [e63]
- **How it works #2** (cites e62, e61, e43, e12, e57, e44, e1, e3, e14) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2011: PRS. ERCOT checks a submitted NPRR for completeness and notifies the submitter within five Business Days if it is incomplete. [e62] Once complete, ERCOT posts it and distributes it to PRS within three Business Days. [e61] Comments must arrive within 14 days of posting to receive consideration, and PRS considers the request at its next regularly scheduled meeting after the 14-day comment period. [e43] PRS reviews and recommends action on revision requests. [e12] It may recommend approval as submitted or modified, reject, table, or refer. [e57] If a motion to recommend approval fails, the request is deemed rejected unless PRS votes at the same meeting to approve, table or refer. [e44] PRS meetings are open to ERCOT, ERCOT Members, Market Participants, the Reliability Monitor, the NERC Regional Entity, the IMM and PUCT Staff. [e1] PRS can send a request to working groups or TAC subcommittees for expertise, but PRS keeps ultimate responsibility for processing it. [e3] The PRS Procedures (December 1, 2011 version) add that PRS also recommends a priority and rank for requests that need an ERCOT project. [e14]
- **How it works #3** (cites e45): Impact Analysis. If PRS recommends approval, ERCOT prepares an Impact Analysis based on the language in the PRS Report. [e45]
- **How it works #4** (cites e4, e46, e56, e53): TAC. TAC takes up requests that PRS has sent forward. It can recommend approval as recommended or as modified, reject, table, remand to PRS with instructions, or refer to another TAC subcommittee or working group. [e4] The TAC Report that goes on includes the credit review, the IMM Opinion, the ERCOT Opinion and the ERCOT Market Impact Statement. [e46] TAC Representatives should explain opposing votes, and the explanations go in the TAC Report sent to the Board. [e56] TAC also sets the priority of projects approved through the NPRR, SCR and guide revision processes. [e53]
- **How it works #5** (cites e5, e59, e52, e58, e54): Board. The ERCOT Board reviews the TAC Report and the Impact Analysis at its next regularly scheduled meeting. [e5] It can recommend approval as recommended or as modified, reject, table, or remand to TAC. [e59] The Board may consider on a consent agenda requests TAC unanimously recommended (including abstentions), but any Director can ask for one to be pulled for full discussion. [e52] The Board may delegate consideration to Board Committees, and a Board vote follows a Committee recommendation. [e58] Once the Board has recommended approval, a request cannot be withdrawn. [e54]
- **How it works #6** (cites e6, e51, e40, e41): PUCT. Every revision request then needs PUCT approval before implementation. [e6] NPRRs are implemented on the first day of the month following PUCT approval unless the PUCT Report says otherwise. [e51] Under PURA, protocols may not take effect before the PUCT approves them, and the formal process must require PUCT approval of a market impact statement. [e40][e41]
- **How it works #7** (cites e9, e47, e48, e42): Appeals. Any ERCOT Member, Market Participant, PUCT Staff, the Reliability Monitor, the IMM, the NERC Regional Entity or ERCOT may appeal a PRS decision to reject, table or refer a request directly to TAC. [e9] The appeal must be filed within seven days of the PRS action, ERCOT rejects late appeals, and the appeal suspends consideration until TAC decides. [e47] A TAC rejection, tabling, remand or referral may in turn be appealed to the Board. [e48] A Board decision may be appealed to the PUCT or other Governmental Authority within the deadline it sets and no later than 35 days after the Board action. [e42]
- **How it works #8** (cites e60, e49): Urgent and Board Priority. A submitter may ask for an urgent timeline only if it can reasonably show an existing Protocol or condition is impairing or could imminently impair reliability or market operations, or is causing or could cause a settlement formula discrepancy. [e60] The Board may designate any existing request a Board Priority Revision Request, and all of those are considered on an Urgent timeline. [e49]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e21, e10, e11) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2025: The subcommittees. The TAC Procedures (approved October 22, 2025) set four standing TAC subcommittees: the Retail Market Subcommittee (RMS), the Reliability and Operations Subcommittee (ROS), the Wholesale Market Subcommittee (WMS) and PRS. [e21] WMS and ROS are also in the Protocols' acronym list. [e10][e11]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e15, e17, e16) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2023: - WMS (procedures approved December 4, 2023) reports to TAC and evaluates and reviews issues related to wholesale market operation, making recommendations to TAC. [e15] It monitors PUCT rulings on wholesale markets and ensures PUCT requirements are reflected in the Market Guides and Protocols, [e17] and reviews NPRRs for effects on the wholesale market. [e16]
- **ERCOT context #3** (cites e18, e19, e20) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2023: - ROS (procedures approved July 25, 2023) reports to TAC and reviews ERCOT system studies and operations related to system security, Operating Guides application and emergency operations. [e18] Its primary functions are developing, reviewing and maintaining the Operating Guides, Planning Guides and other planning criteria and reviewing ERCOT reports on reliable operation. [e19] It reviews Protocol revisions for their impact on reliability and operations. [e20]
- **ERCOT context #4** (cites e24, e23, e25, e22): Voting at the subcommittees. At PRS each Segment has one Segment Vote and each Voting Entity receives an equal fraction of it; the Consumer Segment's vote is divided among three subsegments. [e24] PRS has two Standing Representatives from each Segment. [e23] A subcommittee motion passes with a majority of the aggregate fractional Segment Votes and a minimum total of three (text from the PRS section of the TAC Procedures). [e25] For RMS, ROS and WMS, each Segment has a Segment Vote of 1.0 except Consumer at 1.5. [e22]
- **ERCOT context #5** (cites e2, e64, e27, e28, e26, e29, e30, e31, e36): TAC. TAC is made up of Representatives from each Market Segment: Independent Retail Providers (and Aggregators), Independent Generators, Independent Power Marketers, Municipals, Cooperatives, Investor Owned Utilities and Consumers. [e2][e64] Under the Bylaws, four Members are elected from each of the six non-Consumer Segments. [e27] The Consumer seats are two Industrial, one Small Commercial, one Large Commercial, one Residential, plus the Public Counsel's designee as an ex officio voting member. [e28] An act of TAC requires affirmative votes of two-thirds of the Eligible Voting Representatives and at least 50% of the total Seated Representatives. [e26] The Segments are Cooperative, Independent Generator, Independent Power Marketer, Independent REP, Investor-Owned Utility, Municipal and Consumer (with Commercial, Industrial and Residential subsegments). [e29] A Small Commercial Consumer has peak demand of 1,000 kW or less and a Large Commercial Consumer more than 1,000 kW. [e30] An aggregator that fits no other classification participates as an Independent REP. [e31] Members have no voting rights in ERCOT governance other than Corporate Members' right to elect TAC Representatives. [e36]
- **ERCOT context #6** (cites e32, e33, e35, e34) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2022: The Board. Per the Bylaws (effective December 20, 2022), the Board has eleven Directors: the PUCT Chair as ex officio non-voting, the Public Counsel as ex officio voting, the CEO as ex officio non-voting and eight voting Selected Directors. [e32] Selected Directors serve staggered three-year terms for up to three consecutive terms. [e33] An act of the Board needs at least two-thirds of the affirmative votes of the Eligible Voting Directors. [e35] Directors may not vote or hold themselves out as members of TAC or its subcommittees, with an exception for the Public Counsel and OPUC representatives. [e34]
- **ERCOT context #7** (cites e8, e13, e39, e37, e38): How Board members are selected. The ERCOT board selection committee is made up of one member appointed by each of the governor, lieutenant governor and speaker, [e8] and it selects the members who serve on the governing body and designates its chair and vice chair. [e13] It must retain an outside consulting firm to help select them. [e39] PURA says the governing body is composed of two PUCT members as ex officio nonvoting members (the presiding officer and one designated to serve a one-year term), the "counsellor" as an ex officio voting member representing residential and small commercial consumer interests, the CEO as ex officio nonvoting, and eight members selected by the committee with executive-level experience in listed fields such as finance, business, engineering, trading, risk management, law or electric market design. [e37] A person does not qualify if they have a fiduciary duty or assets in the ERCOT electricity market. [e38]
