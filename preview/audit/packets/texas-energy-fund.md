## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e50, e17, e16, e2, e6, e55): The Texas Energy Fund is a special fund in the state treasury, outside general revenue, administered by the commission for the purposes authorized by Chapter 34 of the Utilities Code; the fund itself is established by Section 49-q, Article III of the Texas Constitution. [e1][e50] SB 2627, the Powering Texas Forward Act, created it, and Texans approved it at the November 7, 2023 constitutional election. [e17] The bill is described as funding mechanisms to support the construction, maintenance, modernization and operation of electric generating facilities. [e16] In ERCOT it funds low-interest loans and completion bonus grants for dispatchable generation; the PUCT runs it as four programs (In-ERCOT loans, completion bonus grants, Outside ERCOT grants and Texas Backup Power Packages). [e2][e6][e55]
- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e4, e5, e3, e49, e47, e48) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2026: Loans. Under Chapter 34 (current text, retrieved 2026-11-19), loans can finance upgrades to existing dispatchable facilities that add at least 100 MW, or construction of dispatchable facilities of at least 100 MW. [e2] A loan has a 20-year term, ratable payments starting on the third anniversary of the estimated commercial operation date, and a three percent interest rate; a construction loan may cover up to 60 percent of the estimated cost. [e4][e5] Energy storage facilities are not eligible, an electric utility other than a river authority may not borrow, and no loan or grant may go to a facility used primarily to serve an industrial load or private use network, or to a gas transmission pipeline. [e3][e49][e47] The PUCT rule allows a facility serving an industrial load or PUN only if that share is under 50 percent of new nameplate capacity and more than 100 MW serves the ERCOT market. [e48]
- **How it works #2** (cites e43, e44, e74, e45, e46, e41, e42, e47): Loan terms in the PUCT rule (16 TAC 25.510, amendment effective 10/02/24): the TEF administrator verifies that the borrower can fund the balance of no less than 40 percent of construction and commissioning costs, and the borrower posts three percent of estimated project cost in escrow with the Comptroller or as a standby letter of credit before the initial loan amount is disbursed. [e43][e44][e74] For new construction, the deposit is returned if the facility interconnects before the fourth anniversary of initial disbursement; escrow the commission may not release goes to the fund. [e45][e46] Each funded resource must keep a 12-month performance availability factor (PAF) of at least 85 percent and a planned outage factor of no more than 15 percent, checked monthly, for the whole 20-year term. [e41][e42] If more than four loan applications are pending when a loan is awarded, that loan may not exceed 25 percent of the fund balance. [e47]
- **How it works #3** (cites e6, e40, e7, e39, e38, e37, e36) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 100 MW: Completion bonus grants are paid on construction of dispatchable generation in the ERCOT region, based on megawatts provided, for facilities of at least 100 MW that were not in the CDR planning model before June 1, 2023. [e6][e40] The statutory cap is $120,000 per MW for facilities interconnected before June 1, 2026, and $80,000 per MW for those interconnected on or after June 1, 2026 and before June 1, 2029, unless the commission extends deadlines for extenuating circumstances; no grant goes to a facility interconnected on or after June 1, 2029 on the same condition. [e7][e39] The grant is paid in equal annual payments over 10 years starting on the first anniversary of commercial operations, each subject to withholding or discount for performance; the rule authorizes a payment for each of the ten successive test periods after interconnection in which the resource meets the performance standard. [e38][e37] Under the PUCT rule, an application may be filed from January 1, 2025 and no later than 180 days after the facility's interconnection date. [e36]
- **How it works #4** (cites e13, e22, e25, e24, e23, e27, e28, e35): Grant performance. The commission must set performance standards for the 100 hours with the least operating reserves each year. [e13] The PUCT rule defines those "assessed hours" as the 100 hours in the June-to-May test period with the highest peak net load (gross load minus wind, solar and storage injection). [e22][e25] ERCOT computes each resource's performance reliability factor (PRF), the average ratio of its real-time telemetered HSL to its obligated capacity, and its availability reliability factor (ARF), the share of assessed hours it was not in a planned outage. [e24][e23] The PRF is compared with a reference group of at least 30 randomly sampled non-grant thermal resources of at least 50 MW first interconnected on or after January 1, 2004; the 90th percentile sets the optimal standard and the 50th percentile the median standard. [e27][e28] ERCOT must report these values and the payment amount within 45 days after each test period. [e35]
- **Formula #1** (cites e31, e33, e34) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 25.511: Annual completion bonus grant payment per generation resource, 16 TAC 25.511(h) (the rule's equation is an image; described here from its variable list and worked example). [e31][e33][e34]
- **Formula #2** (cites e31, e26): - δ: one-tenth of the total award in the notice of eligibility. [e31][e26]
- **Formula #3** (cites e31, e28): - PRF50 and PRF90: the median and optimal performance standards from the reference group. [e31][e28]
- **Formula #4** (cites e30): - If PRF ≤ PRF50: payment is zero (withheld). [e30]
- **Formula #5** (cites e33, e31): - If PRF50 < PRF < PRF90: payment = [1 - 10(1 - ARF)²] × [1/4 + 3/4 × (PRF - PRF50)/(PRF90 - PRF50)] × δ. [e33][e31]
- **Formula #6** (cites e34, e31, e29): - If PRF ≥ PRF90: payment = [1 - 10(1 - ARF)²] × δ, which is the full δ when ARF is 1. [e34][e31] The rule text separately says no discount applies at or above PRF90 with an ARF between 0.9 and one. [e29] Read literally, the formula would still reduce the payment for an ARF between 0.9 and 1 (for example to 0.9 × δ at ARF 0.9; the knowledge base's arithmetic); the saved rule does not reconcile the two, so treat the 0.9-1 band as an open point.
- **Formula #7** (cites e30): - A result at or below zero means the payment is withheld. [e30]
- **Example #1** (cites e32, e31, e33, e79, e34, e80) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 0.25, 0.75, 0.2, 0.6: The rule's own example: a $12,000,000 award for a 100 MW resource interconnected March 1, 2026, so δ = $1,200,000. [e32][e31] Test period 1: PRF 92, ARF 1.0, PRF50 90, PRF90 98; (92 - 90)/(98 - 90) = 0.25, so payment = (0.25 + 0.75 × 0.25) × $1,200,000 = $525,000. [e33][e79] Test period 3: PRF 96 equals PRF90, but ARF is 0.80, so payment = [1 - 10 × 0.2²] × $1,200,000 = 0.6 × $1,200,000 = $720,000. [e34][e80]

[e8] Each funded facility must participate in the ERCOT wholesale market. [e10] SB 2627 required the PUCT to start accepting loan applications by June 1, 2024 and to approve or deny each and disburse initial loan funds by December 31, 2025. - **Say it like this #1** (cites e2, e4, e7, e18, e19, e38, e22, e30): (Guidance) The Texas Energy Fund offers three percent, 20-year loans for dispatchable plants of at least 100 MW, plus per-MW completion bonuses for dispatchable plants built in ERCOT, with a 10,000 MW ceiling on loans and a separate 10,000 MW ceiling on grants. [e2][e4][e7][e18][e19] The grant is paid out over ten years and only if the unit performs in the 100 tightest net-load hours each year, so it rewards availability when the grid needs it. [e38][e22][e30]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e1, e2, e3, e6, e8, e18, e19): (Guidance) TEF money is not a market payment or a capacity market: it is a state loan and grant program run by the commission from a special state fund. [e1][e2] Its in-ERCOT loans and completion bonus grants do not fund energy storage facilities. [e3][e6] The $7.2 billion and 10,000 MW figures are ceilings, not amounts awarded. [e8][e18][e19]
- **Common confusion #2** (cites e7, e26, e30, e42, e24): (Guidance) The grant's per-MW figure is a maximum award, not a guaranteed payment: each annual tenth can be discounted or withheld on performance. [e7][e26][e30] The loan PAF and the grant PRF use the same HSL-to-obligated-capacity idea but over different windows: the PAF over a rolling 12 months, the PRF over 100 assessed hours. [e42][e24]
- **Questions #2** (cites e2, e6, e55, e77, e53, e78) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 100 MW: Low-interest loans for new or upgraded dispatchable generation of at least 100 MW, and completion bonus grants for dispatchable generation built in the ERCOT region. [e2][e6] Outside ERCOT it funds existing transmission and distribution infrastructure and existing generating facilities, and the backup power package program will fund packages at critical facilities. [e55][e77][e53][e78]
- **Questions #4** (cites e4, e5, e44, e41): Twenty years, ratable payments starting at the third anniversary of the estimated commercial operation date, three percent interest, and up to 60 percent of estimated cost for construction loans. [e4][e5] The borrower posts a three percent deposit and must meet an 85 percent PAF covenant for the term. [e44][e41]
- **Questions #6** (cites e26, e29, e30, e31): Each year's payment is one-tenth of the award, paid in full if the resource's PRF is at or above the reference group's 90th percentile and its ARF is 0.9 to one, scaled down between the 50th and 90th percentiles, cut further for low availability, and withheld at or below the median. [e26][e29][e30][e31]
- **Questions #8** (cites e13, e22): The statute tells the commission to set standards for the 100 hours with the least operating reserves each year, and the rule identifies them by the highest peak net load, so the grant pays for being available when wind, solar and storage leave the most load for other resources. [e13][e22]
