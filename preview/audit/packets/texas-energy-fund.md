## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e17, e16, e2, e6): The Texas Energy Fund is a special fund in the state treasury, outside general revenue, administered by the commission for the purposes authorized by Chapter 34 of the Utilities Code. [e1] SB 2627, the Powering Texas Forward Act, created it. [e17] The bill is described as funding mechanisms to support the construction, maintenance, modernization and operation of electric generating facilities. [e16] In ERCOT it funds low-interest loans and completion bonus grants for dispatchable generation. [e2][e6]
- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e4, e5, e3) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 100 MW: Loans can finance upgrades to existing dispatchable facilities that add at least 100 MW, or construction of dispatchable facilities of at least 100 MW. [e2] A loan has a 20-year term, ratable payments starting on the third anniversary of the estimated commercial operation date, and a three percent interest rate; a construction loan may cover up to 60 percent of the estimated cost. [e4][e5] Energy storage facilities are not eligible. [e3]
- **How it works #2** (cites e6, e7, e13): Completion bonus grants are paid on construction of dispatchable generation in the ERCOT region, based on megawatts provided. The cap is $120,000 per MW for facilities interconnected before June 1, 2026, and $80,000 per MW for those interconnected on or after June 1, 2026 and before June 1, 2029, unless the commission extends deadlines for extenuating circumstances. [e6][e7] The commission must set by rule performance standards for grant recipients, based on reliability metrics, for the 100 hours with the least operating reserves each year. [e13]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e8, e17, e12, e10, e9): As enacted in SB 2627, the statute limits the fund: not more than $1 billion for grants under Section 34.0103, not more than $7.2 billion for loans and grants under Sections 34.0104 and 34.0105, and not more than $1.8 billion for grants or loans under Subchapter B. [e8] ERCOT-region figures here are from the SB 2627 text; the 89th Legislature later passed SB 2268 adjusting certain caps, requirements and deadlines, so check current values. [e17] Together, outstanding loans and bonus grants may not support more than 10,000 MW of capacity, and each funded facility must participate in the ERCOT wholesale market. [e12][e10] The PUCT was to start accepting loan applications by June 1, 2024 and to approve or deny each and disburse initial loan funds by December 31, 2025. [e9]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e15, e11, e14) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2025: ERCOT's December 2025 CDR varies the amount and timing of TEF project capacity in two of its three alternative scenarios. [e15] One scenario assumes all TEF projects are in service on their reported dates, and brownfield sites at existing plants are expected to be less likely to be delayed. [e11][e14]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e4, e5): - Loans: 20-year term, three percent interest, up to 60 percent of estimated facility cost for construction. [e4][e5]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e7): - Completion bonus grants: up to $120,000 per MW (interconnected before June 1, 2026) or $80,000 per MW (June 1, 2026 to before June 1, 2029). [e7]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e8): - Fund ceilings: $1 billion, $7.2 billion and $1.8 billion for its three purposes. [e8]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e12): - Loans plus completion bonus grants may support no more than 10,000 MW. [e12]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e3, e6, e10): - Funded facilities must be dispatchable (energy storage is excluded) and participate in the ERCOT wholesale market. [e3][e6][e10]
- **Say it like this #1** (cites e2, e4, e7, e12, e15): The Texas Energy Fund offers three percent, 20-year loans and per-MW completion bonuses to dispatchable plants of at least 100 MW, plus per-MW completion bonuses for dispatchable plants built in ERCOT, within a 10,000 MW ceiling (as enacted in SB 2627). [e2][e4][e7][e12] ERCOT's planning reports then have to decide how much of that pipeline to count on. [e15]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e1, e2, e3, e6, e8, e12): TEF money is not a market payment or a capacity market: it is a state loan and grant program run by the commission from a special state fund. [e1][e2] It does not fund storage. [e3][e6] The $7.2 billion and 10,000 MW figures are ceilings, not amounts awarded. [e8][e12]
- **Questions #2** (cites e2, e6) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 100 MW: Low-interest loans for new or upgraded dispatchable generation of at least 100 MW, and completion bonus grants for dispatchable generation built in the ERCOT region. [e2][e6]
