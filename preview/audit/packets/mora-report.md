## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e2, e3): The MORA, or Monthly Outlook for Resource Adequacy, is an ERCOT report that evaluates resource adequacy for the upcoming assessment month. It does so in two ways: a probabilistic estimate of the risk of emergency conditions on the monthly peak load day, and a deterministic scenario for typical grid conditions. [e2][e3]
- **How it works #1** (cites e1, e2, e6): ERCOT targets MORA releases for the first Friday of each month, and each MORA is released two months before its reporting month. [e1] The probabilistic side gives the chances, across a range of hours, that ERCOT may need to issue an Energy Emergency Alert or begin controlled outages. [e2] ERCOT runs its Probabilistic Reserve Risk Model, a Monte Carlo model, to generate 10,000 outcomes for Capacity Available for Operating Reserves (CAFOR). [e6]
- **How it works #2** (cites e8, e5, e7): CAFOR is the monthly maximum expected resource generation capability less demand and thermal outages. [e8] The MORA reads CAFOR against thresholds of 2,500 MW (EEA risk) and 1,500 MW (controlled outage risk), which are proxies for the 2,500 and 1,500 MW Physical Responsive Capability thresholds. [e5] For the deterministic scenario, the report states its monthly capacity reserve margin and gives the formula ((Total Resources / (Peak Demand - Emergency Resources)) - 1) * 100. [e7]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e4, e10) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2027: In the January 2027 MORA, the hour with the highest EEA risk is HE 8:00 a.m. CST, at a 3.20% probability that ERCOT would need to declare an EEA. [e4] That report also incorporates a new battery energy storage system sub-model integrated into the PRRM. [e10] For the multi-year seasonal view, see the CDR topic.
- **Key facts #1** (cites e1): - Release is targeted for the first Friday of each month, for the reporting month two months ahead. [e1]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e2, e3): - Two approaches: probabilistic EEA and controlled-outage risk, plus a deterministic typical-conditions scenario. [e2][e3]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e5): - CAFOR thresholds: 2,500 MW for EEA risk and 1,500 MW for controlled outage risk. [e5]
