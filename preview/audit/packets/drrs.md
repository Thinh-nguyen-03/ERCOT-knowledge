## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e7, e11, e12, e2, e54) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2026: DRRS is a new Ancillary Service, set up under NPRR1309 (approved by the PUCT on July 9, 2026 but not yet implemented), that provides operating reserves to manage uncertainty on the ERCOT System while mitigating the need for Reliability Unit Commitment (RUC) instructions. [e1][e7][e11][e12] It exists because state law requires ERCOT to procure dispatchable reliability reserve services on a day-ahead and real-time basis to account for market uncertainty. [e2] ERCOT describes it as a tool the control room can use when net demand runs higher than forecast or wind and solar fall short, especially in the evening. [e54]
- **How it works #1** (cites e17, e3, e18): The statute sets the minimum design. ERCOT must size the service from historical variations in generation availability for each season, based on a targeted reliability standard or goal, including renewable intermittency and forced outage rates of dispatchable units. [e17] A participating resource must be able to run for at least four hours at its high sustained limit, be online and dispatchable within two hours of being called, and have the flexibility to handle inter-hour operational problems. ERCOT must also reduce reliability unit commitment by the amount of DRRS it procures. [e3] ERCOT may require more than four hours if it decides that is needed. [e18]
- **How it works #2** (cites e4, e19, e7, e26, e27): Under the approved NPRR1309 language, DRRS comes from both Off-Line and On-Line Generation Resources that can ramp to a set output within two hours and hold it for at least four consecutive hours. [e4] It is offered, awarded and paid in both the DAM and the RTM. [e19] Compared with the withdrawn NPRR1235, which allowed only Off-Line Resources, NPRR1309 lets DRRS be awarded in Real-Time, so its procurement can be co-optimized with energy and the other Ancillary Services. [e7] DRRS may only come from capability that is not fulfilling any other energy or capacity commitment. [e26] An Off-Line Resource qualifies through an eight-hour test window agreed with ERCOT. [e27]
- **How it works #3** (cites e23, e20, e25, e24): Deployment runs through RUC. Off-Line Resources carrying DRRS show a new "DRRS" Resource Status in the COP. [e23] RUC prioritizes committing them because they appear to have lower start-up and minimum-energy costs than other Off-Line units in the optimization. [e20] A RUC commitment of a Resource holding a DRRS award is treated as a DRRS deployment for those hours. [e25] ERCOT deploys Off-Line DRRS by operator Dispatch Instruction, always at 100% of the Resource's DRRS capability; the unit must reach its award within two hours and must have an Energy Offer Curve in SCED. [e24]
- **How it works #4** (cites e16, e5, e4, e41, e13, e42): NPRR1309 also changes Non-Spin: its revision description says that, given DRRS has at least a four-hour duration, the Non-Spin duration requirements are reduced from four hours to two hours. [e16] The IMM describes this as taking effect upon DRRS implementation, and says DRRS is statutorily required to carry a four-hour duration requirement. [e5] Storage participation is not in NPRR1309; after PUCT guidance on March 26, 2026 to handle it separately, ERCOT filed NPRR1340 and NOGRR288 in June 2026, and said that if approved in 2026 it could be implemented with NPRR1309 in 2028.
