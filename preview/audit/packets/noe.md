## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **How it works #2** (cites e19, e20, e21): What a NOIE may and may not do under PURA. A muni that has not chosen customer choice may not offer energy at unregulated prices directly to retail customers outside its certificated retail service area [e19], but keeps the right to offer a full range of customer service and pricing programs inside its area and to buy and sell at wholesale without geographic restriction. [e20] A co-op that chooses not to participate is under the same retail restriction. [e21]
- **How it works #3** (cites e17, e18, e6, e7, e46, e47): Opting in. A muni may choose customer choice at any time on or after January 1, 2002 by resolution of its governing body, and the decision is irrevocable. [e17] A co-op may do so by board resolution, and the resolution may be revoked only if no customer has opted for choice within four years of its adoption. [e18] Each MOU and EC must register with ERCOT and sign the agreements for the functions it performs, whether it plans to be a NOIE or a Competitive Retailer. [e6] If it registers an affiliate as a CR, it must tell ERCOT six months before opting in. [e7] On initiating customer choice a muni may designate itself or others as provider of last resort [e46] and a co-op shall designate itself or another entity. [e47]
- **How it works #4** (cites e11, e4, e15): Registration. Each MOU and EC assigns an ESI ID to each NOIE wholesale point of delivery, and those ESI IDs must be assigned to an LSE. [e11] NOIEs that serve Load are LSEs, so they register with ERCOT as LSEs [e4], and each LSE applicant must designate a QSE. [e15]
- **How it works #5** (cites e45, e22, e23) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2025: Retail market differences once an MOU/EC opts in. The Retail Market Guide (December 1, 2025) says billing in an MOU/EC TDSP territory may be consolidated or separate, versus consolidated billing by the CR in an IOU territory. [e45] In an MOU/EC territory the specific MOU/EC utility service rules apply instead of the PUCT customer protection rules, which in many cases differ, for example on the bill due date. [e22] The same section names Nueces Electric Cooperative (NEC) as an EC and Lubbock Power & Light (LP&L) as an MOU in its tariff references. [e23]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e8, e25, e26, e28, e29, e30, e27): NOIE Load Zones. A NOIE or group of NOIEs can establish a NOIE Load Zone through a one-time election. [e8] Four NOIE Load Zones were approved before the Texas Nodal Market Implementation Date: Austin Energy, City Public Service, Rayburn Country Electric Cooperative and LCRA. [e25] A separate NOIE Load Zone is made up of a group of NOIEs that are parties to the same pre-1999 power supply arrangements and had an overall 2003 peak Load above 2,300 MW. [e26] NOIEs may participate in only one NOIE Load Zone, and all Load a NOIE serves must be in it. [e28] Changes to Load Zones need ERCOT Board approval and take effect 48 months after the end of the month of approval, with exceptions. [e29] Costs allocated by zonal Load Ratio Share use the 2003 "Cost-Allocation Load Zones" rather than the NOIE zones. [e30] When a NOIE offers retail choice, four years later its Load must be merged into the appropriate Competitive Load Zone(s). [e27]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e31, e32, e34, e33, e42, e43, e41, e36, e44, e37): PCRRs. ERCOT allocates a portion of available CRRs as Pre-Assigned CRRs to NOIEs that either have established ownership before September 1, 1999 in a specific Generation Resource or have a long-term contractual commitment (greater than five years) from specific Generation Resources entered into before September 1, 1999. [e31] NOIEs are the only Entities eligible, and may designate an agent to manage them. [e32] PCRRs are limited to pre-September 1, 1999 Generation Resources used by the NOIE to serve Load in its service territory. [e34] An existing Generation Resource that interconnected on or after June 1, 2021 under the Section 1.6.5(3) route cannot qualify as a pre-September 1, 1999 resource. [e33] ERCOT publishes the list of eligible NOIEs on its website. [e42] The NOIE nominates PCRRs for each month of the PCRR Nomination Year before the first CRR Long-Term Auction Sequence of the calendar year. [e43] The NOIE specifies the CRR type (PTP Option, PTP Obligation or a combination) before allocation, and it is binding. [e41] For eligible non-solid-fuel and non-combined-cycle Resources the NOIE must choose either the refund option or the capacity option before allocation, the same for every month of the allocation year. [e36] Under the refund option the NOIE refunds to ERCOT congestion revenues above what flows to it for its Output Schedule at the PCRR source [e44]; under the capacity option it may nominate up to 100% of the lesser of net unit capacity or contractual amount at a capacity factor no greater than 40% over each calendar year. [e37]
- **ERCOT context #3** (cites e40, e38, e39): PCRR pricing: after allocation and the applicable CRR Auction, PCRRs (except refund-option PCRRs for non-solid-fuel, non-combined-cycle Resources, which are allocated at no charge [e40]) are priced as a percentage of the CRR Auction clearing price. For PTP Option PCRRs on nuclear, coal, lignite or combined-cycle Resources the price is 10% of the clearing price. [e38] For PTP Obligation PCRRs on those Resources it is 5% of the clearing price if positive and 100% if negative. [e39] The Protocol text lists other percentages for gas steam and other Resource types. [e38]
- **ERCOT context #4** (cites e35): A NOIE stops being eligible for PCRRs after it opts into competition, except South Texas Electric Cooperative Inc. (STEC), which may be eligible for up to three years after entering competition. [e35]
- **ERCOT context #5** (cites e13, e9, e10, e24): Other features. A QSE offering services in a NOIE service territory must get written authorization from the NOIE and submit it to ERCOT. [e13] There is a CRR type, PTP Obligation with Links to an Option, defined around a NOIE's PTP Obligation and PTP Option. [e9] The IMM notes that ERCOT allocates Pre-Assigned CRRs to NOIEs based on generation ownership or arrangements that predate retail competition, at a fraction of the auction value of comparable CRRs. [e10] The Retail Market Guide says allocation of ESI IDs in a Mass Transition may follow different processes depending on the MOU/EC's retail processing specifications. [e24]

- **Key facts #6** (cites e25, e27): - Four NOIE Load Zones pre-date the nodal market; a NOIE's Load is merged into a Competitive Load Zone four years after it offers retail choice. [e25][e27]
- **Key facts #7** (cites e31, e32): - PCRRs are allocated only to NOIEs, based on pre-September 1, 1999 ownership or contracts. [e31][e32]
- **Key facts #8** (cites e38): - PTP Option PCRR price for nuclear, coal, lignite or combined-cycle: 10% of the CRR Auction clearing price. [e38]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e6, e1, e3, e16, e8, e29): (Guidance) "NOIE" and "opt-in" are not about whether the entity is in ERCOT; both opt-in and non-opt-in munis and co-ops can be inside the ERCOT Region and register with ERCOT. [e6] The difference is Customer Choice. [e1][e3] Also, the PUCT TDU definition excludes MOUs and ECs, so do not call a muni a TDU in PUCT terms. [e16] And a NOIE Load Zone is a settlement-price zone, not a separate market: the Protocols treat NOIE Load Zones like other Load Zones except as specified. [e8][e29]

[e7] Four years after it offers choice, its Load is merged into the Competitive Load Zone(s). [e27]
