- **Definition #1** (cites e29, e3): This scenario asks whether a gas combined-cycle unit earns more from selling power than it pays for fuel and running costs at current prices. EIA's spark spread (power price minus gas price times heat rate) is the unit-level test [e29], and the IMM's implied heat rate (power price divided by fuel price) is the market-level measure: a low value indicates a market where "only the most efficient generators are in the money". [e3]
- **How it works #1** (cites e8, e1, e32, e30, e2, e28) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2024: Fuel is the main cost. Fuel is the largest part of marginal cost for most generators, and gas is ERCOT's most-used fuel. [e8] The heat rate is the energy a plant uses to make one kWh, stated in Btu per net kWh. [e1] Efficiency in percent is 3,412 Btu divided by the heat rate. [e32] EIA's benchmark is 7,000 Btu/kWh, which it describes as a fairly new and efficient combined cycle, while those marketing a unit use its tested heat rate. [e30] In its 2024 report's net revenue work the IMM assumed 7 MMBtu/MWh for a combined cycle, 10.5 MMBtu/MWh for a gas turbine and $4/MWh of variable O&M. [e2] It also assumed a 10% total outage rate and left out start-up and minimum-energy costs and ramping limits. [e28]
- **How it works #2** (cites e9, e17, e18, e19, e10, e4, e33): ERCOT itself builds a cost reference the same way. [e9][e17][e18][e19] That O&M term covers variable costs above LSL and includes an emissions cost adjustment. [e10] The Fuel Index Price is the daily Katy Hub gas price. Under the Verifiable Cost Manual, variable O&M for the MOC is limited to incremental O&M above LSL, submitted as one average $/MWh across the heat rate curve, and emission costs may be included as an additional component. [e20] Emission costs at or above LSL are calculated in $/MWh, and NOx and SO2 costs may be included.
