## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e3): This scenario asks whether a gas combined-cycle unit earns more from selling power than it pays for fuel and running costs at current prices, and the IMM's implied heat rate (power price divided by fuel price) is the market-level measure: a low value indicates a market where "only the most efficient generators are in the money". [e3]
- **How it works #1** (cites e8, e1, e2): Fuel is the main cost. Fuel is the largest part of marginal cost for most generators, and gas is ERCOT's most-used fuel. [e8] The heat rate is the energy a plant uses to make one kWh, stated in Btu per net kWh. [e1] In its net revenue work the IMM has assumed 7 MMBtu/MWh for a combined cycle, 10.5 MMBtu/MWh for a gas turbine and $4/MWh of variable O&M. [e2]
- **How it works #2** (cites e9, e17, e18, e19, e10, e4): ERCOT itself builds a cost reference the same way. The mitigated offer cap takes the larger of a generic incremental heat rate times the higher of the fuel index and a resource fuel price, or the unit's verifiable incremental heat rate times its calculated fuel price plus O&M. [e9][e17][e18][e19] That O&M term covers variable costs above LSL and includes an emissions cost adjustment. [e10] The Fuel Index Price is the daily Katy Hub gas price. [e4]
- **How it works #3** (cites e3, e15): Then compare with the market. The implied heat rate divides the power price by the fuel price. ## Uncited guidance text (check for new facts, numbers, dates, names)

- (Scenarios) - Suppose (illustrative): at $30/MWh off-peak the margin is about $5/MWh; at $55/MWh in the evening it is about $30/MWh.
- (Scenarios) - (Reasoning) The thin off-peak margin can disappear once start-up and minimum generation costs are counted, so ask whether the unit must run through the night to be online for the evening peak.
- (Say it like this) (Guidance) Multiply the heat rate by the gas price, add variable O&M, and compare that with the power price for the hours the unit would run. If power divided by gas is above the plant's heat rate, it covers fuel, but start-up and minimum run costs decide whether a thin margin is really worth running for.
