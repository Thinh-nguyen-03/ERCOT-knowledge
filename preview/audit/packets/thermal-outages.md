## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2, e3, e4): Under the Protocols, a Forced Outage of a Generation Resource or ESR is an outage that requires immediate removal of all or part of its capacity from service, whether controlled or uncontrolled. [e1] A Planned Outage is scheduled in advance with ERCOT, other than a Maintenance Outage or Opportunity Outage, and a Maintenance Outage is work that could be postponed briefly but is required to prevent a potential Forced Outage. [e2][e3] A Forced Derate is the unavailability of a portion of a Resource's capacity relative to its Seasonal net max sustainable rating. [e4]
- **How it works #1** (cites e5, e6, e7): Resource Entities give ERCOT a Planned and Maintenance Outage plan for at least the next 12 months, updated monthly. [e5] ERCOT approves or rejects outage plans submitted more than 45 days ahead, and approves a plan if it would not push aggregate MW of Resource outages above the Resource Planned Outage Limit (RPOL) at any point. [e6][e7]
- **How it works #2** (cites e8, e21, e9): Forced derates have reporting thresholds: a derate over ten MW and 5% of the Seasonal net maximum sustainable rating that lasts longer than 30 minutes must go into the Outage Scheduler within 60 minutes, except that a derate caused by ambient temperature or humidity has eight hours. [e8][e21] The IMM describes derated capacity as the difference between registered maximum capacity and actual capability. [e9]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e20, e10, e11): The IMM says thermal outages and deratings are especially important because they can affect reliability when other resources are limited. [e20] It reports that planned and unapproved outages were lowest in summer and highest in spring and fall, while the most noteworthy change from 2024 to 2025 was a higher rate of forced outages and derates, particularly in summer. [e10] The IMM also says planned outages are only part of the total, many outages are forced, and many go unreported, which makes system planning harder. [e11]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e13, e15, e16, e14) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2023, 2027, 2021: ERCOT's reviews show the same: summer thermal forced outages as a share of installed capacity have been higher in 2025-2026 than in 2019-2020, and in summer 2023 forced thermal outages stayed in a 4-8 GW range except on low net load days when generators did repairs.
