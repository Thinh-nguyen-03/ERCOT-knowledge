## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2, e3, e4, e51): Under the Protocols, a Forced Outage of a Generation Resource or ESR is an outage that requires immediate removal of all or part of its capacity from service, whether controlled or uncontrolled. [e1] A Planned Outage is scheduled in advance with ERCOT, other than a Maintenance Outage or Opportunity Outage, and a Maintenance Outage is work that could be postponed briefly but is required to prevent a potential Forced Outage. [e2][e3] A Forced Derate is the unavailability of a portion of a Resource's capacity relative to its Seasonal net max sustainable rating. [e4] A Startup Loading Failure is a type of Forced Outage in which a Generation Resource cannot operate at LSL at the time scheduled in the COP while ramping up. [e51]
- **How it works #1** (cites e5, e26, e6, e7, e30, e27, e28, e29, e34, e35): Planning and approval. Resource Entities give ERCOT a Planned and Maintenance Outage plan for at least the next 12 months, updated monthly, and should request a Planned Outage no more than 60 months in advance. [e5][e26] ERCOT approves or rejects outage plans submitted more than 45 days ahead, and approves a plan if it would not push aggregate MW of Resource outages above the Resource Planned Outage Limit (RPOL) at any point. [e6][e7] A Resource Entity may not begin a Planned Outage without approval. [e30] For plans filed inside 45 days, the Resource Entity must say immediately whether the outage is Maintenance (Level I, II or III) or Planned; ERCOT accepts the Maintenance plans and coordinates them within the Protocol time frames, but approves Planned plans only if the RPOL and reliability standards allow. [e27][e28][e29] ERCOT's response time depends on lead time: for example, requests between 16 and 45 days ahead (maximum duration 180 days) are answered within five Business Days. [e34] ERCOT may approve longer Planned Outage durations in its sole discretion, and the maximum duration does not apply to Outages under an NSO. [e35]
- **How it works #2** (cites e22, e72) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 3.1, 5.12: Maintenance levels. The definitions classify Maintenance Outages by how soon equipment must come out of service to prevent a Forced Outage: Level I within 24 hours, Level II within seven days, Level III within 30 days. [e22] (Section 3.1.5.12 sets a separate minimum-lead-time table for Transmission Facility outage requests, in which Level II Maintenance Outage is "Two days"; it is not the Resource maintenance definition.) [e72]
- **How it works #3** (cites e31, e32, e33): Exceptions to RPOL approval. ERCOT must approve a requested outage plan for a nuclear Generation Resource, and must approve a plan for a Generation Resource that is part of an industrial generation facility, if the plan says so and the outage is necessitated by the operational needs of the industrial Load it serves, unless ERCOT determines the outage will impair transmission security. [e31][e32] ERCOT may, in its sole discretion, approve a plan that would exceed the RPOL if, among other listed cases, the Resource cannot operate because fuel supply is unavailable, such as gas pipeline outages. [e33]
- **How it works #4** (cites e36, e37, e38): The RPOL. ERCOT calculates a maximum capacity of Resource Planned Outages for each day of the next 60 months, excluding nuclear outages and exempt QF outages. [e36] It posts the RPOL and aggregate approved Planned Outage MW at least twice a day for the next 60 months and hourly for the next seven days. [e37] ERCOT may adjust the RPOL if actual aggregate Forced and Maintenance Outages exceed the amount used in setting it. [e38]
- **How it works #5** (cites e39, e40, e8, e21, e52, e18, e41, e71, e42, e43, e9): Forced outages and derates. After a Resource Forced Outage the QSE or Resource Entity must change the telemetered status to Off-Line within 15 minutes, update the COP within 60 minutes, and update the Outage Scheduler if necessary. [e39] A Resource Forced Outage in Real-Time must be entered into the Outage Scheduler within 60 minutes of its start. [e40] Forced derates have reporting thresholds: a derate over ten MW and 5% of the Seasonal net maximum sustainable rating that lasts longer than 30 minutes must go into the Outage Scheduler within 60 minutes, except that a derate caused by ambient temperature or humidity has eight hours; a change to a reported derate of the same size is entered as a new derate. [e8][e21][e52] The HSL telemetry must be updated within 15 minutes for such a derate, or the QSE may instead use the ONHOLD process. [e18][e41] A QSE is not required to update telemetered HSL and COP on time where, in its reasonable judgment, doing so would create an undue threat to safety, undue risk of bodily harm or undue damage to equipment. [e71] ONHOLD may be set within 15 minutes of an event on an On-Line Resource and the status must be updated within 60 consecutive minutes of ONHOLD. [e42][e43] The IMM describes derated capacity as the difference between registered maximum capacity and actual capability. [e9]
- **How it works #6** (cites e24, e23, e68): Opportunity outages and early returns. An Opportunity Outage is a special category of Planned Outage that may be approved when a Resource has been forced Off-Line and already has an approved Planned Outage in the next two days. [e24][e23] A Resource that completes a Planned Outage early must notify ERCOT at least two hours before beginning startup, and within two hours ERCOT either approves the return, unless it cannot maintain reliability or security with the Resource's injection, in which case it issues a Verbal Dispatch Instruction to stay Off-Line, or coordinates between the TSP and the Resource Entity a time for the Resource to be Off-Line where a Transmission Facilities Outage requires it. [e68]
- **How it works #7** (cites e69, e48, e46, e47): When ERCOT may move planned outages. If ERCOT believes it cannot meet reliability standards after exercising other reasonable options, it conducts a preliminary Outage Adjustment Evaluation and issues an Advance Action Notice [e69]; it can then issue an Outage Schedule Adjustment, which delays or cancels and reschedules an already approved Planned Outage. [e48] ERCOT issues an Advance Action Notice at least 24 hours before any OSA, and may not consider nuclear-powered Generation Resources for an OSA. [e46][e47]
- **How it works #8** (cites e44, e45): Public data. Three days after each Operating Day ERCOT posts a Resource Forced Outage report listing each Forced Outage, Maintenance Outage or Forced Derate that occurred in or extended into the day, with at least the Resource name, unit code, fuel type and outage type.
