## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **Definition #1** (cites e3, e1, e18): Summer 2026 was the summer in which ERCOT set a new all-time demand record of 91,134 MW on July 22, 2026, while, ERCOT says, solar growth outpacing large load additions helped make the daytime hours uneventful. [e3][e1] ERCOT's Board review (September 14-15, 2026) says energy and ancillary service costs were close to or slightly below summers 2024 and 2025. [e1][e18]
- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e21, e3, e32, e37): The weather was hot: June-July 2026 ranked 11th hottest of 132 years on mean temperature, driven especially by minimum temperatures, which ranked 3rd warmest all-time. [e2][e21] On July 22, crypto mining loads cut up to about 3,299 MW and 4CP response about 492 MW; ERCOT estimates the peak load would have been 94,861 MW if there was no reduction. [e3] August's peak demand was 90,817 MW at hour ending 18 on August 24, 2026 (7,110 MW above the August 2025 peak), and September set a monthly record of 86,647 MW on September 12, 2026 (both preliminary values in ERCOT's monthly overviews). [e32][e37]
- **How it works #2** (cites e5, e1, e22, e6, e58, e20, e24): Risk moved later into the evening. ERCOT says the net load hour shifted from HE 21 to HE 22 in June and July, and that batteries helped serve record net loads but ran their state of charge down in the evenings as a regular pattern. [e5][e1] Twelve of the top 20 hourly net load hours have been from 2026. [e22] On the three lowest-SOC days of 2026 (data period June 1 to August 15), SOC percentages averaged 50% lower than on the lowest days of 2025; ERCOT adds that because ESR MWh capacity is higher, the lower SOC percentage is not necessarily lower SOC in MWh. [e6][e58][e20] Across the summer (the slide states no data period), SOC was generally highest between hours 12 and 20, and SOC was under 20% in 15% of hours and under 10% in 0.74% of hours. [e24]
- **How it works #3** (cites e11, e25, e26, e31): Ancillary services were nearly always covered. There were no shortages of Reg-Up, Reg-Down or RRS, and Non-Spin and ECRS met plan in nearly all intervals; most Non-Spin shortfalls came in June during late-evening peak AS Plan requirements, with 40% of intervals in HE 22-23 affected. [e11][e25] ERCOT procures more Non-Spin than the AS Plan calls for in times of abundance when it is cost-effective: Non-Spin above plan grew from 25 GWh/day in June to roughly 48 GWh/day in July and August, offsetting a fall in the plan from 86 to 66 GWh/day. [e26] Batteries dominated ancillary service awards, especially regulation, in both the DAM and the real-time market. [e31]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e7, e29, e10, e8): Market outcomes were soft. Real-time prices were lower than in 2024 and 2025 because of more supply in daylight hours, which also lowered peaker net margin. [e7] Day-ahead ancillary service prices trended lower than in 2024 and 2025 except for Non-Spin, and real-time ancillary service prices fell month over month through the summer. [e29] Real-time congestion rent was 10% lower than summer 2025, and ERCOT credits RTC for part of that and for lower ancillary service costs. [e10][e8]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e12, e51, e48, e54, e27, e28): RTC+B changed how scarcity shows up in prices. ERCOT notes the real-time SWCAP was reduced to $2,000/MWh from $5,000/MWh after RTC go-live on December 5, 2025, and the IMM says there is no longer an explicit shortage price adder: the energy price natively includes any shortage value from the ASDCs through co-optimization. [e12][e51] The IMM told TAC on August 13, 2026 that shortage pricing via the ASDCs has been minimal since RTC began in December 2025. [e48][e54] ERCOT reports that the share of ESR capacity offered within $100/MWh of the SWCAP (20:00 SCED snapshot) was slightly higher in summer 2026 than the previous two summers, largely because of the lower $2,000/MWh real-time cap. [e27][e28]
- **ERCOT context #3** (cites e9, e14, e13, e23, e19): Two things went the other way. RUC rose to 1,313 effective resource-hours, from 872 in 2025 and 148 in 2024, and only 17% was nominally attributed to congestion versus 100% in 2025. [e9][e14] Thermal forced outages as a share of installed capacity were higher in summers 2025-2026 than in 2019-2020, although ERCOT says summer 2026 generally had lower forced outages than summer 2025. [e13][e23] The review's RUC and congestion rent charts count August only through August 15 in each year, so these are not full-summer totals. [e19]
- **ERCOT context #4** (cites e34, e35, e36, e46, e47): RUC by full month comes from ERCOT's monthly overviews: 422.4 effective resource-hours in July (89% for capacity), 782.1 in August (96% for capacity), and 328.1 in September (79.9% for capacity).
