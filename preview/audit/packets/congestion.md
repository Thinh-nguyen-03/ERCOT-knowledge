## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e25, e4, e6, e5): Congestion shows up in prices: differences in the cost of delivering electricity to different locations are reflected in different energy prices at each node. [e2] The IMM adds that in ERCOT LMPs are calculated at individual generation and load nodes but load is settled at the load-weighted average price of its load zone, so some congestion costs are averaged across customers. [e25] A constraint's marginal economic cost is reflected in its shadow price, which is defined as the rate at which system costs would fall or rise with a slight change in the amount of the commodity made available. [e4][e6] The IMM describes day-ahead congestion values as being calculated as power flow over each constraint times that constraint's shadow price. [e6] How much a given injection loads a constraint is its shift factor: the flow on a transmission element from a unit injection at a bus to a fixed reference bus. [e5]
- **How it works #2** (cites e7, e22, e8): SCED caps the price of congestion: the cost it calculates to resolve an additional MW on a network constraint is limited to that constraint's maximum shadow price. [e7] For 2025 the IMM lists caps of $5,251 per MW for base-case or voltage constraints, $4,500 for 345 kV, $3,500 for 138 kV and $2,800 for 69 kV thermal violations, with GTCs treated as base-case stability constraints at the $5,251 cap. [e22] Some constraints are generic transmission constraints (GTCs), which group transmission elements to limit flow between geographic areas to manage stability, voltage and other limits that cannot be modeled directly. [e8]
- **How it works #3** (cites e3, e14, e16, e30, e29): Congestion rent is the difference between what consumers pay and what generators receive, based on locational price differences. It accrues to CRR holders. [e3] A CRR entitles its owner to be charged or paid congestion rent that arises when the grid is congested in the DAM or in real time. [e14] In 2025 the IMM reports day-ahead congestion rent of $2,477 million against CRR payments of $2,185 million, and a day-ahead surplus of about $293 million. [e16][e30][e29]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e18, e19, e20, e12, e15, e9, e10): **Trend.** The IMM reports real-time congestion cost of $2.1 billion in 2021 (almost one third of it during Winter Storm Uri), $2.8 billion in 2022, $2.4 billion in 2023 and $1.9 billion in 2024.
