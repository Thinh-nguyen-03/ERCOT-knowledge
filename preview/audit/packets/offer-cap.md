## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **How it works #2** (cites e21, e6, e24, e47): In the protocols the peaker net margin is calculated in dollars per MW on a cumulative basis over all past intervals in the year (see Formula). [e21] The protocols set the PNM threshold at three times the cost of new entry of new generation plants. [e6] When the PNM exceeds the threshold, the DASWCAP and the VOLL used for the DAM and RTM ancillary service demand curves are both changed to the LCAP for the rest of the calendar year. [e24] The switch is not immediate: the HCAP stays in effect for the rest of the day the threshold is crossed and the next Operating Day (Days 1 and 2); on Day 2, before the DAM runs, ERCOT sends a Market Notice that the LCAP takes effect on Day 3. [e47]
- **How it works #3** (cites e7, e23, e8, e48): A second, separate mechanism is the Emergency Pricing Program: the PUCT rule says it must be activated if the system-wide energy price has been at the HCAP for 12 hours within a rolling 24-hour period. [e7] The Protocols state the test as the sum of the RTM System Lambda and the Real-Time Reliability Deployment Price Adder for Energy being at or above the HCAP for DAM for 12 hours within a rolling 24 hours, after which the DASWCAP and the VOLL used for the ancillary service demand curves are set to the ECAP. [e23] Under the PUCT rule, while the program is active the cap is set to the ECAP, which equals the LCAP value. [e8] The ECAP period lasts until the later of 24 hours after the switch to ECAP or 24 hours after ERCOT exits EEA conditions if it was in EEA while the EPP was active, extended again if ERCOT re-enters EEA within 24 hours. [e48]
- **How it works #4** (cites e11, e10, e20, e39): When the cap is at the LCAP, ERCOT must reimburse resource entities for actual marginal costs above the larger of the LCAP or their real-time energy price. [e11] In the protocols, an energy offer curve must fall between -$250 per MWh and the day-ahead or real-time cap, depending on when it was submitted; an energy offer curve received after 1430 in the Day-Ahead cannot exceed the RTSWCAP. [e10] Resource-specific ancillary service offers follow the same split: those received before 1000 in the Day-Ahead are capped at the DASWCAP and those received after 1430 at the RTSWCAP. [e20] Commission Staff's review question, as quoted in ERCOT's filing, describes the day-ahead/real-time difference as applying to both energy and AS. [e39]
- **Formula #1** (cites e21, e5, e6, e16, e54, e55) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 4.4, 11.1: Peaker net margin (np-04 §4.4.11.1(1)(d)): PNM = ∑((RTEP – POC) × 0.25) for each Settlement Interval where (RTEP – POC) > 0, accumulated in dollars per MW over all past intervals in the current year. [e21] RTEP is the real-time energy price, measured as the ERCOT Hub Average 345 kV Hub price; POC is the Peaking Operating Cost, ten times the effective daily FIP (fuel index price), in dollars per MWh; [e5] (The knowledge base's own reading, not a source statement: comparing this with the PUCT rule's "number of minutes in a settlement interval / 60 minutes per hour" term, 0.25 corresponds to a 15-minute settlement interval.) The cap switches when PNM exceeds the threshold of three times CONE, listed as $315,000 per MW-year. [e6][e16] The PUCT rule writes the same sum with "number of minutes in a settlement interval / 60 minutes per hour" in place of 0.25 and a POC of 10 times the natural gas price index. [e54][e55]
