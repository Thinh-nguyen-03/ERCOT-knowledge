[e10][e20] PUCT rule 25.509 states a high cap (HCAP) of $5,000 per MWh and a low cap (LCAP) of $2,000 per MWh, with the same dollar values per MW per hour for ancillary service offers. [e1][e2] The Protocols apply the high cap as two values: a Day-Ahead System-Wide Offer Cap (DASWCAP) and a Real-Time System-Wide Offer Cap (RTSWCAP), each set equal to the "respective" HCAP. [e15] The Protocol parameter table lists the day-ahead HCAP (DASWCAP) at $5,000 per MWh and the real-time HCAP (RTSWCAP) at $2,000 per MWh. [e16]
- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e4, e22): Under the PUCT rule, the cap starts each calendar year at the HCAP and stays there until the peaker net margin for that year exceeds a threshold of three times the cost of new entry of new generation plants. [e3] If the threshold is crossed, the cap drops to the LCAP for the rest of that calendar year, and energy prices (excluding congestion) cannot exceed the LCAP plus $1. [e4] The rule text also says ERCOT keeps applying the operating reserve demand curve and the reliability deployment price adder for the rest of that year [e4]; since RTC went live the ORDC adder has been replaced by ancillary service demand curves, while the reliability deployment price adder remains. [e22]
- **How it works #2** (cites e21, e5, e6, e24): In the protocols the peaker net margin is calculated in dollars per MW on a cumulative basis over all past intervals in the year, summing (RTEP - POC) times 0.25 for each Settlement Interval where that difference is positive. [e21] The Peaking Operating Cost (POC) is ten times the effective daily FIP, and the real-time energy price (RTEP) used in the test is the ERCOT Hub Average 345 kV Hub price. [e5] The protocols set the PNM threshold at three times the cost of new entry of new generation plants. [e6] The current Protocols write the switch for the day-ahead cap: when the PNM exceeds the threshold, the DASWCAP and the VOLL used for the DAM and RTM ancillary service demand curves are both changed to the LCAP for the rest of the calendar year. [e24]
- **How it works #3** (cites e7, e23, e8): A second, separate mechanism is the Emergency Pricing Program: the PUCT rule says it must be activated if the system-wide energy price has been at the HCAP for 12 hours within a rolling 24-hour period. [e7] The Protocols state the test as the sum of the RTM System Lambda and the Real-Time Reliability Deployment Price Adder for Energy being at or above the HCAP for DAM for 12 hours within a rolling 24 hours, after which the DASWCAP and the VOLL used for the ancillary service demand curves are set to the ECAP. [e23] Under the PUCT rule, while the program is active the cap is set to the ECAP, which equals the LCAP value. [e8]
- **How it works #4** (cites e11, e10, e20): When the cap is at the LCAP, ERCOT must reimburse resource entities for actual marginal costs above the larger of the LCAP or their real-time energy price. [e11] In the protocols, an energy offer curve must fall between -$250 per MWh and the day-ahead or real-time cap, depending on when it was submitted; an energy offer curve received after 1430 in the Day-Ahead cannot exceed the RTSWCAP. [e10] Resource-specific ancillary service offers follow the same split: those received before 1000 in the Day-Ahead are capped at the DASWCAP and those received after 1430 at the RTSWCAP. [e20]
- **How it works #5** (cites e15, e16, e17) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 4.4: The Protocols say both caps are determined under PUCT rules, and they set the VOLL used for the DAM and RTM ancillary service demand curves to the HCAP for DAM.
