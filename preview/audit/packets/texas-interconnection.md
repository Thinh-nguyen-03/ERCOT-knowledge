## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e13, e2, e3): The ERCOT System (the FERC-NERC report calls it the "ERCOT Interconnection"; this topic uses the label Texas Interconnection) is the interconnected power system under PUCT jurisdiction that is not synchronously connected to the Eastern Interconnection or the Western (WECC) grid. [e1][e13] A DC tie is a non-synchronous transmission interconnection between ERCOT and a non-ERCOT system, and the FERC-NERC report describes four asynchronous ties. [e2][e3]
- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e4, e5) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2021: The 2021 FERC-NERC report describes ERCOT as a functionally separate interconnection with four asynchronous ties. Two DC ties, the North Tie and the East Tie, connect it to the Eastern Interconnection through SPP. [e3] Two more, the Laredo Variable Frequency Tie and the Railroad Tie, connect it to Mexico. [e4] Total DC tie capability is about 1,220 MW: 820 MW to SPP and 400 MW to Mexico. [e5]
- **How it works #2** (cites e6, e12) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2021: Power can flow either way across the ties. Exports add to ERCOT demand and imports add to supply. [e6] The 2021 FERC-NERC report says ERCOT does not classify DC tie flows as firm capacity because they are scheduled as day-ahead energy transactions, although its seasonal assessment (SARA) did assume an expected amount of net imports based on winter 2013/2014 EEA intervals. [e12]
- **How it works #3** (cites e7): Isolation shapes reliability. With no synchronous neighbors, all frequency response must come from resources inside ERCOT. Because the system is smaller than the other interconnections, losing a unit causes a steeper frequency drop, so ERCOT needs more robust frequency response. [e7]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e8, e9): Isolation also shapes regulation. The FERC-NERC staff report states that ERCOT's wholesale market is not regulated by FERC because power exchanges between entities occur wholly within the state, not through interstate commerce. [e8] The Protocols back this up. ERCOT must act to keep entities in the ERCOT Region from becoming "public utilities" under the Federal Power Act, including by ordering ties to other Control Areas disconnected or by denying or curtailing e-Tags over DC ties. [e9]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e14): The ERCOT footprint is not all of Texas. It covers about 75 percent of the state's land area, excluding the El Paso area, part of the northern panhandle, and part of east Texas north and east of Houston to the Louisiana border. [e14]
- **ERCOT context #3** (cites e11, e10, e13): Net DC tie flows were small in 2025, and the net direction differed between 2024 and 2025. ERCOT was a net exporter in 2024 for the first time since 2018. [e11] In 2025 it returned to net importer, exporting to Mexico and importing from SPP, with average net inflow of only about 36 MW. [e10] During Winter Storm Uri, ERCOT imported what it could, but with 1,220 MW of tie capability it could not import the many thousands of MW that MISO and SPP brought in. [e13]
