## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2, e3, e4, e5, e9): Ancillary services are the services necessary to support the transmission of energy to load while maintaining reliable operation of the transmission system. [e1] In ERCOT they are procured as reserve capacity: Regulation Up and Down, Responsive Reserve (RRS), ERCOT Contingency Reserve Service (ECRS) and Non-Spinning Reserve (Non-Spin). [e2][e3][e4][e5][e9]
- **How it works #1** (cites e6, e7): Each day ERCOT analyzes expected load for the Operating Day and develops an Ancillary Service Plan listing the MW of each service needed in each hour. The quantities can vary by hour with system conditions, and ERCOT must post the plan by 0600 of the Day-Ahead. [e6] The plan is then split among QSEs: ERCOT assigns each QSE a share by service and by hour, based on its load ratio share, and that share is its Ancillary Service Obligation. [e7]
- **How it works #2** (cites e2, e3, e4, e5): The services differ mainly by speed and duration. Reg-Up responds to ERCOT signals within five seconds to changes from scheduled system frequency. [e2] RRS is automatically self-deployed to restore or maintain frequency after a significant deviation. [e3] ECRS is capacity that can be sustained for two consecutive hours, used when RRS is depleted, as backup regulation, and to supply energy to avoid or during an EEA. [e4] Non-Spin is provided from capacity that can be synchronized and ramped to a specified output within 30 minutes. [e5]
- **How it works #3** (cites e9): Price formation uses an ancillary service demand curve for each service. Reg-Down has a constant demand curve equal to VOLL over the full range of the plan, while the curves for Reg-Up, RRS, ECRS and Non-Spin come from an aggregate ORDC that is split into individual curves. [e9]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e10, e11): The IMM says consumers pay the cost of AS procured through the market, and ERCOT allocates the cost to LSEs on real-time adjusted metered load. [e10] Real-Time Co-Optimization (RTC) changed the day-ahead role: before RTC went live in December 2025 AS were sold only day-ahead as a physical obligation, and now they are also procured in real time while day-ahead awards are financial. [e11]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e14, e12, e13, e8): Costs have fallen: AS cost was $0.98 per MWh of load in 2024, down from $3.74 in 2023, and $0.39 per MWh in 2025, a 60% decrease from 2024. [e14][e12] In 2025 batteries averaged 94% of Reg-Up, 86% of Reg-Down, 51% of RRS, 42% of ECRS and 24% of Non-Spin. [e13] The IMM also argues ERCOT over-procures: its 2025 report says the AS Plan is 140% larger than a 1-in-10 load shed reliability standard would require. [e8]
