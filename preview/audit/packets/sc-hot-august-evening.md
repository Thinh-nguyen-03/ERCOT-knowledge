The real-time offer cap (RTSWCAP) is $2,000/MWh and the day-ahead cap $5,000/MWh. [e20] ERCOT's summer 2026 review says the real-time cap was reduced to $2,000/MWh from $5,000/MWh at RTC go-live on 12/5/2025. [e12] The VOLL used to build the ASDCs is set to the DAM HCAP, and the ASDC maximum prices up to the Minimum Contingency Level are VOLL + 4,052 for Reg-Up, VOLL + 2,051 for RRS, VOLL + 50 for ECRS and VOLL for Non-Spin. [e19][e21] So ERCOT says the energy component of real-time LMPs can exceed $2,000 because of the ASDCs. [e22] System Lambda itself is capped by the under-generation Power Balance Penalty, the effective VOLL plus the effective RTSWCAP plus $4,052.01/MWh. [e23] A nodal LMP is System Lambda minus the sum of the bus's shift factors times constraint shadow prices, so congestion can move a node above or below that. [e24]
- **Formula #2** (cites e24): Nodal price: LMP_EB = λ − Σ SF_EB × SP_line, where λ is System Lambda (the shadow price of power balance), SF_EB is the bus's shift factor on each line and SP_line is the line's shadow price. [e24]

## Uncited guidance text (check for new facts, numbers, dates, names)

- (Scenarios) - (Reasoning) Then give a view: a spike is likely if the net load ramp is steep, batteries reach the net peak with little stored energy, and Non-Spin or ECRS look short. It is less likely if wind picks up in the evening.
- (Scenarios) - (Reasoning) The lesson for the scenario: evening tightness is not only an energy-balance problem; a binding constraint that removes cheap supply at the same moment can turn a predictable spike into an emergency.
- (Say it like this) (Guidance) Evening prices spike because solar drops off faster than demand does. The last megawatts come from fast gas and batteries that are running low on stored energy. Since RTC, any reserve shortage is priced through the AS demand curves inside the energy price, which can take real-time prices above the real-time offer cap.
