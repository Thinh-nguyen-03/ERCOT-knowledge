## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **How it works #1** (cites e1, e4, e29, e5, e28): **Representation.** Resource Entities and LSEs communicate with ERCOT and settle charges through a QSE. [e1] An LSE applicant must designate the QSE that will perform QSE functions on its behalf, and acknowledges that it bears sole responsibility for selecting and maintaining a QSE. [e4][e29] ERCOT must approve that designation before a Competitive Retailer enrolls customer ESI IDs, or before a NOIE or ELSE registers a wholesale point of delivery. [e5] An LSE may change its QSE designation by written notice no more than once in any consecutive three days, and must submit the updated designation no less than six days before the effective date. [e28]
- **How it works #2** (cites e33, e34, e35): **Day-to-day duties named in the Protocols.** A QSE that represents a Resource must update its Current Operating Plan as soon as reasonably practicable and no later than 60 minutes after the event that changed availability. [e33] A QSE must forward a valid Dispatch Instruction to the Resource or group of Resources, or identify a reason for non-compliance to ERCOT. [e34] In the DAM, ERCOT pays each QSE providing procured Ancillary Services the MW procured multiplied by the MCPC, and each QSE pays for its share of each Ancillary Service procured. [e35]
- **How it works #3** (cites e3, e6, e15, e12, e36, e16): **Qualification.** One requirement is that the entity be financially responsible for Settlement charges for the entities it represents. [e3] A QSE must demonstrate to ERCOT's reasonable satisfaction that it does not pose an "Unreasonable Financial Risk", a risk of financial default posed to ERCOT or its Market Participants. [e6] The Protocols say indicators may include, but are not limited to, past market manipulation, trading violations or other finance-related violations based on a final adjudication, financial defaults in ERCOT or other energy markets resulting in losses or uplifts, and indications of imminent bankruptcy or insolvency. [e15] QSEs that are WAN Participants must show they can maintain a 24-hour, seven-day-per-week control or operations center for Day-Ahead and Operating Day communication with ERCOT. [e12] A QSE that seeks to be its own Counter-Party must submit a Counter-Party Credit Application Form. [e36] A QSE must notify ERCOT of material changes, including changes in its Principals; if it does not within two Business Days of becoming aware, ERCOT may, after notice to each represented Entity, refuse to allow it to perform as a QSE. [e16]
- **How it works #4** (cites e19, e20): **Application timeline.** An application must be submitted at least 60 days before the proposed commencement of service. [e19] ERCOT approves or rejects within 60 days after the application is deemed complete, unless it determines more time is needed and tells the applicant the date it expects to finish. [e20]
- **How it works #5** (cites e17, e10): **Subordinate QSEs and the Counter-Party.** A QSE may partition itself into any number of Subordinate QSEs, though a request for more than four is subject to ERCOT's determination that it will not overburden its staffing or systems. [e17] Each Subordinate QSE is treated as an individual QSE for all purposes except liability, financial security and financial liability, which are cumulative for the single Entity signing the QSE Agreement. [e17] The Protocols define a Counter-Party as a single Entity that is a QSE and/or a CRR Account Holder, including all its QSE registrations, subordinate QSEs and CRR Account Holder registrations. [e10]
- **How it works #6** (cites e18): **Data Agent-Only QSEs.** An entity may register as a Data Agent-Only QSE if, among other conditions, it is not also a CRR Account Holder, does not participate in the DAM, RTM or ERS market, and has no decision-making authority over the Resources for which it provides agency services. [e18]
- **How it works #7** (cites e7): **When a QSE leaves or fails.** The following paragraphs cover termination and suspension. [e7]
- **How it works #8** (cites e21, e22, e23): **QSE-initiated termination.** If a QSE intends to stop representing a Resource Entity that owns or controls a Resource in the Network Operations Model, it must give ERCOT and the Resource written notice at least 45 days before the Termination Date; for an LSE, or a Resource Entity without such a Resource, the notice is at least 12 Business Days. Neither applies where the entity serves as its own QSE. [e21] The terminating QSE stops representing the entity at 2400 on the Termination Date and remains responsible for settlement obligations it incurred before then, but is released earlier if the entity affiliates with a new QSE or qualifies as an Emergency QSE before that date. [e22] By noon on the fourth Business Day after the termination notice date the LSE or Resource Entity must designate a new QSE or satisfy QSE creditworthiness requirements and operate as an Emergency QSE. [e23]
- **How it works #9** (cites e37, e7, e8, e14): **ERCOT-initiated suspension or termination.** If ERCOT suspends a QSE or terminates its Standard Form Market Participant Agreement for Default, ERCOT notifies the affected LSEs and Resource Entities. [e37] Those entities are designated a Virtual QSE for up to two Bank Business Days, in which they must designate and start operating with a new QSE, or meet QSE creditworthiness requirements and operate as an Emergency QSE; the Emergency QSE option does not apply to an entity represented by a terminated or suspended QSE that is the same Entity as that QSE. [e7][e8][e14]
- **How it works #10** (cites e24, e25): **Limits on Virtual and Emergency QSEs.** For any Operating Day in which an LSE or Resource Entity has neither a QSE nor Emergency QSE qualification, ERCOT may designate it a Virtual QSE, which is liable for charges in Initial, Final and True-Up Settlements and any Resettlements, plus ERCOT's collection costs. [e24] An Emergency or Virtual QSE may only represent itself. An LSE in that position may only submit buyer-side Energy, Capacity and Ancillary Service Trades and DAM Energy Bids to serve its own Customers' Load; a Resource Entity may only submit transactions directly attributable to and wholly provided by its own Resources. [e25]
- **How it works #11** (cites e26, e38, e39): **Seven-Business-Day deadline.** Within seven Business Days after designation as an Emergency QSE it must designate a QSE to represent it, or fulfil all QSE registration and qualification requirements, after which ERCOT may redesignate it as a QSE. [e26] If an Emergency QSE that is an LSE fails to do so, ERCOT shall initiate a Mass Transition of its ESI IDs after notice as specified in the Retail Market Guide. [e38] If a Resource Entity fails, ERCOT may allow additional time. [e39]
- **How it works #12** (cites e27): **Acceleration.** On termination of a QSE's rights and its agreements with ERCOT, all sums owed to ERCOT are immediately due, and ERCOT may draw on pledged security and offset or recoup amounts due. [e27]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e9, e11): The IMM reports that RUC make-whole costs are allocated first to QSEs that are capacity short against their real-time obligations. [e9] QSEs communicate resource status to ERCOT through the Current Operating Plan. [e11]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e30, e31, e32): Credit and payment discipline sits behind the QSE role. A Counter-Party must keep Financial Security at or above its Total Potential Exposure; if it does not, ERCOT may suspend its rights, including a QSE's ability to bid in the DAM. [e30] ERCOT's Credit FAQs (informational; the Protocols control where they conflict) describe escalating consequences for late payment: TPE increased by 110% for the first late payment in a rolling 12 months, 115% and 120% for the second and third, with a breach notice, one business day to cure and termination if not cured. [e31] If ERCOT revokes rights or terminates a market participant's agreements and that participant is also an LSE, ERCOT initiates a Mass Transition of its ESI IDs without needing a PUCT order. [e32]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e4, e5): - ERCOT approval of an LSE's QSE designation is a precondition to enrolling customers (CRs) or registering a wholesale delivery point (NOIEs, ELSEs). [e4][e5]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e21): - Termination notice by a QSE: at least 45 days for a Resource Entity with a Resource in the Network Operations Model; at least 12 Business Days for an LSE or other Resource Entity. [e21]
- **Key facts #6** (cites e26): - Emergency QSE: seven Business Days to name a QSE or become fully qualified. [e26]
- **Key facts #7** (cites e17): - Subordinate QSEs are separate for operations but cumulative for liability and financial security. [e17]
- **Say it like this #1** (cites e2, e3, e6, e30): (Guidance) A QSE is the qualified middleman between ERCOT and the generators and retailers it represents: it submits schedules and ancillary service bids and settles payments with ERCOT. [e2] Because a QSE must be financially responsible for the Settlement charges of the entities it represents [e3], ERCOT tests whether it poses an Unreasonable Financial Risk [e6] and monitors its credit against Total Potential Exposure. [e30]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e13, e4, e5, e10, e25, e7): (Guidance) A QSE is not a retailer and not a generator; it is the entity that interfaces with ERCOT for them. A REP is a Competitive Retailer, which is a kind of LSE [e13], and each LSE applicant must designate a QSE. [e4][e5] Do not confuse a QSE with a Counter-Party: a Counter-Party is a single Entity that is a QSE and/or a CRR Account Holder and includes all of that Entity's QSE registrations. [e10] Also keep "Emergency QSE" (an LSE or Resource Entity that meets QSE creditworthiness requirements, for itself only) apart from "Virtual QSE" (a temporary ERCOT designation). [e25][e7]
