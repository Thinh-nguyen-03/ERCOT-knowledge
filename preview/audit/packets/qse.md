## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2): A QSE is a Market Participant that ERCOT has qualified to communicate with ERCOT on behalf of Resource Entities and Load Serving Entities and to settle payments and charges with ERCOT. [e1] The PUCT's rule describes the same role as submitting balanced schedules and ancillary services bids and settling payments with ERCOT. [e2]
- **How it works #1** (cites e1, e4, e5): Resource Entities and LSEs communicate with ERCOT and settle charges through a QSE. [e1] An LSE applicant must designate the QSE that will perform QSE functions on its behalf. [e4] ERCOT must approve that designation before a Competitive Retailer enrolls customer ESI IDs, or before a NOIE or ELSE registers a wholesale point of delivery. [e5]
- **How it works #2** (cites e3, e6, e12): One qualification requirement is that the entity be financially responsible for Settlement charges for the entities it represents. [e3] A QSE must also be able to demonstrate to ERCOT's reasonable satisfaction that it does not pose an "Unreasonable Financial Risk", which the Protocols define as a risk of financial default posed to ERCOT or its Market Participants. [e6] Operationally, QSEs that are WAN Participants must show they can maintain a 24-hour, seven-day-per-week control or operations center for Day-Ahead and Operating Day communication with ERCOT. [e12]
- **How it works #3** (cites e10): The Protocols also define a Counter-Party as a single Entity that is a QSE and/or a CRR Account Holder, including all of that Entity's QSE registrations, subordinate QSEs and CRR Account Holder registrations. [e10]
- **How it works #4** (cites e7, e8, e14): If ERCOT has given notice of the termination or suspension of the QSE that represents an LSE or Resource Entity, that entity is designated a Virtual QSE for up to two Bank Business Days. In that window it must designate and start operating with a new QSE, or meet QSE creditworthiness requirements and operate as an Emergency QSE, though the Emergency QSE option does not apply to an entity represented by a terminated or suspended QSE that is the same Entity as that QSE. [e7][e8][e14]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e9, e11): The IMM reports that RUC make-whole costs are allocated first to QSEs that are capacity short against their real-time obligations. [e9] QSEs also communicate resource status to ERCOT through the Current Operating Plan, which is the mechanism QSEs use to communicate the expected status of their resources to ERCOT. [e11]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e1): - QSE qualification is done by ERCOT under Section 16 of the Protocols. [e1]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e3): - A QSE is financially responsible for Settlement charges of the entities it represents. [e3]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e4, e5): - Each LSE applicant designates its QSE, and ERCOT approval of the designation is a precondition to enrolling customers (CRs) or registering a wholesale delivery point (NOIEs, ELSEs). [e4][e5]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e8): - After ERCOT gives notice that a represented entity's QSE is terminated or suspended, the entity becomes a Virtual QSE for up to two Bank Business Days. [e8]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e6): - "Unreasonable Financial Risk" is a defined qualification test. [e6]
- **Say it like this #1** (cites e2, e3, e6): (Guidance) A QSE is the qualified middleman between ERCOT and the generators and retailers it represents: it submits schedules and ancillary service bids and settles payments with ERCOT. [e2] Because a QSE must be financially responsible for the Settlement charges of the entities it represents [e3], ERCOT also tests whether it poses an Unreasonable Financial Risk. [e6]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e13, e4, e5, e10): (Guidance) A QSE is not a retailer and not a generator; it is the entity that interfaces with ERCOT for them. A REP is a Competitive Retailer, which is a kind of LSE [e13], and each LSE applicant must designate a QSE. [e4][e5] Do not confuse a QSE with a Counter-Party: a Counter-Party is a single Entity that is a QSE and/or a CRR Account Holder and includes all of that Entity's QSE registrations. [e10]
- **Questions #2** (cites e4, e5, e1): The Protocols require each LSE applicant to designate a QSE that will perform QSE functions for it. [e4] ERCOT must approve that designation before a CR enrolls customer ESI IDs. [e5] The QSE is the party qualified to communicate with ERCOT and settle charges with ERCOT. [e1]
- **Questions #4** (cites e7, e8, e14): Once ERCOT has given notice of the termination or suspension, each affected LSE or Resource Entity is designated a Virtual QSE for up to two Bank Business Days. It must then designate a new QSE or satisfy QSE creditworthiness requirements and operate as an Emergency QSE, except that the Emergency QSE option does not apply where the represented entity is the same Entity as the QSE. [e7][e8][e14]
- **Questions #6** (cites e6): It is the risk of financial default posed to ERCOT or its Market Participants by an Entity or its Principals participating in the market, and a QSE must be able to demonstrate to ERCOT's reasonable satisfaction that it does not pose it. [e6]

- **Link to resource-entity** (cites e1): related Resource Entity: Resource Entities are also represented by a QSE, and the QSE definition names them alongside LSEs. [e1]
- **Link to rep** (cites e5, e13): related REP (Retail Electric Provider): ERCOT must approve the LSE's QSE designation before a Competitive Retailer (which includes REPs) enrolls customer ESI IDs. [e5][e13]
