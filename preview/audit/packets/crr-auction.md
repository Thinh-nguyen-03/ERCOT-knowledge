## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e10, e3, e15, e5): **Two auction types.** The CRR Monthly Auction is held once per month and sells one-month PTP Options and PTP Obligations for the following month. [e2] Twice a year a Long-Term Auction Sequence runs six successive auctions, starting with the next six-month period (January-June or July-December) and then the five periods after it. [e10][e3] The IMM describes this as CRRs offered up to three years (36 months) ahead, with the last long-term auction six months before real time. [e15] Each auction is a single-round, simultaneous auction across all products. [e5]
- **How it works #2** (cites e4, e6, e16, e51): **How much is offered.** Auction capacity is 90% for the Monthly Auction, and for the Long-Term sequence 70%, 55%, 40%, 30%, 20% and 10% for the first through sixth six-month windows. [e4][e6] For a month in a Long-Term auction, the capacity offered is the expected network topology scaled to that percentage, minus previously allocated CRRs (also scaled to the percentage), minus CRRs already awarded in any earlier auction. [e16] The IMM explains the logic: congestion is harder to forecast far ahead, which raises the risk that payments to CRR holders exceed day-ahead congestion rent, so ERCOT sells a portion of capacity and raises the share in successive auctions. [e51]
- **How it works #3** (cites e1, e12, e17, e18, e19): **Bids and offers.** To bid or offer, an entity must be a CRR Account Holder that meets the financial assurance criteria. [e1][e12] A Participating CRR Account Holder can only offer CRRs it owns of record at the time of the offer, and multi-month offers can only be made in a Long-Term Auction Sequence. [e17] A bid for a PTP Option cannot specify a non-positive Not-to-Exceed Price below the Minimum PTP Option Bid Price, while a PTP Obligation bid can specify a negative price. [e18] A 7x24 block may be bid in a Monthly Auction but not in a Long-Term Auction Sequence. [e19]
- **How it works #4** (cites e12, e20, e22): **Credit.** The auction system prevents awards that exceed the lesser of the Counter-Party's Section 16 credit limit and its self-imposed limit. [e12] Before the auction ERCOT screens: if a Counter-Party's credit limit exceeds its credit exposure computed on bid volumes, the limit is ignored while the auction is solved; PTP Option offers have zero credit exposure. [e20] After preliminary results, a Counter-Party that falls short of post-auction collateral can be given until 1500 on the next Bank Business Day to post more, failing which ERCOT voids all of its bids and offers and reruns the auction without it, if rerunning is practicable. [e22]
- **How it works #5** (cites e23, e24, e7, e26, e27, e25) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 16.11, 6.1: **Clearing.** ERCOT uses a linear programming auction engine that selects the bids and offers that keep the solution simultaneously feasible and maximize awarded bid value less awarded offer cost. [e23] The CRR clearing price equals the Shadow Price for that CRR product. [e24] Awards are tested with the Simultaneous Feasibility Test, which confirms the system can support the awarded CRRs under normal conditions, while observing security constraints. [e7] It uses a DC power-flow model and is not a system reliability test. [e26] Elements already oversold in prior auctions and allocations are increased to match what has been sold or allocated, which ensures feasibility of the auction. [e27] Awarded CRRs do not become the winner's property and cannot be placed in its CRR account until the required CRR Invoice has been paid, except for a one-time auction following repossession or forfeiture under Sections 16.11.6.1.4 and 16.11.6.1.5. [e25]
- **Formula #1** (cites e46, e47, e21): The credit constraint for each Counter-Party b in the auction is written ACR(b) = AOBLCR(b) + AOPTCR(b) - AOBLCRO(b). [e46] AOBLCR is the auction credit requirement for the Counter-Party's awarded PTP Obligation bids, AOPTCR is the same for its awarded PTP Option bids, and AOBLCRO is the auction credit requirement for its PTP Obligation offers. [e47][e46] The Protocols' variable list also defines a path-specific DAM-based adder, calculated as a 99th percentile of the average rolling consecutive DAM settled price for the reference source/sink over a month-equivalent period for each product type (18 days for 5x16, 8 days for 2x16, 28 days for 7x8). [e21]
- **Formula #2** (cites e48, e49, e50): Awarded auction offers are paid, and awarded auction bids charged, at the clearing price: the payment for PTP Obligation offers is OBLSAMT = (-1) x OBLPR x OBLS and the charge for bids is OBLPAMT = OBLPR x OBLP, where OBLPR is the clearing price of the PTP Obligation for the source and sink in that auction for the hour and OBLS and OBLP are the awarded MW of offers and bids. [e48][e49][e50] The Protocols give matching formulas for PTP Options.
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e13, e28, e36, e8, e37): **Revenue and who gets it.** The IMM says ERCOT distributes auction revenue to Load Serving Entities through CARD, by system-wide load ratio share for cross-zone CRRs and zonal share for same-zone CRRs. [e13] The Protocols credit the revenue to QSEs: same-zone-CMZ revenue (including PCRR revenue) on a zonal load ratio share and all other net revenue on an ERCOT-wide share, with the initial distribution based on each QSE's share in the interval coincident with the ERCOT-wide peak 15-minute Settlement Interval for the month. [e28] The IMM reports total CRR auction revenue of $832 million, $1,098 million, $1,442 million, $1,710 million and $1,810 million for 2021 through 2025 (OCR table, Figure 41), and growth of almost 6% in 2025 after 14% to 32% a year in 2021-2024. [e36][e8] In 2025 intrazonal CRRs drove the increase while cross-zone CRR revenue fell to its lowest value since 2021. [e37]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e29, e30, e31, e32, e40, e41, e42, e43, e44, e45): **Funding and shortfalls.** If day-ahead congestion rent equals or exceeds the net amounts due to CRR Owners in a Settlement Interval, ERCOT pays them and puts the excess in the CRR Balancing Account. [e29] If it is less, ERCOT short-pays each CRR Owner pro rata, using only amounts due for CRRs settled in both DAM and Real-Time and not amounts due to ERCOT for PTP Obligations the owner holds. [e30] In the shortfall hours, a charge is made on CRRs settled in the DAM that have positive Settlement prices. [e31] Surplus above the $10 million Balancing Account Fund cap goes to QSEs representing LSEs on monthly load ratio share. [e32] The IMM says shortfalls arise when flows modeled in the auction exceed day-ahead flows, and that the last CRR short payment occurred in November 2020. [e40][e41] For 2025 the IMM's Figure 43 shows day-ahead congestion rent of $2,477 million against CRR payments of $2,185 million, a surplus of about $293 million, with every month fully funded; 2024 was $1,923 million against $1,684 million (OCR, $ millions). [e42][e43][e44] The IMM also says mandatory deration reduced 2025 CRR payments by $33 million, to a funding percentage of about 99%. [e45]
