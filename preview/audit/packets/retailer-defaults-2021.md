## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2, e24): The 2021 defaults are the payments that market participants failed to make to ERCOT for charges from Winter Storm Uri: about $2.9 billion of short payments as of April 14, 2021, raised to $2.99 billion on May 14, 2021, of which $1.86 billion related to Brazos Electric Power Cooperative, which filed for bankruptcy on March 1, 2021. [e1] Retailers such as Griddy Energy were among the other participants that had not paid. [e2] HB 4492 defines the "period of emergency" those charges came from as 12:01 a.m. February 12 to 11:59 p.m. February 20, 2021. [e24]
- **How it works #1** (cites e22, e18, e19, e20, e21, e23, e1): Short-pay rules. Under Protocol Section 9.19 (current text), if ERCOT still lacks funds after collection attempts, collateral draws and offsets, and the short-payer is not on a payment plan, it uplifts the short-paid amount through Default Uplift Invoices. [e22] It collects the month's short-pay, less expected payment-plan amounts, from QSEs and CRR Account Holders and passes the money to those it short-paid; each Counter-Party's share is based on its MWh activity in the month before the default. [e18][e19] Uplift may not charge more than $2,500,000 per set of Default Uplift Invoices, sets must be at least 30 days apart, and the first may come no earlier than 90 days after the short-pay. [e20][e21] The UT report describes the same $2.5 million per 30 days limit and says that under the standard process it would have taken 96 years to collect the outstanding amount. [e23][e1]
- **How it works #2** (cites e2, e3, e47, e48): In 2021, ERCOT received less than it invoiced and reduced payments owed to CRR holders. [e2] For retailers, the UT report says the damage depended on how well their price risk was hedged and how outages changed their obligations. [e3] Griddy, Entrust and Power of Texas filed for bankruptcy, their certificates were revoked, and their customers were moved through mass transition; JP Energy Resources and Rhythm acquired the customer bases of several other exiting retailers. [e3][e47] By February 24, 2021, the rate options advertised on Power to Choose had dropped by half, and the UT report calls the number of retailer exits unprecedented. [e48]
- **How it works #3** (cites e12, e41, e13, e14, e43, e42, e44, e15, e16, e46, e45, e17, e5): Griddy step by step. On February 26, 2021 ERCOT revoked Griddy's rights under the Protocols for a payment breach; Protocol 16.11.6.1.6(3)(b) requires a mass transition on revocation, and ERCOT began moving about 10,127 ESI IDs to POLRs that day (the PUCT later found 9,926 customers were transitioned). [e12][e41][e13][e14] On March 5, 2021 the Commission drew on Griddy's $500,000 and $10,000 letters of credit. [e43] Griddy, which sold on pay-as-you-go billing debiting customers' advance balances daily by consumption, refunded those balances before filing for bankruptcy on March 15, 2021. [e42][e44][e15] ERCOT's amended claim was $29,913,799.84, and the PUCT found Griddy violated PURA by not following ERCOT's collateral-call and invoice-payment rules, and revoked its REP certificate. [e16][e46][e45][e17] More broadly, the IMM says high collateral requirements likely kept some participants out of the day-ahead market in February and into March 2021. [e5]
- **How it works #4** (cites e55, e56, e61, e62): Mass transition mechanics (current rules). ERCOT first assigns ESI IDs to volunteer REPs (VREPs) up to what each offered, then the rest to large service providers (LSPs) by MWh market share, randomly within class and POLR area. [e55][e56] The POLR takes responsibility from the Mass Transition Date, two days after ERCOT sends the enrollment transactions, and a participant whose customers were dropped must re-register with a new DUNS number. [e61][e62]
