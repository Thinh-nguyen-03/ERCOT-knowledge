## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2): The 2021 defaults are the payments that market participants failed to make to ERCOT for charges from Winter Storm Uri: about $2.9 billion of short payments as of April 14, 2021, raised to $2.99 billion on May 14, 2021, of which $1.86 billion related to Brazos Electric Power Cooperative, which filed for bankruptcy on March 1, 2021. [e1] Retailers such as Griddy Energy were among the other participants that had not paid. [e2]
- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e1): When participants short pay, ERCOT receives less than it invoiced. The UT report says ERCOT responded by reducing payments owed to holders of congestion revenue rights, and that under its standard Default Uplift Invoice process it would have taken 96 years to collect the amount outstanding. [e2][e1]
- **How it works #2** (cites e3, e12, e13, e14, e15, e16, e17, e5) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2022: For retailers, the UT report says the damage depended on how well their price risk was hedged and how outages changed their obligations. [e3] The UT report (July 2021) says Griddy, Entrust and Power of Texas each filed for bankruptcy, that their certificates to serve customers were revoked, and that their customers were moved to other retailers through ERCOT's mass transition process. [e3] For Griddy the primary documents give the order of events: on February 26, 2021 ERCOT revoked all of Griddy's rights under the Protocols for a payment breach and began a mass transition of about 10,127 ESI IDs to providers of last resort (the PUCT later found 9,926 customers were transitioned); Griddy filed for bankruptcy on March 15, 2021; ERCOT's amended proof of claim put the balance Griddy owed it at $29,913,799.84; and the PUCT revoked Griddy's REP certificate by order in Docket 51859 (2022). [e12][e13][e14][e15][e16][e17] Credit also bit: the IMM says high collateral requirements likely kept some participants out of the day-ahead market in February and March 2021. [e5]
