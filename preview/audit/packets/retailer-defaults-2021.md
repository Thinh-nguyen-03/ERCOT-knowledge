## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2): The 2021 defaults are the payments that market participants failed to make to ERCOT for charges from Winter Storm Uri: about $2.9 billion of short payments by April 14, 2021, of which $1.86 billion related to Brazos Electric Power Cooperative, which filed for bankruptcy on March 1, 2021. [e1] Retailers such as Griddy Energy were among the other participants that had not paid. [e2]
- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e1): When participants short pay, ERCOT receives less than it invoiced. The UT report says ERCOT responded by reducing payments owed to holders of congestion revenue rights, and that under its standard Default Uplift Invoice process it would have taken 96 years to collect the amount outstanding. [e2][e1]
- **How it works #2** (cites e3, e5): For retailers, the UT report says the damage depended on how well their price risk was hedged and how outages changed their obligations. [e3] Griddy, Entrust and Power of Texas filed for bankruptcy, lost their certificates, and had their customers moved to other retailers through ERCOT's mass transition process. [e3] Credit also bit: the IMM says high collateral requirements likely kept some participants out of the day-ahead market in February and March 2021. [e5]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e1, e7, e8, e9, e10): Brazos accounted for $1.86 billion of the roughly $2.99 billion in short payments the UT report cites. [e1] The IMM's 2022 report says Brazos filed under Chapter 11 after failing to pay about $1.9 billion for energy and ancillary services bought during Uri. [e7] In the bankruptcy, Brazos tried to cut more than $1.1 billion from ERCOT's claim, the part tied to the PUCT's February 15 and 16, 2021 pricing orders. [e8] Under the settlement ERCOT was to be reimbursed $599,709,609.22 for CRR funds it had used to cover Brazos's short pay, and the ERCOT Board approved Brazos's amended plan on October 28, 2022, subject to bankruptcy court approval. [e9][e10]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e6, e11): The Legislature's answer was HB 4492, which defines a "default balance" of up to $800 million covering amounts competitive wholesale market participants owed ERCOT from the period of emergency that would otherwise be uplifted. [e6] That financing closed in November 2021 and ERCOT began collecting default charges in January 2022. [e11]
