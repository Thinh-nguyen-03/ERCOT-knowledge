## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2, e3): ERCOT requires each Counter-Party, meaning an Entity that is a QSE and/or a CRR Account Holder, to maintain Financial Security at or above its Total Potential Exposure (TPE). [e1][e2] TPE is the sum of "Total Potential Exposure Any" (TPEA), the positive net exposure not included in TPES, and TPES, the positive net exposure for Future Credit Exposure and the Independent Amount. [e3]
- **How it works #1** (cites e4, e5, e8): ERCOT monitors and calculates each Counter-Party's TPEA and TPES daily. [e4] A Counter-Party must hold Financial Security at least equal to TPES plus the net positive exposure of approved CRR bilateral trades plus any credit limit locked for a CRR auction, and Remainder Collateral at least equal to TPEA. [e5] Financial Security can be, for example, an unconditional, irrevocable letter of credit naming ERCOT as beneficiary. [e8]
- **How it works #2** (cites e6, e7, e10): When either TPEA or TPES reaches 90% of its requirement, ERCOT uses reasonable efforts to send a warning. [e6] If a Counter-Party does not meet the requirements, ERCOT may suspend its rights under the Protocols. [e7] ERCOT can also set a different TPEA or TPES if it judges the calculated one does not match the Counter-Party's financial risk. [e10]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e11, e9): Credit is tied directly to market access. Suspension of rights can include a CRR Account Holder's ability to bid on future CRRs or a QSE's ability to bid in the DAM. [e11] The same Financial Security also backs other obligations: ERCOT may draw on it to pay short-paid Securitization Default Charge or Uplift Charge invoices if the escrow deposits are not enough. [e9]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e2): - Counter-Parties must maintain Financial Security at or above TPE at all times. [e2]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e3): - TPE = TPEA + TPES. [e3]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e4): - ERCOT recalculates TPEA and TPES daily. [e4]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e6): - A warning goes out at 90% of the requirement. [e6]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e7, e11): - ERCOT may suspend a Counter-Party's rights if it falls short, and the Protocols name DAM bidding and CRR bidding as examples of what suspension can include. [e7][e11]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e3, e5, e1): TPE is not a single collateral amount held by ERCOT; it is an exposure measure, and the collateral requirement is split between Financial Security (against TPES and CRR items) and Remainder Collateral (against TPEA). [e3][e5] "Counter-Party" is the entity level, not each individual QSE registration. [e1]
- **Questions #2** (cites e2, e3): The sum of TPEA and TPES, each a positive net exposure of the Counter-Party. A Counter-Party must keep Financial Security at or above it. [e2][e3]
- **Questions #4** (cites e7, e11): It may suspend the Counter-Party's rights under the Protocols, which can include a QSE's DAM bidding and a CRR Account Holder's CRR bidding. [e7][e11]
- **Questions #6** (cites e4): Daily, for both TPEA and TPES. [e4]
- **Link to qse** (cites e1): related QSE (Qualified Scheduling Entity): A Counter-Party is an Entity that is a QSE and/or a CRR Account Holder. [e1]
- **Link to crr** (cites e1, e5): related CRR (Congestion Revenue Right): CRR Account Holders are Counter-Parties and CRR auction credit is locked against their exposure. [e1][e5]
- **Link to dam** (cites e11): related Day-Ahead Market (DAM): ERCOT's suspension rights for unmet credit requirements are stated to include a QSE's ability to bid in the DAM. [e11]
- **Link to securitization** (cites e9): related Post-Uri Securitization: ERCOT may draw on Financial Security to cover short-paid securitization invoices when escrow deposits are insufficient. [e9]
- **Link to settlement-statements** (cites e2): related Settlement Statements & Timeline: A Counter-Party must keep Financial Security at or above its Total Potential Exposure. [e2]
