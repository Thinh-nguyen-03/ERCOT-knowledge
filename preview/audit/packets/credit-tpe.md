## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2, e3, e61): ERCOT requires each Counter-Party, meaning an Entity that is a QSE and/or a CRR Account Holder, to maintain Financial Security at or above its Total Potential Exposure (TPE). [e1][e2] TPE is the sum of "Total Potential Exposure Any" (TPEA), the positive net exposure not included in TPES, and TPES, the positive net exposure for Future Credit Exposure and the Independent Amount. [e3] The IMM describes TPEA as based on variation in wholesale prices (rising when prices are high) and TPES as based on exposure to positional risk from CRRs. [e61]
- **How it works #1** (cites e8, e66, e13, e14, e58, e59): **What counts as collateral.** Financial Security can be an unconditional, irrevocable letter of credit naming ERCOT as beneficiary, a surety bond, or cash collateral. [e8][e66] Letters of credit must come from banks rated at least A- (S&P and Fitch) or A3 (Moody's), and each issuer is subject to an overall limit of $750 million. [e13][e14] The IMM says ERCOT eliminated Unsecured Credit Limits and guarantee amounts through NPRR 1112 on October 1, 2023, so obligations must be covered by secured instruments. [e58][e59]
- **How it works #2** (cites e4, e5, e12, e10): **The requirement.** ERCOT monitors and calculates each Counter-Party's TPEA and TPES daily. [e4] A Counter-Party must hold Financial Security at least equal to TPES plus the net positive exposure of approved CRR bilateral trades plus any credit limit locked for a CRR auction, and Remainder Collateral at least equal to TPEA. [e5] Remainder Collateral is Financial Security minus TPES, minus net positive exposure of approved CRR bilateral trades, minus the ACL locked for the CRR auction. [e12] ERCOT may also set a different TPEA or TPES if it judges the calculated value does not match the Counter-Party's financial risk. [e10]
- **How it works #3** (cites e6, e41, e42, e43, e44, e45, e11): **Warning, suspension and cure.** When TPEA or TPES reaches 90% of its requirement, ERCOT uses reasonable efforts to send a warning. [e6] ERCOT may suspend a Counter-Party when TPES equals or exceeds 100% of its Financial Security, or TPEA equals or exceeds 100% of its Remainder Collateral. [e41] The cure deadline is set in Bank Business Days: for a notice delivered before 1500, until 1500 on the second Bank Business Day. [e42] Until the Counter-Party increases its Financial Security, ERCOT need not pay it, and may reject its bids and offers in CRR auctions and the DAM. [e43][e44] ERCOT may also notify each LSE and Resource represented by the QSE that it may have to designate a new QSE. [e45] A suspension can include a CRR Account Holder's ability to bid on future CRRs or a QSE's ability to bid in the DAM. [e11]
- **How it works #4** (cites e46, e47, e48, e49, e50, e51, e67, e52, e53): **Late payments and enforcement.** A failure to pay when due, including a Financial Security obligation, is a Late Payment and constitutes a Payment Breach. [e46] In the Protocols, enforcement escalates with each late payment in a rolling 12 months: Level I requires Financial Security at or above 110% of TPE within two Bank Business Days, Level II requires 115% in cash or a letter of credit, and Level III requires 120% with the excess over TPE in cash and remains in effect at least 90 days. [e47][e48][e49][e50] The fourth late payment leads to revocation of rights and termination of agreements. [e51] ERCOT's FAQ summarizes this as 110%, 115% and 120% of TPE on the first, second and third late payments, with the third covered only by cash, and notes the Protocols control if they differ. [e67] On a Payment Breach ERCOT may immediately draw on, hold or distribute the Market Participant's Financial Security. [e52] It may also repossess CRRs held by a Market Participant with a Payment Breach (this paragraph is shown with a pending NPRR1023 replacement). [e53]
- **How it works #5** (cites e71) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 16.11: A pending change (NPRR1023, to take effect upon system implementation) would add a Section 16.11.8 under which ERCOT may at its sole discretion initiate conversion of letters of credit or surety bonds held as Financial Security to cash collateral. [e71]
- **How it works #6** (cites e54, e55): **After termination.** ERCOT retains Financial Security for potential future obligations of a terminated Market Participant, set at the larger of its TPE or $5,000 (NPRR1023 has a pending replacement paragraph that keeps that amount but adds an exception for another paragraph). [e54] On termination all sums owed to ERCOT are accelerated and ERCOT may draw on Financial Security to offset them. [e55]
- **Formula #1** (cites e15): **TPE.** In the Protocols: [e15]
- **Formula #2** (cites e15): `TPEA = Max [0, MCE, Max [0, ((1-TOA) * EAL_q + TOA * EAL_t + EAL_a)]] + PUL` and `TPES = Max [0, FCE_a] + IA` [e15]
- **Formula #3** (cites e73, e74, e75, e76): - EAL is Estimated Aggregate Liability, with separate terms for QSEs where at least one represents Load or generation (q), QSEs where none represents Load or generation (t) and CRR Account Holders (a). [e73][e74][e75] TOA is the Trade-Only Activity switch, set to 0 if the Counter-Party represents a QSE associated with an LSE or Resource Entity (or no QSE) and otherwise 1. [e76]
- **Formula #4** (cites e16): - PUL is potential uplift: amounts expected to be uplifted within a year, plus the lesser of 25% of amounts expected beyond a year or five years' worth of uplift charges. [e16]
- **Formula #5** (cites e15, e18, e17): - MCE enters the TPEA formula as a floor; the saved text defines it with a longer equation not reproduced here, one of whose terms is the Initial Minimum Current Exposure, IMCE = TOA * (DASWCAP * nm * cif%). The parameter table lists nm = 50 and cif = 9%. [e15][e18][e17] (NPRR1188 has a pending replacement for the MCE variable; see Open items.)
- **Formula #6** (cites e77, e3): - FCE is Future Credit Exposure for all CRRs held by the CRR Account Holders the Counter-Party represents, and IA is the Independent Amount. [e77][e3]
- **Formula #7** (cites e20, e21, e22): **EAL for a QSE with Load or generation.** The Protocols' equation is the larger of (i) the Initial Estimated Liability, a term that applies only during the first 40-day period beginning when the Counter-Party commences activity in ERCOT markets, and (ii) the sum of: the larger of the highest RFAF times Real-Time Liability Extrapolated over a look-back and Real-Time Liability Forward; DFAF times the Day-Ahead Liability Extrapolated; the larger of the Real-Time Liability Completed and Not Settled and the highest Unbilled Real-Time Amount over a look-back; Outstanding Unpaid Transactions; and Incremental Load Exposure. [e20] RTLE is the multiplier M1 times the net amount due to or from ERCOT over the 14 most recent Operating Days with RTM Initial Statements, divided by 14; DALE is the same idea over seven DAM statement days. [e21][e22]
- **Formula #8** (cites e23, e24): **Initial Estimated Liability.** For a Counter-Party whose QSEs represent only Load, IEL = DEL * Max[0.2, RTEFL] * RTAEP * (M1 + M2), where RTAEP is the previous seven days' average Real-Time price at ERCOT 345 (Hub Average 345 kV). [e23][e24]
- **Formula #9** (cites e27, e28): **Forward adjustment.** ERCOT uses electricity futures prices to adjust TPEA: RFAF = PRFAP/HRSAP and DFAF = PDFAP/HDSAP, ratios of projected forward average prices to historic settled average prices. [e27][e28]
- **Formula #10** (cites e25, e79, e26): **FCE.** FCE = FCEOBL + FCEOPT, the future credit exposure of the PTP Obligations and PTP Options held by the CRR Account Holders. [e25] The PTP Obligation component, FCEOBL, is calculated using the portfolio weighted adder (PWA), the 100th percentile of a volume-weighted rolling DAM settled price over a product-specific period. [e79][e26]
- **Formula #11** (cites e29, e30, e31): **Available credit limits.** ACLC (CRR auction) = max(0, secured Financial Security - (1+ACLIRF)*TPES - net positive exposure of approved CRR bilateral trades - max(0, (1+ACLIRF)*TPEA)). [e29] ACLD (DAM) = max(0, Remainder Collateral - ACLIRF*TPES - (1+ACLIRF)*TPEA). [e30] The parameter table shows ACLIRF at 10%. [e31]
- **Example #1** (cites e5, e12, e30, e31) FLAG number-not-in-quote: $15, $5, 0.10, 1.10, $4.4, $0,, $0: Suppose a Counter-Party has TPEA of $4 million, TPES of $10 million, no CRR bilateral trades or locked auction limit, and posts $15 million of Financial Security. Remainder Collateral is $15 million - $10 million = $5 million, which exceeds TPEA of $4 million, so it meets both requirements under paragraph (2). Its ACLD would be max(0, $5 million - 0.10 x $10 million - 1.10 x $4 million) = max(0, $5 million - $1 million - $4.4 million) = $0, so its DAM credit limit would be $0 (the limit is ACLD, so bids with positive credit exposure could not be accepted) even though it is compliant. [e5][e12][e30][e31]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e11, e32, e36, e38, e33, e39, e40, e69, e70): Credit is tied directly to market access. Suspension of rights can include a CRR Account Holder's ability to bid on future CRRs or a QSE's ability to bid in the DAM. [e11] The DAM check works through the ACLD: ERCOT imposes a credit limit of the ACLD on each Counter-Party participating in the DAM, accepts bids and offers in the order submitted while exposure stays within that limit, rejects bids that exceed it, and sends a new limit daily. [e32][e36][e38][e33] A bid priced at or below zero gets a zero bid exposure price. [e39] ERCOT must re-examine DAM credit parameters immediately if a Counter-Party exceeds 90% of its ACL available to DAM. [e40] The FAQ notes that e-Factors affecting DAM bid exposure are calculated and set every two weeks. [e69] ERCOT posts the credit monitoring reports twice each Business Day. [e70]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e34, e35, e37): For CRRs, the CRR Auction system prevents a CRR Account Holder from being awarded bids and offers beyond the lesser of its self-imposed credit limit and the limit prescribed in Section 16.11.4.6.1, under which the ACL locked for the CRR auction is the lower of the requested limit, ACLC at the close of the bid window, and the pre-auction screening exposure; PTP Option offers carry zero credit exposure in the pre-auction screening. [e34][e35][e37]
- **ERCOT context #3** (cites e9, e56, e57): The same Financial Security also backs other obligations: ERCOT may draw on it to pay short-paid Securitization Default Charge or Uplift Charge invoices if the escrow deposits are not enough, but Securitization Uplift Charge escrow cannot be used for short-pays of Securitization Default Charges. [e9][e56] Securitization Default Charge escrow is separately held at or above the Securitization Default Charge Credit Exposure. [e57]

[e59] In 2025 total collateral held was $6.86 billion, nearly unchanged from 2024, and TPE was almost the same; over 2024 and 2025 TPES has been the larger component, in years of low energy prices and high CRR participation. [e63][e62]
