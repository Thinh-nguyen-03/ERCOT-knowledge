## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e4, e1): The evening ramp, which the IMM calls the "duck curve," is the sharp rise in net load in the evening as solar production falls away while demand is still high. [e4][e1] In ERCOT the tightest system conditions have moved from the firm peak load hour, usually around 4 to 5 p.m., to the 7 to 9 p.m. net load peak. [e1]
- **How it works #1** (cites e1, e8, e9): Net load is demand minus renewable generation, so when solar ramps down in the evening and wind has only begun to ramp up, the rest of the fleet must cover a fast increase. [e1] ERCOT's summer 2023 review described the same pattern: the solar down ramp made late-evening conditions tighter than at peak demand, and the net peak hours moved from HE 17 to HE 21. [e8][e9]
- **How it works #2** (cites e2, e7) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2026: The ramp is met by fast resources. SCED often dispatches quick-start natural gas plants or ESRs with high-priced offer curves during the ramp-down, which produces frequent and predictable price spikes. [e2] ERCOT's summer 2026 review says that on the lowest state-of-charge days it examined, ESRs were charging during high solar generation and discharging during the solar ramp down. [e7]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e2, e11, e3, e10): In 2025 the evening ramp accounted for 30% of all price spikes, up from 22% in 2024, while the share of spikes in the middle of the day fell from 9.3% to 2.7%. [e2][e11] The IMM also reports that summer price spikes were 40% less frequent in 2025 than in summer 2024 as added ESR capacity improved ERCOT's ability to manage ramp needs. [e3] ERCOT's summer 2026 review says more solar and storage tends to lower daytime prices, which lets ESRs charge then and discharge over the evening ramp. [e10]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e5, e4): The IMM treats market design as part of the issue. It notes the real-time market optimizes over a single interval based on the next five minutes, and says a multi-interval market would help with the duck curve by pre-positioning slow-ramping resources earlier.
