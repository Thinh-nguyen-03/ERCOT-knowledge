## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e3, e12, e4, e5, e33): **Who has choice.** In Texas, each retail customer has choice from January 1, 2002, except customers of cooperatives and municipally owned utilities that have not opted for choice. [e2] The incumbent utility's affiliated retail provider could keep serving its customers until each chose a different REP, or a cooperative or municipal utility offering choice. [e3] A utility with a qualifying systemwide freeze in effect September 1, 1997 that extended beyond December 31, 2001 was not subject to the chapter until the freeze expired. [e12] After the start date the transmission and distribution utility may not sell electricity except to buy for its own needs, and metering services for the area continue to be provided by its transmission and distribution utility affiliate, except that metering for commercial and industrial customers required by the ISO to have an interval data recorder meter may be provided on a competitive basis. [e4][e5] A customer is entitled to choose its provider, to have the choice honored and to assume the provider will not be changed without informed consent. [e33]
- **How it works #2** (cites e14, e15, e18, e19, e20, e21, e16, e17, e22, e7): **How a co-op or municipal utility opts in.** A municipal governing body, or a body managing the utility, has discretion to decide when or whether the MOU will provide choice; the decision may be made at any time on or after January 1, 2002 by resolution and is irrevocable. [e14] An electric cooperative's board has the same discretion, by resolution, but the decision may be revoked only if no customer has opted for choice within four years of the resolution, and a cooperative may start a choice pilot at any time. [e15] After opting in, all retail customers within the certificated area have the right of choice and the utility must provide open access; metering may remain with the utility as sole provider. [e18][e19] An opted-in cooperative must designate itself or another entity as provider of last resort, [e20] and an opted-in MOU may service customers outside its area at unregulated prices, subject to the PUCT's code of conduct, with PUCT jurisdiction over terms but not rates for access to its distribution facilities. [e21] An MOU or EC that has not opted in may not sell at unregulated prices to retail customers outside its certificated area but keeps its in-area programs and wholesale rights. [e16][e17] At ERCOT, each MOU and EC must register whether it plans to be a NOIE or a Competitive Retailer, and one that registers an affiliate as a CR must notify ERCOT six months before opting in. [e22] Opt-in entities must install IDRs at premises subject to the mandatory requirement before joining ERCOT's choice systems. [e7]
- **How it works #3** (cites e6, e8, e9, e25, e26, e27, e28): **Switching.** ERCOT administers retail switching. [e6] A competitive retailer submits a Switch Request to ERCOT using an 814_01 transaction, and the customer stays with its current retailer during the switch process. [e8][e9] ERCOT responds to the CR with the 814_05 within one Retail Business Day of the TDSP's 814_04, and the TDSP must effectuate the switch within two Retail Business Days of the scheduled meter read date. [e25] A switch is effectuated on the actual meter read date and becomes effective at 0000 (midnight) on that date, or on the date of a special meter read. [e26][e27] ERCOT rejects a Switch Request with the 814_02 for listed reasons, which include an inactive ESI ID, a mismatched ZIP code, a CR not certified by the PUCT, a CR not registered with ERCOT, and a PUCT direction to reject registration requests from the CR. [e28]
- **How it works #4** (cites e11, e29, e30): **When a retailer fails.** If an LSE fails to cure a QSE Affiliation Breach and serves Load, ERCOT initiates a Mass Transition of its ESI IDs. [e11] In a Mass Transition ERCOT requests a meter read for a date two days after it initiates the transactions, and sends a drop to the transitioning CR and an enrollment request to the POLR(s) or designated CR. [e29] ERCOT then monitors the transitioned ESI IDs for 60 days from the Mass Transition Date, or until the first completed switch, move in or move out. [e30]
- **How it works #5** (cites e31, e32) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 150 kW: **ESI IDs with no REP.** The TDSP prepares a monthly list of ESI IDs with consumption of 150 kWh or more in a single meter reading cycle but no REP of record. [e31] The TDSP may disconnect such an ESI ID no earlier than ten days after notifying the customer, and may not disconnect one a REP has claimed. [e32]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e23, e24): The Retail Market Guide notes that in an IOU TDSP territory the PUCT customer protection rules apply, whereas in an MOU/EC territory the utility's own service rules apply, which often differ, for example on bill due date. It also notes that billing may be consolidated or separate in an MOU/EC territory but only consolidated by the CR in an IOU territory. [e23] Its Section 8 points to the PUCT tariff-access rules for Nueces Electric Cooperative and for Lubbock Power & Light, whose rule covers MOUs and ECs that implement customer choice after May 1, 2023. [e24]
- **Timeline #1** (cites e13): - Customer choice pilot projects: the PUCT was to require each electric utility to offer choice for five percent of its combined load beginning June 1, 2001. [e13]
- **Timeline #2** (cites e2): - January 1, 2002: customer choice for most retail customers. [e2]
