## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1): Customer Choice is the freedom of a retail customer to buy electric services, individually or aggregated with other customers, from the provider of its choice, and to choose among fuel types, energy efficiency programs and renewable power suppliers. [e1]
- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e3, e4, e5): In Texas, each retail customer has choice from January 1, 2002, except customers of cooperatives and municipally owned utilities that have not opted for choice. [e2] The incumbent utility's affiliated retail provider could keep serving its customers until each chose a different REP, or a cooperative or municipal utility offering choice. [e3] After that date the transmission and distribution utility may not sell electricity except to buy for its own needs, and it continues to provide metering in the area. [e4][e5]
- **How it works #2** (cites e6, e8, e9, e7): ERCOT administers retail switching. [e6] A competitive retailer submits a Switch Request to ERCOT using an 814_01 transaction, and the customer stays with its current retailer during the switch process. [e8][e9] Co-ops and municipal utilities that opt in must meet interval-metering requirements before joining ERCOT's choice systems. [e7]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e6): ERCOT states that it administers retail switching for more than 8 million premises in competitive choice areas. [e6]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e2): - Customer choice began January 1, 2002 for most retail customers. [e2]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e2, e7): - Co-ops and municipal utilities are in only if they opt in. [e2][e7]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e4, e5): - The wires utility may not sell electricity and keeps metering after choice begins. [e4][e5]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e6): - More than 8 million premises are in competitive choice areas, per ERCOT. [e6]
- **Say it like this #1** (cites e4, e6): (Guidance) Most Texas customers outside co-op and municipal territory pick their own retailer, and the wires utility may not sell electricity. ERCOT administers the switching. [e4][e6]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e2): (Guidance) "Retail choice" does not cover the whole state: non-opted-in co-ops and municipal utilities are outside it. [e2]
- **Questions #2** (cites e2): Customers of electric cooperatives and municipally owned utilities that have not opted for customer choice. [e2]
- **Questions #4** (cites e7): Among other things, it must install interval data recorders at premises subject to the mandatory requirement before joining ERCOT's choice testing and integration. [e7]
- **Link to rep** (cites e3): related REP (Retail Electric Provider): Customers may choose a retail electric provider, or a cooperative or municipal utility that offers choice. [e3]
- **Link to tdsp** (cites e4, e5): related TDSP (Transmission & Distribution Service Provider): The wires utility cannot sell electricity and continues to provide metering after choice begins. [e4][e5]
- **Link to noe** (cites e2): contrasts-with NOIE (Non-Opt-In Entity: Munis & Co-ops): Co-ops and municipal utilities are outside customer choice unless they opt in. [e2]
- **Link to esi-id** (cites e10): related ESI ID: A Switch Request must include an ESI ID. [e10]
- **Link to tx-set** (cites e8): used-in TX SET (Texas Standard Electronic Transactions): A CR submits a Switch Request to ERCOT using the 814_01 transaction. [e8]
- **Link to mass-transition** (cites e11): related Mass Transition: ERCOT initiates a Mass Transition of an LSE's ESI IDs if the LSE serving Load fails to cure a QSE Affiliation Breach. [e11]
