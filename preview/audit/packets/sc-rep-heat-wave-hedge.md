## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1): This scenario is about a retailer's exposure to real-time prices when a heat wave pushes its load above what it bought ahead. In 2024 the IMM found that only 17% of load was exposed to real-time prices on average, with the rest hedged by day-ahead positions including PTPs. [e1]
- **How it works #1** (cites e4, e22, e5): Step 1, find the open position. The QSE's Energy Imbalance charge at a Load Zone is calculated at the Real-Time Settlement Point Price on amounts that include its Self-Schedules, DAM Energy Bids cleared and Energy Trades as buyer, less (among other terms) its AML at the Settlement Point. [e4][e22][e5] Load above those purchases is therefore left to settle at the real-time price.
- **How it works #2** (cites e23, e24, e9, e10, e11) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 25.509: Step 2, size the price risk. Offer caps differ by market since RTC go-live: the Protocol parameter table lists a day-ahead HCAP (DASWCAP) of $5,000/MWh and a real-time HCAP (RTSWCAP) of $2,000/MWh, ERCOT says the SWCAP was reduced to $2,000/MWh from $5,000/MWh after RTC go-live on 12/5/2025, and PUCT rule 25.509 still states a $5,000 HCAP. [e23][e24][e9] In 2025 most price spikes came in the 7 to 9 p.m. peak net load hours as solar ramped down. [e10] ERCOT set an all-time demand record of 91,134 MW on July 22, 2026. [e11]
- **How it works #3** (cites e2, e6, e7, e25, e16, e17): Step 3, list the hedging tools. The IMM says LSEs manage price exposure by owning generation, signing bilateral PPAs, using futures, or buying in the day-ahead market. [e2] The day-ahead market cleared 62% of real-time load in 2025, and PTP obligations are also used to hedge real-time exposure from transactions outside the ERCOT market. [e6][e7] On the exchange side, ICE lists a monthly cash-settled ERCOT North 345KV Real-Time Peak Fixed Price Future, priced on the average of SPPs for hours ending 0700-2200. [e25][e16][e17]
- **How it works #4** (cites e3, e8): Step 4, know the limits of buying day-ahead. The IMM says day-ahead prices in a well-performing market tend to reflect expected real-time prices, so forward hedging gives some protection against real-time volatility but little against sustained elevated prices. [e3] In 2025 the average day-ahead premium was $1.17 per MWh, but the average absolute day-ahead to real-time difference was $12 per MWh. [e8]
- **How it works #5** (cites e12, e13, e14, e20, e15, e18): Step 5, check credit. The QSE's Counter-Party must keep Financial Security at or above its Total Potential Exposure at all times. [e12] ERCOT's real-time liability estimate includes the Load Zone imbalance charge, estimated from 14-day or seven-day-old Load Ratio Share. [e13][e14] ERCOT warns at 90% of the requirement, may suspend a Counter-Party that does not meet the requirement, and, for a breaching participant that is an LSE, initiates a Mass Transition of its ESI IDs on revocation. [e20][e15][e18]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e1): - Only 17% of load was exposed to real-time prices on average in 2024. [e1]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e6): - The day-ahead market cleared 62% of real-time load in 2025. [e6]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e8): - The 2025 average day-ahead premium was $1.17 per MWh; the average absolute DA/RT difference was $12 per MWh. [e8]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e23, e24, e9) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 25.509: - Offer caps: $5,000/MWh day-ahead and $2,000/MWh real-time in the Protocol table and per ERCOT since RTC go-live on 12/5/2025; PUCT rule 25.509 still states a $5,000 HCAP. [e23][e24][e9]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e11): - All-time demand record: 91,134 MW on July 22, 2026, HE19. [e11]
- **Key facts #6** (cites e20): - ERCOT issues a credit warning when TPEA or TPES reaches 90% of the requirement. [e20]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e3, e13, e14, e20): (Guidance) A day-ahead purchase fixes the price for the volume bought; it does not fix the price if the heat wave lasts long enough to lift day-ahead prices too. [e3] Another confusion is treating collateral as a back-office issue: ERCOT estimates real-time liability from RTM charges including the Load Zone imbalance charge, and warns the Counter-Party when exposure reaches 90% of its requirement. [e13][e14][e20]
- **Questions #2** (cites e4, e5): Because the REP pays the real-time Load Zone price on its actual Adjusted Metered Load net of what it bought ahead, every MWh above the hedge is bought at whatever real-time prints.
