## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2): This scenario is about a data center that wants to connect to ERCOT and buy power for its load. In the Protocols a Large Load is one or more facilities at a single site with aggregate peak demand of 75 MW or more, and a Large Computational Load is a Large Load where at least half the demand is computational, such as data centers. [e1][e2]
- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e4, e5, e9, e10) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 75 MW: Step 1, the connection rules. SB 6 told the PUCT to set a 75 MW threshold for large load interconnection standards and a flat study fee of at least $100,000. [e3][e4] The PUCT rule requires financial security of $50,000 per MW of requested peak demand for new requests. [e5] ERCOT now studies large loads together in batches: a studied load in Batch Zero may get its full request, a partial allocation, or have to wait, and projects face a commitment deadline, currently planned for June 2027, to post financial security and prove site control. [e9][e10]
- **How it works #2** (cites e6, e7, e8, e23): Step 2, the operating conditions. Under SB 6, after ERCOT deploys all available market services except frequency responsive ones, the independent organization may direct the utility to require the large load customer to deploy its on-site backup generation or curtail load. [e6] New transmission-voltage loads must also have equipment installed so they can be curtailed during firm load shed. [e7] If the data center plans a net metering arrangement with an existing stand-alone generation resource, the generation company, municipally owned utility or cooperative must notify ERCOT before implementing it. [e8] ERCOT's December 2025 CDR called these SB 6 provisions the most impactful resource adequacy development since May 2025. [e23]
- **How it works #3** (cites e11, e12, e13, e14): Step 3, the forecast context. More than 438,000 MW of large-user requests were in ERCOT's queue by mid-2026, nearly 90% of them data centers.
