## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e2, e8): Controlled outages, also called load shed, are electric service interruptions ordered by ERCOT but implemented by transmission and distribution service providers, to quickly reduce demand, balance the grid and prevent an uncontrolled system-wide outage. [e2] Manual firm load shed is often called rolling or rotating blackouts: operators order a percentage of load temporarily disconnected to avoid system instability or other emergencies. [e8]
- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e1): Load shed is a last resort used to bring operating reserves back to a safe level and hold frequency. [e2] In the Protocols it is an EEA Level 3 step: ERCOT directs all transmission owners to shed firm load in 100 MW blocks, to hold steady-state frequency at a minimum of 59.91 Hz and recover 1,500 MW of PRC within 30 minutes. [e1]
- **How it works #2** (cites e3, e4, e5, e6): ERCOT gives each utility a megawatt reduction amount based on its share of historic peak demand; each utility decides how to meet it and manages the outage locally, and ERCOT has no oversight of those plans. [e3] Transmission owners may use load on under-frequency or under-voltage relays for manual shed during Level 3 only if they keep meeting their automatic UFLS obligation. [e4] ERCOT can also instruct TSPs or DSPs to drop load when an Emergency Condition comes from a transmission problem, and must post an operations message within one day with the event's date, times, MW and location. [e5][e6]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e11, e12, e7): ERCOT's overview lists four controlled-outage events: 500 MW in 1989, 1,000 MW in 2006, 4,000 MW in 2011 and 20,000 MW in February 2021. [e11] In February 2021 ERCOT ordered its first 1,000 MW of load shed at 1:20 a.m. and ended up shedding 20,000 MW at the worst point. [e12][e7]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e9, e10): The FERC and NERC report found that the size and duration of the shed made rotation hard and pushed operators into circuits normally reserved for automatic under-frequency load shed. In ERCOT at least 25 percent of load is reserved for automatic shed, not counting critical loads such as hospitals. [e9][e10]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e1): - Firm load shed is directed in 100 MW blocks at EEA Level 3. [e1]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e3): - ERCOT sets each utility's required MW reduction by its share of historic peak demand; utilities choose how to do it. [e3]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e6): - ERCOT must post within one day an operations message on any load curtailment due to a transmission problem. [e6]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e11, e7): - Peak controlled outage in February 2021: 20,000 MW, the largest of four events listed by ERCOT. [e11][e7]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e10): - At least 25 percent of ERCOT load is reserved for automatic load shedding. [e10]
- **Say it like this #1** (cites e1, e2, e3): Load shed is ERCOT's last-resort tool: when reserves run too low it orders utilities to cut a set number of megawatts, and the utilities decide whose power to turn off. It is the Level 3 step of the emergency alert ladder, not the first thing ERCOT reaches for. [e1][e2][e3]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e1, e2, e8): Load shed is not the same as an EEA: EEA Levels 1 and 2 use supply and demand-side tools, and firm load shed begins at Level 3. [e1] "Controlled outages", "rotating outages" and "load shed" are used for the same thing in these sources. [e2][e8]
- **Questions #2** (cites e3): ERCOT sets the megawatts each utility must reduce, based on historic peak demand share, and each utility decides how to do it in its own area. [e3]
- **Questions #4** (cites e9, e10): The combined size and duration of the shed made it hard to rotate, so operators had to use circuits normally reserved for under-frequency load shed, while ERCOT also has to keep at least 25 percent of load reserved for automatic shed. [e9][e10]
- **Questions #6** (cites e7, e13, e11): ERCOT shed 20,000 MW at the worst point; the FERC and NERC report puts the combined manual firm load shed across the affected regions at 23,418 MW. [e7][e13] ERCOT's own overview lists it as the largest of four controlled-outage events. [e11]
- **Scenarios #2** (cites e1): - At EEA Level 3 it directs all TOs to shed firm load in 100 MW blocks to hold 59.91 Hz and recover 1,500 MW of PRC within 30 minutes. [e1]
- **Scenarios #3** (cites e3, e4): - Utilities carry out the shed using their local plans, using under-frequency relay load only within their UFLS obligations. [e3][e4]
