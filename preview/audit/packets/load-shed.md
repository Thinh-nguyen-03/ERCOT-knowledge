## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e16, e1, e29, e30, e34): Load shed is a last resort used to bring operating reserves back to a safe level and hold frequency. [e2] In the Protocols, upon declaring an EEA Level 3 ERCOT shall take any of a list of measures as necessary to recover frequency or PRC. [e16] One of them is to direct all transmission owners to shed firm load in 100 MW blocks, to hold steady-state frequency at a minimum of 59.91 Hz and recover 1,500 MW of PRC within 30 minutes. [e1] Another is to instruct ESRs to suspend charging. [e29] Before Level 3, at Level 2, ERCOT may already deploy ECRS or RRS from Load Resources. [e30] ERCOT must also implement Level 3 immediately whenever steady-state frequency is below 59.5 Hz for any duration. [e34]
- **How it works #2** (cites e3, e21, e35, e22): Who sheds how much: ERCOT gives each utility a megawatt reduction amount based on its share of historic peak demand; each utility decides how to meet it and manages the outage locally, and ERCOT has no oversight of those plans. [e3] The Operating Guides set the Level 3 obligation percentages by calculating each TO's load as a percentage of the ERCOT system summer and winter peak 15-minute Demand interval. [e21] The Protocols say the percentages are based on the previous year's TSP peak loads and are reviewed and modified annually. [e35] ERCOT uses the summer table for a hot weather load shed event and the winter table for a cold weather one, deciding in its sole discretion which applies. [e22]
- **How it works #3** (cites e38, e39, e4, e20, e5, e6): Under the Operating Guides, load shed by SCADA must be executed without delay and within 30 minutes of the instruction, and any remainder by non-SCADA methods within one hour; each TSP reports load shed progress to ERCOT within 30 minutes of the instruction or on request. [e38][e39] Transmission owners and TDSPs may manually shed load connected to under-frequency or under-voltage relays under an ERCOT Level 3 directive so long as the TO has determined system conditions warrant it and each affected TO keeps complying with its UFLS and load shed obligations. [e4] If all load identified for manual shed, and the relay-connected load described in the preceding provision, has been shed, they may also shed load armed for the 58.5 Hz, 58.7 Hz and anti-stall UFLS stages, even if that takes UFLS load below the TO's 25% relief obligation. [e20] ERCOT can also instruct TSPs or DSPs to drop load when an Emergency Condition comes from a transmission problem, and must post an operations message within one day with the event's date, times, MW and location. [e5][e6]
- **Formula #1** (cites e17, e18, e19, e37): There is no single formula for the manual shed; the quantities are the TO percentages above. Automatic shed is a table of frequency stages. At least 25% of ERCOT system load must be equipped at all times with automatic under-frequency load shedding (UFLS), unless Level 3 provisions are needed to meet ERCOT operating instructions for manual shed. [e17] The standard stages are: 59.3 Hz, at least 5% of TO load; 59.1 Hz, a total of at least 10%; 58.9 Hz, 15%; 58.7 Hz, 20%; and 58.5 Hz, 25%, each with a delay of at least six and no more than 30 cycles. [e18] Three supplemental anti-stall stages at 59.5 Hz require at least 1.5%, a total of at least 3.0% and a total of at least 4.5% of TO load, with delays of 90, 120 and 150 seconds. [e19] A TO may use load it would otherwise use for the 59.1 to 58.5 Hz stages to meet the anti-stall stages, but not load needed for the 59.3 Hz stage. [e37]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e11, e12, e7, e32, e33): ERCOT's overview lists four controlled-outage events: 500 MW in 1989, 1,000 MW in 2006, 4,000 MW in 2011 and 20,000 MW in February 2021. [e11] In the early hours of 15 February 2021 ERCOT ordered its first 1,000 MW of load shed at 1:20 a.m. and ended up shedding 20,000 MW at the worst point. [e12][e7] FERC and NERC say ERCOT maintained firm load shed for nearly three days, and that more than 4.5 million people in Texas lost power in the Event, some for as long as four days. [e32][e33]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e9, e10, e31): The FERC and NERC report found that the size and duration of the shed made rotation hard and pushed operators into circuits normally reserved for automatic under-frequency load shed. In ERCOT at least 25 percent of load is reserved for automatic shed, not counting critical loads such as hospitals. [e9][e10] The report says operators needed to protect at least 25 percent of load beyond the 14-28 percent of manual shed ordered on February 15-16 and the identified critical loads. [e31]
- **ERCOT context #3** (cites e23, e28, e27): Critical loads: each TO must coordinate with each TDSP to minimize overlap of manual-shed circuits with circuits serving designated critical loads and with UFLS and UVLS circuits. [e23] Under the PUCT's reliability rule, a utility must incorporate critical natural gas facilities, which are critical loads during an energy emergency, into its load-shed and restoration planning. [e28] The PUCT's emergency operations plan rule requires transmission and distribution utilities, municipally owned utilities and cooperatives to include a load shed annex covering procedures for controlled shedding and priorities for restoring shed load. [e27]
- **ERCOT context #4** (cites e26, e24, e25): Restoration: ERCOT continues the EEA until sufficient offers are received and deployed and normal SCED operations are restored; intermittent SCED solutions do not set new LMPs until ERCOT declares the EEA no longer needed. [e26] When terminating, ERCOT must maintain a stable frequency when restoring load, and loads are restored only when specifically authorized by ERCOT. [e24][e25]
- **ERCOT context #5** (cites e14, e15, e36) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 3,000 MW: Scarcity pricing: the IMM says the ORDC reflects the relationship between operating reserves and the probability of load shed, and that the ORDC adder was discontinued in December 2025 with RTC. [e14][e15] In the Protocols, the Aggregate Operating Reserve Demand Curve (AORDC) used to determine the Ancillary Service Demand Curves is fitted by regression as (1 minus a normal cumulative probability at the reserve level less 3,000 MW, with fitted mean and standard deviation) times VOLL. [e36]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e10, e17): - At least 25 percent of ERCOT load is reserved for automatic load shedding. [e10][e17]
- **Key facts #6** (cites e38): - SCADA-controlled load shed must be completed within 30 minutes of the instruction, non-SCADA within one hour. [e38]
- **Key facts #7** (cites e18): - Standard UFLS stages run from 59.3 Hz (5% of TO load) to 58.5 Hz (25%). [e18]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e16, e1, e40, e8, e18): Load shed is not the same as an EEA: the Protocols list directing TOs to shed firm Load among the measures ERCOT may take upon declaring EEA Level 3. [e16][e1] ERCOT's overview says controlled outages are also referred to as load shed, and the FERC and NERC report says manual firm load shed is often referred to as rolling or rotating blackouts. [e40][e8] Manual firm load shed ordered by ERCOT is also not the automatic under-frequency load shed that relays perform at set frequencies. [e1][e18]
- **Questions #8** (cites e18, e20): Automatic UFLS stages trip load from 59.3 Hz down to 58.5 Hz, reaching at least 25% of TO load. [e18] If all load identified for manual shed and the relay-connected load under the preceding provision has been shed, TOs may also use load armed for the 58.5 Hz, 58.7 Hz and anti-stall UFLS stages even if it takes UFLS load below the 25% obligation. [e20]
- **Questions #10** (cites e23, e41) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2021: The Operating Guides require TOs to minimize that overlap, and the FERC and NERC report on the 2021 event says using UFLS circuits for manual shed would render them unavailable if frequency dropped and UFLS was needed. [e23][e41]
- **Questions #12** (cites e21, e22): The obligations are computed from summer and winter peak demand, and ERCOT chooses the table, in its sole discretion, by whether it treats the event as hot weather or cold weather. [e21][e22]
