## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e4): The CDR is ERCOT's Report on Capacity, Demand and Reserves in the ERCOT Region. Its main purpose is to provide forecasted Planning Reserve Margins by season and year, where the Planning Reserve Margin is the percentage of resource capacity, in excess of firm electricity demand, available to cover uncertainty in future demand, generator availability and new resource supply. [e4]
- **How it works #1** (cites e1, e2, e3) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 25.505: The Protocols require ERCOT to publish the CDR twice a year, with a preliminary version targeted for the third week of May and a final version for the third week of December. [e1] Forecasts must cover at least the next five years beyond the publication year. [e2] PUCT rule 25.505 separately requires an annual resource adequacy report by December 31 that projects at least five years of capability against projected demand. [e3]
- **How it works #2** (cites e6, e5): The CDR gives a deterministic view of future resource adequacy alongside several alternative load and planned-resource scenarios designed to capture a range of potential reserve margin outcomes. [e6] It is a planning tool: ERCOT notes it is not intended to characterize the risk of capacity scarcity from a real-time operations perspective. [e5]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e7, e10, e9): In the December 2025 CDR, the base scenario's summer 2026 Planning Reserve Margins are 18.3% for the peak Load hour (HE 5:00 p.m. CST) and 20.9% for the peak Net Load hour (HE 9:00 p.m. CST). [e7] The report says the base scenario's summer and winter margins for 2026 through 2030 decrease significantly from year to year and cross over to negative values in 2028. [e10] The three drivers it gives are high load growth largely from new Loads reported by TSPs, lower wind and solar capacity contributions from using ELCCs, and fewer planned resources passing additional eligibility criteria. [e9]

## Uncited guidance text (check for new facts, numbers, dates, names)

- (Say it like this) (Guidance) The CDR is ERCOT's multi-year look at whether expected resources cover forecast firm demand, shown as planning reserve margins by season. It is a rules-based planning snapshot, so treat its negative later-year margins as a signal about the trend, not a forecast of outages.
