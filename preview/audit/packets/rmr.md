- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e19, e45, e20, e23, e21) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 25.502: **Trigger and timeline.** The process starts when a Resource Entity files a Notification of Suspension of Operations (NSO); ERCOT may then begin procuring RMR Service. [e3] Except for a Forced Outage, the Resource Entity must give written notice at least 150 days before it intends to cease or suspend operation for more than 180 days, or at least 90 days before a seasonal mothball. [e19] PUCT rule 25.502(e) likewise requires ERCOT to make a final determination of the need for RMR service within 60 calendar days of receiving the notice. [e45] In the Protocols, Market Participants have 21 days to comment (unless ERCOT shortens it), ERCOT's reliability analysis must be completed within 60 days after receiving Parts I and II of the NSO, and ERCOT conducts that analysis for resources with a summer net max sustainable rating of at least 20 MW. [e20][e23][e21]
- **How it works #2** (cites e22, e4, e16, e17): **The need test.** A thermal-overload performance deficiency counts as materially impacted by the resource only if the resource has a more than 2% unloading Shift Factor and more than 5% unloading impact on the overloaded facility. [e22] Before entering an agreement ERCOT must assess alternatives, including re-dispatch or reconfiguration, Automatic Mitigation Plans and Remedial Action Plans, and Remedial Action Schemes. [e4][e16] ERCOT must minimize the use of RMR Units as much as practicable. [e17]
- **How it works #3** (cites e24, e8, e47, e25, e46, e26): **MRA RFP and Board approval.** Within ten days after a determination that the proposed suspension would cause a performance deficiency the resource materially affects, ERCOT issues an RFP for Must-Run Alternatives. [e24] If it finds an RMR or MRA agreement cost-effective (or finds a deficiency but that an agreement is not cost-effective), it presents the finding to the ERCOT Board for approval, and PUCT rule 25.502 makes ERCOT staff's recommendations subject to board approval. [e8][e47] If ERCOT has neither cleared the unit nor obtained Board approval after 150 days, the Resource Entity may file a complaint at the PUCT under 25.502(e)(1); if it does not file, it is deemed to have accepted ERCOT's most recent offer as of the suspension date. [e25][e46] If the Board approves an RMR agreement but it is not yet executed by the suspension date, the unit must stay available for RUC commitment. [e26]
- **How it works #4** (cites e6, e18, e5, e7, e27): **Terms.** RMR is voluntary for the owner: a Resource Entity cannot be compelled to sign, and one with an uneconomic unit can petition for RMR status. [e6] ERCOT must contract for the entire capacity of each RMR Unit. [e18] Agreements run from one month to one year, with longer terms allowed when a significant capital expenditure is needed. [e5] While under RMR the unit may not participate in the bilateral energy market, self-provide energy (other than plant auxiliary load) or provide Ancillary Service to any entity. [e7] No later than 90 days after an RMR agreement is executed, ERCOT must report a list of feasible alternatives that may later be more cost-effective than continued renewal. [e27]
- **How it works #5** (cites e28, e29, e30, e31, e32, e33, e37, e49, e48, e36): **Money.** Eligible Costs are those the owner would incur to provide RMR Service, excluding fuel costs or other costs it would have incurred anyway had it been mothballed or shut down; depreciation, return on equity and debt costs are among the examples that are not eligible. [e28][e29] The initial Standby Cost for each month is the monthly approved budget divided by the hours in the month, and it is recalculated if the remaining-term costs change by more than 10% from the most recently approved budget. [e30][e31] The incentive factor is 10% of actual Eligible Costs, excluding fuel and firm fuel reservation and transportation costs, and it is reduced linearly for failed capacity tests and for availability below target. [e32][e33] Energy is paid on the contract input/output curve and FIP at initial settlement and on actual fuel costs in later settlements. [e37] If the resource later participates in the energy or ancillary service markets, it must refund contributed capital under the Protocols' schedule (100% of remaining book value plus 10% of accelerated tax depreciation, less salvage previously repaid), and the PUCT rule says ERCOT may require less than the full amount to reflect depreciation. [e49][e48] The total RMR cost for all RMR Units is allocated to QSEs representing Load based on load ratio share. [e36]
- **How it works #6** (cites e39, e38, e40, e41): **Operations.** ERCOT may manually commit an RMR Unit through DRUC or HRUC to resolve a transmission constraint, and may submit Energy Offer Curves at the effective VOLL on its behalf. [e39][e38] ERCOT sets the unit's mitigated offer cap at the highest value expected to allow SCED to dispatch it. [e40] A pending change (NPRR826, to take effect upon system implementation) would instead use one single price for the full operating range of the Resource, determined through a shadow price test. [e41]
