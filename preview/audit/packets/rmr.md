## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2): A Reliability Must-Run (RMR) Unit is a Generation Resource operated under an agreement with ERCOT that would not otherwise be operated, because it is necessary for voltage support, stability or management of localized transmission constraints where market solutions do not exist. [e1] A Must-Run Alternative (MRA) is a resource operated under an agreement with ERCOT as an alternative to an RMR Unit. [e2]
- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e4, e8): The process starts when a Resource Entity files a Notification of Suspension of Operations; ERCOT may then begin procuring RMR Service. [e3] Before signing, ERCOT must assess alternatives to the proposed RMR agreement. [e4] If it finds an RMR or MRA agreement is cost-effective (or finds a deficiency but that an agreement is not cost-effective), it presents the finding to the ERCOT Board for approval. [e8]
- **How it works #2** (cites e6, e5, e7): RMR is voluntary for the owner: a Resource Entity cannot be compelled to sign, and one with an uneconomic unit can petition for RMR status. [e6] Agreements run from one month to one year, with longer terms allowed when a significant capital expenditure is needed. [e5] While under RMR the unit may not participate in the bilateral energy market, self-provide energy (other than plant auxiliary load) or provide Ancillary Service to any entity. [e7]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e9, e10, e11, e12): The IMM reports that the ERCOT Board approved RMR service on February 24, 2025 for Braunig Unit 3, which has an HSL of about 410 MW. [e9] The IMM counts RMR settlement as part of the 2025 uplift, with RMR uplift the largest contributor to the increase at $68 million of $668 million in total uplift. [e10] The IMM also reports that since the beginning of the RMR period the mobile generating units have produced about 1,226 MWh, and the maximum aggregate output of RMR units has been under 40 MW. [e11] ERCOT allocates the cost of RMR agreements to LSEs. [e12]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e13): ERCOT has proposed changes. For example, NPRR 1330 (proposed April 2026) would set mitigated offer caps for RMR units so they do not interfere with market outcomes. [e13]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e5): - RMR agreements run one month to one year unless a significant capital expenditure justifies longer. [e5]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e7): - A unit under RMR may not trade bilaterally, self-provide energy or provide Ancillary Service. [e7]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e8): - RMR and MRA agreements require ERCOT Board approval. [e8]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e10) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2025: - RMR uplift was $68 million of $668 million total uplift in 2025. [e10]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e12): - RMR costs are allocated to LSEs. [e12]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e1, e6, e2): RMR is not capacity market revenue and not a routine commitment tool: it applies only where market solutions do not exist, and the owner cannot be forced into it. [e1][e6] An MRA is the alternative to an RMR unit, not the same contract. [e2]
- **Questions #2** (cites e1, e6): It must be necessary to provide voltage support, stability or management of localized transmission constraints where market solutions do not exist, and the owner must be willing. [e1][e6]
- **Questions #4** (cites e4, e8): ERCOT presents its finding to the ERCOT Board for approval after assessing alternatives. [e4][e8]
- **Questions #6** (cites e12, e10) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2025: The IMM says ERCOT allocates RMR costs to LSEs, and RMR settlement counts in the uplift the IMM reports ($68 million of $668 million in 2025). [e12][e10]

## Uncited guidance text (check for new facts, numbers, dates, names)

- (Say it like this) RMR is ERCOT paying a plant that wants to retire to stay available because the local grid would be at risk without it. It is a short, voluntary contract outside the market, so the cost shows up as uplift on load.
