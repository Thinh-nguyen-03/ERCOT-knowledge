## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2, e3, e4) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 25.508: The ERCOT reliability standard is set in PUCT rule 16 TAC 25.508. The ERCOT bulk power system meets it if an ERCOT probability-based model simulation shows the system meets each of three criteria: frequency, duration and magnitude of loss of load events. [e1][e2][e3][e4]
- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e3, e4, e5): The frequency criterion is no more than one loss of load event per ten years on average, i.e., 0.1 loss of load expectation (LOLE). [e2] The duration criterion is that the maximum expected length of an event must be under 12 hours, with a 1.00 percent exceedance tolerance. [e3] The magnitude criterion is that the expected highest level of load shed must be below the maximum load that can be safely rotated, as determined by ERCOT with PUCT staff and transmission operators, again with a 1.00 percent exceedance tolerance. [e4] A loss of load event is when firm load plus the minimum operating reserves needed to avoid EEA Level 3 exceeds available capacity, resulting in involuntary load shed. [e5]
- **How it works #2** (cites e6, e7, e8): Starting January 1, 2026, ERCOT must assess whether the system meets the standard and is likely to keep meeting it for the following three years, at least once every three years. [e6] If deficiencies are identified, ERCOT must give the PUCT a summary of deficiencies and a menu of proposed market design changes with a primary recommendation. [e7] The model assumptions include a weatherization effectiveness, the assumed percentage reduction in weather-related unplanned outages from complying with 25.55. [e8]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e9, e11, e10, e12): The IMM says SB 3, passed after Winter Storm Uri, directed the PUCT to develop a formal reliability standard, which is the basis of the standard in 25.508. [e11] The IMM argues that meeting the standard through price signals alone would imply an extraordinarily high VOLL in an energy-only market, and asks the PUCT to clarify whether the standard is a binding requirement or an aspirational benchmark.
