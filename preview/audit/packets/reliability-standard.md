## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e3, e4, e15, e5): **The three criteria.** The frequency criterion is no more than one loss of load event per ten years on average, i.e., 0.1 loss of load expectation (LOLE). [e2] The duration criterion is that the maximum expected length of an event must be under 12 hours, with a 1.00 percent exceedance tolerance. [e3] The magnitude criterion is that the expected highest level of load shed, measured as the average lost load for a given hour, must be below the maximum load that can be safely rotated, as determined by ERCOT with PUCT staff and transmission operators, again with a 1.00 percent exceedance tolerance. [e4] An exceedance tolerance is the maximum acceptable percentage of simulations in which a loss of load event exceeds the threshold for a criterion. [e15] A loss of load event is when firm load plus the minimum operating reserves needed to avoid EEA Level 3 exceeds available capacity, resulting in involuntary load shed. [e5]
- **How it works #2** (cites e16, e30, e29): **The magnitude value is updated yearly.** Beginning in 2024, on or before December 1 each year, ERCOT must file the maximum MW of load shed that can be safely rotated and a summary of its methodology. [e16] ERCOT gets the number by sending a Request for Information to transmission operators on the total MW in each one's load shed rotation program and how many MW can be shed at any one time to facilitate rotation. [e30] ERCOT's November 2026 filing gives the 2026 value as 13,988 MW, based on a systemwide winter 2025-2026 peak load of 77,338 MW. [e29]
- **How it works #3** (cites e6, e17, e18, e40, e19, e20, e8): **The assessment process.** Starting January 1, 2026, ERCOT must assess whether the system meets the standard and is likely to keep meeting it for the following three years, at least once every three years. [e6] Before assessing, ERCOT files proposed modeling assumptions, which must include the number of historic weather years, new resources and retirements in MW by resource type, the weatherization effectiveness, and any other assumptions that would affect results. [e17] PUCT staff gives interested persons at least 30 days to comment on them, and after reviewing comments ERCOT, in consultation with staff, files its final recommended assumptions for Commission review. [e18][e40] The assessment covers two points in time: the current year's configuration and the expected configuration three years from the date of the current year's analysis. [e19] Results must include, at minimum, LOLE, the probability of an event exceeding the duration threshold, the probability of exceeding the magnitude threshold, expected unserved energy and normalized expected unserved energy. [e20] The rule defines weatherization effectiveness, one of the assumptions, as the assumed percentage reduction in weather-related unplanned outages from complying with 25.55. [e8]
- **How it works #4** (cites e7, e23, e13, e21, e22, e14): **If the standard is not met.** ERCOT gives the PUCT a summary of deficiencies and a menu of proposed market design changes with a primary recommendation, plus expected system costs for each. [e7][e23] The IMM must independently review the proposals and costs. [e13] PUCT staff gives at least 30 days for comments on the assessment and then recommends, weighing expected system costs and reliability benefits, whether market design or other changes may be necessary. [e21][e22] The PUCT reviews ERCOT's assessment and recommendations, the IMM review, staff's recommendations and stakeholder comments to decide whether market design changes may be necessary. [e14]
