## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e3, e19, e10, e11, e14): **Registering as a Resource Entity.** Every Resource Entity operating in the ERCOT Region must register with ERCOT. [e2] To register it must execute the Standard Form Market Participant Agreement, name its Authorized Representatives, contacts and a User Security Administrator, and show ERCOT it can perform the functions of a Resource Entity under the Protocols. [e3] ERCOT may reject an application within ten Business Days after it is deemed complete; if it does not, the application is deemed approved. [e19] The Resource Entity also provides Resource Registration data under Planning Guide Section 6.8.2 for each Resource or SOG, except for an Unregistered Distributed Generator. [e10] A Resource Entity may propose to register an aggregation of generators as an Aggregate Generation Resource, which ERCOT may grant at its sole discretion and which excludes Intermittent Renewable Resources; it may also propose a SOG made up of an energy storage system or a mix of storage and non-storage generation. [e11] Distribution-connected generation with installed capacity greater than one MW that exports energy into a Distribution System must register with ERCOT. [e14]
- **How it works #2** (cites e23, e24, e25, e5): **Resource Registration data.** On receipt of registration data ERCOT reviews completeness and accuracy and notifies the Resource Entity of acceptance or deficiencies; acceptance is not an approved Network Operations Model Change Request but only means the data moves to the next step of being converted into one, and the data stays subject to continuous validation. [e23] After acceptance ERCOT gives a model ready date, and a Resource Entity wanting earlier effect than the Protocol Section 3.10.1 timeline may request an interim update. [e24] A Resource Entity must resubmit updated registration data within ten Business Days of an approved Net Dependable Capability or reactive capability test, of an ERCOT request, or of a known change to any registration data. [e25] Before commissioning, Resource Entities regularly update the data necessary for modeling. [e5]
- **How it works #3** (cites e26, e27, e28, e12, e13, e29, e31, e30, e32): **Interconnection and commissioning.** Planning Guide Section 5 applies to any Entity proposing to interconnect a generator of one MW or greater, including any Generation Resource or ESR, and to any Entity proposing to interconnect a SOG. [e26] A generator is "large" if it has or is proposed to have aggregate nameplate capacity of ten MW or greater and "small" below that; the Guide adds exceptions for modifications and Self-Limiting Facilities that are not quoted here. [e27] An Entity initiates a Generator Interconnection or Modification through the RIOO system and pays the Generation Interconnection Fee. [e28] Once ERCOT has the SGIA (or an MOU/EC letter) and determines the project meets Planning Guide Section 6.9, ERCOT reviews the proposed Resource against operational standards and must give its determination to the TSP and owner within 90 days of the date the conditions for review are met, although that determination does not preclude later findings of violation. [e12] An Interconnecting Entity may not proceed to Initial Synchronization if ERCOT has reasonably determined the Resource may violate operating standards and the Resource Entity has not demonstrated compliance, or if a required Quarterly Stability Assessment has not been completed. [e13] For each interconnecting Generation Resource or ESR, the entity must meet ERCOT's conditions before Initial Energization, Initial Synchronization and commercial operations, which may include proof of reactive capability, voltage ride-through, dynamic models, AVR, Primary Frequency Response, PSS, SSR models and telemetry. [e29] A project that will consume Load other than Wholesale Storage Load and is not behind a NOIE tie meter needs ESI IDs established in settlement before Initial Energization. [e31] Within 300 days of ERCOT's approval for Initial Synchronization above 20 MVA of a new or repowered Generation Resource or ESR, the Resource Entity must show it meets commercial-operations conditions and request commissioning, though a good cause exception may be requested. [e30] Within 30 days after the Resource Commissioning Date it submits as-built model updates and a plant verification report. [e32]
- **How it works #4** (cites e1, e8, e15, e17, e18, e20): **QSE representation.** A Resource Entity is the owner/controller role. [e1] A QSE is qualified to communicate with ERCOT for Resource Entities and to settle payments and charges with ERCOT. [e8] Each Resource Entity applicant must designate a QSE and, for a Resource Entity that owns or operates a Resource, the designation must be submitted to ERCOT no later than 45 days before the Network Operations Model change date for the Resource. [e15] A Resource Entity that wants to change QSE may do so by written notice no more than once in any consecutive three days; for an owner or operator of a Resource the designation must reach ERCOT at least 45 days before the effective date unless ERCOT approves otherwise. [e17] For Resources required to be in the Network Operations Model the designated QSE must install the required telemetry and schedule point-to-point data verification. [e18] A Resource Entity that intends to own or control a Load Resource in a NOIE's service territory must designate the NOIE's QSE or an alternate QSE authorized by the NOIE, with the NOIE's written permission. [e20]
- **How it works #5** (cites e9, e4, e16, e6): **When the QSE goes away.** If ERCOT gives notice of a Resource Entity's QSE's termination or suspension, the Resource Entity is designated a Virtual QSE for up to two Bank Business Days, during which it must designate a new QSE or satisfy QSE creditworthiness requirements and operate as an Emergency QSE, except that the Emergency QSE option does not apply to a Resource Entity that is itself the terminated or suspended QSE. [e9][e4] A Resource Entity that fails to maintain a QSE as its representative may be designated an Emergency QSE as provided in Section 16.2.6.1. [e16] If an Emergency QSE that is a Resource Entity cannot meet the requirements in time, ERCOT may give it more time. [e6]
- **How it works #6** (cites e21): **Switchable Generation Resources.** A Resource Entity with a Switchable Generation Resource must report in writing whether any contractual requirement in a non-ERCOT Control Area may make capacity unavailable to ERCOT for the subsequent ten years. [e21]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e22): A Resource Entity that registers a Large Load as a Provisional Controllable Load Resource and wants to de-register it before its end date must give ERCOT at least 120 days' written notice, and upon de-registration the load is limited to the maximum Low Power Consumption amounts in the Batch Zero Interconnection Study and Load Commissioning Plan until new studies are performed. [e22]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e26, e27): - Planning Guide Section 5 applies from one MW; "large" generator means ten MW or more. [e26][e27]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e15): - QSE designation for a Resource must reach ERCOT 45 days before the Network Operations Model change date. [e15]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e12): - ERCOT decision on a proposed Resource: within 90 days of the date the conditions for review are met. [e12]
- **Key facts #6** (cites e30): - Commissioning request within 300 days of Initial Synchronization approval above 20 MVA (good cause exception available). [e30]
- **Say it like this #1** (cites e8): (Guidance) A Resource Entity is the registered owner or controller of the plants, batteries or controllable loads. It registers with ERCOT, takes each Resource through interconnection and commissioning, and relies on a QSE to communicate with ERCOT and settle charges. [e8]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e1, e7, e4, e9, e23): (Guidance) A Resource Entity is the Entity that owns or controls Resources; "Resource" on its own means an ESR, Generation Resource or Load Resource. [e1][e7] A Resource Entity is also not a QSE, although the Virtual and Emergency QSE rules can make it act as one. [e4][e9] Registration with ERCOT as a Resource Entity is not the same as having an approved Network Operations Model Change Request. [e23]
- **Questions #4** (cites e7, e1): The Protocols use "Resource" alone to mean ESR, Generation Resource or Load Resource and exclude SOGs and ERS Resources, but the Resource Entity definition names SOGs among what it owns or controls. [e7][e1]
- **Questions #6** (cites e17, e18): For an owner or operator of a Resource the new QSE designation must reach ERCOT at least 45 days before the effective date unless ERCOT approves otherwise, and the designated QSE must install telemetry and schedule point-to-point verification for Resources in the Network Operations Model. [e17][e18]
- **Questions #8** (cites e30): The Resource may request a good cause exception with sufficient detail, and must notify ERCOT before the planned commercial operation date with an updated date. [e30]
- **Questions #10** (cites e23): No. Acceptance is not an approved Network Operations Model Change Request; it only means the data moves on to be converted into one, and it stays subject to validation. [e23]
- **Scenarios #2** (cites e17): - The Resource Entity's designation of a new QSE for a Resource must reach ERCOT at least 45 days before the effective date unless ERCOT approves otherwise. [e17]
- **Scenarios #3** (cites e38, e9): - Once a QSE has given notice of its intent to terminate, the Resource Entity must by noon on the fourth Business Day after the termination notice date either designate a new QSE or satisfy creditworthiness requirements and operate as an Emergency QSE. [e38] (If ERCOT itself gives notice of the QSE's termination or suspension, the Virtual QSE rule in [e9] applies instead.)
- **Scenarios #4** (cites e18): - The new QSE installs telemetry and schedules data verification for Resources in the Network Operations Model. [e18]
