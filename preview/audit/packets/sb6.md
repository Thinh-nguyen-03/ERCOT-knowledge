## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e11, e2): SB 6 is the 2025 Texas law (89th Legislature) on the planning, interconnection, operation and cost of serving certain large electrical loads. [e1][e11] Its central rule tells the PUCT to set standards for interconnecting large load customers in ERCOT. The standards must support business development while limiting stranded infrastructure costs and maintaining reliability. [e2]
- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e4, e6) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 75 MW: **Interconnection standards (PURA §37.0561).** The PUCT must set a demand threshold of 75 MW unless it finds a lower one is needed. [e3] The standards must include a flat study fee of at least $100,000 for initial transmission screening studies. [e4] The PUCT must also set criteria for how ERCOT includes forecasted large load in its transmission planning and resource adequacy models. [e6]
- **How it works #2** (cites e5, e8): **Emergency curtailment.** Some large loads have on-site backup generation. Once ERCOT has deployed all available market services other than frequency responsive services, it may direct the utility to have such a customer either run its backup generation or curtail. [e5] Each utility serving a transmission-voltage customer must also have a protocol, with any needed equipment, that lets the load be curtailed during firm load shed; this applies only to loads interconnected after December 31, 2025, and not to critical load industrial customers or designated critical natural gas facilities. [e8]
- **How it works #3** (cites e7, e9, e10) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 39.169, 39.170, 75 MW: **Co-location and a new service (PURA §39.169, §39.170).** Before netting a new large load against a generator that was registered as stand-alone on September 1, 2025, the generation owner must notify ERCOT. [e7] ERCOT must also develop a reliability service that competitively procures demand reductions from loads of 75 MW or more, for use in an anticipated emergency. [e9] Separately, Section 6 requires the PUCT to review wholesale transmission cost allocation and amend its rules by December 31, 2026. [e10]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e19, e18, e22) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2026: The December 2025 CDR calls SB 6 the most impactful resource adequacy development since the May 2025 CDR. [e19] ERCOT told the Senate in July 2026 that it and the PUCT are carrying out the directive that large loads added at existing generators must not stop ERCOT from using those generators in emergencies. [e18] ERCOT is also building a Large Load Curtailment Manager, a control-room system whose first phase handles SB 6 net-metered facilities. [e22]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e24, e15, e16, e25, e17, e13, e14, e20): Implementation status differs by piece in the saved sources. The net metering rule (§25.205) carries an effective date of 4/15/2026. [e24][e15] The large load forecasting rule (§25.370) was adopted and effective March 1, 2026. [e16] The interconnection standards (§25.194) carry an effective date of 10/08/2026. [e25][e17] The Large Load Demand Management Service rule was a proposal (new §25.521) when filed July 30, 2026; no adopted version is saved. [e13] The proposal includes a $54 million annual budget cap. [e14] The IMM's 2025 report expected that rulemaking to finish sometime in 2026. [e20]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e3) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 75 MW: - The large load threshold is 75 MW unless the PUCT finds a lower one is needed. [e3]
