## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2): The spark spread is the difference between the price a gas-fired generator receives for its electricity and the cost of the natural gas needed to produce that electricity, and it is used as a measure of the profitability of running a gas-fired plant. [e1][e2]
- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e4, e8): The fuel cost is the gas price multiplied by a heat rate, so the spark spread is the power price minus gas price times heat rate, in $/MWh. [e3][e4] The result is the margin a plant would earn if it bought gas, converted it to power at the stated heat rate and sold the power at the market price. [e4] The heat rate is the plant's conversion rate between gas and power. [e8][e3]
- **How it works #2** (cites e5, e20, e4): Which heat rate is used depends on the purpose. Someone marketing one unit uses that unit's tested heat rate; observers of the whole market use a generic benchmark, and EIA uses 7,000 Btu/kWh, representing a fairly new and efficient combined-cycle unit. [e5] A less efficient unit (higher heat rate) needs more fuel per MWh and so earns a smaller spark spread. [e20][e4]
- **How it works #3** (cites e7, e2, e6, e8): The sources treat other costs differently, and you should say which version you mean. EIA's spark spread leaves out other costs such as pipeline charges, variable O&M, taxes and fixed costs, so it is an indicator of market conditions rather than an exact profit figure for one plant. [e7] The SFU thesis says a spark spread may or may not include fixed or variable O&M depending on context, and separates a gross spread from a net spread that also deducts at least gas transportation and power transmission costs. [e2][e6] The Fields Institute toll formula subtracts VOM (variable operation and maintenance) as well as heat rate times gas. [e8]
- **How it works #4** (cites e10, e16, e15, e19): What matters is the relationship between the two prices, not their absolute level: the relative price of power and gas, and how efficiently the plant converts one into the other, determine whether generating on gas is favourable. [e10][e16] Spark spreads are volatile, mainly because wholesale power prices swing with demand and available supply. [e15][e19]
- **How it works #5** (cites e11, e12, e9): The same idea is applied to other fuels: the dark spread for coal and the quark spread for nuclear fuel. [e11] Because fuels and plant efficiencies differ, spark and dark spreads are specific to a location and a market. [e12][e9]
- **How it works #6** (cites e13, e14): The market-inferred (implied) heat rate is the same calculation turned around: the heat rate at which a plant would earn exactly zero spark spread. [e13] The IMM calculates its implied heat rate as power price divided by fuel price, the heat rate that equates revenue from selling electricity with the cost of buying gas. [e14]
- **Formula #1** (cites e3, e4): Spark spread ($/MWh) = power price ($/MWh) - [natural gas price ($/MMBtu) x heat rate (MMBtu/MWh)]. [e3][e4]
- **Formula #2** (cites e1, e9) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2017: - Power price: the price received for electricity in the same period; EIA typically uses daily spot prices at regional trading points, and its 2017 article used day-ahead wholesale prices. [e1][e9]
- **Formula #3** (cites e9, e1): - Gas price: the cost of the fuel, which EIA describes as the delivered input price, adjusted for energy content. [e9][e1]
- **Formula #4** (cites e8, e5): - Heat rate: the plant's conversion rate between gas and power, in MMBtu per MWh (7,000 Btu/kWh is 7.0 MMBtu/MWh). [e8][e5]
- **Formula #5** (cites e6, e8, e7): - Net versions also subtract at least gas transportation and power transmission costs (SFU), or VOM (Fields); EIA's version subtracts neither. [e6][e8][e7]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e25): ERCOT's own reference gas price, the Fuel Index Price (FIP), is the daily midpoint or average gas price at Katy Hub in $/MMBtu, so a spark spread built on ERCOT's own gas index uses Katy, not Henry Hub. [e25]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e14, e13, e23): The IMM does not publish a spark spread in the reports saved here; it publishes the implied heat rate, which is the zero-spark-spread view of the same two prices. [e14][e13] For 2025 the IMM reported that the average implied heat rate was almost 28% lower than in 2024 and now in line with the marginal heat rate of ERCOT gas plants. [e23] Read with the definition above, a plant whose heat rate equals the implied heat rate would roughly break even on fuel at those prices; this is reasoning from the definitions, not an IMM statement, and an annual average implied heat rate says nothing about the spread in individual hours. [e13][e14]
- **ERCOT context #3** (cites e24): In ERCOT, zero-cost wind and solar create hundreds of hours at or below $0 per MWh, where a gas plant's spark spread is negative, while shortage pricing creates hundreds of hours above gas plants' marginal cost, where their spreads are positive. [e24]
- **ERCOT context #4** (cites e21, e22) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2024: ERCOT metrics also differ on variable O&M.
