## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **How it works #1** (cites e12, e14, e13, e15, e11, e1, e18) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2021: Markets. A FERC staff handbook (tier 2) says that under the Federal Power Act FERC regulates the transmission of electric energy in interstate commerce and the sale of electric energy at wholesale in interstate commerce, and that every public utility must file its rates and charges for such transmission or sales with FERC. [e12] Under FPA sections 205 and 206 FERC ensures those rates, charges and related rules are just and reasonable and not unduly preferential or discriminatory. [e14] FERC has jurisdiction over wholesale electric rates and not retail electric rates, and for wholesale markets it authorizes jurisdictional entities to sell at market-based or cost-based rates. [e13][e15] The same handbook says FERC regulates all RTOs/ISOs except ERCOT. [e11] The 2021 FERC-NERC report's footnote gives the reason: ERCOT's wholesale energy market is not regulated by FERC because the exchange of power occurs wholly within the state and not through interstate commerce. [e1] The report's body text, by contrast, says ISOs and RTOs are regulated by FERC and that ERCOT is an Independent System Operator, with the ERCOT exception in the footnote. [e18]
- **How it works #2** (cites e3, e2, e16, e20) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2021: Reliability. Congress granted FERC jurisdiction over electric grid reliability through the Energy Policy Act of 2005, which added a section to the Federal Power Act. [e3] Using that authority FERC certified NERC as the Electric Reliability Organization responsible for establishing mandatory Reliability Standards, which FERC must approve. [e2] FERC also approved Regional Entities as regional compliance authorities and approved over 100 NERC-proposed mandatory Reliability Standards. [e16] The Regional Entities relevant to the February 2021 event included the Texas Regional Entity. [e20]
- **How it works #3** (cites e9, e7, e8, e10): State law reflects the federal layer. PURA requires each power region to have independent organizations that ensure nondiscriminatory access to the transmission and distribution systems and ensure the reliability and adequacy of the regional network. [e9] The PUCT certifies the independent organization and must apply those provisions so as to avoid conflict with a ruling of a federal regulatory body. [e7] The PUCT may not implement, by order or rule, a requirement contrary to an applicable federal law or rule. [e8] For power regions outside ERCOT, PURA treats the independent-organization requirement as met if FERC has approved a regional transmission organization that meets the statute's requirements. [e10]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e1, e4, e5, e17, e19, e6): The 2021 FERC-NERC staff report states that ERCOT's wholesale energy market is not regulated by FERC. [e1] The same report notes that ERCOT, MISO and SPP all served as Reliability Coordinators in the event area. [e4] FERC and NERC staff joined the Regional Entities in a joint inquiry into the February 2021 outages. [e5] The inquiry's scope excluded market manipulation and market design issues, which the report says FERC's Office of Enforcement and others were examining, and it did not aim to determine whether violations occurred. [e17] The inquiry's final report was issued on November 16, 2021, with 28 formal recommendations, per the FERC staff handbook. [e19] The Protocols also reference FERC rules, for example in the list of references that Administrative NPRRs may correct. [e6]
