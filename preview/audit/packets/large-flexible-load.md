## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1): Under PURA, a large flexible load is a facility operated by a virtual currency mining operator, with an agreement for retail electric service in ERCOT, that requires a total load of more than 75 megawatts and whose load is interruptible. The PUCT must by rule require such a facility to be registered as a large flexible load. [e1]
- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e3, e9): The registration rules must require the operator to register the facility with the commission no later than one business day after the retail agreement begins, and to give the commission the facility's location and its anticipated demand for the five years from registration. [e2] The PUCT must adopt criteria for deciding whether a load is interruptible, based on whether the facility operator can choose to interrupt it. [e3] The statute was added in 2023 by SB 1929. [e9]
- **How it works #2** (cites e4, e5): Separately, the ERCOT Protocols define a Large Load as one or more facilities at a single site with an aggregate peak Demand of at least 75 MW behind one or more common points of interconnection or service delivery points. [e4] A Large Computational Load is a Large Load where 50% or more of site Demand is power electronic based computational load, such as data centers and cryptocurrency mining facilities. [e5]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e7, e8, e6): The IMM's 2025 report says aggregate crypto-currency mining demand was about 4,600 MW in 2025, and that by the end of 2025 such operations accounted for more than half of all ERS capacity. [e7][e8] ERCOT's long-term load forecast treats large flexible loads as one of five major components, next to base economic load, EV load, rooftop PV and large loads. [e6]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e1) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 39.361, 75 MW: - PURA §39.361: virtual currency mining facility over 75 MW with interruptible load must register as an LFL. [e1]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e2): - Registration is due within one business day after the retail agreement begins. [e2]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e4): - The Protocols' Large Load threshold: aggregate peak Demand of at least 75 MW at a site. [e4]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e5): - A Large Computational Load is a Large Load that is at least 50% computational power-electronic load. [e5]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e7): - 2025: crypto-currency mining demand about 4,600 MW. [e7]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e1, e4, e5): (Guidance) "Large Flexible Load" is a PURA registration category for virtual currency mining facilities above 75 MW with interruptible load. [e1] "Large Load" and "Large Computational Load" are the Protocol terms, with a 75 MW site threshold, and they are not limited to flexible or crypto loads. [e4][e5] The saved np-02 does not define "Large Flexible Load".
