## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e3, e7, e25, e28) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2023: The Uri pricing controversy is the dispute over the PUCT's February 15 and 16, 2021 orders in Project 51617, which directed ERCOT to account for firm load shed in its scarcity pricing so that real-time prices reflected the $9,000 per MWh system-wide offer cap during the Winter Storm Uri load shed. [e1][e3] The IMM later questioned how long prices stayed at $9,000, and Luminant challenged the orders in court: the Third Court of Appeals reversed the orders in March 2023, but the Supreme Court of Texas reversed that judgment and rendered judgment affirming the orders on June 14, 2024. [e7][e25][e28]
- **How it works #1** (cites e1, e2, e3, e20, e4, e29): What the PUCT said: on February 15, 2021, the PUCT stated that energy prices were clearing below the $9,000 offer cap, at times as low as about $1,200, while firm load was being shed. [e1] It reasoned that when load is being shed, scarcity is at its maximum and the price should be at its highest. [e2] The first order directed ERCOT to count shed firm load in its scarcity pricing signals and to correct past prices the same way. [e3] It also suspended use of the LCAP and kept the HCAP as the cap until the next open meeting; the order's stated concern was that, with very high gas prices, the "50 times the natural gas price index" formula could exceed the $9,000 HCAP. [e20][e4] On March 3, 2021 the PUCT terminated that suspension and ordered ERCOT to resume applying the LCAP. [e29]
- **How it works #2** (cites e5, e6) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2021: On February 16, 2021, a second order kept the forward-looking direction but rescinded the instruction to correct past prices. [e5] Commissioner Botkin abstained from the part of the second order that rescinded the retroactive correction. [e6]
- **How it works #3** (cites e7, e8, e9, e10, e11): What the IMM said: in its March 4, 2021 letter the IMM said that, to comply with the Commission Order, the pricing intervention should have ended when ERCOT recalled the last firm load shed instructions at 23:55 on February 17, but ERCOT held prices at VOLL for another 32 hours by inflating the reliability deployment price adder. [e7] The IMM put the effect at $16 billion of additional costs to the market, about $1.5 billion of which was uplifted to load-serving entities as make-whole payments, and asked the PUCT to correct prices from 0:00 February 18 to 09:00 February 19, 2021. [e7][e8] Its March 1 letter separately asked that day-ahead ancillary service prices for February 15-20 be capped at $9,000. [e9] Its March 11 letter estimated that, after netting day-ahead positions and offsetting positions at the corporate level, its recommendations would change ERCOT settlements by $5.1 billion in total, and warned that many futures had already settled on the original prices. [e10][e11]
- **How it works #4** (cites e25, e26, e16, e17, e27, e28): What the courts said: on March 17, 2023 the Third Court of Appeals, in Luminant Energy Co. LLC v. PUCT, reversed the February 15 and 16, 2021 orders and remanded, saying the Commission "exceeded its power by eliminating competition entirely". [e25][e26] A Haynes Boone law-firm alert of the same day (a tier 3 source) said the ruling might require resettlement of most Uri hours and that further appeal was expected, which would keep the ruling from taking effect in the meantime; the alert describes Luminant's argument as being that the cap and the orders went against PURA's framework that generation prices be set by competition. [e16][e17] That ruling did not stand. On June 14, 2024 the Supreme Court of Texas disagreed with the court of appeals' holding that the orders exceeded the Commission's authority under PURA Chapter 39, also held that the Commission substantially complied with the Administrative Procedure Act's rulemaking requirements, and reversed the court of appeals and rendered judgment affirming the orders. [e27][e28]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e12, e14, e30): The IMM's 2021 State of the Market report repeats its view: the last firm load shed recall was just before midnight on February 17, but prices stayed at $9,000 through mid-morning on February 19, which raised the value of energy substantially but raised loads' settlement costs much less because of owned generation and supply purchases. [e12] On ancillary services, it says the day-ahead algorithm set prices above $25,000 per MW, overvaluing those services by close to $2 billion and changing net settlements by about $900 million. [e14] On a separate IMM ancillary service recommendation, a PUCT order signed March 12, 2021 directed ERCOT to settle each QSE that failed its ancillary service supply responsibility under Protocol Section 6.4.9.1.3 (failure to provide) for operating days February 14 through February 19, 2021. [e30]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e13, e15, e23, e21, e22) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 25.509, 2026: Rule changes followed. NPRR1081, approved June 28, 2021, wrote the load shed treatment into the reliability deployment price adder so that prices equal VOLL when ERCOT directs firm load shed in EEA3. [e13] The PUCT removed the link between the LCAP and gas prices and lowered the HCAP to $5,000 per MWh from January 1, 2022. [e15] That is no longer the whole picture: PUCT rule 25.509 still states an HCAP of $5,000 per MWh [e23], but ERCOT's September 2026 summer review says the system-wide offer cap was reduced to $2,000/MWh from $5,000/MWh after RTC go-live on 12/5/2025, and the Protocol table lists $5,000 day-ahead and $2,000 real-time. [e21][e22]
