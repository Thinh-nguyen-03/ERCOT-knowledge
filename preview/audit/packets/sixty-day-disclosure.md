## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e3, e6, e7) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 25.506, 3.2: The 60-day disclosures are the entity-specific market data ERCOT posts publicly 60 calendar days after the day the information is accumulated, under PUCT Substantive Rule 25.506 and Protocol Section 3.2.5. [e3][e6][e7]
- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e3, e8, e6, e7): The rule has two tiers. Aggregated information, such as supply curves, actual output and actual load, is posted two calendar days after the day. [e2] Entity-specific information, with resource-specific offer information linked to the resource, submitting entity and controlling entity names, is posted 60 calendar days after. [e3] The Protocols mirror this: SCED-derived aggregate data is posted two days after the Operating Day, and resource-level SCED data and DAM data are posted 60 days prior to the current Operating Day. [e8][e6][e7]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e1, e4, e9, e5): The 60-day lag exists inside a rule written to increase market transparency while never disclosing competitively sensitive consumption data. [e1] The 60-day set includes real-time and ancillary service offer curves, other resource-specific information, and hourly State Estimator data. [e4][e9][e5] Not everything waits: the highest-priced offer selected or dispatched in each interval, with the offering entity's name, is posted after three calendar days. [e4]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e5): ERCOT must release relevant State Estimator data earlier than 60 days if, in its sole discretion, it determines the release is necessary to explain unexpected market operations or system events, including pricing anomalies. [e5]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e2): - Aggregated data: two calendar days after the day. [e2]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e3): - Entity-specific data: 60 calendar days after the day, unless otherwise prescribed. [e3]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e4): - The highest-priced offer in each interval and its entity name: three calendar days. [e4]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e6, e7) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 3.2: - Protocol 3.2.5 requires 60-day postings of SCED resource data and DAM data. [e6][e7]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e3, e6, e2, e4): "60-day" does not mean data arrives 60 days after settlement or invoices; the clock runs from the day the information is accumulated (the Operating Day). [e3][e6] Not every posting waits 60 days. [e2][e4]
- **Questions #2** (cites e2, e3): Two-day postings are aggregated (curves, output, load). The 60-day postings are entity-specific, with names. [e2][e3]
- **Questions #4** (cites e4, e5): Yes: the highest-priced offer each interval (three days), and State Estimator data when ERCOT finds it necessary for explaining unexpected events. [e4][e5]
- **Link to puct** (cites e1, e3) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 25.506: related PUCT (Public Utility Commission of Texas): The disclosure timing comes from PUCT Substantive Rule 25.506. [e1][e3]
- **Link to sced** (cites e6): related SCED (Security-Constrained Economic Dispatch): The Protocols require posting SCED offer and bid data 60 days after the Operating Day. [e6]
- **Link to dam** (cites e7): related Day-Ahead Market (DAM): The Protocols require posting DAM offer data 60 days after the Operating Day. [e7]
- **Link to market-information-system** (cites e1): related MIS (Market Information System) & Public Data: These postings go to a publicly accessible location on the ERCOT website. [e1]
- **Link to imm** (cites e3): related Independent Market Monitor (Potomac Economics): Entity-specific offer data is posted 60 calendar days after the day it is accumulated. [e3]
- **Link to energy-offer-curve** (cites e6): related Energy Offer Curve: Generation Resource Energy Offer Curves are among the items posted at 60 days. [e6]
