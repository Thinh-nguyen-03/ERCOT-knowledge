## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e5, e1): A battery's revenue stack is the mix of income an Energy Storage Resource earns from ancillary services and from energy arbitrage, buying electricity when prices are low and selling it when prices are high. [e5] In ERCOT the stack has shifted: as ESR capacity grew, batteries earned more of their revenue from energy arbitrage than from ancillary services. [e1]
- **How it works #1** (cites e5, e4, e13): Batteries first earned mainly from ancillary services because they respond quickly. As ESRs saturated several AS markets, they began shifting toward energy arbitrage in 2024. [e5] The IMM expects revenue per unit of capacity to fall as more ESRs enter, because AS markets saturate and arbitrage spreads tighten. [e4] Location also matters: the IMM says ESRs can act as both supply and demand, and can often charge at prices below $0 when increasing load at a location resolves congestion. [e13]
- **How it works #2** (cites e9): Under RTC+B, SCED can award energy and AS to an ESR in the same run. Awards are limited by qualification, physical capability, state of charge, ramp rates and AS duration requirements, and one ESR can receive energy and several AS types in the same SCED execution; before, the QSE had to reserve enough state of charge itself. [e9]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e2, e3, e10, e6, e8): 2025 results (IMM): revenue per unit of ESR capacity fell almost 37%, even though total net ESR revenue rose about 8%. [e2] Net energy revenue rose 57% after charging costs, while AS revenue fell more than 37% as added ESR supply pushed AS prices down. [e3] ESRs still provided most regulation: 94% of Reg-Up and 86% of Reg-Down on average in 2025. [e10] Average duration rose from 1.67 to 1.83 hours, which the IMM ties to the shift to arbitrage. [e6] In 2024 total ESR revenue had already fallen versus 2023 because of the supply surplus and fewer extreme heat days. [e8]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e11, e14, e12): Summer 2026 (ERCOT): AS costs were relatively low under real-time co-optimization, and ESR state-of-charge exhaustion in the evenings was a pattern all summer. [e11][e14] ERCOT linked a higher share of ESR capacity offered within $100/MWh of the cap to the lower $2,000/MWh real-time offer cap introduced with RTC+B. [e12]
