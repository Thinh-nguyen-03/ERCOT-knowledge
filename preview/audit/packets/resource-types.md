- **Definition #1** (cites e1): In the Protocols, a "Resource" means an Energy Storage Resource, a Generation Resource or a Load Resource. Used alone, the term does not include a Settlement Only Generator or an Emergency Response Service Resource. [e1]
- **How it works #1** (cites e1): **The four base types.** The Protocols define each as follows. [e1]
- **How it works #2** (cites e3): - A Generation Resource is a generator capable of providing energy or ancillary service and registered as a Generation Resource. [e3]
- **How it works #3** (cites e2): - An ESR is an energy storage system registered to provide energy and/or ancillary service. [e2]
- **How it works #4** (cites e9, e4): - A Load Resource is a load capable of providing ancillary service and/or energy as demand response, registered as a Load Resource. [e9] A Controllable Load Resource is one that can reduce or increase consumption under ERCOT dispatch control. [e4]
- **How it works #5** (cites e8): - A Settlement Only Generator is settled for exported energy only and may not participate in the ancillary services market, RUC, SCED or make energy offers. [e8]
- **How it works #6** (cites e7): Resource Entities own or control these types. [e7]
- **How it works #7** (cites e15, e14, e13, e12): **Connection-point and size splits.** A Transmission Generation Resource is a Generation Resource connected to the ERCOT transmission system that is either greater than ten MW and not registered with the PUCT as a self-generator, or greater than one MW and chooses to register as a Generation Resource to participate in the ERCOT markets. [e15] A Distribution Generation Resource is the same test applied to a Generation Resource connected to the Distribution System. [e14] The Protocols apply the same two-limb test to storage: a Transmission Energy Storage Resource is an ESR on the transmission system, and a Distribution Energy Storage Resource is an ESR on the Distribution System, in each case either greater than ten MW and not a registered PUCT self-generator, or greater than one MW and choosing to register with ERCOT. [e13][e12]
- **How it works #8** (cites e16, e17, e18): **Settlement Only Generator sub-types.** A Settlement Only Distribution Generator is connected to the Distribution System with a rating of one MW or less that chooses to register as an SODG, or greater than one and up to ten MW that can provide a net export to ERCOT and does not register as a DGR. [e16] A Settlement Only Transmission Generator is connected to the ERCOT transmission system, rated ten MW or less and registered with the PUCT as a power generation company; [e17] a Settlement Only Transmission Self-Generator is connected to the transmission system, rated one MW or more and registered with the PUCT as a self-generator. [e18]
- **How it works #9** (cites e19, e31, e32, e21) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2024: **Load Resource variants.** An Aggregate Load Resource is a Controllable Load Resource that aggregates individual metered sites, each with less than ten MW of Demand response capability, all within a single Load Zone. [e19] Qualification as a Load Resource is a prerequisite for providing Demand response in the Ancillary Services markets and Real-Time Energy Market; Attachment O, in its March 1, 2024 version, limits ALR qualification to participation in SCED and Non-Spin. [e31] Premises in an ALR need 15-minute interval meter data, which ERCOT uses for telemetry validation and event performance measurement. [e32] A Provisional Controllable Load Resource is a CLR that must keep its registration and qualification as a CLR until defined exit or de-registration criteria are met. [e21]
- **How it works #10** (cites e22, e23, e24, e25, e26, e28, e27): **Resource attributes and categories.** The Protocols layer attributes on top of the base types, for example: an Aggregate Generation Resource aggregates generators (other than IRRs) each under 20 MW with identical operational characteristics behind the same Main Power Transformer; [e22] an Intermittent Renewable Resource can only produce energy from variable, uncontrollable sources such as wind, solar or run-of-the-river hydro; [e23] an Inverter-Based Resource connects completely or partially through a power electronic converter; [e24] a Switchable Generation Resource can connect to either the ERCOT grid or a non-ERCOT Control Area; [e25] a Quick Start Generation Resource can come on-line within ten minutes from a cold-temperature state and has passed an ERCOT test; [e26] a Split Generation Resource is a Generation Resource split to function as two or more independent Generation Resources. [e28] A Combined Cycle Generation Resource is a specified configuration of gas and steam turbines in a registered Combined Cycle Train. [e27]
- **How it works #11** (cites e29): **Wholesale Storage Load.** Energy separately metered from all other Facilities to charge a technology that can store and later release energy is Wholesale Storage Load, including conversion losses captured by the WSL EPS meter. [e29]
- **How it works #12** (cites e33) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2026: **Resource Nodes.** For a new Generation Resource or ESR, the Resource Entity has five Business Days to accept or reject the Resource Node parameters ERCOT suggests, and the normal timeline for the procedure does not exceed 20 Business Days after submission of validated registration information with a one-line drawing (Attachment R, April 1, 2026 version). [e33]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e5, e6): Distribution-connected generation has its own split: a Distribution Generation Resource is a Generation Resource connected to the Distribution System, while a Settlement Only Distribution Generator is a generator connected to the Distribution System with a defined rating. [e5][e6] The size tests above are the dividing line; see the dgr-sodg topic.
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e10, e11, e20, e30): **Pending changes (not in effect).** NPRR995 would, upon system implementation, add a Settlement Only Energy Storage System to the list excluded from the bare term "Resource" and delete the SOG definition, as the saved note states. [e10][e11] NPRR1188 would, upon system implementation, delete the Aggregate Load Resource definition, per the saved note. [e20] NPRR1029 would insert a "DC-Coupled Resource" definition; in the saved pending text it is a type of ESR combining an ESS with wind and/or solar behind the same inverter(s). [e30]

## Uncited guidance text (check for new facts, numbers, dates, names)

- (Say it like this) (Guidance) ERCOT sorts market assets into Generation Resources, Load Resources and storage that are dispatched, and settlement-only generators that are just paid for exports. Which bucket you sit in decides what you can offer, and the one-MW and ten-MW thresholds decide which bucket small and distribution-connected units fall into.
