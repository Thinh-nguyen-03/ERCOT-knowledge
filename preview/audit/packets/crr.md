## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **How it works #1** (cites e5, e7, e6, e27, e28): **Products.** ERCOT auctions PTP Options, PTP Obligations and Flowgate Rights. [e5] A CRR runs from a designated source Settlement Point to a different sink Settlement Point; the exception is a Flowgate Right, which names a network element instead. [e7] A PTP Option only pays: the holder receives the positive sink-minus-source price difference. [e5][e6] A PTP Obligation can produce either a payment or a charge. [e5] In the auction ERCOT evaluates PTP Options hourly on the positive flows they create on directional network elements (excluding negative flows) and PTP Obligations on both positive and negative flows. [e27] CRRs are auctioned in Time Of Use blocks: 5x16 (hours ending 0700-2200, Monday through Friday excluding NERC holidays), 2x16 (the same hours on weekends and NERC holidays) and 7x8 (hours ending 0100-0600 and 2300-2400, every day), in one-month strips. [e28]
- **How it works #2** (cites e16, e17, e3, e4, e21): **Ways to get one.** CRRs can be acquired in the CRR Auction (ERCOT also lets owners sell CRRs there), through PCRR allocations, bilaterally, and, for PTP Obligations, by bidding in the DAM. [e16][e17][e3] PCRRs are allocated to eligible municipally owned utilities and electric cooperatives. [e4] The auction sells network capacity not allocated as PCRRs or sold in a previous auction. [e21]
- **How it works #3** (cites e8, e9, e18, e24, e47, e48, e25, e26): **Auction schedule and capacity.** The Monthly Auction is held once a month and sells one-month terms for the following month; twice a year a Long-Term Auction Sequence sells six successive auctions of six-month terms. [e8][e9][e18] ERCOT only offers a fraction of network capacity: the CRR Auction Capacity is 90% for the Monthly Auction and 70%, 55%, 40%, 30%, 20% or 10% for the first through sixth six-month windows of a sequence. [e24] The IMM explains the rationale: congestion forecasts get less certain over longer horizons, raising the risk that payments exceed day-ahead congestion rent, so ERCOT sells a smaller share early and more later. [e47][e48] The auction model is the CRR Network Model, based on but not the same as the Network Operations Model, using peak Load conditions of the months modeled. [e25][e26]
- **How it works #4** (cites e29, e30, e31, e32, e33, e34): **Clearing and credit.** The auction is a single-round, simultaneous auction. [e29] The auction system will not award bids and offers beyond the lesser of the account holder's self-imposed limit and the credit limit prescribed in Section 16.11.4.6.1. [e30] The ACL locked for a CRR Auction is the lower of the requested limit, the Counter-Party's available credit limit for the CRR Auction (ACLC) at the close of the CRR bid submission window, and the pre-auction screening exposure. [e31] ERCOT's credit FAQ defines excess collateral as total Financial Security less TPEA, TPES and CRR Locked ACL, and says only collateral received by 3 pm CT on CRR lock days is reflected in the portal for the auction. [e32][e33] A PTP Option Award Charge applies when a PTP Option bid clears below the Minimum PTP Option Bid Price. [e34]
- **How it works #5** (cites e44, e45) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 4.3, 2022: **Pricing.** The ERCOT CRR handbook (version 4.3, June 27, 2022) says a CRR may be used as a financial hedge, which locks in the price of congestion at the CRR's purchase price, or as an investment. [e44] It also says a CRR clearing price equals the shadow price of that CRR product: for Obligations the sum of binding-constraint shadow prices times shift factors, and for Options only the non-negative shift factors. [e45]
- **How it works #6** (cites e11, e12, e19, e20): **DAM settlement.** In the DAM, a PTP Obligation owner is paid or charged the Day-Ahead Settlement Point Price difference between sink and source, and a PTP Option owner is paid that difference if it is positive. [e11][e12] A PTP Obligation payment (for an Obligation with a positive value) or a PTP Option payment can be reduced when the CRR sinks at a Resource Node and directional network elements or Transmission Elements were oversold in earlier CRR Auctions. [e19][e20]
- **How it works #7** (cites e42, e43): **Real-time settlement.** ERCOT pays or charges the QSE of each PTP Obligation acquired in the DAM the real-time Settlement Point Price difference between sink and source, and if ERCOT cannot execute the DAM it pays or charges PTP Obligation owners on the real-time difference. [e42][e43]
- **How it works #8** (cites e35, e39, e38, e40, e41, e36, e37, e62, e63) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 3.2, 7.6: **Funding: the balancing account.** If DAM congestion rent is at least the net amount due to CRR Owners, ERCOT pays them and puts any excess into the CRR Balancing Account; if rent is short, ERCOT short-pays owners pro rata and tracks the amount. [e35] Hourly, a credit is the greater of zero and DAM congestion rent plus the CRR payment and charge totals (as defined in 7.9.3.2). [e39] Shortfall charges fall on CRR Owners' CRRs with positive settlement prices, calculated as the negated minimum of zero and that same sum. [e38][e40] Monthly refunds may be paid to owners with shortfall charges. [e41] Excess first repays short-paid owners; the rest goes to fund the rolling CRR Balancing Account Fund up to a cap of $10 million, and any surplus above the cap is paid to QSEs representing LSEs on monthly Load Ratio Share, the 15-minute LRS for the peak-Load Settlement Interval. [e36][e37] NPRR1030 pending boxes would replace Section 7.6 paragraph (3) and Section 7.9.3.5 paragraph (1) upon system implementation; the text above is the text now in the Protocols, and the pending replacements are not described as the rule in force. [e62][e63]
- **How it works #9** (cites e10, e51, e52, e53): **Auction revenue distribution.** Net auction revenue from CRRs sourcing and sinking in the same 2003 congestion management zone is credited to QSEs in that zone by zonal Load Ratio Share, and all other net auction revenue is allocated by ERCOT-wide Load Ratio Share. [e10] The IMM calls this the CARD process and says only DC tie operators have clearly exhibited the incentive to raise consumption at the peak; it notes the project to implement NPRR 1030, approved in 2020, has not been initiated. [e51][e52][e53]
- **Formula #1** (cites e46, e39, e40): DAM settlement for a CRR (ERCOT handbook, v4.3): hourly revenue to the holder of an Obligation is the MWh amount times the sink LMP minus the source LMP; for an Option the same, but zero if that difference is negative. [e46] The hourly balancing-account credit is the greater of zero and the sum of Day-Ahead Congestion Rent and the CRR payment and charge totals, where the payment total is the sum of the Obligation, Obligation-with-refund, Option and Option-with-refund payment totals and the charge total is the sum of the Obligation and Obligation-with-refund charge totals. [e39] The shortfall total is the negated minimum of zero and that same sum, and each owner's charge is that total times the owner's factor (CRRCRRSDA). [e40]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e8, e18, e9, e47, e23, e22): ERCOT sells CRRs in a monthly auction and in a long-term auction sequence held twice per year, each sequence being six auctions of six-month terms. [e8][e18][e9] The IMM says long-term auctions offer CRRs up to 36 months ahead. [e47] The IMM says NPRR 1288, approved in November 2025, removed multi-month blocks so participants now buy single months only; the saved Protocol text still shows NPRR1288 as a pending box, and the IMM's statement is not reconciled with it. [e23][e22]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e13, e49, e50, e14, e54, e15, e60): The IMM's 2025 numbers: auction revenues rose 6% (versus 14-32% annual growth in 2021-2024), intrazonal CRRs drove the increase and cross-zone CRR revenue fell to its lowest since 2021, with total auction revenue shown as $1,810 million in its table. [e13][e49][e50] CRR profitability rose 21% after a 1.5% net loss in 2024. [e14][e54] Surplus payments to load were $293 million with the balancing account fully funding CRRs every month. [e15] Day-ahead congestion rent in the IMM's table is $2,477 million for 2025. [e60]
- **ERCOT context #3** (cites e55, e61, e56, e57): On funding, the IMM says ERCOT pays less than target only when day-ahead congestion rent is insufficient, usually when flows modeled in the auction exceed those in the DAM after unforeseen outages; oversold flows on a facility reduce payments to CRRs sinking at affected generators, and any remaining shortfall is prorated across positively valued CRRs. [e55][e61][e56] The last CRR short payment occurred in November 2020 per the IMM. [e56] In 2025 mandatory derations still cut payments by $33 million and the funding percentage to about 99%, and the IMM argues derating when revenue is sufficient adds unnecessary risk for buyers. [e57]
