## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **How it works #3** (cites e9, e35): Under PURA, a transmission and distribution utility must provide transmission or distribution service at retail to an electric utility, a REP, a municipally owned utility, an electric cooperative or an end-use customer, on rates, terms and conditions comparable to those applying to the utility and its affiliates. [e9] PURA also requires a utility that owns or operates transmission to provide wholesale transmission service on comparable terms and the PUCT to ensure nondiscriminatory access for qualifying facilities, exempt wholesale generators, power marketers, power generation companies, REPs and other utilities. [e35]
- **How it works #5** (cites e14, e53, e16): Each TSP must file a tariff for transmission service establishing its rates and terms and apply it on a non-discriminatory basis. The tariff applies to all DSPs and to any entity scheduling the export of power from ERCOT, but not to an entity engaging in wholesale storage as described in §25.501(m). [e14] A TSP's transmission cost of service is based on FERC expense accounts 560-573 and plant accounts 350-359 (or accounts with similar contents or amounts functionalized to transmission), plus depreciation, taxes and the commission-allowed return, less accumulated depreciation and deferred taxes. [e53] The rule deems certain facilities to be transmission facilities, including lines, substations and reactive devices operated at 60 kilovolts or above except step-up transformers and a protective device at the generating-station interconnection. [e16]
- **How it works #6** (cites e23, e24, e25, e26, e36): Responsibilities in the transmission service terms rule: the TSP must plan, construct, operate and maintain its system in accordance with good utility practice. [e23] A transmission service customer owning electrical facilities in ERCOT must execute an interconnection agreement with the TSP it is physically interconnected to. [e24] If new facilities are needed, the TSPs must construct or acquire them unless ERCOT identifies a less costly, operationally sound and equally effective alternative. [e25] A TSP may require a reasonable deposit or other security for the costs of planning, licensing and constructing new facilities. [e26] PURA says that when a utility provides wholesale transmission within ERCOT at a third party's request, the PUCT must ensure the utility recovers its reasonable costs from that third party so other customers do not bear them. [e36]
- **How it works #8** (cites e15, e19, e18, e22): DSPs pay transmission charges based on 4CP: see Formula below. [e15] The TCRF is the pass-through. A DSP that is billed for transmission service under §25.192 may include in its tariff a Transmission Cost Recovery Factor clause to charge or credit customers for wholesale transmission cost changes approved by the PUCT, to the extent they vary from the cost used to set base rates, and must update the TCRF twice a year on March 1 and September 1. [e19] This applies to investor-owned DSPs providing service in ERCOT to REPs and other customers of the distribution system. [e18] A TCRF cannot make the DSP recover more than its actual cost of wholesale transmission service included in the TCRF. [e22]

- **Formula #1** (cites e15, e17): Transmission service charge to a DSP: a TSP's annual transmission rate is its commission-approved transmission cost of service divided by the average of ERCOT coincident peak demand for June, July, August and September (4CP), excluding coincident peak demand attributable to wholesale storage load, converted to a monthly rate; each DSP's monthly charge is the TSP's monthly rate times the DSP's previous year's average 4CP demand coincident with the ERCOT 4CP. [e15] The rate stays in effect until the PUCT approves a new one. [e15] ERCOT files each DSP's current-year average 4CP demand with the PUCT no later than December 1, and it is used to bill transmission service for the next year. [e17]
- **Formula #2** (cites e54, e20, e21, e55, e56): TCRF: the TCRF for each class is computed with a formula. As the saved text reads, the expression is {[sum over i of (NWTRi x NLi) minus sum over i of (BWTRi x NLi)] x 1/2 x ALLOC} + ADJ, with BD on the line below it; the layout is garbled, so whether BD divides the whole expression is not stated here. [e54] NWTRi is the new wholesale transmission rate of a TSP approved since the DSP's last rate case; BWTRi is the base wholesale transmission rate used to develop the DSP's retail transmission charges in its last rate case; NLi is the DSP's 4CP load component used to develop NWTRi. [e20] ALLOC is the class allocator approved in the DSP's last rate case, unless the PUCT orders otherwise. [e21] ADJ is the adjustment to the Rate Class TCRF, built from transmission expenses not included in base rates (EXPp) and TCRF revenue (REVp) by period. [e55] BD is each class's billing determinant (kWh, kW or kVA) for the previous March 1 through August 31 period for the March 1 update and the previous September 1 through February 28 period for the September 1 update. [e56]
