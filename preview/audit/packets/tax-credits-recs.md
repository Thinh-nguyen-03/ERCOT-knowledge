## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e12, e1, e14, e2, e4, e7): This topic covers two separate things. The federal clean electricity credits are the production credit in 26 USC 45Y and the investment credit in 48E, which Public Law 119-21 (July 4, 2025) ends for wind and solar facilities placed in service after December 31, 2027, where construction begins more than 12 months after enactment. [e12][e1][e14][e2][e4] A Texas renewable energy credit (REC) is a tradable instrument representing all the renewable attributes of one MWh from a certified renewable generator, and it can trade separately from energy. [e7]
- **How it works #1** (cites e1, e14, e2, e4, e12, e3, e5): Federal credits: for wind and solar "applicable facilities", 45Y does not apply to a facility placed in service after December 31, 2027, and 48E does not apply to qualified property placed in service after that date. [e1][e14][e2] These terminations apply only to facilities whose construction begins more than 12 months after the law's enactment. [e4][e12] The 48E termination does not apply to energy storage technology placed in service at a wind or solar facility. [e3] Separately, facilities whose construction begins after December 31, 2025 lose 45Y eligibility if construction includes material assistance from a prohibited foreign entity. [e5]
- **How it works #2** (cites e6, e7, e11) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 39.9113: RECs: PURA 39.9113 has the independent organization certified for the ERCOT region keep an accreditation and banking system that awards and tracks voluntary RECs. [e6] ERCOT distributes RECs to REC generators quarterly, they may trade separately from energy, and each has a useful life of three compliance periods. [e7][e11]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e10): The credits affect ERCOT prices through offer behavior. The IMM says 35% of real-time generation capacity was offered at or below $0 in 2025 (32% in 2024), mostly from wind and solar resources that keep producing at negative prices because many receive federal production tax credits. [e10]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e9, e13, e8, e6) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 39.9113: The Texas REC market is now voluntary. HB 1500 repealed PURA 39.904, which held the state's goal for renewable generating capacity. [e9][e13] The REC Trading Program, effective July 1, 2001, became a voluntary market on September 1, 2025, and PURA 39.9113 has the ERCOT-region independent organization track voluntary RECs. [e8][e6]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e1, e14, e2): - 45Y and 48E: no credit for wind or solar placed in service after December 31, 2027 (Public Law 119-21), subject to the construction-start rule in the next bullet. [e1][e14][e2]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e4, e12): - The termination covers facilities that begin construction more than 12 months after enactment (enacted July 4, 2025). [e4][e12]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e3): - Storage placed at a wind or solar facility is excepted from the 48E termination. [e3]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e8): - Texas REC program became voluntary on September 1, 2025. [e8]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e10): - 2025: 35% of real-time capacity offered at or below $0, mainly tax-credit-supported wind and solar. [e10]
- **Say it like this #1** (cites e1, e4, e3, e8): (Guidance) The 2025 federal law ends the 45Y and 48E clean electricity credits for wind and solar placed in service after 2027, but projects that started construction within a year of July 2025 are outside that cutoff, and storage at those sites is excepted from the 48E cutoff. In Texas the REC market is now voluntary and run by ERCOT, so RECs back voluntary renewable claims rather than a state mandate. [e1][e4][e3][e8]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e1, e7, e4, e3): (Guidance) A tax credit and a REC are different: the credit is a federal tax benefit tied to production or investment, while a REC is a Texas certificate for one MWh of renewable attributes that trades apart from the energy. [e1][e7] The 2027 date is a placed-in-service cutoff, but it only bites for projects that begin construction after the 12-month window. [e4] The storage exception is in 48E (the investment credit) as amended. [e3]
- **Questions #2** (cites e1, e14, e2, e4, e5): It ended 45Y and 48E for wind and solar facilities placed in service after December 31, 2027, for facilities whose construction begins more than 12 months after enactment. [e1][e14][e2][e4] It also added a prohibited-foreign-entity material assistance test for facilities beginning construction after 2025. [e5]
- **Questions #4** (cites e10): Production-based credits make wind and solar willing to run when prices are below zero. The IMM ties most of the 35% of real-time capacity offered at or below $0 in 2025 to such resources. [e10]
