## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2): The Emergency Pricing Program (EPP) is a PUCT-rule mechanism that ERCOT must activate if the system-wide energy price has been at the high system-wide offer cap (HCAP) for 12 hours within a rolling 24-hour period. [e1] While it is active, the system-wide offer cap is set to the Emergency Offer Cap (ECAP) for both energy and ancillary service offers, and the ECAP equals the LCAP. [e2]
- **How it works #1** (cites e8, e10): In the protocols the test uses the sum of the real-time system lambda and the real-time reliability deployment price adder for energy, compared with the day-ahead HCAP over 12 hours in a rolling 24-hour period. [e8] If met, ERCOT activates the EPP and sets the day-ahead cap and value of lost load to the ECAP from the next Operating Hour it can implement. [e8] The protocols state that the period ends at the next Operating Hour after the later of those two 24-hour conditions, measured against EEA conditions. [e10]
- **How it works #2** (cites e3, e4, e6): The EPP lasts until the later of 24 hours after activation or, if ERCOT entered or stayed in emergency operations, 24 hours after ERCOT exits emergency operations without re-entering. [e3] While it is active, ERCOT must reimburse resource entities for actual marginal costs above the larger of the ECAP or their real-time energy price. [e4] For costs above the HCAP, the resource entity must submit a reimbursement request in the manner ERCOT prescribes. [e6]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e7, e12, e5, e9) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2021: The program comes from SB 3 (2021), which told the commission to establish an emergency pricing program by rule and to have it take effect when the HCAP has been in effect for 12 hours in a 24-hour period. [e7][e12] Within 10 working days of termination ERCOT must file an initial report with the commission. [e5] The rule's transition provision says that, until any system and protocol changes are complete, ERCOT may use a manual activation process and consider the real-time energy price, exclusive of any congestion. [e9]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e11): The PUCT will review each system-wide offer cap program starting January 1, 2026, and every five years after. [e11]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e1): - Trigger: system-wide energy price at the HCAP for 12 hours within a rolling 24-hour period. [e1]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e2): - While active, the cap is the ECAP, equal to the LCAP value. [e2]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e3): - Duration: the later of 24 hours after activation or, if ERCOT entered or remained in emergency operations while the EPP was active, 24 hours after ERCOT exits emergency operations without re-entering. [e3]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e4, e6, e13): - Costs above the ECAP are reimbursed and allocated on a load ratio share basis. [e4][e6][e13]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e5): - ERCOT must file an initial report within 10 working days after termination. [e5]

## Uncited guidance text (check for new facts, numbers, dates, names)

- (Say it like this) If prices sit at the high cap for 12 hours in any rolling day, ERCOT must lower the cap to the lower level for at least 24 hours, and generators whose real costs are above that can claim reimbursement. It is a circuit breaker on a prolonged price spike, not a yearly cap.
