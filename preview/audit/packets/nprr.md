## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1): A Nodal Protocol Revision Request (NPRR) is a request to make additions, edits, deletions, revisions or clarifications to the ERCOT Protocols, including their attachments and exhibits. [e1]
- **How it works #1** (cites e2, e15, e11, e3, e4): Any Market Participant or ERCOT Member can file one, as can PUCT Staff, the Reliability Monitor, the NERC Regional Entity, the IMM and ERCOT itself. [e2] Any other Entity that resides (or represents residents) in Texas or operates in the Texas electricity market, and is affected by the Customer Registration or REC/EAC program sections, can also file. [e15] NPRRs are submitted electronically by completing the designated form on the ERCOT website, and ERCOT has five Business Days to tell the submitter if the NPRR is incomplete. [e11][e3] Once it is complete, comments must be delivered within 14 days of posting to receive consideration, and the Protocol Revision Subcommittee then considers it. [e4]
- **How it works #2** (cites e13, e14, e6, e7): After PRS, TAC considers a PRS Report, the ERCOT Board reviews the TAC Report and Impact Analysis at its next regularly scheduled meeting, and then PUCT approval is required. [e13][e14][e6] ERCOT implements an approved NPRR on the first day of the month after PUCT approval, unless the PUCT Report says otherwise. [e7]
- **How it works #3** (cites e5, e10, e12, e8): Two shortcuts exist. A submitter can ask for an urgent timeline only if it can reasonably show that an existing Protocol or condition is impairing or could imminently impair reliability or market operations, or is causing or could cause a settlement-formula discrepancy. [e5] And the ERCOT Board may designate any existing request a Board Priority request. [e10] An Administrative NPRR consists of non-substantive corrections such as typos and internal references. [e12] If no Entity comments within ten Business Days, it becomes subject to PUCT approval. [e8]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e9): Not every change is an NPRR: a request to change ERCOT's computer systems without changing the Protocols is a System Change Request (SCR). [e9]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e2, e15) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 21.2: - Who can file: any Market Participant, ERCOT Member, PUCT Staff, the Reliability Monitor, the NERC Regional Entity, the IMM, ERCOT, or another Entity meeting the Section 21.2(1)(h) qualifications. [e2][e15]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e3): - ERCOT tells the submitter within five Business Days if an NPRR is incomplete. [e3]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e4): - To receive consideration, comments must be delivered within 14 days of posting. [e4]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e6): - All revision requests need PUCT approval before implementation. [e6]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e7): - Implementation is on the first day of the month after PUCT approval, unless the PUCT Report says otherwise. [e7]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e1, e9, e6, e5): (Guidance) An NPRR changes the Protocols; an SCR changes ERCOT's systems without changing the Protocols. [e1][e9] Board approval is not the end: PUCT approval is still required. - (Say it like this) (Guidance) An NPRR is the formal route for changing the ERCOT rulebook. It runs through the PRS and TAC committees and the ERCOT Board, and nothing takes effect until the PUCT approves it.
