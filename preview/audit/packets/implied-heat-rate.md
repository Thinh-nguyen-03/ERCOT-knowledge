- **Link to ordc** (cites e4, e9): related ORDC (Operating Reserve Demand Curve): The IMM says shortage pricing raises the implied heat rate; the ORDC adder was discontinued in December 2025 with the introduction of RTC. [e4][e9]
- **Link to state-of-market-report** (cites e1, e2): related IMM State of the Market Report: The implied heat rate is published in the IMM State of the Market reports. [e1][e2]
- **Link to spark-spread** (cites e20): related Spark Spread: EIA writes the spark spread as power price minus natural gas price times heat rate, so the implied heat rate is the heat rate at which that spread is zero. [e20]

- **Link to merit-order** (cites e15): related Merit Order / Supply Stack: The IMM ties the rise of the implied heat rate with load to resources with higher marginal costs being dispatched as load approaches peak. [e15]
- **Link to evening-ramp** (cites e8, e23): related Evening Ramp / Duck Curve: The IMM's price-spike threshold is an implied heat rate of 18, and most 2025 spikes fell in the evening net load peak. [e8][e23]

## Uncited guidance text (check for new facts, numbers, dates, names)

- (Say it like this) (Guidance) The implied heat rate is the power price expressed in gas terms, so it shows whether power is expensive or cheap relative to the fuel behind it. A reading above what gas plants actually need can point to scarcity pricing rather than fuel costs.
