## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1): Wind is an Intermittent Renewable Resource: in the Protocols, a Generation Resource that can only produce energy from variable, uncontrollable sources such as wind, solar or run-of-the-river hydroelectricity. [e1]
- **How it works #1** (cites e7, e4, e5): Wind output is described with the capacity factor, the ratio of a resource's energy output to its maximum capability. [e7] The IMM reports it three ways: overall, during daily peak load intervals and during the daily net peak interval. [e4] Wind's overall capacity factor was 31.6% in 2025, with 28.5% at daily peak load and 29.3% at the daily net peak. [e5][e4]
- **How it works #2** (cites e4, e5, e6) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2023: Wind is less aligned with afternoon peak load than solar, but it tends to ramp up in the evening, so its capacity factor at the net peak has held up while solar's has collapsed. [e4][e5] ERCOT's 2023 review found that evening wind ramp-up was key to serving demand on most days, and low wind was a primary factor on the days ERCOT asked for conservation. [e6]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e2, e12, e3, e11): Growth has slowed: wind generation grew 2.9% in 2025 and 3.9% in 2024, with relatively low capacity growth since 2022. [e2][e12] Because wind is concentrated where the resource is best, curtailment matters: 4.7% of 2025 wind generation was curtailed. [e3] In 2025 the average captured price for solar ($21 per MWh) was nearly at parity with wind ($19 per MWh), after years in which solar captured a premium. [e11]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e8, e10): Wind is also the main source of renewable forecast uncertainty. The IMM says wind dominates total IRR forecast error variance because of its higher volatility and lower predictability. [e8] In ERCOT's winter risk modeling, wind variability is the greatest risk factor for declaring an EEA in January. [e10]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e5, e4): - 2025 capacity factors: 31.6% overall, 28.5% at daily peak load, 29.3% at daily net peak (IMM). [e5][e4]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e2): - 2025 wind generation growth was 2.9%; 4.7% of wind generation was curtailed. [e2]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e12): - 2024 wind generation growth was 3.9% with more than 4% curtailed for congestion. [e12]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e11) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2025: - 2025 captured price: wind about $19 per MWh versus solar about $21 per MWh. [e11]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e9) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2021: - Winter 2021: generating unit outages in the week of February 7 were primarily caused by wind turbine blade freezing and natural gas fuel supply issues. [e9]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e4, e10): (Guidance) A high overall capacity factor does not mean high output at the system's critical hour. Look at the capacity factor at the relevant interval: peak load, net peak, or the January EEA risk. [e4][e10]

## Uncited guidance text (check for new facts, numbers, dates, names)

- (Say it like this) (Guidance) Wind in ERCOT is a lot of energy but not much of it lines up with the afternoon peak; it does show up in the evening, which is why its capacity factor at the net peak is higher than solar's. Its volatility, congestion-driven curtailment and forecast error are the things to model.
