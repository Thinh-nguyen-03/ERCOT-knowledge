## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2): Firm Fuel Supply Service (FFSS) is a service provided by certain Generation Resources to maintain Resource availability in the event of a natural gas curtailment or other fuel supply disruption. [e1] A resource procured for the service is obligated to provide it from November 15 through March 15. [e2]
- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e4, e5): ERCOT procures FFSS ahead of each obligation period, with a maximum of $54 million in procurement costs for a single obligation period, and at least 70% of that budget goes to the on-site and resource-controlled categories unless offers fall short. [e3][e4] The protocol sets deployment hours at 48 hours per deployment. [e5]
- **How it works #2** (cites e6, e7): Deployment is by ERCOT approval. When ERCOT issues a Watch for winter weather it tells market participants, including QSEs with FFSS resources, to begin preparation. [e6] If a gas curtailment or other fuel disruption is anticipated or happens, the QSE notifies ERCOT and asks to deploy; the QSE may not deploy FFSS unless ERCOT approves. [e7]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e8, e11, e12, e9) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 39.159: The IMM says FFSS was implemented in 2022 in response to fuel supply disruptions during Winter Storm Uri. [e8] SB 3 separately directs the PUC to ensure that winter resource capability qualifications for a service described in PURA 39.159(b) include on-site fuel storage, dual fuel capability or fuel supply arrangements. [e11] In the 2024-2025 contract period ERCOT procured 4,195 MW; the offer cap was $12,240 per MW, built from a fuel oil price of $17 per MMBtu, a heat rate of 15 MMBtu per MWh and 48 hours. [e12] FFSS uplift rose from $33.8 million in 2024 to $45.9 million in 2025. [e9]
- **ERCOT context #2** (cites e14, e10, e15, e13): The IMM is critical of deployment practice. It says the criteria for deployment beyond a winter weather watch remain unclear and are left to operator discretion, and (in its 2024 report) that deployed FFSS resources were removed from the online reserves used for the ORDC adder, which raised scarcity prices even when physical capacity remained. [e14][e10] The ORDC adder was discontinued in December 2025 with RTC, so that specific effect is historical. [e15] In 2025 ERCOT deployed FFSS over three consecutive days in February, with four resources deployed for a maximum of 470 MW. [e13]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e2): - Obligation period is November 15 through March 15. [e2]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e3): - Budget: a maximum of $54 million in procurement costs per obligation period. [e3]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e5): - Deployment hours: 48 hours per deployment. [e5]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e12): - 2024-2025 procurement: 4,195 MW; offer cap $12,240 per MW. [e12]
- **Key facts #5** (cites e9): - FFSS uplift: $45.9 million in 2025, up from $33.8 million in 2024. [e9]
- **Say it like this #1** (cites e14, e10, e15): FFSS is a winter insurance payment: ERCOT pays selected generators to hold backup fuel so they can keep running if gas is curtailed. The IMM's complaint is that deployment criteria are vague, and before RTC it said deployment could also push up scarcity prices through the ORDC adder. [e14][e10][e15]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e2, e7, e1): FFSS pays for fuel readiness in a fixed winter window and is deployed only with ERCOT approval, so it is a paid, contracted service and not the same thing as the weatherization rules (see that topic). [e2][e7] It is also not ERS: ERS is a separate emergency service (see the ers topic), whereas FFSS is provided by Generation Resources to maintain availability in a fuel disruption. [e1]
- **Questions #2** (cites e2, e3): The obligation period runs from November 15 through March 15, and ERCOT may spend a maximum of $54 million in procurement costs per period. [e2][e3]
- **Questions #4** (cites e14, e10, e15): The IMM says deployment criteria beyond a winter weather watch are unclear and left to operator discretion, and, in its 2024 report, deploying FFSS resources removed their capacity from the online reserves used for the ORDC adder, which raised scarcity prices. That adder has since been discontinued with RTC. [e14][e10][e15]
- **Questions #6** (cites e13, e14): According to the IMM, ERCOT deployed it over three consecutive days in February 2025 with four resources for a maximum of 470 MW, and over five consecutive days in January 2024 during a winter weather watch. [e13][e14]
