- **How it works #1** (cites e24, e25, e3, e4, e26): **Procurement calendar.** ERCOT issues an RFP by August 1 each year, offers are due on or before September 1, and awards are issued by September 30. [e24][e25] ERCOT procures FFSS ahead of each obligation period, with a maximum of $54 million in procurement costs for a single obligation period, and at least 70% of that budget goes to the on-site and resource-controlled categories unless offers fall short. [e3][e4] An offer is an offer to supply the awarded capacity, hold enough reserved fuel for at least one deployment, and reserve emissions hours for at least three deployments in the obligation period. [e26]
- **How it works #2** (cites e19, e20, e21, e22, e61) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2026: **Three categories.** The PUCT rule, shown in the saved copy as effective 2/25/2026, defines three ways to qualify. On-site FFSS needs demonstrated dual fuel capability, the ability to burn alternative on-site stored fuel, and on-site fuel storage. [e19] Resource-controlled FFSS needs on-site gas or fuel oil storage, or off-site gas storage where the resource or QSE owns and controls both the storage facility and the pipeline delivering to the resource. [e20] Contractual off-site FFSS needs a firm gas storage agreement and a firm transportation agreement with a pipeline that is a critical natural gas facility. [e21] The Protocols add that a resource may take part under only one category. [e22] The IMM says the contractual off-site gas expansion was added in July 2023. [e61]
- **How it works #3** (cites e23, e69, e35): **Qualification and testing.** Every year a QSE must show each offered primary and alternate resource can use its reserved fuel and sustain output for 60 minutes at the MW level it wants to qualify, and the most that can be offered is the average Real-Time net real power telemetered during that demonstration. [e23] ERCOT's guidance says a previous deployment can be used if it falls in the one-year testing period, with an August 15 deadline to inform ERCOT. [e69] An offer form may name up to three alternate resources per primary resource. [e35]
- **How it works #4** (cites e27, e5, e28, e29, e12): **Offer cap.** ERCOT sets an administrative offer cap for each category before each obligation period. In the Protocols it equals deployment hours times the projected fuel price times the category heat rate. [e27] Deployment hours are set at 48 hours per deployment. [e5] The projected fuel price is a projected fuel oil index price plus $0.05 per gallon for on-site resources, and a projected natural gas index price plus $0.5/MMBtu for contractual off-site resources. [e28][e29] The 2024-2025 cap of $12,240 per MW came from a $17 per MMBtu fuel oil index, a heat rate of 15 MMBtu per MWh and 48 hours. [e12]
- **How it works #5** (cites e30): **Clearing.** ERCOT must run one market clearing price mechanism for the on-site and resource-controlled categories and a separate one for the contractual off-site category. [e30]
- **How it works #6** (cites e6, e7, e31, e32, e33, e70, e38): **Deployment.** When ERCOT issues a Watch for winter weather it tells market participants, including QSEs with FFSS resources, to begin preparation. [e6] If a gas curtailment or other fuel disruption is anticipated or happens, the QSE notifies ERCOT and asks to deploy; the QSE may not deploy FFSS unless ERCOT approves. [e7] ERCOT may also issue the instruction without a QSE request, but not without evidence of an impending or actual fuel supply disruption affecting the resource. [e31] A deployed resource keeps generating until the earlier of exhausting its reserved fuel (including approved restocked fuel), the disruption ending, or ERCOT deciding the deployment is no longer needed. [e32] The QSE must tell the control room at least six hours before it expects to exhaust reserved fuel and again on exhaustion. [e33] ERCOT's guidance adds that a deployed resource should telemeter on average an HSL at or above the awarded FFSS MW. [e70] If an FFSS instruction and a RUC instruction cover the same Operating Hour, the RUC instruction is treated as cancelled for Settlement. [e38]
- **How it works #7** (cites e34, e41, e42): **Restocking and fuel cost recovery.** After final approval from ERCOT, the QSE must restock reserved fuel from existing inventory or new purchases. [e34] The Fuel Replacement Cost covers only replacement fuel costs not already recovered through Day-Ahead energy sales and Real-Time energy imbalance revenues from that resource. [e41] If the fuel is replaced from existing inventory, the cost may be based on new purchases or on the Fuel Oil Index Price for the approval day, which includes $0.05 per gallon for transportation. [e42]
- **How it works #8** (cites e36, e51, e50, e52, e53): **Payment and charges.** ERCOT pays the hourly standby fee to the QSE of the primary resource, set through competitive bidding and adjusted for reliability, capacity and deployment. [e36] The PUCT rule has ERCOT charge each LSE for procurement costs (the standby payments) by load ratio share during the obligation period, and allows non-procurement costs (fuel restocking payments) to be charged by load ratio share during the deployment. [e51][e50][e52][e53]

- **Say it like this #1** (cites e43, e14, e56): FFSS is a winter insurance payment: ERCOT pays selected generators a standby fee to hold backup fuel so they can keep running if gas is curtailed, and the fee is clawed back if they fail to show up. The IMM's complaint is that deployment criteria are vague and that deployments have been called when prices and reserves showed no scarcity. [e43][e14][e56]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e2, e7, e1, e36, e41): FFSS pays for fuel readiness in a fixed winter window and is deployed only with ERCOT approval, so it is a paid, contracted service and not the same thing as the weatherization rules (see that topic). [e2][e7] It is also not ERS: ERS is a separate emergency service (see the ers topic), whereas FFSS is provided by Generation Resources to maintain availability in a fuel disruption. [e1] The standby fee (paid for being ready) and the fuel replacement cost (paid after a deployment burns fuel) are separate payments. [e36][e41]
- **Questions #4** (cites e27, e5, e28, e29): Deployment hours times projected fuel price times category heat rate. The hours are 48 per deployment, the fuel price is a projected oil index price (on-site and resource-controlled) or a projected gas index price (contractual off-site) plus the adders in the Protocols. [e27][e5][e28][e29]
- **Questions #8** (cites e13, e57, e14): According to the IMM, ERCOT deployed it over three consecutive days in February 2025 with four resources for a maximum of 470 MW, and over five consecutive days in January 2024 during a winter weather watch. [e13][e57][e14]
- **Questions #10** (cites e43, e45, e46, e47): The standby fee pays for availability, so ERCOT claws it back if a resource is unavailable during a winter weather Watch or underperforms in a deployment: 90 days for fuel-related failures and 15 days for non-fuel-related failures, with no clawback if a transmission outage or limitation prevented deployment to LSL. [e43][e45][e46][e47]
- **Questions #12** (cites e32, e33, e43): The deployment ends for that resource, the QSE tells the control room at least six hours ahead, and an exhausted resource is not penalised for unavailability during the Watch because the clawback rule excepts resources that exhausted reserved fuel. [e32][e33][e43]
