## Statements to check (new or changed since the last audit)

- **Definition #1** (cites e1, e2, e40) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2024: The ORDC was ERCOT's pre-RTC way of pricing reserve scarcity. It linked the level of operating reserves to the probability of load shed and was scaled to the Value of Lost Load (VOLL). [e1] Until RTC went live on December 5, 2025, ERCOT added an ORDC price adder to energy and reserve prices when reserves were low. [e2] ERCOT's 2024 report calls it a market-based construct for valuing operating reserves according to their scarcity, first described by Dr. William Hogan in PUCT Project No. 40000. [e40]
- **How it works #1** (cites e5, e4) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2025, 2024: This section describes the design before December 5, 2025. Day-ahead procurement covered the full AS Plan, and the real-time market could not use those reserves unless they were manually released to dispatch. The ORDC then set shortage pricing outside the dispatch model, based on how many operating reserves were available, as the IMM's 2024 report summarized it. [e5] ERCOT described this as reflecting scarcity "through a process that is outside of the optimization". [e4]
- **How it works #2** (cites e18, e19): Two adders. From summer 2014 ERCOT computed separate adders in the five-minute real-time market: the Real-Time On-Line Reserve Price Adder (RTORPA), tied to on-line capacity (RTOLCAP, reserves available within 30 minutes), and the Real-Time Off-Line Reserve Price Adder (RTOFFPA), tied to off-line capacity (RTOFFCAP, reserves needing 30 minutes to an hour). [e18] Both were computed from VOLL, which was set equal to the system-wide offer cap, and from PBMCL, the probability of reserves falling below the minimum contingency level (MCL). [e19]
- **How it works #3** (cites e22, e21, e23): Where the probability came from. ERCOT says net load forecast error and other factors make the reserves available over the next 30 minutes to one hour uncertain. [e22] The loss-of-load probability (LOLP) was estimated by comparing hour-ahead forecast reserves with reserves realized in real time; PBMCL is that LOLP distribution shifted by the MCL. [e21] The on-line adder had an extra term for the first half of the hour, using a shortened curve with Mu scaled by one-half and Sigma by 0.707. [e23]
- **How it works #4** (cites e20, e24, e25): Price ceiling. The adder was built on VOLL minus System Lambda, so that the final energy price would not exceed VOLL. [e20][e24] ERCOT notes that the Protocols limited System Lambda plus all adders to the SWCAP, so RTORPA could reach the SWCAP only when System Lambda was zero. [e25]
- **Formula #1** (cites e20, e24, e23, e31) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2016, 0.5, $9,000: Pre-RTC adder (2016 methodology OBD, as described in prose): for each SCED run the ORDC is PBMCL multiplied by (VOLL − System Lambda). [e20] Here v = VOLL − System Lambda is "the net value of Load curtailment". [e24] RTORPA adds to the off-line term an extra term for the first 30 minutes, computed with Mu × 0.5 and Sigma × 0.707. [e23] Parameters in the 2016 methodology: VOLL $9,000/MWh and minimum contingency level X of 2,000 MW. [e31]
- **Formula #2** (cites e14, e15, e17) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 4.4: Post-RTC reconstruction (np-04 §4.4.12): the current protocols compute "historical reserve pricing outcomes" for the AORDC regression with the expression below, where RTOLCAP and RTOFFCAP are the real-time on-line and off-line reserve capacities for SCED intervals from June 1, 2014 through August 31, 2025, and μ and σ are the mean and standard deviation of the shifted LOLP distribution published for Summer 2026. [e14][e15][e17]
- **Formula #4** (cites e27, e23, e15, e24) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 2024, 2016: The 3,000 is the MCL in MW since 2022, and the 0.5 and 0.707 scaling matches the on-line term described in ERCOT's 2024 report. [e3] Under RTC, the ORDC price adder was replaced by a set of ASDCs. [e11] ERCOT told the PUCT in September 2026 that the ASDCs were designed to replicate the ORDC's pricing outcomes under a prior Commission directive, and that at its August 20, 2026 open meeting the Commission agreed the ASDC design no longer has to follow that directive.
