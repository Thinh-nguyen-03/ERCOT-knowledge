## Statements to check

- **Definition #1** (cites e10, e1): The Texas Legislature enacts the statutes that govern the ERCOT market, including the Public Utility Regulatory Act (PURA). [e10] In PURA it found that electric services and their prices should be determined by customer choices and the normal forces of competition, except for transmission and distribution services and stranded-cost recovery. [e1]
- **How it works #1** (cites e3, e4, e5): Legislation sets the structure around ERCOT. PURA creates the ERCOT board selection committee, with one appointee each from the governor, the lieutenant governor and the speaker of the house. [e3] It requires that new or revised protocols not take effect until the PUCT approves a market impact statement. [e4] The PUCT is itself subject to the Texas Sunset Act, and HB 1500 sets September 1, 2029 as the date in the abolition provision. [e5]
- **How it works #2** (cites e2, e9): The Legislature also asks for information back. Each year by December 1 the PUCT must file a report with the legislature that includes estimated annual costs to load-serving entities of backing up dispatchable and non-dispatchable generation. [e2] The market monitor may appear and speak before the legislature without PUCT restriction. [e9]
- **ERCOT context #1** (cites e6, e7, e8, e11, e10): Recent sessions have written market and reliability design directly into PURA. SB 6 (89th Legislature, 2025) added the Large Load Demand Management Service, which directs the PUCT to require ERCOT to develop a reliability service that competitively procures demand reductions from large loads of at least 75 megawatts in anticipated emergencies. [e6][e7] An earlier 2023 law, SB 1929, led to large flexible load registration, covering virtual currency mining facilities of more than 75 megawatts with interruptible load. [e8][e11] HB 1500 (88th Legislature, 2023) added PURA §39.1514 on PUCT directives to ERCOT. [e10]
- **Key facts #1** (cites e1): - PURA states a policy preference for customer choice and competition, except for transmission and distribution and stranded-cost recovery. [e1]
- **Key facts #2** (cites e3): - ERCOT board selection committee: one appointee each from the governor, lieutenant governor and house speaker. [e3]
- **Key facts #3** (cites e5): - HB 1500 (2023) sets September 1, 2029 as the date in the PUCT's Sunset abolition provision. [e5]
- **Key facts #4** (cites e6, e7) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 39.170: - SB 6 (2025, effective June 20, 2025) added PURA §39.170 on large load demand management. [e6][e7]
- **Common confusion #1** (cites e4): (Guidance) A statute does not change the Protocols by itself. PURA directs the PUCT and ERCOT, and protocol changes still need PUCT approval before taking effect. [e4]
- **Questions #2** (cites e3, e4, e5): Through PURA: it creates the board selection committee, requires a formal protocol process with PUCT approval, and makes the PUCT subject to the Texas Sunset Act. [e3][e4][e5]
- **Questions #4** (cites e6, e7) FLAG number-not-in-quote: 39.170: PURA §39.170, which requires a reliability service to competitively procure demand reductions from large load customers of at least 75 megawatts in an anticipated emergency. [e6][e7]
- **Link to puct** (cites e5): related PUCT (Public Utility Commission of Texas): HB 1500 (2023) sets September 1, 2029 as the date in the Sunset provision for abolishing the PUCT, which is subject to the Texas Sunset Act. [e5]

- (Say it like this) (Guidance) The Legislature sets the rules of the game in PURA and amends them by statute, as with the large-load rules in SB 6. The PUCT turns that law into rules and ERCOT turns the rules into Protocols.
