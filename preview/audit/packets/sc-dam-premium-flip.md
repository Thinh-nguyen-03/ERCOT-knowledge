## Uncited guidance text (check for new facts, numbers, dates, names)

- (Scenarios) - (Reasoning) That bias makes real time look looser than day-ahead expected, so if it reverses (load comes in above forecast, renewables below), real time tends to settle higher.
- (Scenarios) - Suppose (illustrative): you buy 25 MW at North Hub day-ahead for HE 20 at $60/MWh and real time settles at $140/MWh. The virtual earns 25 × $80 = $2,000 for that hour; if real time had settled at $40, it would lose $500.
- (Say it like this) (Guidance) Day-ahead usually carries a small premium because buyers pay for certainty. When real time keeps settling higher, the system is coming in tighter than forecast, through heat, forecast misses, reserve procurement, congestion or storage running out, and that is what virtual traders try to arbitrage, as long as the cause is predictable.
