## Definition

Summer 2026 was the summer in which ERCOT set a new all-time demand record of 91,134 MW on July 22, 2026, while daytime hours stayed uneventful because solar growth outpaced large load additions. [e3][e1] ERCOT's Board review says energy and ancillary service costs were close to or slightly below summers 2024 and 2025. [e1]

## How it works

The weather was hot: June-July 2026 ranked 11th hottest of 132 years on mean temperature. [e2] On July 22, crypto mining loads cut up to about 3,299 MW and 4CP response about 492 MW; ERCOT estimates the peak load would have been 94,861 MW if there was no reduction. [e3]

Risk moved later into the evening. ERCOT says the net load hour shifted from HE 21 to HE 22 in June and July, and that batteries helped serve record net loads but ran their state of charge down in the evenings as a regular pattern. [e5][e1] On the three lowest-SOC days of 2026, SOC percentages averaged 50% lower than on the lowest days of 2025. [e6] ERCOT adds that because ESR MWh capacity is higher, the lower SOC percentage is not necessarily lower SOC in MWh. [e20]

## ERCOT context

Market outcomes were soft. Real-time prices were lower than in 2024 and 2025 because of more supply in daylight hours, which also lowered peaker net margin. [e7] There were no shortages of Reg-Up, Reg-Down or RRS, and Non-Spin and ECRS met plan in nearly all intervals. [e11] Real-time congestion rent was 10% lower than summer 2025, and ERCOT credits RTC for part of that and for lower ancillary service costs. [e10][e8]

Two things went the other way. RUC rose to 1,313 effective resource-hours, from 872 in 2025 and 148 in 2024, and only 17% was nominally attributed to congestion versus 100% in 2025. [e9][e14] Thermal forced outages as a share of installed capacity were higher in summers 2025-2026 than in 2019-2020. [e13] The review's RUC and congestion rent charts count August only through August 15 in each year, so these are not full-summer totals. [e19]

## Key facts

- New all-time peak: 91,134 MW on July 22, 2026, 5,581 MW above the August 10, 2023 record of 85,508 MW (ERCOT monthly report). [e4]
- ERCOT's estimated peak load if there was no demand reduction: 94,861 MW. [e3]
- Peaker net margin and real-time prices lower than 2024 and 2025. [e7]
- RUC: 1,313 effective resource-hours in summer 2026 versus 872 (2025) and 148 (2024), with August counted through August 15 in each year. [e9][e19]
- Real-time congestion rent 10% below summer 2025 (August through August 15 in each year). [e10][e19]

## Say it like this

(Guidance) Summer 2026 set a new peak above 91 GW, but solar and storage kept the daytime quiet and prices, ancillary costs and peaker margins came in lower than the past two summers. [e3][e1][e7] The pressure point is now late evening, around HE 22, when batteries are running low, and RUC activity went up, largely to address capacity needs and ensure sufficient ancillary service quantities. [e5][e1][e9][e14]

## Common confusion

(Guidance) A record peak does not mean a scarcity summer: ERCOT reports lower real-time prices and peaker net margin than in 2024 and 2025 even with a new demand record. [e7][e3] The 94,861 MW figure is ERCOT's estimate of peak load if there was no demand reduction, not the recorded peak. [e3] On offer caps: ERCOT's slide says the SWCAP was reduced to $2,000/MWh from $5,000/MWh after RTC go-live, the Protocol table lists a $5,000 day-ahead and $2,000 real-time cap, and PUCT rule 25.509 still states a $5,000 HCAP; see the offer-cap topic. [e12][e16][e17]

## Questions

### Why were prices low in a record-demand summer?

ERCOT says higher supply, especially in daylight hours, kept real-time prices below 2024 and 2025, which also lowered peaker net margin. [e7] Solar growth outpaced large load additions. [e1]

### Where is the reliability risk now?

In the late evening: the net load hour moved to HE 22 in June and July, and battery state of charge regularly ran down in the evenings. [e5][e1]

### Why did RUC go up?

ERCOT says RUC activity in summer 2026 was largely attributed to addressing capacity needs, particularly to ensure sufficient Ancillary Service quantities; only 17% of effective resource-hours were nominally attributed to congestion versus 100% in 2025. [e14][e9]

## Open items

- Peak hour differs: the Summer 2026 review says 91,134 MW in HE19; the July 2026 monthly overview says hour ending 18. Both call it the July 22 record; the monthly overview calls it unofficial and preliminary.
- Previous record differs across sources: ERCOT's July 2026 overview gives 85,508 MW on 8/10/2023; the IMM 2023 SOM and the ERCOT 2023 Board deck give 85.7 GW; ERCOT's September 2023 EEA2 report gives 85,464 MW.
- Real-time SWCAP: the Summer 2026 review says SWCAP was reduced to $2,000/MWh from $5,000/MWh after RTC go-live; 16 TAC §25.509 in the saved sources sets the HCAP at $5,000 per MWh. Not resolved here.
- The Summer 2026 review's data runs only through August 15, 2026; no full-summer price levels or hub averages are given as quotable numbers.
- ercot-senate-2026-07 (July 29, 2026) covers SB 6, Batch Zero and queues, not summer operations; ercot-mora-2027-01 is a forward resource adequacy outlook. Neither added summer 2026 facts.
