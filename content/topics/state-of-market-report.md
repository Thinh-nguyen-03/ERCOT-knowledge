## Definition

The State of the Market report is the annual report the Independent Market Monitor (IMM) submits to the PUCT assessing how the ERCOT wholesale market performed and recommending changes. PUCT rule 25.365 requires an annual report on the state of the market covering competitiveness, ERCOT's management of the balancing energy, ancillary services and congestion rights markets, congestion management, flaws and opportunities for manipulation in the market design, and recommendations for improving it. [e1]

## How it works

**Legal basis and independence.** The IMM is a market monitor that ERCOT contracts with, selected by the commission, to detect and prevent market manipulation strategies, recommend ways to improve efficiency and give independent analysis of proposed market changes. [e3] Under rule 25.365 all of its reports must reflect its independent analysis, findings and expertise, and it prepares and submits the annual report to the commission. [e2][e1] The annual report sits alongside other required reports: daily, monthly and quarterly reports on prices and congestion, and periodic or special reports on market conditions or events the commission directs. [e11] The market monitor operates under the commission's supervision and oversight, and the statute may not be construed to give the monitor enforcement authority. [e12] The IMM must immediately report potential market manipulation and discovered or potential rule violations directly to the commission. [e13]

**What the 2025 report is.** The 2025 report is written by Potomac Economics as IMM and presents its assessment of the 2025 wholesale market outcomes plus recommended changes. [e4] It describes ERCOT as managing power for more than 27 million Texas customers, about 90% of the state's demand. [e9] It frames ERCOT as three wholesale markets: real-time, day-ahead, and the CRR auctions, calling the real-time market the most consequential. [e10] On market power it evaluates two perspectives, structural and behavioral. [e57] The IMM presented an overview of the 2025 report to ERCOT's TAC; the deck is dated August 13, 2026. [e60][e61]

**The recommendation system.** Each year the IMM produces recommendations to improve market efficiency, enhance reliability and mitigate market power; some are new, others carry over because they remain unaddressed, and they are retired when implemented, no longer applicable, or folded into a broader or updated recommendation. [e14] Recommendations are numbered by the year first introduced, followed by order in that report. [e5] Retired recommendations leave future editions because protocol changes have addressed them, they are no longer relevant, or they were incorporated into a new one; where protocol changes were approved but not implemented, they stay listed until implementation. [e18]

**Recommendations in the 2025 report.** New in 2025: 2025-1, Redesign and Improve the Firm Fuel Supply Service. [e15][e5] Outstanding from prior years, with the titles in the 2025 table:

- 2024-1 (a) define ASDCs by marginal reliability value and (b) adopt a stochastic risk methodology for the AS Plan; 2024-2, set the NSRS duration requirement to one hour; 2024-3, implement a process to mitigate market power at system and zonal levels; 2024-4, establish real-time offer requirements, penalties and proxy pricing; 2022-1, implement a multi-interval real-time market; 2021-2, implement an uncertainty product; 2020-3, reconfigure load zones to reflect prevailing congestion patterns; 2020-4, implement a point-to-point obligation bid fee. [e15]
- 2019-2, price ancillary services based on the shadow price of procuring each service; 2015-1, transition away from the 4CP method of allocating transmission costs. [e16]
- Retired in 2025: 2023-3, 2023-4, 2022-3, 2022-4 and 2021-1. [e17]

**What each one says (the IMM's reasoning, as stated in the 2025 report).** The IMM numbers each recommendation by the year it was first introduced. [e5]

- 2025-1 FFSS: ERCOT implemented the FFSS in 2022 in response to fuel supply disruptions during Winter Storm Uri, and it provides a seasonal payment to selected generators for maintaining fuel storage from mid-November through mid-March. [e19]
- 2024-1a: until December 2025 shortage pricing came from the ORDC; RTC replaced it with ASDCs that set penalty prices for shortages of each ancillary service, and the IMM recommends removing the requirement that the ASDCs aggregate to the AORDC and building each ASDC to reflect its product's reliability value. [e20][e21]
- 2024-1b: the IMM says the proposed 2026 AS Methodology procures 140% more than required for the 1-in-10 reliability standard for load shed, and the last 2 GW provide no additional reliability. [e22]
- 2024-2: a four-hour NSRS duration requirement means, for example, that a 10 MW NSRS award would require 40 MWh of stored capacity; the IMM says four hours of state of charge limits ESRs' ability to supply reserves and raises the likelihood of energy and ancillary service shortages. [e23]
- 2024-3: ERCOT has a mitigation procedure for local market power but not at the system level, [e24] and the IMM points to a conduct-impact framework with a conduct test (attempted economic or physical withholding) and an impact test (a material effect on price). [e25]
- 2024-4: ERCOT sets proxy offers for energy at $1,500 for IRRs and at the SWCAP for non-IRRs, and for ancillary services at the lesser of $2,000 or the ASDC price at 95% of the target procurement level. [e26]
- 2022-1: a Multi-Interval Real-Time Market would produce a dispatch based on forecast conditions across multiple future intervals, looking at least thirty minutes ahead and ideally an hour or more. [e27]
- 2021-2: in 2023 HB 1500 directed ERCOT to implement an uncertainty-type product, now referred to as DRRS. [e28]
- 2020-3: a load zone change would have to take effect at least four years after approval because of the CRR auction design. [e29]
- 2020-4: because ERCOT charges no fee to submit PTP bids, participants have no incentive to limit their bids. [e30]
- 2019-2: ERCOT nests multiple operating reserve variations under one ancillary service, sets volume limits for each sub-product and clears all at the same price. [e31]
- 2015-1: the IMM supports allocating transmission costs on an annual load ratio share basis. [e32]

**Why recommendations leave the list.** 2023-3 (ECRS deployment): the IMM says the excess cost of ECRS deployment practices fell to $720 million in 2024 and $90 million in 2025, and that RTC largely resolved the issue. [e33] 2022-3 was addressed by NPRR 1198, approved July 2024 and scheduled for implementation by February 2027, and stays listed until then. [e34] 2022-4 was addressed by NPRR 1006, approved in June 2020 but not yet implemented, with ERCOT Projects earmarking October 2027. [e35] 2021-1 was retired because the PUC removed the small fish rule from 25.504(c) through Project 58379. [e36]

## ERCOT context

**2025 results (calendar year 2025 unless noted).** Average electricity demand was about 6.1% higher than in 2024, up approximately 3,205 MW. [e6] Crypto-currency mining demand was about 4,600 MW, and by the end of 2025 it accounted for more than half of ERS capacity. [e7][e8] ESR installed capacity exceeded 17,000 MW and 31,500 MWh, up almost 80% from the end of 2024. [e48]

- Prices: natural gas rose 61% but the all-in price of electricity rose by less than 14%; [e45] the IMM's August 2026 TAC deck gives 13.8% and says the implied heat rate fell 28% year over year. [e56][e55] ORDC shortage pricing added only $0.02 per MWh. [e46]
- Reserves: average Physical Responsive Capability was over 11,000 MW, per the TAC deck. [e54]
- Ancillary services: RTC went live in December 2025; [e47] the IMM says ERCOT's procurement practices raise AS requirements to more than double the quantities needed for a reasonable reliability standard. [e52]
- Congestion: day-ahead and real-time congestion costs rose 26% and 32%, reversing a three-year decline. [e49]
- Market operations: 5,129 RUC resource-hours, up 176% from 2024, about 77% of them for congestion. [e50]
- Competition: the share of top-quartile net load hours with a pivotal supplier fell from over 90% in 2022 to 35% in 2025. [e51] The IMM nevertheless continues to recommend system-wide market power mitigation, citing a 20 GW net load scenario. [e58]
- Load outlook: the projected 2030 load (base and adjusted forecast) is between 138 GW and 148 GW, with a high scenario up to 209 GW. [e53]

## Key facts

- Legal basis: PUCT rule 25.365(k)(2) requires the IMM's annual state-of-the-market report. [e1]
- The report must assess market competitiveness, ERCOT's management of balancing energy, ancillary services and congestion rights markets, and design flaws. [e1]
- 2025 report: average demand up about 6.1% (about 3,205 MW) from 2024. [e6]
- 2025 report: one new recommendation (Firm Fuel Supply Service); recommendations are numbered by year introduced. [e5]
- Ten recommendations were outstanding from prior years in the 2025 table, and five were being retired. [e15][e16][e17]
- IMM reports must reflect its independent analysis. [e2]

## Say it like this

(Guidance) The State of the Market report is the IMM's annual assessment of ERCOT's markets, delivered to the PUCT. Analysts use it for what actually happened in a year and for the IMM's list of design fixes, which it keeps open until they are implemented or retired. [e18]

## Common confusion

(Guidance) The IMM's role is to recommend measures and give independent analysis, not to decide them or enforce. [e3][e12] A recommendation in the report does not by itself mean a rule change is coming. Also, this is the IMM's report, not ERCOT's. [e4] Statements about what ERCOT's practices "should" be, such as the AS methodology being excessive, are the IMM's view. [e52]

## Questions

### Who requires the State of the Market report and who writes it?

PUCT rule 25.365 requires the IMM to submit it to the commission; Potomac Economics acts as the IMM. [e1][e4]

### What must the annual report cover?

Market competitiveness, ERCOT's management of balancing energy, ancillary services and congestion rights markets, congestion management effectiveness, design flaws and manipulation opportunities, and recommendations. [e1]

### How are the report's recommendations numbered?

By the year first introduced, then the order in that report; for example the 2025 report's one new recommendation concerns the Firm Fuel Supply Service. [e5]

### Why does the IMM want to remove the link between the ASDCs and the AORDC?

The IMM says ASDCs forced to match the aggregate ORDC shape yield scarcity prices that reflect an administrative construct rather than the type and severity of reserve shortages; it recommends building each ASDC to reflect the reliability value of its product. [e21] Context: RTC replaced the ORDC with ASDCs in December 2025. [e20]

### Why does the IMM want the NSRS duration cut to one hour?

A four-hour requirement ties up ESR state of charge (10 MW of NSRS needs 40 MWh), which the IMM says limits ESRs' ability to supply reserves and raises the likelihood of shortages. [e23]

### If the market looks competitive, why does the IMM still recommend system-wide mitigation?

It reports that pivotal-supplier hours fell sharply to 35% of top-quartile net load hours in 2025, but says that with extreme projected load growth even 20 GW of new net load would shrink the operating margin significantly. [e51][e58]

### What happens to a recommendation once ERCOT adopts it in a protocol?

It is retired once implemented, though if the protocol change was approved but not yet implemented it stays listed until implementation; NPRR 1198 (2022-3) is an example. [e18][e34]

## Scenarios

### A client asks: "The IMM wants system-level market power mitigation. Is it coming?"

- The IMM says ERCOT has local mitigation but not system-level, and recommends starting development. [e24]
- The IMM monitors and recommends; the commission retains enforcement authority. [e12]
- The report's own recommendation list shows 2024-3 as outstanding in 2025, not as implemented. [e15]
- (Guidance) Say it is an open IMM recommendation, not a rule.
