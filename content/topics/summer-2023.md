## Definition

Summer 2023 was a hot summer in which ERCOT set new demand records 49 times, peaking at 85.7 GW on August 10, 2023 (IMM figure). [e1] On September 6, 2023 frequency fell to 59.77 Hz and ERCOT declared EEA2 at 7:25 p.m. [e5]

## How it works

The stress came in the evening, not at the demand peak. ERCOT says the solar down ramp made late evening tighter than peak demand, and high demand with lower wind output on some days led to the need to request conservation. [e2] Peak net load hours shifted from HE 17 to HE 21 in 2023, and ERCOT appealed for conservation on 11 days, generally the highest net load days. [e3][e4] In its EEA report ERCOT explains that on September 6 abnormally high demand persisted into the evening, overlapping the period when solar output falls, so other generation had to meet demand and reserves. [e21]

On September 6 a transmission limit made it worse. ERCOT says that after the solar ramp-down, with low wind in the North, Central and West regions but high wind in the South, flow out of the South pushed a significant 345 kV line beyond its post-contingency limit, and ERCOT was forced to direct manual curtailment of 1,590 MW of generation from the South region. [e22] The chronology records this at 7:00 p.m. on the Pawnee-to-Calaveras line to manage the DELSAN5 constraint, followed by 1,023 MW of ERS at 7:14 p.m. [e7] ERCOT also says forced outages during the EEA were lower than on the preceding days and in line with the rest of the summer. [e23]

Frequency then fell. ERCOT told its Board that frequency-responsive reserves appeared to remain above 2,000 MW, yet frequency declined to 59.77 Hz by 7:25 p.m., and that it was investigating this as incorrect response or reporting by Resources. [e25] After the EEA2 declaration, 1,593 MW of Load Resources were deployed at 7:28 p.m.; frequency was back to 60 Hz at 7:37 p.m. with no firm load shed; ERCOT moved to EEA1 at 8:27 p.m. and normal operations at 8:37 p.m. [e31][e6]

## ERCOT context

Prices: the IMM counts more than 55 hours above $1,000 in 2023, and more than four hours at the $5,000 system-wide offer cap that applied then, mainly August 17 and September 6. [e11] Ancillary service costs were substantially higher than the previous two summers, particularly in August (ERCOT). [e8] For the full year 2023 the IMM puts ancillary service costs at $4.21 per MWh of load against $3.29 in 2022, driven by high ECRS prices in June, August and September, with ECRS averaging $76.77 per MWh. [e41] The IMM attributes some of the shortage pricing to ECRS, which it says produced prices up to $5,000 per MWh when the system was not short of reserves under the ORDC. [e10] In the IMM's estimate the total reliability value of the 2023 ECRS procurements was $12 million against procurement costs of more than $600 million. [e40] The ORDC adder was discontinued in December 2025 with RTC, so this describes the 2023 design. [e18]

Despite the records, the IMM says 2023 had fewer true shortages than 2022, largely because of about 7.6 GW of new wind and solar, 1.9 GW of storage and almost 1 GW of new gas. [e12] RUC fell to 534 effective resource-hours in summer 2023 from 2,573 in summer 2022, which ERCOT attributes to greater self-commitment under high demand and prices. [e13][e47]

The IMM's reading of September 6 is a pricing problem. It says the marginal cost of the manual curtailments was much higher than the shadow price caps, so the real-time market did not curtail the resources, and it puts the curtailment at 1,500 MW including 1,300 MW of wind. [e17][e9] The 2023 cap for 345 kV constraints was $4,500 per MW. [e48] The IMM adds that the -$250 per MWh proxy offer curve for renewables without an offer makes curtailing them appear very expensive to the dispatch model, typically more expensive than the shadow price cap, which it says is how the constraint went into violation on September 6. [e45] It reran the period with a $15,000 per MWh cap (in its words, "without the manual curtailments lifted") and reports that SCED found a dispatch that avoided the constraint violation and reduced the power balance violation. [e42][e44] In March 2024 ERCOT proposed a similar measure for the South Texas Export GTCs (IMM). [e43]

## Key facts

- 49 new demand records in summer 2023; peak 85.7 GW on August 10, 2023 (IMM). [e1] ERCOT's EEA report gives the August 10 peak as 85,464 MW, about 6.6% above the 2022 summer peak of 80,148 MW. [e19]
- ERCOT's Board deck counts 49 days in summer 2023 with a peak above 80 GW; the deck's September data runs only through September 15. [e49][e58]
- September 8, 2023 peak of 84,182 MW was about 16% above the previous September record of 72,370 MW (2021); September 6 peaked at 82,705 MW in HE 18. [e19]
- September 6, 2023: frequency low of 59.77 Hz; EEA2 declared at 7:25 p.m.; normal operations at 8:37 p.m. [e5][e6]
- September 6 wind in HE 20: 7,153 MW forecast, 5,492 MW actual (ERCOT). [e51][e52]
- Lowest operating reserves: 3,685 MW on September 7 and 4,612 MW on September 8, 2023 (ERCOT). [e20]
- 11 conservation appeal days in summer 2023. [e4]
- More than 55 hours above $1,000 per MWh in 2023; more than four hours at the $5,000 cap. [e11]
- ERS was deployed twice in 2023 (August 17 and September 6); the September 6 event performance factor was 94% (IMM). [e39]
- Summer RUC fell from 2,573 effective resource-hours (2022) to 534 (2023). [e13]

## Say it like this

(Guidance) Summer 2023 broke demand records again and again, but ERCOT says late evening was tighter than the demand peak because of the solar down ramp. [e2] On September 6 a South Texas export limit forced ERCOT to curtail about 1,590 MW of South generation just as solar fell off, frequency dropped to 59.77 Hz and ERCOT declared EEA2, deployed Load Resources, and returned to normal operations at 8:37 p.m. with no firm load shed. [e22][e5][e6] The IMM's lesson is about pricing: the shadow price cap was too low for SCED to solve the constraint itself, and ECRS sequestration produced shortage prices when reserves were not truly short. [e17][e10]

## Common confusion

(Guidance) The record peak (August 10) and the emergency (September 6) were different days; ERCOT says late evenings were tighter than peak demand because of the solar down ramp [e2], and on September 6 it directed manual curtailment to manage a transmission constraint before declaring EEA2. [e7][e5] EEA2 is not load shed: no firm load was shed on September 6. [e6] The Load Resources deployed in EEA2 were responsive-reserve loads called by ERCOT, not rotating outages. [e38] And "49" means different things in the two sources: the IMM counts 49 new demand records, ERCOT's Board deck counts 49 days above 80 GW. [e1][e49]

## Questions

### Why did ERCOT go into EEA2 on September 6 rather than on the record peak day?

ERCOT says late evening was tighter than the demand peak because of the solar down ramp. [e2] On September 6 the South Texas constraint forced ERCOT to curtail 1,590 MW of South generation, wind in HE 20 came in at 5,492 MW against 7,153 MW forecast, and frequency fell to 59.77 Hz between about 7:10 and 7:25 p.m. before EEA2 was declared. [e22][e51][e52][e5]

### What does the IMM think should change after September 6?

It says the established maximum shadow price caps for thermal constraints are in certain cases too low, so the real-time market may not dispatch enough local resources and manual actions follow, and that operators should have the authority to raise the caps as needed. [e15][e16] It also says the -$250 per MWh proxy offer curve for renewables (IRRs) without an energy offer makes them hard for the dispatch model to curtail, and recommends a proxy offer curve more closely aligned with IRRs' production costs. [e45][e56]

### Why were prices high in summer 2023 even when reserves were not short?

The IMM says the sharp increase in 10-minute reserve procurements, primarily of dispatchable resources, led to shortage pricing as high as $5,000 per MWh when the system was not short of reserves under the ORDC. [e10] It values the reliability benefit of the 2023 ECRS procurements at $12 million against more than $600 million of cost. [e40] The ORDC adder was discontinued in December 2025. [e18]

### What if the shadow price cap on the South Texas constraint had been $15,000 instead?

The IMM reran the September 6 period with a $15,000 per MWh cap (in its words, "without the manual curtailments lifted") and reports that SCED found a dispatch that avoided the constraint violation and reduced the power balance violation, and that a higher shadow price would send stronger locational signals. [e42][e44] This is the IMM's simulation, not an outcome that happened.

### Why did frequency fall when PRC looked adequate?

ERCOT told its Board that frequency-responsive reserves appeared to remain above 2,000 MW while frequency declined to 59.77 Hz, and that it was investigating incorrect response or reporting by Resources. [e25] Its chronology shows the 3,000 MW PRC Advisory at 7:15 p.m. and FFR deployed at 7:19 p.m. before the low. [e30]

### What emergency tools beyond ancillary services did ERCOT use that week?

ERS (1,023 MW), distribution voltage reduction, Load Resources (1,593 MW), TCEQ enforcement discretion requested at 7:29 p.m. on September 6, and a DOE 202(c) order (No. 202-23-1) for 5-9 p.m. on September 7 and 8. [e7][e32][e31][e33][e35][e36] Under 202(c) DOE may let specific units run to maximum output despite air permit limits; at the time of the request only WA Parish Unit 8 had told ERCOT of output limits from opacity limits. [e34][e37]

## Scenarios

### You trade South Texas and North Hub on a hot September evening with weak North and West wind. What do you watch, based on September 6, 2023?

- On September 6, 2023 the South Texas export line was overloaded after the solar ramp-down, with low wind in the North, Central and West regions and high wind in the South. [e22]
- ERCOT may manually curtail South generation if SCED cannot solve the constraint within the shadow price cap, which the IMM says can result in higher prices system-wide. [e15][e17]
- Watch PRC against 3,000 MW: ERS may be deployed before an EEA when PRC falls below 3,000 MW and is not expected to recover within 30 minutes, and on September 6 ERCOT issued an Advisory at that level. [e57][e30]
- ERCOT's Board review notes that ESRs north of the constraint generally had high offer prices. [e26]
