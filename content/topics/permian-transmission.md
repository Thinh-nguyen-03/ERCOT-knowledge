## Definition

The Permian Basin Reliability Plan (PBRP) is the transmission plan that PURA §39.167 (HB 5066, 88th Legislature) required the PUCT to have ERCOT develop for the Permian Basin under the general PURA §39.166 reliability-plan process. [e1] Section 39.166 has ERCOT identify regions where transmission capacity is insufficient for existing and forecast load, as reasonably determined by the certificated TSP, and develop a plan to serve that load. [e54] Alongside local upgrades, the PBRP adds new import paths into the region, with options at 345 kV and at extra-high-voltage 765 kV. [e3]

## How it works

The plan has two layers. The first is common local projects: new and upgraded 138 kV and 345 kV lines, substations and equipment that extend service to mineral areas and cut interconnection times. [e29] The PUCT approved these for service through 2038 whatever voltage it later chose for the import paths. [e4] The second layer is the import paths that bring more generation into the region. [e3] The October 7, 2024 order authorized TSPs to start CCN applications for all eight candidate paths (three at 765 kV, five at 345 kV) and told ERCOT to work with TSPs to prioritize the paths needed for 2030 load. [e22][e24] It made the voltage choice conditional: if the PUCT decided by May 1, 2025 to allow 765 kV statewide, the three 765 kV paths would be considered approved. [e23] TSPs could record CCN preparation costs as a regulatory asset until their authorization expired. [e25]

The PUCT then approved the three 765 kV import paths in Table 7.5 of the plan, and the authorization to prepare CCNs for the 345 kV paths expired on the date of that order. [e18] ERCOT dates that approval April 24, 2025, and calls 765 kV a new voltage class in the ERCOT Region. [e6] Approval of the plan is not a certificate. Each project needing a CCN must still show in its own proceeding that it is necessary for the service, accommodation, convenience or safety of the public (PURA §37.056(a)), although these projects need no further RPG review before filing. [e26][e27] More generally, the Planning Guide has ERCOT notify the PUCT of the disposition of Tier 1 and 2 projects and support ERCOT-endorsed projects in later CCN proceedings. [e56] By statute the PUCT must decide a CCN application for a new transmission facility within 180 days of filing. [e52] Under 16 TAC §25.101, lines that ERCOT formally designates as critical to reliability are considered on an expedited basis, with a decision within 180 days of a complete application unless good cause is shown for more time. [e59] In weighing need for a reliability project in ERCOT, the PUCT must consider historical load, forecast growth and load seeking interconnection, including load without a signed agreement, as determined by the serving utility. [e53]

The plan used Permian load levels of 23,659 MW in 2030 and 26,400 MW in 2038, consistent with HB 5066. [e2] It drew on the S&P Global Commodity Insights Permian load forecast and TSP-provided Large Load information. [e28] ERCOT says the PBRP studied a 345 kV option and evaluated 500 kV or 765 kV, and concluded that 765 kV was the optimal engineering solution. [e37] ERCOT says three studies (the PBRP, the 2024 RTP and the 2025 RTP) showed a need for 765 kV additions starting in 2030. [e12][e19] It adds that more Permian generation than already expected would not eliminate the need for the plan's transmission additions, and that many large loads are rapidly developing in the Permian Basin, which it says is projected to grow to an electrical size equivalent to other large load areas such as Dallas-Fort Worth and Houston. [e20][e14]

## ERCOT context

For a market analyst, the Permian is an import-constrained pocket, and the IMM's 2025 numbers show it. The West zone had the highest average energy price in 2025, $41.59/MWh excluding adders against an ERCOT average of $36.94/MWh, which the IMM attributes to Permian import constraints and Panhandle export constraints. [e45] Congestion costs within the West rose 27% from 2024 to 2025. [e46] A gas CT could earn up to $177/kW-year in net revenue in the West zone against roughly $59/kW-year in Houston in 2025, a gap the IMM attributes to the import-constrained Permian. [e47] The IMM expects the Waha-Katy net revenue gap to narrow again as work on these projects concludes. [e48] Comparing today's West zone with its proposed new load zones, the IMM says a FarWest zone, which roughly matches the Permian Basin, would price higher because it stays import constrained until more transmission is built. [e21][e10]

ERCOT has warned of possible local load shed in Far West Texas on low-wind nights, especially during transmission and thermal outages. [e11] The worst case, per ERCOT, is high-load, low-wind nights when the region depends on limited local thermal generation and limited import capability. [e41] Since 2014, about $23 billion of projects have been approved in the Permian Basin. [e16] As of December 2025 ERCOT counted about 630 circuit miles of 345 kV built, about 1,585 circuit miles of 345 kV approved, and about 1,329 miles of 765 kV approved for new build there. [e42] ERCOT's December 2025 top-10 planned improvements list shows the Permian Basin 765 kV import paths beside the year 2031. [e43] Across ERCOT, the October 2025 project tracking data showed $30.21 billion of transmission projects expected in service between 2026 and the end of 2031. [e44]

The costs are large and contested. In January 2025 ERCOT put the Permian Basin Reliability Plan projects at $12.95 billion under the 345 kV plan and $13.77 billion under the 765 kV STEP, and the full STEP including Permian projects at about $32.99 billion. [e33][e8] ERCOT estimated STEP at about $2.24 billion more than the 345 kV plan, narrowing to about $1.35 billion after counting $890 million of extra outage-related construction cost for the 345 kV plan. [e35][e34] ERCOT says West Texas lines can no longer be released for long, continuous outages, which makes an all-345 kV rebuild harder. [e36] The IMM puts the Permian plan at more than $13 billion and argues that the customers the plan is meant to support are well positioned to game the 4CP methodology by curtailing demand when they expect a coincident peak. [e9]

In August 2026 Rep. Geren asked ERCOT about 765 kV applications pending before the PUCT. [e15] ERCOT answered that a denial would require a new reliability study and different solutions, and a delay would require operational mitigation plans that make regional load shed more likely. [e38][e60] It said more than 600 supporting TSP studies behind the Batch process, and 58 completed RPG project reviews, would be invalidated if the 765 kV additions were delayed or denied. [e13][e39]

## Key facts

- Statute: PURA §39.167 (HB 5066) required the PUCT, not later than January 30, 2024, to direct ERCOT to develop a reliability plan for the Permian Basin. [e1]
- Planning load: 23,659 MW (2030) and 26,400 MW (2038). [e2]
- Decided (PUCT): the plan and all common local projects, October 7, 2024; the three 765 kV import paths in 2025 (April 24, 2025 per ERCOT). [e5][e4][e6][e18]
- Decided (ERCOT Board, not PUCT): the STEP Western Loop and Eastern Backbone, approved December 9, 2025 after RPG final recommendation on November 11, 2025. [e51][e7]
- Cost (ERCOT estimate, January 2025): PBRP projects $13.77 billion in the 765 kV plan; STEP in total about $32.99 billion. [e33][e8]
- Price signal (IMM, 2025): West zone average $41.59/MWh against $36.94/MWh ERCOT-wide; West intrazonal congestion up 27% from 2024. [e45][e46]
- CCN clock: 180 days for a new transmission facility under PURA §37.057. [e52]
- Pending (as described in Rep. Geren's August 2026 question to ERCOT): 765 kV applications before the PUCT. [e15]

## Say it like this

(Guidance) The Permian is import constrained, and the IMM ties West zone prices and congestion in 2025 to that constraint, so the PUCT approved a local upgrade plan and three 765 kV import paths, a new voltage class for ERCOT. Approval of the plan is not a CCN: each line still has to win its own certificate case, and in August 2026 ERCOT said delaying or denying those applications would make regional load shed more likely and invalidate the TSP studies behind Batch Zero. [e45][e46][e6][e26][e38][e13]

## Common confusion

(Guidance) The Permian Basin Reliability Plan and the statewide 765 kV STEP are different things. The PBRP is the Permian plan required by HB 5066, approved by the PUCT. [e1][e5] STEP is the wider 765 kV network from the 2024 RTP, which includes the Permian Basin Reliability projects; its remaining portions were approved by the ERCOT Board in December 2025, not by a PUCT order. [e7][e8][e51] Cost figures also differ by scope: $13.77 billion for PBRP projects in ERCOT's 765 kV case, more than $13 billion per the IMM, and about $32.99 billion for STEP including the Permian projects. [e33][e9][e8]

(Guidance) The Euclid-Hillje 765 kV line is a Central Texas import path added in the 2025 RTP, not a Permian path. [e19][e49]

## Questions

### Why 765 kV rather than more 345 kV?

ERCOT says its studies showed a NERC-based need for 765 kV additions beginning in 2030, and that the 345 kV-only plan relied heavily on rebuilds needing outages that West Texas lines can no longer take for long periods. [e12][e36] On cost, ERCOT put STEP about $2.24 billion above the 345 kV plan, or about $1.35 billion after the 345 kV plan's extra outage-related construction cost. [e35][e34] The PUCT approved the three 765 kV import paths, and its prior authorization to prepare CCN applications for the 345 kV paths expired on the date of that order. [e18]

### What does the Permian constraint mean for prices?

The IMM says the West zone had the highest average energy price in 2025 and that a CT there could earn up to $177/kW-year against $59 in Houston, both driven by the import-constrained Permian. [e45][e47] It says that if today's West load zone were split under its proposed configuration, a FarWest zone (roughly the Permian) would price higher until more transmission is built. [e21][e10]

### What happens if the 765 kV lines are delayed?

ERCOT says a delay would require operational mitigation plans that make regional load shed more likely, and a denial would require a new reliability study and different solutions. [e38][e60] It also says more than 600 TSP studies and 58 RPG project reviews would be invalidated. [e13][e39]

### Why does ERCOT say new gas plants in the Permian do not remove the need?

ERCOT already includes known future generation, for example 17 Texas Energy Fund gas units at six West Load Zone sites in the 2025 RTP. [e40] It says additional generation would change some aspects of planning but not eliminate the PBRP transmission additions. [e20]

### What if the load forecast comes in much lower?

ERCOT says the 2024 RTP assumed about 150 GW for 2030 and ran a lower-load sensitivity at about 125 GW, and that the entire STEP plan was needed by 2030 at both levels. [e58] The 2025 RTP used a reduced forecast reaching about 159 GW in 2031. [e58]

### Does PUCT approval of the plan mean the lines are certificated?

No. The order requires each TSP to establish in its CCN proceeding that the certificate meets all legal requirements, including necessity under PURA §37.056(a). [e26] The order does remove the need for further RPG review before filing. [e27]

## Scenarios

### A trader asks whether to expect West zone congestion to fade.

- The IMM says the West's 2025 price premium and congestion reflect the Permian import constraint, and it expects the Waha-Katy gap to narrow as the projects conclude. [e45][e46][e48]
- ERCOT's December 2025 list shows the Permian 765 kV import paths beside 2031, and in August 2026 the applications were described as pending. [e43][e15]
- Until then, ERCOT has flagged local load shed risk on high-load, low-wind nights in Far West Texas. [e41]
