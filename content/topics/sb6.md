## Definition

SB 6 is the 2025 Texas law (89th Legislature) on the planning, interconnection, operation and cost of serving certain large electrical loads. [e1][e11] Its central rule tells the PUCT to set standards for interconnecting large load customers in ERCOT. The standards must support business development while limiting stranded infrastructure costs and maintaining reliability. [e2]

## How it works

**Interconnection standards (PURA §37.0561).** The PUCT must set a demand threshold of 75 MW unless it finds a lower one is needed. [e3] Large load customers must disclose whether they are pursuing a substantially similar request elsewhere in Texas that would change, delay or withdraw the request. [e54] The standards must include a flat study fee of at least $100,000 for initial transmission screening studies. [e4] Proof of financial commitment may include per-MW security in an amount the PUCT sets, among other forms. [e23] Separately, §35.004(c-1) requires large load customers to contribute to the utility's interconnection costs; it applies only to interconnection agreements entered into on or after SB 6's effective date. [e34][e52] The PUCT must also set criteria for how ERCOT includes forecasted large load in its transmission planning and resource adequacy models. [e6]

**Emergency curtailment of loads with backup (§37.0561(e)).** "On-site backup generating facilities" means generation that cannot export to the ERCOT grid and can serve at least 50% of on-site demand. ERCOT must set a threshold, before or during an EEA, at which it may give notice that such customers may be directed to run backup or curtail. [e28] Once ERCOT has deployed all available market services other than frequency responsive services, it may direct the utility to have the customer do so. [e5] ERCOT must count such a deployment as firm load shed when calculating price adjustments for reliability deployments. [e29]

**Curtailment in firm load shed (§39.170(a)).** Each utility serving a transmission-voltage customer must have a protocol, with any needed equipment, that lets the load be curtailed during firm load shed. This applies only to loads interconnected after December 31, 2025, and not to critical load industrial customers or designated critical natural gas facilities. [e8]

**Co-location with existing generation (§39.169).** Before netting a new large load against a generator that was registered as stand-alone on September 1, 2025, the generation owner must notify ERCOT. [e7] The section does not apply to a generator whose registration already included a co-located large load at energization, or one majority-owned as of January 1, 2025 by a parent company of the load customer. [e57] ERCOT then has 120 days, after receiving all required information, to study the arrangement; the PUCT has 60 days after that to approve, deny or condition it. [e30] If the PUCT misses that deadline, the arrangement is considered approved. [e33] Any conditions must require a generator that supplied dispatchable capacity before the deal to keep supplying at least that much, at ERCOT's direction, ahead of an anticipated emergency. [e32]

**A new demand-reduction service (§39.170(b)-(c)).** ERCOT must develop a reliability service that competitively procures demand reductions from loads of 75 MW or more, for use in an anticipated emergency. [e9] The rules must give at least 24 hours' notice, keep the load curtailed for the EEA or until it can be safely recalled, and bar loads that curtail in response to wholesale prices or that are in another reliability or ancillary service; deployments count in reliability price adjustments. [e31]

**Transmission cost allocation (Section 6).** The PUCT must evaluate whether the current four coincident peak method makes all loads contribute appropriately to transmission costs, and whether alternatives (multiple seasonal peaks, different daily intervals, peak energy intervals) would assign costs better. [e27] It must amend its rules by December 31, 2026. [e10]

## ERCOT context

The December 2025 CDR calls SB 6 the most impactful resource adequacy development since the May 2025 CDR. [e19] ERCOT told the Senate in July 2026 that it and the PUCT are carrying out the directive that large loads added at existing generators must not stop ERCOT from using those generators in emergencies. [e18] ERCOT added that the arrangement applies only where the generation resource existed before September 1, 2025. [e51] ERCOT is also building a Large Load Curtailment Manager, a control-room system whose first phase handles SB 6 net-metered facilities. [e22] SB 6 curtailment is already showing up in planning: for the reliability standard's magnitude value, ERCOT has previously given TOs a winter peak that excludes load curtailable under §§37.0561(e) and 39.169, on the understanding that such load would be curtailed before rotating outages, which begin only in EEA3. [e42][e56]

Implementation status differs by piece in the saved sources; the IMM's 2025 report expected the LLDMS rulemaking to finish sometime in 2026. [e20]
- Decided: §25.370 large load forecasting, adopted and effective March 1, 2026. It bars large loads from ERCOT's planning and resource adequacy forecasts without an executed §25.194 interconnection agreement. [e16][e40]
- Decided: §25.205 co-location net metering, effective 4/15/2026. No such arrangement may be implemented without PUCT approval. [e24][e15][e39]
- Decided: §25.194 interconnection standards, effective 10/08/2026, with a $100,000 study fee and $50,000/MW intermediate security. [e25][e17][e36][e35]
- Proposed: §25.521 LLDMS, filed July 30, 2026, with comments due September 4, 2026; no adopted version is saved. [e13][e49]
- Proposed: the Section 6 transmission rule (Project 58000, for the July 9, 2026 open meeting), which would replace 4CP with 12CP measured on 30-minute intervals; no adopted version is saved. [e37][e38]

Under §25.205, if the PUCT requires a hold harmless proceeding, each affected TSP must file within 60 days to remove the stranded or underutilized asset costs from its rates. [e41] Those costs are then collected from the generator owner and the large load customer. [e43] "Underutilized" means expected to carry, on an average annual basis, at least 25% less power relative to capacity, with no significant reliability benefit. [e44]

The LLDMS proposal (July 30, 2026) would let ERCOT procure the service as needed to prevent or relieve an EEA caused by extreme weather. [e46] As proposed, QSEs for available-but-not-deployed loads would receive 25% of the clearing price, and deployed loads 100%, with costs charged to LSEs by load ratio share. [e45][e47] Offers above $5,000/MWh would be rejected, and annual procurement spending would be capped at $54 million. [e48][e14] The IMM says it has recommended using the LLDMS as a targeted tool sized to the severity of an event, with a risk method based on hourly load-shed probabilities. [e50]

## Key facts

- The large load threshold is 75 MW unless the PUCT finds a lower one is needed. [e3]
- The study fee is at least $100,000 by statute; §25.194 sets it at $100,000 plus $50,000/MW intermediate security. [e4][e36][e35]
- On-site backup generating facilities: cannot export, and can serve at least 50% of on-site demand. [e28]
- §39.169 clock: ERCOT study within 120 days, PUCT decision within 60 days, or the deal is deemed approved. [e30][e33]
- SB 6 was signed by the Governor and effective immediately on June 20, 2025; §37.0561, §39.169 and §39.170 took effect that day. [e26][e11][e55]
- Proposed (July 30, 2026), not adopted: §25.521 LLDMS, with a $54 million annual budget cap and a $5,000/MWh offer ceiling. [e13][e14][e48]
- Transmission cost review (Section 6): rules due by December 31, 2026; Staff opened Project 58484 on August 1, 2025; the July 2026 proposal would move from 4CP to 12CP. [e10][e21][e38]

## Say it like this

(Guidance) SB 6 is the 2025 law that sets the terms for very large loads like data centers to connect to the grid and lets ERCOT curtail them in emergencies. The PUCT's forecasting, co-location and interconnection rules took effect in 2026; the large-load demand management service and the 4CP replacement were still proposals in the saved record, and the transmission cost rules are due by December 31, 2026. [e16][e15][e17][e13][e38][e10]

## Common confusion

(Guidance) SB 6 creates several different curtailment tools. One is emergency direction to run backup generation or curtail. [e5] Another is curtailment during firm load shed. [e8] A third is the competitively procured demand reduction service (LLDMS). [e9] A fourth is conditions on co-located loads under §39.169. [e32] Only the LLDMS is a procured service, and its rule was a proposal as of the July 30, 2026 filing. [e13] Also, SB 6 did not itself set a dollar-per-MW security amount; the statute leaves that figure to the PUCT, and §25.194 sets $50,000/MW. [e23][e35]

(Guidance) SB 6 itself does not abolish 4CP. It orders an evaluation that names 4CP and alternatives, and rules by December 31, 2026; the 12CP change is a proposal. [e27][e10][e38]

## Questions

### What does SB 6 require for a new data center's interconnection?

It must meet PUCT standards. The threshold is 75 MW by default and there is a flat study fee of at least $100,000 for initial transmission screening studies. [e3][e4] The PUCT's rule on these standards is §25.194, effective 10/08/2026, which also requires $50,000/MW of intermediate security. [e25][e17][e35]

### Can ERCOT curtail a data center under SB 6?

Yes, in several ways. ERCOT can direct a load with on-site backup to run that backup or curtail once market services are exhausted. [e5] Transmission-voltage loads interconnected after December 31, 2025 (other than critical load industrial customers and critical natural gas facilities) must be curtailable during firm load shed. [e8] ERCOT can also procure demand reductions through a new service. [e9]

### Why does SB 6 make curtailments count in price adjustments?

The statute requires ERCOT to count §37.0561(e) deployments as firm load shed, and LLDMS deployments, when calculating price adjustments for reliability deployments. [e29][e31] In effect, taking load off the system by instruction is not supposed to make prices look less scarce than conditions are.

### What happens if a generator wants to sell to a co-located data center?

If the generator was registered stand-alone as of September 1, 2025, the owner must notify ERCOT and get PUCT approval. [e7][e39] ERCOT studies it within 120 days and the PUCT decides within 60 days, or it is deemed approved. [e30][e33] Conditions must keep the pre-deal dispatchable capacity available to ERCOT ahead of an anticipated emergency. [e32]

### What if the co-location strands a transmission line?

The PUCT may require a hold harmless proceeding; the TSP then files within 60 days to remove those costs from rates, and they are collected from the generator owner and the large load customer. [e41][e43]

### What is still pending from SB 6?

In the saved sources the LLDMS rule (§25.521) is only a proposal, filed July 30, 2026. [e13] The PUCT must amend its wholesale transmission charge rules by December 31, 2026; the July 2026 proposal would replace 4CP with 12CP. [e10][e38]

## Scenarios

### A 300 MW data center with on-site gas backup asks what SB 6 means for its operations in a winter emergency.

- If its backup cannot export and can serve at least half its demand, it is "on-site backup generating facilities", and ERCOT may direct it to run backup or curtail once market services other than frequency responsive services are exhausted. [e28][e5]
- If it interconnected after December 31, 2025 at transmission voltage, it must also be curtailable during firm load shed. [e8]
- It could not sell LLDMS while curtailing on wholesale price signals or providing another reliability or ancillary service. [e31]
