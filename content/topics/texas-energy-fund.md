## Definition

The Texas Energy Fund is a special fund in the state treasury, outside general revenue, administered by the commission for the purposes authorized by Chapter 34 of the Utilities Code. [e1] SB 2627, the Powering Texas Forward Act, created it. [e17] The bill is described as funding mechanisms to support the construction, maintenance, modernization and operation of electric generating facilities. [e16] In ERCOT it funds low-interest loans and completion bonus grants for dispatchable generation. [e2][e6]

## How it works

Under SB 2627 as enacted, loans can finance upgrades to existing dispatchable facilities that add at least 100 MW, or construction of dispatchable facilities of at least 100 MW. [e2] A loan has a 20-year term, ratable payments starting on the third anniversary of the estimated commercial operation date, and a three percent interest rate; a construction loan may cover up to 60 percent of the estimated cost. [e4][e5] Energy storage facilities are not eligible. [e3]

Completion bonus grants are paid on construction of dispatchable generation in the ERCOT region, based on megawatts provided. The SB 2627 cap is $120,000 per MW for facilities interconnected before June 1, 2026, and $80,000 per MW for those interconnected on or after June 1, 2026 and before June 1, 2029, unless the commission extends deadlines for extenuating circumstances. [e6][e7] The commission must set by rule performance standards for grant recipients, based on reliability metrics, for the 100 hours with the least operating reserves each year. [e13]

## ERCOT context

As enacted in SB 2627, the statute limits the fund: not more than $1 billion for grants under Section 34.0103, not more than $7.2 billion for loans and grants under Sections 34.0104 and 34.0105, and not more than $1.8 billion for grants or loans under Subchapter B. [e8] ERCOT-region figures here are from the SB 2627 text; the 89th Legislature later passed SB 2268 adjusting certain caps, requirements and deadlines, so check current values. [e17] Together, outstanding loans and bonus grants may not support more than 10,000 MW of capacity, and each funded facility must participate in the ERCOT wholesale market. [e12][e10] The PUCT was to start accepting loan applications by June 1, 2024 and to approve or deny each and disburse initial loan funds by December 31, 2025. [e9]

ERCOT's December 2025 CDR varies the amount and timing of TEF project capacity in two of its three alternative scenarios. [e15] One scenario assumes all TEF projects are in service on their reported dates, and brownfield sites at existing plants are expected to be less likely to be delayed. [e11][e14]

## Key facts

- Loans (SB 2627 as enacted): 20-year term, three percent interest, up to 60 percent of estimated facility cost for construction. [e4][e5]
- Completion bonus grants (SB 2627 as enacted): up to $120,000 per MW (interconnected before June 1, 2026) or $80,000 per MW (June 1, 2026 to before June 1, 2029). [e7]
- Fund ceilings (SB 2627 as enacted): $1 billion, $7.2 billion and $1.8 billion for its three purposes. [e8]
- Loans plus completion bonus grants may support no more than 10,000 MW (SB 2627 as enacted). [e12]
- Facilities funded by in-ERCOT loans and completion bonus grants must be dispatchable (energy storage is excluded) and participate in the ERCOT wholesale market. [e3][e6][e10]

## Say it like this

The Texas Energy Fund offers three percent, 20-year loans for dispatchable plants of at least 100 MW, plus per-MW completion bonuses for dispatchable plants built in ERCOT, within a 10,000 MW ceiling (as enacted in SB 2627). [e2][e4][e7][e12] ERCOT's planning reports then have to decide how much of that pipeline to count on. [e15]

## Common confusion

TEF money is not a market payment or a capacity market: it is a state loan and grant program run by the commission from a special state fund. [e1][e2] Its in-ERCOT loans and completion bonus grants do not fund energy storage facilities. [e3][e6] The $7.2 billion and 10,000 MW figures are ceilings, not amounts awarded. [e8][e12]

## Questions

### What does the Texas Energy Fund finance in ERCOT?

Low-interest loans for new or upgraded dispatchable generation of at least 100 MW, and completion bonus grants for dispatchable generation built in the ERCOT region. [e2][e6]

### What are the loan terms?

Twenty years, ratable payments starting at the third anniversary of the estimated commercial operation date, three percent interest, and up to 60 percent of estimated cost for construction loans. [e4][e5]

### How does ERCOT use TEF in its reliability reports?

The December 2025 CDR varies the amount and timing of TEF project capacity in two scenarios; one assumes all TEF projects are in service on their reported dates. [e15][e11]
