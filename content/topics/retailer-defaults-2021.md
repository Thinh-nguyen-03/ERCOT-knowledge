## Definition

The 2021 defaults are the payments that market participants failed to make to ERCOT for charges from Winter Storm Uri: about $2.9 billion of short payments by April 14, 2021, of which $1.86 billion related to Brazos Electric Power Cooperative, which filed for bankruptcy on March 1, 2021. [e1] Retailers such as Griddy Energy were among the other participants that had not paid. [e2]

## How it works

When participants short pay, ERCOT receives less than it invoiced. The UT report says ERCOT responded by reducing payments owed to holders of congestion revenue rights, and that under its standard Default Uplift Invoice process it would have taken 96 years to collect the amount outstanding. [e2][e1]

For retailers, the UT report says the damage depended on how well their price risk was hedged and how outages changed their obligations. [e3] Griddy, Entrust and Power of Texas filed for bankruptcy, lost their certificates, and had their customers moved to other retailers through ERCOT's mass transition process. [e3] Credit also bit: the IMM says high collateral requirements likely kept some participants out of the day-ahead market in February and March 2021. [e5]

## ERCOT context

Brazos accounted for $1.86 billion of the roughly $2.99 billion in short payments the UT report cites. [e1] The IMM's 2022 report says Brazos filed under Chapter 11 after failing to pay about $1.9 billion for energy and ancillary services bought during Uri. [e7] In the bankruptcy, Brazos tried to cut more than $1.1 billion from ERCOT's claim, the part tied to the PUCT's February 15 and 16, 2021 pricing orders. [e8] Under the settlement ERCOT was to be reimbursed $599,709,609.22 for CRR funds it had used to cover Brazos's short pay, and the ERCOT Board approved Brazos's amended plan on October 28, 2022, subject to bankruptcy court approval. [e9][e10]

The Legislature's answer was HB 4492, which defines a "default balance" of up to $800 million covering amounts competitive wholesale market participants owed ERCOT from the period of emergency that would otherwise be uplifted. [e6] That financing closed in November 2021 and ERCOT began collecting default charges in January 2022. [e11]

## Key facts

- Short payments were about $2.9 billion on April 14, 2021 and $2.99 billion on May 14, 2021 (UT report citing ERCOT). [e1]
- The IMM's 2021 report says short payments exceeded $3 billion. [e4]
- Brazos filed for Chapter 11 on March 1, 2021; the UT report attributes $1.86 billion of short payments to it, the IMM about $1.9 billion. [e1][e7]
- Griddy, Entrust and Power of Texas filed for bankruptcy and their customers were mass transitioned. [e3]
- The HB 4492 default balance is capped at $800 million. [e6]

## Timeline

- March 1, 2021: Brazos Electric Power Cooperative files for Chapter 11. [e7]
- April 14, 2021: ERCOT reports about $2.9 billion of cumulative short payments. [e1]
- May 14, 2021: estimate raised to $2.99 billion. [e1]
- November 2021: Subchapter M default balance financing closes. [e11]
- January 2022: ERCOT begins collecting default charges. [e11]
- October 28, 2022: ERCOT Board approves Brazos's amended Chapter 11 plan incorporating the ERCOT settlement. [e10]

## Say it like this

(Guidance) After Uri, about $3 billion owed to ERCOT went unpaid, most of it from Brazos Electric, plus retailers like Griddy, Entrust and Power of Texas that went bankrupt and had their customers mass transitioned. [e1][e3] The state financed part of the hole through HB 4492 so it would not all be uplifted to other participants at once. [e6]

## Common confusion

(Guidance) Brazos was an electric cooperative, which the IMM lists separately from the retail electric providers that exited, yet it accounted for $1.86 billion of the short payments. [e4][e1] Keep the figures apart: $2.9-3 billion is total short payments, about $1.9 billion is Brazos's share, and $800 million is only the cap on the default balance that HB 4492 financed. [e1][e4][e6]

## Questions

### Why did ERCOT not just uplift the short payments to everyone?

The UT report says that using its standard Default Uplift Invoice process it would have taken 96 years to collect the outstanding amount. [e1] HB 4492 instead let ERCOT finance a default balance of up to $800 million. [e6]

### How did the Brazos bankruptcy connect to the pricing orders?

Brazos sought to reduce ERCOT's claim by more than $1.1 billion, the portion attributable to the price adjustments under the PUCT's February 15 and 16 orders. [e8]

### What happened to Griddy's customers?

Griddy filed for bankruptcy, its certificate was revoked, and its customers were moved to other retailers through ERCOT's mass transition process. [e3]
