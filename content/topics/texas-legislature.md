## Definition

The Texas Legislature enacts the statutes that govern the ERCOT market, including the Public Utility Regulatory Act (PURA). [e10] In PURA it found that electric services and their prices should be determined by customer choices and the normal forces of competition, except for transmission and distribution services and stranded-cost recovery. [e1]

## How it works

Legislation sets the structure around ERCOT. PURA creates the ERCOT board selection committee, with one appointee each from the governor, the lieutenant governor and the speaker of the house. [e3] It requires that new or revised protocols not take effect until the PUCT approves a market impact statement. [e4] HB 1500 (2023) amended PURA so that an independent organization certified by the PUCT is directly responsible and accountable to the PUCT, and the PUCT may take appropriate action against one that does not perform adequately or comply. [e21]

The PUCT itself is a creature of statute: five commissioners appointed by the governor with Senate advice and consent, serving staggered six-year terms. [e13] PURA gives it the general power to regulate and supervise public utilities in its jurisdiction. [e15] It is subject to the Texas Sunset Act: under PURA Sec. 12.005 as amended by HB 1500, unless continued in existence as provided by Chapter 325 or Chapter 39, the commission is abolished September 1, 2029. [e12][e5]

The PUCT may impose an administrative penalty of up to $25,000 per violation of PURA, a rule or an order, with each day a separate violation, and up to $1,000,000 per violation for a violation of a voluntary mitigation plan or of Sec. 35.0021 or 38.075. [e14]

The Legislature also asks for information back. Each year by December 1 the PUCT must file a report with the legislature that includes estimated annual costs to load-serving entities of backing up dispatchable and non-dispatchable generation. [e2] The market monitor may appear and speak before the legislature without PUCT restriction. [e9]

### Limits on how the PUCT directs ERCOT

Under PURA Sec. 39.1514 (added by HB 1500), the PUCT may not use a verbal directive to direct an independent organization to take official action. It may do so only through a contested case, rulemaking, or a memorandum or written order adopted by majority vote. [e15] It must use a contested case or rulemaking for an action that creates a new cost or fee, increases an existing one, or imposes significant operational obligations on an entity. [e16]

## ERCOT context

Recent sessions have written market and reliability design directly into PURA, for example the emergency pricing program (2021), the dispatchable reliability reserve program (2023) and large-load demand management (2025). [e25][e17][e7] Each law below is dated by session and effective date.

### 87th Legislature (2021): SB 3 and HB 4492 after Winter Storm Uri

SB 3 added PURA Sec. 35.0021, under which the PUCT by rule requires municipally owned utilities, cooperatives, power generation companies and exempt wholesale generators selling at wholesale in ERCOT to implement weather-emergency preparation measures according to reliability standards the PUCT adopts. [e23] It added Sec. 39.159, directing the PUCT to ensure that the independent organization establishes reliability requirements and procures ancillary or reliability services competitively. [e24] It added Sec. 39.160, requiring an emergency pricing program that takes effect if the high system-wide offer cap has been in effect for 12 hours in a 24-hour period after first reaching it, and that must allow generators to be reimbursed for reasonable, verifiable operating costs above the emergency cap. [e25][e26] SB 3 also added Sec. 38.076, which requires a system to allocate load shedding among cooperatives, municipally owned utilities and TDUs based on historical seasonal peak demand in each territory; the history note shows it effective June 8, 2021. [e27][e28]

HB 4492 authorized debt financing for Winter Storm Uri balances. The "default balance" is not more than $800 million of amounts owed by competitive wholesale market participants, financial revenue auction receipts used to reduce short-payments, and reasonable implementation costs. [e29] The "uplift balance" is not more than $2.1 billion uplifted to load-serving entities on a load ratio share basis for reliability deployment price adder charges and ancillary service costs above the system-wide offer cap, excluding amounts securitized under Chapter 41 Subchapter D, and it does not include amounts that were part of the prevailing settlement point price. [e31] Both refer to the "period of emergency" from 12:01 a.m. February 12, 2021 to 11:59 p.m. February 20, 2021. [e30] A debt obligation order for the default balance must state the repayment period, which may not exceed 30 years. [e32]

### 88th Legislature (2023): HB 1500, SB 2627 and SB 1929

HB 1500 took effect September 1, 2023. [e22] It added PURA §39.1514 on PUCT directives to ERCOT. [e10][e15] It added Sec. 39.159(d), requiring an ancillary services program to procure dispatchable reliability reserve services day-ahead and real-time, with resource criteria including capability to run at least four hours at the high sustained limit, being online and dispatchable no more than two hours after being called, and dispatchable flexibility for inter-hour challenges. [e17][e18] The PUCT was to require implementation not later than December 1, 2024. [e19] The Act also directed a PUCT and ERCOT study, to be reported to the legislature not later than December 1, 2025, on whether an alternative to the single market clearing price would reduce costs to residential and small commercial customers or their load-serving entities. [e20]

SB 2627 (the Powering Texas Forward Act) added PURA Chapter 34. The Texas energy fund is a special fund in the state treasury administered by the PUCT. [e33] Loans may finance upgrades to existing dispatchable generation or construction of dispatchable generation of at least 100 MW; an electric energy storage facility is not eligible. [e34] A construction loan may not exceed 60 percent of estimated facility cost and must be senior debt secured by the facility. [e35] The Act takes effect on the date the related constitutional amendment takes effect, and has no effect if voters did not approve it. [e36] An earlier 2023 law, SB 1929, led to large flexible load registration, covering virtual currency mining facilities of more than 75 megawatts with interruptible load. [e8][e11]

### 89th Legislature (2025): SB 6 and SB 2268

SB 6 passed the Senate March 19, 2025 (31 to 0) and the House May 27, 2025 (103 to 25, two present not voting), and the Senate concurred in House amendments May 29, 2025 (31 to 0). [e52][e53] It was signed by the Governor June 20, 2025 and effective immediately; the bill itself provides for immediate effect on a two-thirds vote of each house, and otherwise September 1, 2025. [e54][e51][e50] Its parts:

- Cost recovery: the PUCT by rule must ensure a large load customer subject to the Sec. 37.0561 standards contributes to the recovery of the interconnecting utility's interconnection costs. [e38] That Sec. 35.004(c-1) applies only to interconnection agreements entered into on or after the effective date. [e49]
- Interconnection standards: the PUCT by rule sets standards for interconnecting large loads in ERCOT; they apply to new or expanded interconnections above a demand threshold the PUCT sets, which the PUCT shall set at 75 megawatts unless it determines a lower threshold is necessary. [e39] The standards must set a flat study fee of at least $100,000, with any unused portion credited toward later obligations at the same site. [e40] They must include uniform financial commitment requirements for needed transmission infrastructure, which may include security per megawatt, a contribution in aid of construction, advance payment for significant equipment or services, or another form the PUCT accepts. [e41]
- Backup generation: large loads must disclose on-site backup generation, and after ERCOT deploys all available market services except frequency responsive services it may direct the utility to require the customer to deploy backup generation or curtail load, counted as firm load shed for price adjustments, without authorizing an emissions violation or barring participation in the Sec. 39.170(b) service. [e42]
- Co-location: a generator, municipally owned utility or cooperative must notify ERCOT before a net metering arrangement between a generation resource registered as stand-alone as of September 1, 2025 and a new large load. [e43] ERCOT has 120 days to study and the PUCT 60 days after to approve, deny or condition; if it does not act in time the arrangement is considered approved. [e44]
- Demand management: Sec. 39.170 requires a curtailment protocol for transmission-voltage customers interconnected after December 31, 2025 (excluding critical load industrial customers and critical natural gas facilities), and a reliability service to competitively procure demand reductions from large loads of at least 75 megawatts. [e45][e7] The service rules must set usable periods, give at least 24 hours' notice, require loads to stay curtailed for the EEA event or until safely recalled, and bar participation by loads that curtail in response to wholesale price or participate in another reliability or ancillary service. [e46]
- Transmission cost allocation: the PUCT must evaluate whether the four coincident peak methodology ensures all loads contribute to cost recovery and whether alternatives such as multiple seasonal peaks or peak energy intervals would better assign costs. [e47] It must begin the evaluation not later than the 90th day after the effective date and, not later than December 31, 2026, amend its rules to ensure wholesale transmission charges appropriately assign costs for transmission investment. [e48]

SB 2268 (2025) amended Sec. 34.0104(l) so that the PUCT may disburse initial loan funds after December 31, 2025 if market factors necessitate an extension. [e37]

## Key facts

- PURA states a policy preference for customer choice and competition, except for transmission and distribution and stranded-cost recovery. [e1]
- ERCOT board selection committee: one appointee each from the governor, lieutenant governor and house speaker. [e3]
- PUCT is abolished September 1, 2029 unless continued in existence (PURA Sec. 12.005 as amended by HB 1500, effective September 1, 2023). [e12][e22]
- SB 6 (2025, effective June 20, 2025) added PURA §39.170 on large load demand management. [e6][e7][e51]
- SB 6 requires the PUCT to amend transmission cost rules not later than December 31, 2026. [e48]
- HB 1500 requires a dispatchable reliability reserve program, to be implemented not later than December 1, 2024. [e17][e19]

## Say it like this

(Guidance) The Legislature sets the rules of the game in PURA and amends them by statute, as with the large-load rules in SB 6. Protocol changes still need PUCT approval before they take effect. [e4]

## Common confusion

(Guidance) A statute does not change the Protocols by itself. PURA directs the PUCT and ERCOT, and protocol changes still need PUCT approval before taking effect. [e4] Also, a bill that says "the commission shall" usually sets a deadline and a topic for a PUCT rulemaking, so the detailed numbers (such as large-load fees) arrive in PUCT rules, not in the bill. [e38][e40][e48]

## Questions

### How does the Legislature influence ERCOT's governance?

Through PURA: it creates the board selection committee, requires a formal protocol process with PUCT approval, makes the PUCT subject to the Texas Sunset Act, and says the independent organization is directly accountable to the PUCT. [e3][e4][e12][e21]

### What did SB 6 add for large loads?

PURA §39.170, which requires a reliability service to competitively procure demand reductions from large load customers of at least 75 megawatts in an anticipated emergency, plus interconnection standards (Sec. 37.0561), net metering review (Sec. 39.169) and a transmission cost evaluation. [e6][e7][e39][e43][e48]

### Why did the Legislature restrict verbal PUCT directives in HB 1500?

The saved text shows the rule but not the Legislature's reason. It says the PUCT may direct the independent organization to act only through a contested case, rulemaking or a written order or memorandum adopted by majority vote, and must use a contested case or rulemaking where the action creates cost or significant obligations. [e15][e16]

### What if the Legislature does not continue the PUCT?

Under PURA Sec. 12.005 it is abolished September 1, 2029 unless continued in existence as provided by the Sunset Act or Chapter 39. [e12]

### Why does SB 6 tell the PUCT to look at the four coincident peak method?

The bill asks the PUCT to evaluate whether the 4CP method ensures all loads appropriately contribute to recovery of transmission costs and whether alternatives would assign costs more appropriately. [e47] The saved text does not state the Legislature's motive beyond those evaluation questions.

### What would an emergency pricing program trigger look like under SB 3?

Sec. 39.160 says the program takes effect if the high system-wide offer cap has been in effect for 12 hours in a 24-hour period after initially reaching it, with generators reimbursable for reasonable, verifiable costs above the emergency cap. [e25][e26]

## Scenarios

### A data center wants to co-locate behind the meter with an operating stand-alone generator.

- It falls under SB 6 Sec. 39.169 if the generator was registered as stand-alone as of September 1, 2025: the notice goes to ERCOT before implementation. [e43]
- ERCOT studies system impacts within 120 days; the PUCT approves, denies or imposes conditions within 60 days of receiving the study, otherwise the arrangement is considered approved. [e44]
