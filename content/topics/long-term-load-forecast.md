## Definition

The IMM describes the long-term load forecast (LTLF) as ERCOT's primary annual tool for projecting system load growth over a ten-year horizon. [e2] The saved 2025 report's Appendix A table itself lists the years 2025 to 2031. [e5] The 2025 edition is called the Long-Term Demand and Energy Forecast (LTDEF). [e25] It was built from six parts: base economic load, EVs, rooftop PV, large flexible load, Large Load Contracts and Large Load Officer Letters. [e1]

## How it works

The 2025 forecast stacks its parts in a "waterfall": base economic + EV + LFL + adjusted contracts + adjusted officer letters, minus PV. [e3] A Contract was a prospective load with a signed agreement and a financial commitment; an Officer Letter was a request without a signed agreement, whose viability a TSP officer attested in a letter to an ERCOT officer. [e57] ERCOT did not take TSP-reported large loads at face value. It delayed their ramp schedules by 180 days, cut data center additions to 49.8% and cut officer-letter additions to 55.4%. [e4] Appendix A of the 2025 report lists a 2031 summer peak of 144,522 MW; the table does not say whether it is the ERCOT Adjusted or the TSP Provided case. [e5]

For the 2026 cycle the PUCT's rule 16 TAC §25.370 (effective March 1, 2026, Project 58480) changed the inputs. [e37] A "large load customer" is one with total expected non-coincident peak demand of 75 MW or more at a single site. [e27] ERCOT must not include such a customer's demand in a forecast used for transmission planning or resource adequacy unless the customer executed an interconnection agreement under §25.194 and provided the disclosures and financial commitments that rule requires. [e28] A DSP submitting load data must attach a notarized attestation, sworn by someone with binding authority, that each large load customer meets the §25.194 interconnection-agreement criteria. [e29] The IMM summarizes the change as including large loads by development milestones, replacing the old signed-agreement-or-officer-letter method. [e6]

ERCOT must use the DSP data, but it may adjust it in two ways. It may correct errors or remove withdrawn or cancelled requests without PUCT approval, but only if the DSP agrees. [e30] Any other adjustment needs PUCT approval, which the PUCT may give only if the adjustment is supported by actual historical realization rates or other objective, credible, independent information; ERCOT must file and publish market notice at least 30 days before the open meeting, unless the PUCT chooses to take it up earlier. [e31] The rule also lets ERCOT use different forecasts for different study scopes and horizons, and requires an annual assessment comparing past forecasts to outcomes, identifying sources of error and recommending improvements. [e32] The IMM, for its part, said it would submit its detailed analysis of the load forecast through the stakeholder process rather than in its 2025 report. [e61]

Because the permanent rule could not be built into the Protocols in time, §25.370(g) required a compliance plan for the 2026 Regional Transmission Plan: load data due by April 1, 2026, and each included large load must have an executed agreement meeting listed criteria. [e33] The criteria include disclosure of any separate request for service elsewhere, site control (a lease of at least five years from the date the load expects to reach contracted peak demand, or a deed), a load ramping schedule if applicable, and a financial commitment such as security of $100,000 per MW of contracted peak demand. [e33][e34] The other financial-commitment options are security equal to the DSP and TSP's expected cost of equipment with a lead time of at least six months, or a CIAC payment equal to the directly attributable interconnection costs. [e35] Until any protocols or compliance plan were in place, ERCOT had to keep using its prior forecast practices. [e36] ERCOT filed its proposed compliance plan in Project 58480 on January 22, 2026, and the plan sets interim criteria until the permanent §25.370 criteria can be implemented. [e38][e39]

## Formula

The 2025 waterfall: Net Forecast = Base Economic Forecast + EV Forecast + LFL Forecast + Adjusted Contracts + Adjusted Officer Letters - PV Forecast. "Adjusted" means after the 180-day delay and the 49.8% (data centers) and 55.4% (officer letters) haircuts. [e3][e4]

Option A (proposed May 2026, not adopted): historical realization rate = number of computational Large Loads observed energized by March 1, 2026, divided by the number of computational Large Loads that TDSPs identified in the 2025 Large Load RFI. [e40] "Computational" means data centers and cryptomining facilities only. [e41] Applied to the 2026 LTLF, this would have cut all computational Large Loads by 32%. [e9]

Option B (approved June 2026): adjusted LTLF = base economic forecast + medium loads + the Batch Zero base loads that ERCOT identifies under Planning Guide §9.3.1(2)(a) in PGRR145, replacing the Large Loads TDSPs submitted under the 2026 RTP Compliance Plan. [e43][e11]

## ERCOT context

The preliminary 2026 LTLF, filed April 15, 2026, showed about 278,003 MW in 2029 and 367,790 MW by 2032. [e7] Its top line adds new Medium (25 to 74.9 MW) and Large (75 MW and above) Loads that TSPs reported to the base forecast. [e60] The IMM notes that TDSPs normally submit large-load ramp schedules by the end of January, but in this cycle ERCOT extended the deadline to April to let SB 6 rulemakings progress. [e59] ERCOT said it had concerns with using the preliminary values for the Reliability Assessment and other planning analysis. [e20] It expected the summer 2026 peak to be about 90,500 to 98,000 MW, while the preliminary LTLF said about 112,000 MW. [e8] At the April 17, 2026 open meeting the PUCT indicated that an adjustment to a more realistic value may be warranted, and on May 7 ERCOT confirmed it would propose one. [e47]

ERCOT's May 18, 2026 filing offered two fixes. Option A cut computational large loads by 32% based on historical realization. Option B used Batch Zero Base Load, and ERCOT recommended using the Batch Zero forecast. [e9][e23][e10] ERCOT did not propose adjusting medium loads; in the 2032 LTLF there were 258 medium loads totaling 13,473 MW, of which 157 computational loads totaled 8,766 MW. [e42] ERCOT's stated trade-off was timing: with Option A, it anticipated Reliability Assessment results in September or October 2026; with Option B, it expected the LTLF could be finalized only in mid-August and the assessment delayed to November or December 2026. [e44][e45] ERCOT said it was confident the Batch Zero values would be significantly lower than the compliance-plan values. [e46] PUCT Staff's June 11 memo recommended Option B. [e48] The PUCT approved Option B and ordered ERCOT to file the resulting MW values in Project 59772 once they are known. [e11][e49] The same day (June 18, 2026) it approved PGRR145, the Batch Zero rule. [e50] It also let ERCOT skip the May 2026 CDR and the 2026 RTP. [e18]

The Batch Zero numbers then moved in stages. ERCOT's July 29, 2026 Senate slides said classifications would issue no later than August 7, and on preliminary July 28 data, about 205 GW of Large Load was eligible for Batch Zero based on existing studies. [e51][e52] In August 2026, ERCOT said base load plus mid-size loads alone came to about 110 GW in 2032, before adding any Batch loads, and that it expected a final forecast with Batch loads by the end of 2026. [e14][e19] A September 22, 2026 ERCOT slide put the Batch Zero conditional base and studied load at about 100 GW in 2028 to about 192 GW in 2032, and said the total load forecast "may potentially" reach 200 GW for 2028 and 300 GW for 2032. [e53][e15] On November 6, 2026 ERCOT told the PUCT it still did not have a final load forecast, including an official winter 2026-2027 peak forecast. [e26]

The missing forecast holds up other filings. ERCOT normally gives Transmission Operators the 75th percentile winter peak forecast (excluding load curtailable under PURA §§37.0561(e) and 39.169) to size the reliability standard's magnitude value, and in its November 6 motion it asked to file the 2027 magnitude by the second quarter of 2027 instead of December 1, 2026. [e54][e55][e26] That request was a motion with a proposed order; no ruling is in the saved sources.

## Common confusion

(Guidance) The "forecast peak" depends on which forecast and which date you mean. The 2025 report's Appendix A lists 144,522 MW for 2031. [e5] The IMM's 2025 report cites 138 to 148 GW for 2030, with a high scenario up to 209 GW. [e16] The preliminary 2026 LTLF showed 367,790 MW for 2032. [e7] ERCOT's September 2026 view was "potentially" 300 GW for 2032. [e15] None of these is the final adjusted 2026 LTLF, which ERCOT said it did not yet have as of November 6, 2026. [e26]

(Guidance) Do not mix up the three Batch Zero numbers: about 205 GW was preliminarily "eligible" on July 28 data [e52]; "conditional base and studied load" was about 192 GW for 2032 in September [e53]; and the LTLF adjustment uses only the base loads, added to the base economic forecast and medium loads. [e43] Also, "rejected" applies to Option A only; the 32% figure was never applied. [e9][e11]

## Questions

### Why did ERCOT ask to adjust its own forecast in 2026?

The preliminary LTLF reached 367,790 MW by 2032, with the vast majority attributable to Large Loads from TDSP submissions. [e7][e21] ERCOT said it had concerns with using the preliminary values for the Reliability Assessment and other planning analysis, and noted that its own summer 2026 outlook was 90,500 to 98,000 MW against the forecast's 112,000 MW. [e20][e8]

### What did the PUCT decide?

It approved Option B, which adjusts the forecast with Batch Zero Base Load instead of a 32% historical-realization cut, and ordered ERCOT to file the MW values once known. [e9][e23][e11][e49] In June 2026 the adjusted values were expected to wait on Batch Zero classifications in mid-August [e12]; on November 6, 2026 ERCOT said it still had no final load forecast. [e26]

### Why could ERCOT not simply cut the forecast on its own?

Under §25.370(e)(2), ERCOT can adjust DSP data without the PUCT only to fix errors or remove withdrawn or cancelled requests, and only if the DSP agrees. [e30] Anything else needs PUCT approval, supported by historical realization rates or other objective, credible, independent information, with a filing and market notice at least 30 days ahead. [e31]

### Why did ERCOT prefer the slower option?

ERCOT argued that Batch Zero leads to an actionable transmission plan and is therefore a firmer starting point, and that the benefit of a more reliable forecast outweighs the extra time. [e10][e45] The cost was timing: ERCOT expected Option B to push the Triennial Reliability Assessment results to November or December 2026, against September or October under Option A. [e44][e45]

### What if a data center developer has a TSP officer letter but no signed agreement?

Under the 2025 method it could enter the forecast as an Officer Letter load, at 55.4% after a 180-day delay. [e57][e4] Under §25.370 it cannot enter a planning or resource adequacy forecast until it executes a §25.194 interconnection agreement and makes the required disclosures and financial commitments. [e28]

### How did the summer 2026 actual compare?

The record peak was 91,134 MW on July 22, 2026. [e13] That is inside ERCOT's April range of 90,500 to 98,000 MW and well below the preliminary LTLF's roughly 112,000 MW. [e8]

### What else waits on the final forecast?

The 2026 Triennial Reliability Assessment, for which ERCOT recommended the adjusted LTLF as the load input. [e10][e45] Also the reliability standard magnitude value: on November 6, 2026 ERCOT asked to file the 2027 value by Q2 2027 because TOs need the winter peak forecast to answer its RFIs. [e26][e55]
