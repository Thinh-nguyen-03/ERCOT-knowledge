## Definition

The ERCOT Protocols are the document adopted by ERCOT, created with representatives of all segments of Market Participants, containing the scheduling, operating, planning, reliability and Settlement (including Customer registration) policies, rules, guidelines, procedures, standards and criteria of ERCOT. [e1] Market Participants, the IMM and ERCOT must abide by them. [e3]

## How it works

The Protocols implement ERCOT's functions as the PUCT-certified Independent Organization for the ERCOT Region and as Program Administrator of the REC Program. [e3] The version in effect when an action is performed or not performed governs that action. [e2]

### Other Binding Documents and precedence

"Other Binding Documents" are the policies, guidelines, procedures, forms and applications that the ERCOT Board, TAC, other authorized ERCOT subcommittees or ERCOT may develop for implementing and operating under the Protocols. [e4] They exclude ERCOT's internal administrative procedures needed for its role as Independent Organization or as a NERC-registered entity. [e16] ERCOT posts the list and the documents, and a TAC designated subcommittee reviews the list at least every four years. [e17] A revision of an Other Binding Document follows the process in that document; if it has none, it goes through Section 21 as if it were a Protocol. [e18]

Together with the Protocols they make up the scheduling, operating, planning, reliability and Settlement rules that bind Market Participants under PURA section 39.151. [e6]

When texts conflict, the order is: Sections 1 through 21 and 24 through 27 prevail over conflicting attachments and Other Binding Documents; an Agreement prevails over the Protocols to the extent of a conflict [e5]; and an Agreement provision that deviates from the Protocols is effective only upon ERCOT Board approval on a showing of good cause. [e15] The Protocols are not intended to govern direct relationships among Market Participants except as they expressly provide. [e19]

### Reading the text

Capitalized terms take their meaning from Section 2, but a definition in another Section controls in that Section. [e24] "Shall" denotes a duty, "will" a duty unless context says otherwise, "must" a condition precedent or subsequent, "may" a privilege or discretionary power, and "may not" a prohibition. [e23] Times are Central Prevailing Time on a 24-hour clock unless noted. [e25]

### Changing the Protocols

Changes go through a Nodal Protocol Revision Request. [e9] Parties must use that process before asking the PUCT or another Governmental Authority to make ERCOT change the Protocols, except for good cause shown to the PUCT or other authority. [e27] PRS reviews and recommends action on revision requests, and after a TAC report and impact analysis the ERCOT Board reviews them at its next regularly scheduled meeting. [e11][e12] All revision requests require PUCT approval before implementation. [e10] An Urgent track exists where an existing Protocol or condition is impairing or could imminently impair reliability or market operations, or creates a discrepancy between a settlement formula and the Protocols. [e13] Approved changes are filed with the PUCT for information and posted at least a day before they take effect. [e28] See the nprr topic for the steps.

## ERCOT context

The statute behind this is PURA section 39.151: protocols adopted by an independent organization are subject to commission oversight and may not take effect before commission approval, and revised protocols may not take effect until the commission approves a market impact statement. [e7][e8]

Section 1.2 sets ERCOT's role. ERCOT is the central counterparty for transactions it settles, the sole buyer to each seller and sole seller to each buyer of energy, Ancillary Services, RUCs, ERS and other products, except bilateral and self-arranged products. [e20] ERCOT may not profit from its Independent Organization activities and may not use discretion in procuring Ancillary Services or deploying energy to influence, set or control prices. [e21] Nothing in the Protocols transfers control of TSP, DSP or Resource Facilities to ERCOT. [e22] ERCOT must also act to keep non-"public utility" Entities in the ERCOT Region from becoming public utilities under the Federal Power Act. [e33]

The published Protocol text carries boxes for revisions to apply "upon system implementation". Section 1.8 requires approved provisions not yet implemented to be set out in boxes within the Protocols. [e26] For example, Section 4.4.10 (Credit Requirement for DAM Bids and Offers) of the DAM text contains a box beginning "NPRR1188: Replace paragraph (a) above with the following upon system implementation". [e14] Anything in such a box is pending, not current.

## Key facts

- The Protocols cover scheduling, operating, planning, reliability and Settlement (including Customer registration) rules. [e1]
- Market Participants, the IMM and ERCOT shall abide by the Protocols. [e3]
- The version in effect at the time of an action governs it. [e2]
- Other Binding Documents are policies, guidelines, procedures, forms or applications that the ERCOT Board, TAC, other authorized subcommittees or ERCOT may develop for the Protocols. [e4]
- Precedence: Sections 1-21 and 24-27 over conflicting attachments and Other Binding Documents; Agreements over the Protocols, with deviations effective only on Board approval for good cause. [e15]
- All revision requests need PUCT approval before implementation. [e10]
- Statute: protocols may not take effect before commission approval. [e7]
- Approved-but-not-yet-implemented provisions must sit in boxes (Section 1.8). [e26]

## Say it like this

The Protocols are the rulebook ERCOT runs the market by, covering everything from day-ahead operations to settlement. Changes are made through NPRRs that go through the stakeholder process and need PUCT approval.

## Common confusion

The Protocols are not the same as the PUCT's substantive rules or PURA, though the Protocols are subject to PUCT approval and rest on PURA. [e7][e6] Also, a pending NPRR in a box is not yet the rule: read the text outside the box. [e14][e26] And the Protocols are not the only binding text: Other Binding Documents also bind Market Participants, but Sections 1 through 21 and 24 through 27 of the Protocols prevail over them where they conflict (with exceptions the Protocols set out, and an Agreement prevails over the Protocols). [e6][e15]

## Questions

### Who has to follow the Protocols?

Market Participants, the IMM and ERCOT. [e3]

### How does a Protocol change get made?

Someone submits an NPRR; PRS reviews it and recommends action, a TAC Report and Impact Analysis go to the ERCOT Board, which reviews them at its next regularly scheduled meeting, and the PUCT must approve before implementation. [e9][e11][e12][e10]

### Why does the PUCT approve ERCOT's Protocols?

PURA section 39.151 says protocols adopted by an independent organization are subject to commission oversight and may not take effect before commission approval. [e7]

### What do the boxes in the Protocol text mean?

Section 1.8 requires provisions approved through Section 21 but not yet implemented (or awaiting prerequisites) to be set out in boxes. [e26] Treat the boxed text as approved-but-pending, and the unboxed text as the rule in force.

### Why can a contract override a Protocol?

Section 1.1(6) says an Agreement prevails over a Protocol to the extent of a conflict, but an Agreement provision that deviates from the Protocols is effective only with ERCOT Board approval on a showing of good cause. [e15]

### What if a party thinks a Protocol is wrong and goes straight to the PUCT?

Parties are required to use the Section 21 process first, except for good cause shown to the PUCT or other authority. [e27]

### Where is a counterparty's obligation, "shall" or "may"?

"Shall" is a duty and "may" a discretionary power, so reading the verb tells you whether ERCOT or a Market Participant has a choice. [e23]
