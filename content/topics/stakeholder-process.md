## Definition

The stakeholder process is the committee path that ERCOT Protocol changes follow: the Protocol Revision Subcommittee (PRS), then the Technical Advisory Committee (TAC), then the ERCOT Board, then the PUCT. [e3][e4][e5][e6] PURA requires ERCOT's governing body to maintain a formal process for adopting new or revised protocols as a condition of keeping its certification. [e7]

## How it works

PRS reviews and recommends action on formally submitted revision requests. [e12] Its meetings are open to ERCOT, ERCOT Members, Market Participants, the Reliability Monitor, the NERC Regional Entity, the IMM and PUCT Staff. [e1] Each Market Segment may participate and each has equal voting power. [e2] PRS can send a request to working groups or TAC subcommittees for expertise, but PRS keeps ultimate responsibility for processing it. [e3]

TAC takes up requests that PRS has sent forward. It can recommend approval as recommended or as modified, reject, table, remand to PRS with instructions, or refer to another TAC subcommittee or working group. [e4] The ERCOT Board then reviews the TAC Report and the Impact Analysis at its next regularly scheduled meeting. [e5] Every revision request then needs PUCT approval before implementation. [e6]

PRS decisions can be challenged. Any ERCOT Member, Market Participant, PUCT Staff, the Reliability Monitor, the IMM, the NERC Regional Entity or ERCOT may appeal a PRS decision to reject, table or refer a request directly to TAC. [e9]

## ERCOT context

Other subcommittees appear in the Protocols' acronym list: the Wholesale Market Subcommittee (WMS) and the Reliability and Operations Subcommittee (ROS). [e10][e11] The saved Protocol text for the revision process (Section 21) names PRS and TAC as the committees on the NPRR path, and it treats other TAC subcommittees as places PRS or TAC can refer a request. [e3][e4]

The top of the path is shaped by statute. ERCOT board members are chosen by a selection committee made up of one appointee each from the governor, the lieutenant governor and the speaker of the house. [e8]

## Key facts

- Path for an NPRR: PRS, then TAC, then ERCOT Board, then PUCT. [e3][e4][e5][e6]
- Each Market Segment has equal voting power at PRS. [e2]
- TAC can recommend, reject, table, remand to PRS, or refer to another subcommittee. [e4]
- PUCT approval is required for every revision request before implementation. [e6]
- PURA conditions ERCOT's certification on a formal protocol-adoption process. [e7]

## Say it like this

(Guidance) Protocol changes in ERCOT are worked out in open stakeholder committees, PRS first and TAC second, and then go to the ERCOT Board and the PUCT. The committees recommend; the PUCT has the final say.

## Common confusion

(Guidance) A TAC vote is not the end of the line. TAC only recommends, and the Board and then the PUCT must act before a change is implemented. [e5][e6] Also, WMS and ROS are subcommittees listed in the Protocols' acronyms, but the saved text does not put them on the standard NPRR route; PRS is the subcommittee that reviews NPRRs. [e3][e10][e11]

## Questions

### What does PRS do?

It reviews and recommends action on formally submitted revision requests, and it keeps ultimate responsibility for processing them even when it refers them out for expertise. [e12][e3]

### What can TAC do with a request from PRS?

It can recommend approval as recommended or modified, reject, table, remand to PRS with instructions, or refer it to another TAC subcommittee or working group. [e4]

### Who must approve a change before it is implemented?

The PUCT. All revision requests require PUCT approval before implementation. [e6]
