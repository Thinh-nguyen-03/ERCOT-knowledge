## Definition

Competitive Renewable Energy Zones (CREZ) were a set of zones for which the Texas Legislature, via SB 408 in 2005, directed the PUCT to facilitate the design and construction of new transmission. [e1] SB 408 provided authority for the PUCT to direct ERCOT to plan for transmission to connect approximately 18 GW of wind capacity. [e3]

## How it works

The mechanism in the saved sources is legislative: SB 408 designated the zones and authorized the PUCT to direct ERCOT to plan the transmission. [e3] It came alongside an increase in the renewable portfolio standard (RPS) to 5,880 MW of renewable capacity. [e1]

## ERCOT context

By the end of 2020, approximately 25,000 MW of wind and 4,000 MW of solar photovoltaic capacity were installed in ERCOT, surpassing the RPS. [e2]

## Key facts

- SB 408 (2005) created the CREZ framework and raised the RPS to 5,880 MW. [e1]
- The transmission planning was for roughly 18 GW of wind capacity. [e3]
- End of 2020: about 25,000 MW of wind and 4,000 MW of solar PV installed in ERCOT. [e2]

## Say it like this

(Guidance) CREZ was the Texas legislative policy to build new transmission to designated renewable zones, with planning aimed at about 18 GW of wind. [e3] This entry only covers the origin, not the build-out details.

## Common confusion

CREZ is the transmission-planning policy, not a market product like a CRR; the saved sources do not describe CREZ as a settlement or pricing mechanism. [e1][e3]
